If the step is a cycle rather than a structural change, mean reversion does not take FCFF from $38M to $32M, it takes it toward $20M — and the market's -11.7% a year, which lands almost exactly there over five years, is not pessimism but arithmetic. Note that the screen's peak-cycle flag cannot catch this: it compares the newest FCF in the window to the oldest, and all three years in the window are at the elevated level, so the flag stays silent precisely when the whole window is the peak.
- What would prove it: Gross margin and pre-tax margin over the next three or four quarters, decomposed against unit growth. If revenue keeps falling low-to-mid single digits while pre-tax margin compresses from ~12.5% toward the high single digits, the reversion is happening and the market is right. If revenue stabilises and margin holds near 12% while the international and franchise mix keeps growing, the step change was structural and I am right.
