Across the whole grid the gap ranges -36.8% to +241.1% — that spread is the honest precision of this model, not the point estimate.

**The case for the price.** Magnolia is the low-leverage, low-reinvestment Eagle Ford/Austin Chalk operator: 106 MBoe/d, $125M of quarterly capital expenditure, no revolver drawn and $295.9M of cash at 2026-06-30, three years of remarkably flat free cash flow ($430.9M, $434.1M, $409.2M) and a stated policy of shrinking the share count. At $26.80 the market pays about 15x that free cash flow. It is now paying it for a company that has just agreed to double down: the $4B WildFire acquisition adds 53 MBoe/d at ~70% oil across ~810,000 net acres, taking the combined business to roughly 160 MBoe/d with a much higher oil weighting. The bull case is that Magnolia bought PDP-heavy, oil-weighted acreage adjacent to its own at a defensible price using permanent capital; the bear case is that it paid $4B near the top of a cycle and levered a balance sheet whose main attraction was that it had no leverage.

**What changed.** Everything material, and all of it after the 2026-06-30 balance sheet the model prices. On 2026-07-19 Magnolia agreed to acquire 100% of WildFire Intermediate Holdings from WildFire Energy I LLC for approximately $4 billion — about $2.65B of cash, 32.2 million Class A shares, and the assumption of $600M of WildFire's 7.50% senior notes due 2029 — with an effective date of 2026-06-01 and closing expected roughly 45 days after signing, i.e. around now. The funding is already done: a 53.3 million share public offering priced 2026-07-20 at $23.75 raising approximately $1.2B net and completed 2026-07-22; $500M of 6.625% senior notes due 2034 issued 2026-08-05 (net ~$492.5M); and a third amended and restated reserve-based revolving credit facility with $2.25B of commitments, a $2B borrowing base and $1.75B of borrowing capacity. A bridge facility was arranged and is not expected to be drawn. At 2026-06-30 there were 183.7 million Class A shares outstanding.

**Base case.** Magnolia's own model is the opposite of a growth story: it targets low-single-digit production growth, funds capital expenditure well within cash flow, and returns the balance through buybacks and a growing dividend. WildFire changes the size of the company, not the philosophy — it is PDP-weighted acreage (100.3 MMBbls of proved developed oil within 196.5 MMBbls of proved oil) bought to add oil-weighted volumes and inventory, not to inflect the growth rate. So my five-year FCFF growth assumption for the combined entity is low single digits in nominal terms: roughly 2%, which is production growth of about 1-2% plus a flat real oil price, less the higher interest burden on roughly $2.1B of new and assumed debt where there was almost none. I differ sharply from the naive baseline of +19.7%: that is a five-year revenue CAGR measured from the 2021 trough in oil prices, which says nothing about forward cash flow for a mature Eagle Ford operator, and it is the sole reason this name shows a +202% gap.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest case that I am wrong to record no_edge is that the reverse DCF's -5.6% implied growth genuinely is too pessimistic for an operator whose free cash flow has been flat within 6% for three straight years across a wide oil-price range, and that buying it as it doubles its oil-weighted production is exactly when you want to own it. That argument is coherent. It fails on the discount rate, not on the business: the -5.6% is solved at a 6.7% WACC built on a beta of 0.38 whose R-squared is 0.047, and the sensitivity is on the face of the brief — one point of WACC moves implied growth by roughly four points. At a defensible exploration-and-production beta of 1.1-1.3 the WACC is 10-11%, and the growth the current price requires goes from -5.6% to roughly +10-14%. The name flips from deeply cheap to demanding on the discount rate alone, which means the gap is not information.
- What would prove it: The closing 8-K and the first post-close 10-Q: actual RBL draw, pro-forma net debt, and combined production and capital expenditure. Separately, a beta regression with an R-squared above ~0.15 — Energy is the highest-beta cohort in this index, so 0.38 is a measurement failure, not defensiveness.
- Already visible today: Yes, on both counts. The financing is public and complete — $1.2B of equity, $500M of new notes, $600M of assumed notes, a $2.25B facility — none of which is in the enterprise value the model used. And the low R-squared is printed on the brief.
- Left unresolved: I could not put a number on the combined entity's normalized free cash flow, because WildFire's standalone cash flow is not disclosed beyond production and capital expenditure guidance ($110-120M of capex against 53 MBoe/d in Q2 2026). Until pro-forma financials are filed, any FCFF base for the combined company is a guess and I have not manufactured one.

**Key risks.** A $4B acquisition, roughly 60% of the pre-deal market cap, integrated into a company whose whole appeal was balance-sheet conservatism; Net debt goes from roughly zero to approximately $2.1B including the assumed 7.50% notes and an RBL draw; Oil price — the combined entity is materially more oil-weighted (~70% oil on the acquired volumes) and therefore more exposed; The deal can still terminate: HSR clearance and title/environmental defect thresholds run to 2026-10-13, with an outside date of 2027-03-12
**Watch for.** The WildFire closing 8-K and pro-forma financial statements; The first combined-company 10-Q: RBL draw, pro-forma net debt and combined free cash flow; A beta regression with an R-squared above 0.15 before any gap on this name is taken seriously

**Data quality.** No flags were raised and three separate inputs are wrong, all in the cheap direction. (1) The FCFF base of $424.7M is standalone pre-WildFire Magnolia, while the market cap of $6.4B already includes the 53.3 million shares issued on 2026-07-22 — the model is pricing a post-raise equity against a pre-deal cash flow, which is the TEX error of 2026-09-02 in reverse. (2) Enterprise value is built on a 2026-06-30 balance sheet with $295.9M of cash and no revolver borrowings, and therefore excludes the $500M of 2034 notes issued 2026-08-05, the $600M of WildFire notes to be assumed, and whatever is drawn on the $1.75B of RBL capacity to complete the $2.65B cash consideration — plausibly $1.6-2.1B of debt missing from a $6.4B EV. (3) The 6.7% WACC rests on a beta of 0.38 with an R-squared of 0.047, the BETA_MIN_R2 defect recorded on 2026-09-03, on which Energy was the single most affected sector. The +201.7% gap comes from the naive +19.7% baseline, which is a five-year revenue CAGR off the 2021 oil trough and is meaningless as a forward FCFF growth rate for a mature Eagle Ford producer. Because there is no field to override the cost of equity, the fair value recorded against my +2% assumption is discounted at a rate roughly 350-450bp too low and is materially too high. Read the verdict, not the number.
