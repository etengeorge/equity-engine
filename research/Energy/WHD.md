**The case for the price.** Cactus is a high-return wellhead and pressure-control manufacturer with essentially no net debt, and the market has paid up for it — the stock is +62.4% over twelve months and +13.7% over sixty-three days. A buyer is underwriting the Cactus International integration (the former Baker Hughes surface pressure control business, a 65%-owned group acquired with a 35% redeemable minority interest) plus a differentiated North American wellhead franchise with a genuine share position. The engine cannot express any of that, because it has computed neither a free cash flow base nor an enterprise value for this name.

**What changed.** The 2026-06-30 10-Q (filed 2026-07-31) shows the Cactus International acquisition accounted for in full: a 35% noncontrolling interest and a net put/call valued at $146.7M sitting in Mezzanine Equity, remeasured to redemption value each period at a multiple of EBITDA, plus $19.6M of other noncontrolling interests. Liquidity: $365.8M of cash, of which $92.5M is held for restructuring related to that acquisition, $223.7M available under the Amended ABL Credit Facility with no outstanding borrowings, a $100.0M delayed-draw Term Loan Facility that remains UNDRAWN and whose maturity was extended in June 2026 to 2026-12-31 (8-K items 1.01 and 2.03, 2026-06-02), and a separate $75.0M secured facility for Cactus UAE. A September 2026 investor presentation was filed as EX-99.1 on 2026-09-08.

**Base case.** No growth rate is supplied because there is no base to grow. `capex_series` is EMPTY — not understated, absent — so CFO less capex cannot be computed at all, and the brief reports 'Normalized FCFF base n/a (mean of CFO-capex over 0y)' with `no_cashflow_history` and `no_enterprise_value` flags. Operating cash flow is present and healthy ([$258.4M, $316.1M, $340.3M]) and this is a manufacturer with real plant, so the missing capex is a failed extraction rather than a company with no capital spending. This is the mirror of the CTOS/ALTG/WLFC rental-capex bug and the same shape as the SND depreciation case: where those understated a real number and manufactured false cheapness, this one erases it entirely and manufactures a false REFUSAL.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The multiples table is fully populated here on large cohorts — 69, 99, 56 and 95 Energy names — and every row points the same way: ev_ebitda 15.6x against a 9.0x median, ev_sales 4.5x against 2.4x, ev_gross_profit 12.3x against 7.9x, p_tbv 13.1x against 1.7x, blending to $32.13 against a $67.04 price, a -52.1% gap. Four rows agreeing on a large cohort is a real signal that Cactus is expensive, and refusing discards it.
- What would prove it: Whether the market capitalisation those four ratios are built on is the right one. Every row uses either market cap or enterprise value in its numerator, so a single error in the share count corrupts all four identically.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it defeats the counter completely — this is the MXL lesson in a new form. `shares_asof` is 2018-12-31, i.e. 2,815 days stale, and the 74,889,772 shares recorded are the class A count from around the 2018 IPO. Worse, Cactus is an Up-C: the 10-Q refers to 'pro rata cash distributions to holders of CC Units other than Cactus Inc.', so the class A count never was the full economic share count, and the consolidated financials now also carry a 35% redeemable minority in Cactus International. The four rows are not four independent ratios convicting the stock; they are one error appearing four times, because they share a corrupted numerator. The p_tbv row makes this visible on the face of the brief: 13.1x against an equity_now of $1,240.8M less $287.3M of goodwill and $349.7M of intangibles, which is $603.8M of tangible book and would give roughly 8.3x, not 13.1x. The rows do not even reconcile to the extract they came from.
- Left unresolved: I could not determine the current fully-diluted economic share count including CC Units, which is the single number that would let any of this be priced, and it is not in the sections of the 10-Q I read. I therefore do not know whether Cactus is expensive or cheap, and I record explicitly that this refusal is NOT a judgment in either direction — the counter's conclusion may well be right, and I cannot support it with a defensible number.

**Key risks.** Share count dated 2018-12-31 (2,815 days stale) on an Up-C structure whose class A count is not the economic count; `capex_series` is empty, so no free cash flow base exists for a manufacturer that plainly has capital spending; 35% redeemable noncontrolling interest in Cactus International, valued at $146.7M in Mezzanine Equity and remeasured at a multiple of EBITDA each period; Every multiples row shares the corrupted market capitalisation, so their agreement is not corroboration
