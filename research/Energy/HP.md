**The case for the price.** Helmerich & Payne is the highest-spec US land driller, now with an international arm bought with the KCA Deutag acquisition, and the market is paying for a recovery it can see the shape of: Q3 fiscal 2026 revenue of roughly $1.0bn beat expectations on North American rig reactivations, the stock is up 21.5% since that print, capital expenditure has been cut hard (nine-month fiscal 2026 capex of $200.2M against $362.2M) and nine-month operating cash flow rose to $372.7M from $336.0M. The buyer is paying for super-spec pricing power in a consolidating land market plus an international backlog (Oman rig construction, Saudi) that US rig count does not capture. At a defensible discount rate the price requires roughly 5-14% five-year FCFF growth, which an international build-out can in principle deliver.

**What changed.** Q3 fiscal 2026 (filed 2026-08-06) reported revenue above expectations with an adjusted net LOSS; management framed a constructive outlook and the shares rose. Interest expense FELL to $75.9M for nine months from $79.8M on repayment of the unsecured term loan. Capex guidance came down on 'lower equipment overhauls and certain long-term projects'. Nothing here invalidates the model; what invalidates it is on the balance sheet extract, not in the news.

**Base case.** No fair value is recorded because the two inputs that decide this name are both broken and the schema can override neither. First, the WACC. The engine's 12.25% at a 31.3% debt weight implies an AFTER-TAX cost of debt of 17.2%, i.e. a pre-tax 23.0%, for an investment-grade issuer whose $1.8bn of unsecured notes mature 2027-2034 and whose actual interest bill of $101.1M annualised on $1,862.1M of debt is a rate of 5.43%. The cause is the CLVT defect with a new origin: `ebit` in the extract reads $3,318,000 — three million dollars for a company with $3.7bn of revenue — with `ebit_derived` false, so the synthetic rating is computed on an interest-coverage ratio of about 0.19x and prices the firm as distressed. The `speculative_cost_of_debt` gate stayed silent because EBIT is technically POSITIVE. Second, `interest_expense` reads $17.3M against the $75.9M the 10-Q reports for NINE months, so the after-tax add-back is understated by about $66M. The grid: at the engine's base and engine WACC the market implies +22.9% (the recorded -32.7% gap); at the real interest expense, +17.1%; on a fiscal 2026 run-rate base of $230M of owner earnings plus the real add-back, +18.6%. Replace the fabricated cost of debt with the 5.43% actually paid and the same three bases give +8.8%, +3.7% and +5.1% at beta 0.91, or +14.4%, +9.0% and +10.5% at a land-driller beta of 1.30. The honest span is +3.7% to +22.9%, and the single largest term in it is a number I know to be wrong. My own view of the business is that HP compounds FCFF at roughly +2% over five years — North American land drilling is a mature, share-shifting market and the growth is the international book, which is real but small against a $3.7bn revenue base.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The devil's advocate won this one and changed my verdict. I was heading for `rich`: the engine reports a -32.7% gap and a +22.9% implied growth requirement, and a land driller with a Q3 adjusted net loss cannot compound free cash flow at 23% for five years. The counter asked the right question — which input is actually broken, the base or the discount rate? — and the answer is the discount rate. A 23.0% pre-tax cost of debt for an issuer paying 5.43% is not a modelling difference, it is a fabricated number traceable to an `ebit` of $3.3M on $3.7bn of revenue. Correct it and the requirement falls to +3.7%-+5.1%, which HP can plausibly deliver, and the name is no longer expensive. Two large errors sitting on opposite sides of the answer is the SM rule, and the answer is no_edge.
