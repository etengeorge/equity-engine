## 2026-10-09 — NO_EDGE (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $13.01
- **Not repriced:** not a repriceable fcff name or no growth supplied

**The case for the price.** Crescent is a KKR-sponsored, acquisition-driven Eagle Ford and Permian operator, and at $13.01 the market is paying roughly 1.2x the PV-10 of its own proved reserves for a company that has bought itself three times in under two years. Pro forma for the Devon transaction, Crescent's own 8-K discloses 1,127.3 net MMBoe of proved reserves with $11.0 billion of proved and $8.9 billion of proved-developed PV-10 at 2025 year-end prices, against a post-deal enterprise value near $13.1 billion. That is a full, not a cheap, price for proved barrels, and the gap between the two is the undeveloped inventory — 'more than 600 Tier 1 net locations' — which the market is declining to pay much for. The reason is visible in the same filing: the estimated 2026 PDP decline rate is approximately 32%, with five- and ten-year PDP declines of 12% and 9%. A barrel base that falls a third in a year has to be replaced with capital before any of it is free, and the market's refusal to capitalise Crescent's cash flow at a growth multiple is the arithmetically correct response to that, not pessimism.

**What changed.** The company is in the middle of its third transformation, announced the day the screen priced it. 8-K filed 2026-10-08 (items 1.01, 2.02, 7.01, 8.01, 9.01, with five EX-99 exhibits): a subsidiary signed a Purchase and Sale Agreement to acquire Eagle Ford assets from Devon Energy Production Company for a PURCHASE PRICE OF $4,220,000,000 IN CASH, which the company's own press release describes as an estimated NET purchase price of approximately $3.85 billion after adjustments from the 2026-07-01 economic effective date. Closing expected Q4 2026 or early 2027. Financing: a JPMorgan debt commitment letter for a bridge facility of up to $2.0 billion, plus a $1 billion underwritten public offering of Class A common stock commenced the same day (not contingent on the acquisition), with KKR-affiliated Independence Energy Aggregator indicating interest in up to $500 million. The assets add approximately 68-71 MBoe/d of production, more than 600 Tier 1 net locations, and Devon-owned minerals into Crescent Royalties; $140 million of annual synergies identified. The same 8-K confirms the two PRIOR deals inside the cash-flow window: the Vital Energy merger completed 2025-12-15 and the Ridgemar (Eagle Ford) acquisition completed 2025-01-31.

**Base case.** I do not record a growth rate, because there is no base to apply one to. The engine's $649.7M FCFF base is the mean of fiscal 2023, 2024 and 2025 — a company that produced 260 net MBoe/d in 2025 — while the enterprise value it is divided into is the company that produced 338 MBoe/d in H1 2026 and is contracting to add another ~71 MBoe/d. The disclosed pro formas show the size of the mismatch: against FY2025 actual Levered Free Cash Flow of $856.1M, the pro forma for all three Transactions is $0.8 billion for the full year, while H1 2026 pro forma for Devon alone is $0.8 billion for the HALF year. Those two pro formas annualise to figures that differ by a factor of two, so even Crescent's own disclosure does not pin the base within a factor of two, and applying a five-year growth rate to a number that uncertain is false precision.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest counter is that +311% at the 100th percentile of 50 Energy names is too large to be entirely artifact, and that I am hiding behind complexity. Crescent generated $609.5M of Levered Free Cash Flow in H1 2026 alone, which annualises to roughly $1.2 billion against a $4.3 billion equity market capitalisation — a 28% levered free cash flow yield on the company as it exists TODAY, before the Devon assets. If even half of that is durable, the equity is materially mispriced and 'no_edge' is a refusal to do the work.
- What would prove it: Whether that cash flow is free, or is being recycled into the acquisitions that produce it. The test is acquisition cash paid against the change in production, and the PV-10 of proved reserves against enterprise value.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it answers the counter. Crescent's strategy is, in its own words, 'returns-driven growth through acquisition' and 'buy assets and make them better'. Free cash flow computed as CFO less development capex never charges for the $4.22 billion it is about to pay Devon, nor for Vital, nor for Ridgemar — the documented GHC defect in research/LESSONS.md, where a serial acquirer's growth is bought with cash the free-cash-flow measure does not see. The honest anchor is the one Crescent itself publishes: enterprise value near $13.1 billion post-deal against $11.0 billion of proved and $8.9 billion of proved-developed PV-10, i.e. 1.2x and 1.5x. That is a normal-to-full price, and it is consistent with a 28% levered yield only because a 32% PDP decline means most of that yield must be reinvested to stand still. A two-stage model that then grants 2% growth in perpetuity to a reserve base of roughly seven years' production is capitalising barrels that do not exist.
- Left unresolved: Whether the $1 billion equity offering priced, and at what price — it commenced 2026-10-08, the same day as the price in this brief, so the share count and market capitalisation here are pre-offering and the enterprise value is pre-deal. I also could not reconcile the 8-K's $4.22 billion contractual purchase price with the press release's approximately $3.85 billion estimated net figure; the filing attributes the difference to adjustments including hydrocarbon sales after the 2026-07-01 effective date, but I did not find the reconciliation itself.

**Key risks.** A $3.85-4.22 billion cash acquisition funded with a $2.0 billion bridge into a commodity downturn; 32% estimated 2026 PDP decline — the reinvestment requirement is the business, not an option; Dilution from a $1 billion Class A offering priced at a 252-day price that is already +48.8%; KKR affiliate as both sponsor and offering participant: minority holders do not set capital allocation
**Watch for.** Pricing and size of the $1 billion Class A offering, and whether the bridge is drawn or termed out; Closing of the Devon transaction (8-K item 2.01) and the first quarter of combined reporting; A year-end 2026 reserve report showing whether the 600 Tier 1 locations convert to proved
