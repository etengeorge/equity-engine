**The case for the price.** Ardmore owns MR product and chemical tankers and is at or just past a rate peak. Q2 2026 adjusted earnings were $48.3M, or $1.18 per share, on revenue of $116.2M, with MR spot TCE of $51,870 per day - close to three times the company's operating cash breakeven of roughly $17,300 - and chemical tanker TCE of $26,887 against $20,409 a year earlier. The company pays out two thirds of adjusted earnings and declared $0.79 for the quarter. The market at $17.61 is implying about -17% five-year FCFF growth, and that is not pessimism, it is how a mean-reverting rate cycle is correctly priced. Management's own Q3 guidance says so: MR at $29,600 per day with 45% of revenue days fixed, which is 43% BELOW the quarter just reported even though it is 20% above the prior year. Nobody who owns a tanker at a peak gets paid for extrapolating it.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 results (late July): the figures above. Q3 2026 guidance of MR $29,600/day (45% fixed) and chemical $25,000/day (50% fixed) - a sharp step down from Q2's realisation and the single most important fact for this name. Rates are attributed to geopolitical disruption and structural shifts in global refining capacity, the same drivers visible in Par Pacific's refining margins in today's other Energy name. I could NOT read any of this from a primary filing (see data quality).

**Base case.** I am supplying no growth rate, because for a product tanker owner 'five-year FCFF growth' is not a parameter that exists - the cash flow is a rate cycle, and the answer is entirely set by where mid-cycle sits. The engine's $138.3M base is the mean of [$81.6M, $160.4M, $159.1M] and carries `possible_trough_cycle_base`, whose advisory text says growth applied to a trough understates value. Per standing practice I do not read that recommendation: the newest year was the product-tanker trough, and the company has since earned $48.3M of adjusted net income in a single quarter. Sizing mid-cycle from the company's own disclosure - roughly 26 vessels, cash breakeven near $17,300/day - gives about $30-50M of free cash flow at a $20-22,000/day long-run MR rate, about $115M at the guided Q3 level of $29,600, and about $190M annualising the Q2 peak. Across that base range and a discount rate of 10.0-11.5%, the growth the market requires spans +7.2% to -22.5%, with the sign flipping at a base somewhere between $70M and $85M. A thirty-point span on a factor-of-five base range is not a valuation, and the honest output is the range.
