**The case for the price.** RPC is a debt-free oilfield services company with $179.5M of cash, no borrowings on its $100M revolver, and a business that has been deliberately shifted away from commodity pressure pumping toward higher-margin technical services. Q2 2026 revenue of $460.9M was up 1% sequentially and up 9.5% year-on-year with adjusted EBITDA of $66.0M against $53.5M in Q1 — a 250bp sequential margin expansion to 14.3% achieved in a flat activity market, driven by job mix in Pressure Pumping and double-digit growth in Snubbing, Cementing and Downhole Tools. The buyer at $6.57 is paying roughly 5.4x EV/EBITDA on an annualized run rate for a balance sheet that can survive a downturn intact and, per management, 'invest opportunistically' while levered competitors cannot. **What changed.** Q2 2026 (30 Jul): revenue $460.9M, adjusted EBITDA $66.0M, margin +250bp sequentially. The Pintail Completions wireline business (acquired 1 Apr 2025 for ~$245M) saw revenue DECLINE sequentially, and a $20.0M principal payment was made on the Pintail note early in Q2. In June the revolver maturity was extended to 30 June 2031.

**Base case.** North American pressure pumping is structurally oversupplied and has been deflationary for three years; RPC's own management will not reactivate idle fleets at current pricing, which is the clearest possible statement that marginal returns do not clear the cost of capital. What RPC has instead is mix — Snubbing, Cementing, Downhole Tools and Rental Tools growing while Wireline shrinks — and that improves margin without adding much to the free cash flow base, because the growth is being funded by a capex budget just raised to $170-190M against roughly $240M of annualized adjusted EBITDA. Netting those leaves $50-70M of pre-tax free cash flow in 2026, and year-to-date free cash flow of $3.8M says the conversion is worse than that in practice. I therefore underwrite zero real FCFF growth over five years off the $132.3M normalized base: mix improvement and eventual activity recovery on one side, secular frac deflation and a permanently higher capital intensity on the other. That is materially below the naive +22.1% baseline and I explain why in the devil's-advocate section — the baseline is not a growth rate at all.

**The case for the price.** RPC is a debt-free, net-cash diversified oilfield services company in a structurally oversupplied US onshore market, and at $6.38 the market is paying roughly 4.2x enterprise value to annualised adjusted EBITDA for a business that cannot convert that EBITDA into free cash flow. The reason is capital intensity, and it is visible in two adjacent numbers: depreciation and amortisation ran $42.7M in the third quarter alone, about $170M a year, against capital expenditure of $110.7M for nine months, about $148M a year. Capex is therefore BELOW depreciation — this is sustaining spend, not a growth build — and nine-month free cash flow on the company's own definition was $5.0 million against $21.7M a year earlier. Three consecutive reporting periods have now produced free cash flow near zero. On top of that the market has just been handed management's own written admission that the acquisition which drove the apparent growth was a mistake: a $74.5M non-cash impairment of the Pintail wireline asset group, booked in the third quarter for 'persistent pricing weakness, lower activity and cost pressure'. Management has also said it will not reactivate frac fleets at current pricing. A market asking for roughly flat-to-modestly-positive real cash flow growth off genuine current earning power is not being pessimistic about any of that; it is being approximately right.

**What changed.** The Q3 10-Q and earnings release were filed 2026-10-29, the day before this pass, and between them they answer both observables the previous devil's advocate (2026-09-03) nominated — and both answers go against the cheap case. (1) THE NOMINATED FREE-CASH-FLOW TEST FAILED. The prior pass wrote that the trough thesis required 'H2 2026 free cash flow above roughly $60M, with the working capital build reversing.' Nine-month free cash flow is $5.0M (CFO $115,732k less capex $110,727k), so third-quarter free cash flow was about $1.2M against the $3.8M reported at the half year. Not $60M — essentially nothing, for a third consecutive period. The release attributes the drag to 'higher accounts receivable resulting from increased customer activity and timing of collections, and strategic inventory purchases including key components in short supply', so part of it is genuine timing. (2) THE MAINTENANCE-VERSUS-GROWTH CAPEX SPLIT IS NOW EFFECTIVELY SETTLED, from the other direction. The prior pass left this open — 'if maintenance is $110M the business generates real cash at trough; if it is $160M it does not' — because management gives one combined number. The 10-Q still gives no split, but D&A of $42.7M a quarter (~$170M a year) exceeds annualised capex of ~$148M, so the company is not out-spending its own depreciation and there is no material growth-capex add-back available. The AROC pairing rule therefore does not rescue this base. (3) NEW AND DECISIVE ON THE BASELINE: a $74.5M non-cash impairment of Pintail ($58.3M of intangibles, $16.2M of PP&E), measured at 2026-08-31, because 'estimated undiscounted cash flows were less than the carrying amount of the asset group'. The prior pass's first key risk was that the +22.1% five-year revenue CAGR baseline was 'largely the Pintail Completions purchase' rather than mispricing; the write-down is the company confirming that in its own accounts. Net loss of $41.9M for the quarter. (4) Genuinely in the other direction, and the bull's best evidence: adjusted EBITDA has ramped monotonically through 2026 — $53.5M in Q1 (11.8% margin), $66.0M in Q2 (14.3%), $71.5M in Q3 (15.3%) — with revenue up 1% sequentially to $467.7M, double-digit sequential growth in coiled tubing, nitrogen and snubbing, and the $0.04 quarterly dividend maintained.

**Base case.** I underwrite zero real FCFF growth over five years off an overridden base of $78M rather than the engine's $132.3M, and the override is the substance of this pass. The engine's base is the mean of free cash flow of $52.9M, $129.5M and $213.8M (newest first) — a declining series whose oldest year is the 2022-23 post-COVID activity spike — and the business is not earning it. Built from current economics instead: full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA annualises to about $263M (nine months actual $191.0M plus a fourth quarter near the third's $71.5M); sustaining capital expenditure is between the $148M actually being spent and the $170M of depreciation, and since capex sits below D&A I take that whole range as sustaining; taxing the difference at 25% gives $70M at the D&A charge and $86M at the cash-capex charge, so $78M is the midpoint. On the growth rate, zero is the honest number and I keep the prior pass's figure for the same reasons plus one new one: US pressure pumping remains structurally oversupplied, management will not reactivate fleets at current pricing, inflation in labour, trucking and fuel is eating the activity recovery, and the wireline business has just been written down for persistent price weakness — against which the 2026 margin ramp and a debt-free balance sheet with net cash argue for eventual mid-cycle normalisation. Those roughly offset at zero. I differ from the naive +22.1% baseline because that figure is not a growth rate at all: it is five years of revenue CAGR largely created by buying Pintail for about $245M in April 2025, and applying acquired revenue growth to per-share free cash flow is the roll-up error from the standing lessons.
