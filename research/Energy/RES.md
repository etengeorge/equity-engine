**The case for the price.** RPC is a debt-free oilfield services company with $179.5M of cash, no borrowings on its $100M revolver, and a business that has been deliberately shifted away from commodity pressure pumping toward higher-margin technical services. Q2 2026 revenue of $460.9M was up 1% sequentially and up 9.5% year-on-year with adjusted EBITDA of $66.0M against $53.5M in Q1 — a 250bp sequential margin expansion to 14.3% achieved in a flat activity market, driven by job mix in Pressure Pumping and double-digit growth in Snubbing, Cementing and Downhole Tools. The buyer at $6.57 is paying roughly 5.4x EV/EBITDA on an annualized run rate for a balance sheet that can survive a downturn intact and, per management, 'invest opportunistically' while levered competitors cannot. That is a call option on Permian consolidation priced as a melting ice cube.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 (30 Jul): revenue $460.9M, adjusted EBITDA $66.0M, margin +250bp sequentially. Critically, year-to-date through Q2 net cash from operating activities was $74.6M and FREE CASH FLOW was $3.8M — essentially nothing — with working capital a large use of cash on higher receivables. 2026 capex guidance was RAISED to $170-190M to support targeted growth. Management stated it has no plans to reactivate frac fleets at current pricing and flagged that New Mexico natural gas takeaway constraints could cap customer activity. The Pintail Completions wireline business (acquired 1 Apr 2025 for ~$245M) saw revenue DECLINE sequentially, and a $20.0M principal payment was made on the Pintail note early in Q2. In June the revolver maturity was extended to 30 June 2031.

**Base case.** North American pressure pumping is structurally oversupplied and has been deflationary for three years; RPC's own management will not reactivate idle fleets at current pricing, which is the clearest possible statement that marginal returns do not clear the cost of capital. What RPC has instead is mix — Snubbing, Cementing, Downhole Tools and Rental Tools growing while Wireline shrinks — and that improves margin without adding much to the free cash flow base, because the growth is being funded by a capex budget just raised to $170-190M against roughly $240M of annualized adjusted EBITDA. Netting those leaves $50-70M of pre-tax free cash flow in 2026, and year-to-date free cash flow of $3.8M says the conversion is worse than that in practice. I therefore underwrite zero real FCFF growth over five years off the $132.3M normalized base: mix improvement and eventual activity recovery on one side, secular frac deflation and a permanently higher capital intensity on the other. That is materially below the naive +22.1% baseline and I explain why in the devil's-advocate section — the baseline is not a growth rate at all.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: This is the case FOR the screen's +220.8% gap, and I want to state it at full strength before rejecting it. RPC's three-year free cash flow series is ['$52.9M', '$129.5M', '$213.8M'] newest first — the engine itself flags this as a possible TROUGH, and a trough it plainly is: the newest year is a quarter of the oldest. If 2025-26 is the bottom of a US onshore capex cycle and RPC is a debt-free survivor with $179.5M of cash, then normalizing to anything near the $213.8M of the oldest year, let alone growing from there, produces a fair value far above $6.57. The stock is +14.8% over 21 days on exactly that thesis, and the sector is in a melt-up. Zacks and Simply Wall St both published undervaluation pieces in August. On a trough-normalized basis the naive +22.1% growth number is not even needed for the name to look cheap.
- What would prove it: Free cash flow conversion, not EBITDA. RPC needs to show a half-year where operating cash flow less capex is a real number rather than $3.8M. Specifically: H2 2026 free cash flow above roughly $60M, with the working capital build reversing as management says it typically does, and 2027 capex guided back below $150M once the 'targeted growth' investments are complete. Second observable: RPC reactivating frac fleets — management said it will not at current pricing, so a reactivation announcement is management's own signal that pricing has cleared.
- Already visible today: The evidence that is visible cuts AGAINST the trough thesis, and this is where the red team loses on the specific number while winning on the direction. Year-to-date free cash flow of $3.8M with capex guidance RAISED to $170-190M is not a company about to convert a cyclical recovery into cash; it is a company spending its cash flow to hold position. And the trough framing is contaminated: the $213.8M oldest year is a 2022-23 post-COVID activity spike, not a normal year. So I reject the +220.8% gap — but I reject it as unmeasurable rather than as proven wrong, because I cannot rule out that 2026 is genuinely the bottom.
- Left unresolved: I cannot determine RPC's maintenance capex separately from growth capex, and the entire free cash flow question turns on that split. Management gives one combined $170-190M number. If maintenance is $110M the business generates real cash at trough; if it is $160M it does not. Nothing in the Q2 release or the 10-Q breaks it out, and I did not find it in the transcript coverage.

**Key risks.** The +220.8% gap is manufactured by an acquisition, not by mispricing: the +22.1% five-year revenue CAGR baseline is largely the Pintail Completions purchase (~$409M of acquired 2024 revenue, bought 1 Apr 2025 for ~$245M), so acquired revenue growth is being applied to per-share free cash flow; The 8.79% WACC rests on a beta of 0.727 with an R-squared of 0.085 — a failed regression passed through as beta_source=regression, assigning a Permian pressure-pumping company a beta well below the Russell 2000; Year-to-date free cash flow of $3.8M against a normalized FCFF base of $132.3M — the base is a three-year mean the business is not currently earning; US pressure pumping is structurally oversupplied; management explicitly will not reactivate fleets at current pricing; New Mexico natural gas takeaway constraints could cap Permian customer activity independent of oil price
**Watch for.** H2 2026 free cash flow above ~$60M with the working capital build reversing — the single observable that would convert the trough thesis from a story into a number; 2027 capex guidance: back below $150M means the growth cycle is over and cash conversion resumes; another raise means it is not; Any announcement that RPC is reactivating frac fleets, which is management's own stated threshold for pricing having cleared

**Data quality.** The engine's trough-cycle flag is real but its stated direction ('growth applied to a trough understates value') is not safe here: the oldest year in the window, $213.8M, is a post-COVID activity spike rather than a normal year, so mean-reverting to it would overstate rather than correct. I did NOT use fcff_base_override — the three-year mean of $132.3M is a defensible mid-cycle proxy even though 2026 is running far below it, and substituting a trough year would have been the opposite error. Two flags the engine did not raise remain open and both manufacture cheapness. The +22.1% revenue CAGR baseline is contaminated by the Pintail acquisition, so the +220.8% headline gap is not a valuation statement. And the beta of 0.727 at R-squared 0.085 gives an oilfield services company a lower cost of equity than the index — at a defensible frac-services beta of ~1.5 the implied growth rises from -7.9% to roughly +5%, which removes the gap entirely. I could not correct that in the record: there is no cost-of-equity field in the schema.
