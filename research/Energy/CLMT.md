**The case for the price.** Calumet at $48.53 is up 143.9% over twelve months on a sustainable-aviation-fuel story that the company has just radically re-scoped in its own favour. On 2026-08-28 Montana Renewables (MRL, an unrestricted subsidiary) and the DOE's Office of Energy Dominance Financing amended the January 2025 Loan Guarantee Agreement, and the new plan is strictly cheaper: by repurposing proven, installed refining equipment from the adjacent Calumet Montana Refining facility rather than building the greenfield megaproject, total remaining project capital falls to approximately $137 million against the $1.2 billion originally contemplated, while still targeting 17,000 barrels per day of total product sales by year-end 2028. Phase 2 DOE funding is cut from up to $658 million to a single final $34 million draw, with the balance self-funded from MRL earnings and no third-party equity. The CMR turnaround to complete the tie-in is set for the fourth quarter of 2026, timed so CMR captures roughly $50 million of EBITDA at currently elevated global refining margins first, and CMR stays in service producing retail asphalt. A market paying up for that is paying for a de-risked, self-funded capacity ramp rather than for current cash flow — which is just as well, because there is no current free cash flow to speak of.

**What changed.** Three material items, all after the 2026-06-30 balance sheet the extract is built on. (1) THE DOE FINANCING WAS RESTRUCTURED DOWNWARD AND THE PROJECT RE-SCOPED, announced 2026-08-28/09-01: the First Amendment to the Loan Guarantee Agreement reduces the maximum guaranteed principal and capitalised interest amounts, cuts remaining Phase 2 availability from up to $658 million to a single final $34 million draw, and accompanies a 'new flight plan' that takes remaining project capital from $1.2 billion to about $137 million by repurposing CMR equipment. Calumet is also making an equity investment into MRL from cash on hand. (2) THE BANK AND COMMODITY FACILITIES WERE AMENDED on 2026-09-11, filed under items 1.01 and 2.03 on 2026-09-15: an Eleventh Amendment to the Third Amended and Restated Credit Agreement with Bank of America and a Fourth Amendment to the Monetization Master Agreement with J. Aron & Company, with a $500-600 million figure referenced. (3) THE CMR TURNAROUND IS SCHEDULED FOR Q4 2026, which converts a conventional refinery into feedstock and equipment for the renewables business — so the fourth quarter carries both a roughly $50 million EBITDA harvest ahead of the transition and the outage itself. An earlier item 1.01/2.03 financing 8-K was filed 2026-03-17. None of this is in the model's inputs, and the engine could not price the name in any case.

**Base case.** No valuation is possible and the engine already says so: the normalised FCFF base, enterprise value, FCFF yield and implied growth are all reported as n/a, because `nonpositive_normalized_fcff` fired on a cash-flow window containing a negative year and a 5.4x spread of free cash flow around its own mean. A discounted cash flow cannot value negative cash flow, and that is the correct refusal rather than a failure. What I can add is that the comparables fallback must be disregarded too, and that the business is mid-transformation in a way that would defeat a model even if the cash flows were positive: CMR is being converted from a conventional refinery into feedstock and installed equipment for MRL, MRL is an unrestricted subsidiary carrying its own DOE-guaranteed debt outside the parent's covenant perimeter, the project capital plan was cut by 89% eight weeks ago, and the credit and commodity-monetisation agreements were amended three weeks after that. 'Unmodellable' is not 'worthless' — the re-scoping is unambiguously good news for the equity — but the honest output is a refusal with the mechanism named, not a number.
