**The case for the price.** At $39.82 the market is paying $2.7bn of enterprise value for a deepwater drilling franchise with $3.3bn of contract backlog — roughly two years of revenue — more than 85% of available drillship days contracted for 2027, $800M of new awards and extensions secured in three months, net debt of only $244M against guided adjusted EBITDA of $440-460M, and a buyback running. The bull case is a cycle call with contracts behind it: 2025 was the trough (operating cash flow of negative $28M), 2026 EBITDA guidance has been raised twice, the West Carina award of ~$420M completes the third and final repricing of three legacy contracts with the improved dayrate effective in 2027, and deepwater demand is tightening while supply is fixed. On that view the current year understates earning power and 2027-28 is materially better.

**What changed.** The Q3 2026 results of 2026-11-03, and they are the whole basis for rebuilding the base the engine could not compute. Full-year 2026 guidance was increased and narrowed: total operating revenues to $1.56-1.58bn excluding $90M of reimbursables (from $1.50-1.55bn), ADJUSTED EBITDA TO $440-460 MILLION (from $420-450M, itself raised from $370-420M in August), and capital expenditure and long-term maintenance narrowed to $210-230M. The quarter generated $195M of operating cash flow and $179M of what the company calls free cash flow, with $22M of shares repurchased, but reported a net LOSS of $30M including a legal provision, and adjusted EBITDA FELL to $121M from $144M in Q2. Contract backlog is $3.3bn at 2026-11-03. Gross principal debt is $750M against $506M of cash including restricted, so net debt is $244M. Nine-month figures from the 10-Q: operating cash flow $155M (against $12M), additions to drilling units and equipment $54M, and the financing of a $700M senior bond issue repaying a $575M secured bond with a $25M make-whole.

**Base case.** I rebuild the base from the company's own guidance rather than from the engine's $9.75M, which is meaningless. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $450M at the midpoint less capital expenditure and long-term maintenance of $220M leaves $230M before cash tax; offshore drillers pay material cash tax in Brazil, Angola and Norway, so netting roughly $50M gives FCFF of about $180M. The nine-month actuals corroborate it from the other direction: operating cash flow of $155M less $54M of investing capex is $101M, which annualises to about $135M, plus the after-tax interest add-back of roughly $46M gives about $181M — though the company flags that Q3 benefited from a lump-sum West Tellus mobilisation receipt and a working-capital release, so the annualisation is generous. I use $180M, the top of a defensible $150-185M range, deliberately: concluding that the name is expensive off the generous end is the conservative way to reach that conclusion. I pair it with +3% growth rather than zero, crediting the contracted backlog and the 2027 repricings, but not a full cycle re-rating — because the capex and long-term maintenance guidance of $210-230M sits BELOW depreciation of $238M, which by the RES test means essentially all of it is sustaining and there is no growth-capex add-back available to justify a higher pairing.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I am pricing a cyclical off a single year and calling it expensive, which is exactly the error the AMR and LPG entries warn against in reverse. The whole value of an offshore driller is in the contracts it has already signed, and Seadrill has $3.3bn of them against $1.6bn of annual revenue, with more than 85% of 2027 drillship days booked and the final legacy repricing landing in 2027. A $2.7bn enterprise value against $3.3bn of contracted revenue and $244M of net debt is not a demanding price, and the buyback says management agrees. If 2027-28 EBITDA is $550-600M rather than $450M, my $180M base is simply the wrong year.
- What would prove it: A 2027 EBITDA guide materially above $500M, or a capex guide clearly below depreciation once the current survey cycle completes — either would show that the $220M of capital spending is not all sustaining and that my base is a trough rather than a run rate.
- Already visible today: The backlog is visible and it is the strongest fact on the page; the 2027 number is not. What IS visible runs against the counter in two places. First, management's own Q4 implication: nine-month adjusted EBITDA of $362M (Q1 $97M, Q2 $144M, Q3 $121M) against full-year guidance of $440-460M means Q4 is guided DOWN 19-36% from Q3 — a guide that steps down off the year's best quarter is the company saying the quarter was not repeatable, which is the Dorman tell. Second, the capex test closes the growth-capex escape: $210-230M of capital expenditure and long-term maintenance against $238M of depreciation means a company not out-spending its own depreciation, so the AROC rescue that would justify pairing a sustaining base with real growth is unavailable — the same test that settled RES. Taking those together, the market at $39.82 requires +10.5% five-year FCFF growth off my generous $180M base at the engine's 11.6% WACC; zero growth gives $24.59 and +3% gives $28.37. To reach the price you need either a base near $280M, which contradicts the company's own capex guidance, or double-digit compound growth off a peak-of-the-recovery year. The counter narrows the margin honestly — it is why conviction is low — but it does not close it.
- Left unresolved: The 2027 earnings power, which is the whole question and which the backlog constrains but does not determine, because backlog gives revenue and not margin. Also unresolved: the recurring level of special periodic survey spending across the fleet, which drives the single input my verdict is most sensitive to — at a $230M base and +5% growth the name is fair, and I cannot rule that out. And the size and nature of the legal provision inside the Q3 net loss, which the release names but does not quantify.

**Key risks.** Management's full-year guidance implies Q4 adjusted EBITDA of $78-98M against Q3's $121M — a step down off the year's best quarter; Capex and long-term maintenance guidance of $210-230M against $238M of depreciation leaves no growth-capex add-back; roughly half of EBITDA is consumed sustaining the fleet; Three of the prior four years of cfo-less-capex were negative or near zero: -$242M, -$5M and +$139M, with 2025 operating cash flow of negative $28M; Q3's $179M of free cash flow is not a run rate — the company attributes it to a lump-sum West Tellus mobilisation receipt and a working-capital release, and nine-month operating cash flow is only $155M; A Q3 net loss of $30M including an unquantified legal provision; Backlog fixes revenue, not margin; white space between contracts already cost Q3 revenue on the West Vela, West Elara and Sevan Louisiana
