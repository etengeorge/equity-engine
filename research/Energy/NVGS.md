**The case for the price.** Navigator owns the largest handysize liquefied-gas fleet and a half-share of the Morgan's Point ethylene export terminal, and 2026 has been the best year in its history. Second-quarter net income was $53.0 million, or $0.86 a share — the highest Navigator has ever reported — on record average charter rates, a time-charter equivalent of $33,946 a day, 90.8% utilisation and record terminal throughput of 374,278 tons as Europe and Asia pulled US ethylene. The dividend was raised to $0.08. The order book for handysize gas carriers is thin and US natural-gas-liquids production keeps growing, so the structural case is real. The shares are up 53% over twelve months and 17.7% over the last month, and at 1.5 billion dollars of market capitalisation the market is paying for a franchise, not for the 2026 rate environment to persist.

**What changed.** The rate environment, in both directions, and none of it is in the model's window. The record Q2 came from geopolitical disruption to gas trade routes; that premium has since unwound with the agreement ending the US-Iran conflict and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, which appears in this brief's own Energy sector feed. Management explicitly expects third-quarter performance to MODERATE from the record second quarter as terminal volumes, utilisation and time-charter-equivalent rates ease. Separately the company secured financing for six newbuild vessels — four ethylene carriers delivering between December 2026 and December 2027 — and reports $1.389 billion of future obligations including debt, vessels under construction and leases, of which $336 million falls due within twelve months against a $1.5 billion market capitalisation.
