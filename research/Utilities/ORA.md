**The case for the price.** Ormat owns and operates geothermal and energy-storage generation, and it is in the middle of a build cycle: it spends more on constructing power plants than those plants currently return in cash, which is what a growing independent power producer is supposed to do. The market values it on the contracted, inflation-linked, twenty-year-plus cash flows those assets will throw off once complete, not on trailing free cash flow. Q2 2026 revenue rose 10.6% to $258.8M and the company RAISED its full-year revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance on first-half momentum. Geothermal is the only renewable technology that delivers genuine 24/7 baseload, which is precisely what data-centre load growth is bidding for, and Ormat has just introduced the Ormega100, a standardised binary surface unit rated to 100MW gross, aimed at making its builds larger and more repeatable. At $104.19 the market is paying roughly 3.2x tangible book and about 19.6x EBITDA for a contracted baseload developer with a decade of visible project pipeline.

**What changed.** Q2 2026: revenue $258.8M, up 10.6% year on year, with full-year 2026 revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance RAISED on first-half performance. Net income attributable to stockholders of $27.1M ($0.43 per diluted share) versus $28.0M ($0.46) a year earlier, with the decline driven primarily by a $6.6M write-off on storage projects the company has decided not to pursue — a discretionary portfolio decision, not an operating miss. Introduction of the Ormega100, a standardised binary surface power unit rated up to 100MW gross, part of a strategic move toward larger, repeatable geothermal builds with shorter delivery times. Sector context from the brief's own utility feed is unusually loud: California wildfire legislation omitting a liability cap sent PG&E down 18% and Edison down 23% on 2026-08-31, and utilities as a group hit record lows versus the S&P 500 in late August. That cohort de-rating, rather than anything company-specific, is the most plausible explanation for Ormat's -26.8% 63-day move against a +6.4% 21-day move and raised guidance. No company-specific news in the engine's store.

**Base case.** No base case. Normalised FCFF is non-positive because Ormat is capitalising the construction of long-lived generating assets, so CFO less capex is negative by construction during a build phase. Applying a growth rate to a negative base produces a number with a sign but no meaning. The right valuation for an IPP is a project-level, contract-by-contract discounted cash flow — capacity under power purchase agreement, contracted tariff, capacity factor, tax equity and ITC/PTC structure, and the cost and timing of the development pipeline — and this engine has none of those inputs.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The case for engaging rather than refusing is that the -26.8% 63-day move is the largest de-rating in today's ten and it came alongside RAISED guidance. That combination — price down 27%, guidance up — is the classic setup this screen exists to find, and answering no_model means walking past it. A patient analyst could value Ormat on EV per installed MW against contracted capacity, or on an EBITDA multiple against the raised guidance, and form a view.
- What would prove it: Contracted capacity and pipeline MW with PPA tariffs and commercial-operation dates, plus the tax-equity structure — that is what turns a build-phase IPP into a valuation. Whether the de-rating is cohort-wide or Ormat-specific would be settled by comparing its move against a geothermal and IPP peer set over the same 63 days.
- Already visible today: The cohort explanation is partly visible and I find it persuasive: the utility sector hit record relative lows in late August, two large utilities fell 18% and 23% on a single legislative headline, and the brief's macro feed carries 'the best stock sector ETFs for rising interest rates'. A rate-driven de-rating of a long-duration, capital-intensive asset owner explains a 27% move without any company-specific deterioration, and the raised guidance is consistent with that.
- Left unresolved: I cannot value the asset base. I did not obtain contracted MW, PPA tariffs or the pipeline schedule, and without those an EV-per-MW or contracted-EBITDA valuation would be assembled from numbers I do not have. I am recording no_model rather than a low-conviction guess, but I want the record to show that the de-rating deserves a proper look with the right inputs, and that this engine cannot supply them.

**Key risks.** Capital intensity: a build-phase IPP is exposed to the cost of capital, and utilities are de-rating on rate expectations; Geothermal resource risk — wells that underdeliver after capital is committed; Policy and tax-credit dependence (ITC/PTC and tax-equity availability); Project execution and interconnection queues on the development pipeline; The $6.6M storage write-off suggests the storage segment's economics are not settled
**Watch for.** The raised full-year 2026 revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance being met; New PPA signings at tariffs above the legacy book — the direct test of whether data-centre demand is repricing geothermal; Ormega100 first deployments and whether they shorten the build cycle as claimed; Whether the 63-day de-rating reverses with the utility cohort or persists, which distinguishes a sector move from a company problem
