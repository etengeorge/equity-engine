# CMC — COMMERCIAL METALS
*Materials · Russell 2000*

Append-only research log. Newest entries at the bottom.

## 2026-09-01 — NO_EDGE (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $67.47 · fair value $64.49 · gap -4.4%
- **Growth:** market implies +6.1%, analyst says +5.0% (delta -1.1%)

**The case for the price.** At $67.47 the market asks for roughly +6% FCFF growth from Commercial Metals, which is close to the company's own +7.3% five-year revenue CAGR and is a reasonable ask for a low-cost rebar and merchant bar producer with a structurally advantaged position in US construction. The buyer at this price believes the steel cycle is near a trough — the newest year's free cash flow is 0.42x the oldest in the window — and that CMC has just bought its way into a less cyclical, higher-margin adjacency by acquiring two precast concrete businesses. Infrastructure spending and reshoring support volumes; CMC's micro-mill footprint keeps it on the right side of the cost curve. Paying a mid-single-digit growth expectation for a business at a cyclical low with a new secular growth leg is defensible.

**What changed.** The material change is a $2.5 billion transformation of the balance sheet that happened entirely after the fiscal year the model reads. From the 2026-06-29 10-Q for the nine months ended 31 May 2026, which I fetched and read: CMC completed the acquisition of Foley Products Company on 15 December 2025 for approximately $1.84 billion in cash, funded from the November 2025 issuance of the 2033 and 2035 Notes, and completed the acquisition of Concrete Pipe and Precast (CP&P) on 1 December 2025 for approximately $675 million in cash from cash on hand. Together they added roughly $1.72 billion of goodwill ($1.3B Foley, $416.2M CP&P) and moved the business into precast concrete solutions for drainage, water management and road construction, reported inside the Construction Solutions Group. Total debt went from $1,364,085K to $3,428,920K — an increase of $2.06 billion. Cash flow from operations for the nine months was $603.0M against $399.9M a year earlier, with capital expenditure of $404.3M.

**Base case.** I take a through-cycle view rather than a trough-extrapolation one. CMC's FCFF series is $737.4M, $575.4M, $312.2M newest last, so the $578.6M mean sits above the latest year and already embeds a partial cycle. Rebar demand is tied to US non-residential construction and infrastructure, which is supported but not booming, and CMC's pricing is set by a global steel market with persistent Chinese overcapacity. The precast acquisitions genuinely diversify toward shorter-cycle, higher-margin construction products, but they were bought at full price and their earnings are not in the base. I settle on 5% — a little below the naive +7.3% revenue baseline, because that baseline is a revenue CAGR measured across an exceptional 2021-2023 steel-price spike and FCFF should not be assumed to compound with a price cycle that has already turned.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest attack on any conclusion here is that the model's enterprise value is a year out of date and the name is materially more expensive than it looks. fundamentals_age_days is 366: the extract is the fiscal year ended 31 August 2025, and it records total debt of $1.354B against an actual $3.429B at 31 May 2026. Rebuilding enterprise value with the $2.07B of acquisition debt takes EV from $7.77B to $9.85B, and the growth the market implies on the unchanged $578.6M FCFF base jumps from +6.1% to +12.0% — well above the +7.3% baseline. On that arithmetic the screen's +5.5% 'cheap' reading inverts into a demanding expectation, and CMC is modestly expensive rather than modestly cheap.
- What would prove it: Whether the acquired earnings offset the acquired debt. The debt is certain; the question is what $2.52 billion of purchase price actually generates in free cash flow to the firm, and whether it arrives fast enough to service the notes.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it rescues the name from the counter — but only if both sides are treated consistently, which is the point. Precast concrete businesses of this scale typically transact around 10-12x EBITDA, implying roughly $210-250M of acquired EBITDA and something like $150-200M of incremental FCFF. Adding that to the base while holding the corrected $9.85B EV gives implied growth of +6.2% at $150M added and +4.6% at $200M added — essentially back where the model started. So the correct reading is that the engine reached a defensible implied growth by omitting the debt and the earnings simultaneously, and the errors offset. The conclusion survives; the reasoning behind it in the brief does not.
- Left unresolved: I could not verify the acquired EBITDA directly — CMC does not disclose Foley and CP&P standalone earnings, and my $150-200M incremental FCFF estimate is a transaction-multiple inference, not a read of a number. If the precast businesses were bought at a richer multiple than I assume, the debt is not fully offset and the name really is expensive. I also could not assess integration risk on two acquisitions closed two weeks apart.

**Key risks.** Total debt rose from $1.36B to $3.43B to fund $2.52B of acquisitions; leverage is materially higher than the model's stale extract shows; $1.72B of new goodwill on two precast acquisitions closed two weeks apart, with integration risk and impairment exposure if construction slows; Rebar pricing is set by a global market with persistent overcapacity; the FCFF window already spans a price spike and its unwind; The valuation inputs are 366 days old, so any conclusion drawn from them is a year behind the company
**Watch for.** First full-year disclosure of Construction Solutions Group segment earnings including Foley and CP&P — this settles whether the acquired earnings cover the acquired debt; A refreshed fundamentals extract once the FY2026 10-K lands (fiscal year ends 31 August 2026, so it is imminent); US non-residential construction starts and rebar spreads

**Data quality.** The one screen flag, possible_trough_cycle_base_newest_fcf_0.42x_oldest, is real and I engaged it: the $578.6M mean sits above the newest year, so the base is not itself depressed, and I did not override it. The much larger problem is unflagged and I found it by reading the 10-Q: fundamentals_age_days is 366 and the extract predates $2.52B of acquisitions (Foley $1.84B, CP&P $675M, both December 2025) and $2.07B of associated new debt. Enterprise value is understated by that $2.07B, and the FCFF base excludes the acquired earnings by the same token. Treated consistently the two corrections roughly cancel, which is why I let the published fair value stand on the unadjusted extract — that pairing of pre-acquisition debt with pre-acquisition cash flow is at least internally coherent, whereas correcting only one side would not be. But note the model's own EV, taken literally, is $2.07B too low, and a reader should not use the printed fair value as a post-deal valuation.
