- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $30.07

**The case for the price.** The market is paying $2.98bn for a primary aluminium smelter that earned $889.4M attributable to shareholders in nine months — about 2.5x annualised earnings and roughly 2.4x annualised adjusted EBITDA of $1.25bn. That is not an oversight; it is what the market always pays at a commodity peak, and the peak here is extreme and policy-dependent. The same company generated $40.0M of nine-month net income and $82.3M of nine-month operating cash flow ONE YEAR EARLIER. Management has already guided Q4 adjusted EBITDA down to $275-300M from $331.6M. The margin rests on Section 232 tariffs supporting the US Midwest premium, and the company's own release flags that the administration is proposing to REDUCE aluminium tariffs for parties committing to new US capacity. On top of that sits a greenfield smelter — the first in the US in over 45 years — with $500M of DOE funding and a possible Emirates Global Aluminium joint venture, which is where the cash is going. The market is refusing to capitalise a tariff-supported peak into perpetuity, and that refusal is defensible.

**What changed.** The engine's inputs are a year out of date and the current year is a different business. The 2026-11-05 Q3 10-Q reports nine-month operating cash flow of $630.9M against capex of $172.2M — free cash flow of $458.7M, against an FCFF base of $18.1M. Nine-month net income attributable to Century is $889.4M against $40.0M. Q3 alone: net sales $961.2M, net income $302.6M, adjusted EBITDA $331.6M, driven by the restart of Line 2 at Grundartangi returning to near full production and favourable realised metal prices, with Mt. Holly restarted too. Cash went from $134.2M at 2025-12-31 to $713.0M, and liquidity is $1,161.5M. Three qualifiers arrived with it: Q4 adjusted EBITDA is guided to $275-300M, i.e. DOWN 10-17% sequentially; Hurricane Melissa struck Jamalco in late October and the restart is still in progress; and 45X advanced-manufacturing tax-credit receipts are explicitly named as a contributor to operating cash flow, so part of the cash is a legislated policy transfer rather than operating margin. Separately, capex has already more than doubled to $172.2M for nine months from $65.1M on the Mt. Holly restart.

**Base case.** No number is recordable, because the base spans a factor of six and the sign of the answer sits inside it. The engine's $18.1M is not a cash-flow estimate at all: mean(cfo-less-capex) over the window is [$84.8M, -$106.9M, $10.6M], a mean of NEGATIVE $3.8M, so about 121% of the recorded base is the after-tax interest add-back on $564.3M of debt. That is the SABR condition in its purest recorded form, and `nonpositive_normalized_fcff` stays silent only because the post-add-back figure is positive by $18M. At the other end, the current run rate is roughly $610M of annualised free cash flow. Between those lie the only two defensible anchors and they disagree: on the 2026 run rate the enterprise is on 2.4x EBITDA and the market requires deeply negative growth, i.e. very cheap; on a through-cycle base drawn from this company's own history — free cash flow of $10.6M, -$106.9M and $84.8M across 2023-25, essentially zero across a full cycle — the enterprise is on roughly 20x and the market requires a great deal, i.e. expensive. Century has destroyed capital across prior cycles, and 2026 is the best year in its history by a factor of twenty. Per the AMR and CSTM rule, when the base is far too low AND the current level is a peak, the answer is the range, not the average.

First, the same company produced $82.3M of nine-month operating cash flow in 2025 and $630.9M in 2026 — a 7.7x swing with no change in the asset base beyond restarts, which is the definition of a price-driven peak rather than an earnings base.
