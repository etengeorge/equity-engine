**The case for the price.** At $79.20 the market is paying roughly 9x a normalized unlevered cash flow for a carbon-black franchise in visible earnings decline. Cabot has just tightened fiscal 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $6.15-6.45, which is DOWN about 12% on fiscal 2025, and the decline is concentrated in Reinforcement Materials, where Chinese overcapacity and soft replacement-tyre demand cap pricing while feedstock costs rise faster than contract resets. The market's implied -4.9% is not pessimism about the enterprise, it is an assumption that the highest-margin segment shrinks and that Battery Materials - the growth story the sell-side likes - remains too small to offset it for several more years. Cabot is also absorbing a real cash-conversion problem: nine months of fiscal 2026 produced $278M of operating cash flow against $665M for the whole of fiscal 2025.

**What changed.** Q3 fiscal 2026 (2026-08-03): adjusted EPS $1.67, up 4% sequentially, with Performance Chemicals offsetting Reinforcement Materials margin pressure. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance TIGHTENED to $6.15-6.45 from $6.00-6.50. Operating cash flow of $75M in the quarter absorbed $44M of working capital on rapidly rising raw-material costs. Net debt/EBITDA 1.4x, liquidity $1.3B, cash $250M. A $19M discrete tax charge on ceasing carbon black production at Campana, Argentina drove a 79% quarterly effective rate. 2026-08-12: priced $350M of 4.950% senior notes due 2029. 2026-09-15: Steve Delahunt appointed INTERIM CFO - this is not a governance red flag, it is the consequence of CEO Sean Keohane retiring after 25 years and CFO Erica McLaughlin being promoted to succeed him.

**Base case.** Cabot discloses sustaining and compliance capital expenditure SEPARATELY from total capex, which per the AROC rule means CFO-less-total-capex is the wrong definition of owner earnings here. Its own reconciliation for the nine months of fiscal 2026 reads: operating cash flow $278M, total capex $152M, of which sustaining and compliance capex is $111M - so roughly 27% of capital spending is capacity expansion, mostly Battery Materials brownfield. Discretionary free cash flow (CFO less sustaining capex less working-capital movement) was $225M over nine months, about $300M annualised, and adding back after-tax interest of roughly $57M gives a sustaining base near $357M. The pairing rule is absolute: a sustaining base takes near-zero growth, because the growth capex that would buy the growth has been excluded. I underwrite +1.5% - essentially inflation-linked pricing on a mature reinforcement franchise, with Performance Chemicals and Battery Materials contributing just enough to offset Reinforcement Materials' structural decline. That is materially below the naive +7.3% revenue CAGR baseline, which is measured off the 2020 trough and is mostly carbon-black price inflation rather than volume.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I have over-corrected, and the fiscal 2026 cash-flow collapse is a working-capital artifact I should have looked through rather than capitalised. Cabot itself publishes Discretionary Free Cash Flow precisely BECAUSE it strips the working-capital movement, and that figure - $225M over nine months - is holding up far better than free cash flow. The $44M of working capital consumed in the June quarter is inventory and receivables inflating with feedstock prices; when prices flatten it reverses, and a metals-and-chemicals converter that is growing its borrowing base is not a business in distress. On this reading the correct base is closer to the engine's $454.7M than to my $357M, the market's -4.9% is a real discount, and the +86.7% gap is at least partly genuine.
- What would prove it: The fiscal 2026 fourth-quarter and full-year cash flow statement (September year end, reported late October/November) showing whether the working-capital build reverses and full-year operating cash flow recovers toward the $595-692M range of fiscal 2023-2025, or settles near the ~$380M the nine-month run rate implies.
- Already visible today: Some of it, and it genuinely moderated my answer - it is why I used the sustaining base of $357M rather than the $247M that fiscal 2026 total-capex free cash flow implies, and why this is no_edge rather than rich. What the counter cannot explain is the EARNINGS line, which working capital does not touch: adjusted EPS guidance of $6.15-6.45 is down about 12% year on year, and that is a margin fact, not a timing fact. Nor does it rescue the engine's base, because $454.7M is the mean of fiscal 2023-2025 - three years that ended before the decline began. Two defensible bases, $357M and $247M, put the required growth at +0.5% and +9.2%; the engine's number is outside that range entirely.
- Left unresolved: Whether Battery Materials has any near-term earnings materiality. Cabot describes brownfield expansion and capacity investment but I could not find a segment revenue or EBITDA disclosure for it in the Q3 release, so I cannot size the growth the $41M/year of growth capex is buying. That is the single input that would move the bull case, and I could not obtain it.

**Key risks.** Reinforcement Materials spread compression from Chinese carbon-black overcapacity and soft replacement-tyre demand; Working-capital absorption on rising feedstock costs may persist rather than reverse; Simultaneous CEO and CFO transition: Keohane retiring, McLaughlin promoted, an interim CFO in the seat from October; Battery Materials capex is being spent ahead of disclosed segment economics; Plant closures (Campana, Argentina) signal footprint rationalisation with associated discrete charges
**Watch for.** Fiscal 2026 full-year cash flow statement: does the working-capital build reverse, or is ~$380M of operating cash flow the new run rate; First disclosed Battery Materials segment revenue or EBITDA; Fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS guidance against the declining $6.15-6.45 fiscal 2026 range

**Data quality.** The brief raised NO flags, and the most important input was nonetheless wrong. The $454.7M FCFF base is the mean of fiscal 2023, 2024 and 2025 (cfo [$665M, $692M, $595M] less capex [$274M, $241M, $244M]) for a September-year-end filer whose fiscal 2026 is nine-tenths complete and running at $278M of operating cash flow and $126M of free cash flow. The engine has no flag for 'the base year is stale because the fiscal year in progress is materially worse' - fundamentals_age_days looks unremarkable because the 10-K is recent. This is the AROC lesson with an additional twist: Cabot discloses sustaining and compliance capex separately ($111M of $152M over nine months), so CFO-less-TOTAL-capex charges it for expansion and then applies growth to the remainder, but the larger error is simply that the window predates the decline. Correcting the base inverts the verdict: the +86.7% gap and 95th-percentile cohort rank become a required growth of +0.5% on the sustaining base and +9.2% on the fiscal 2026 total-capex base. One flag I checked and CLEARED: the $350M of 4.950% notes priced 2026-08-12 postdate the 2026-06-30 balance sheet, but the 10-Q shows $261M of current-portion debt maturing against $828M long-term (total $1,089M, matching the extract exactly), so this is substantially a refinancing and changes enterprise value by at most ~2%. A second false alarm cleared: the 2026-09-15 interim-CFO 8-K reads as a finance-leadership departure and is in fact a CEO succession.
