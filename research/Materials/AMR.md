**The case for the price.** Alpha is the highest-quality pure-play US metallurgical coal asset and it is being priced as a call option on the met cycle, held by an owner who has already shrunk the equity dramatically. The share count is down to 12,679,045 after roughly $1.2 billion of buybacks at an average of $166.29, with $300M still authorised under a $1.5B programme — so every dollar of a future upcycle lands on a very small number of shares. The balance sheet is effectively unlevered: $11.4M of total long-term debt, $307.6M of cash, $30.9M of short-term investments and $447.8M of total liquidity, with the company guiding to net cash interest INCOME of $2-6M. 97% of sales are metallurgical, mostly exported, into a market where India's import-dependent steel build-out is the structural demand story. Q2 is a trough — adjusted EBITDA of $25.6M on a realisation of $118.71 per ton — and a director bought roughly $7.2 million of stock across three open-market purchases in August. If met coal reverts anywhere near mid-cycle, 12.7 million shares against 14 million tons of annual capacity is enormous operating and share-count leverage, and that is what the 48.2% twenty-one-day move is buying.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 (2026-08-07): net loss $12.3M ($0.96/share) against $11.0M in Q1; adjusted EBITDA $25.6M against $30.0M in Q1 and $46.1M a year earlier; revenue $492.9M against $550.3M; 3.5 million tons sold against 3.9 million. Met segment realisation fell to $118.71 per ton from $124.39; cost of coal sales improved to $103.07 from $107.98. Operating cash flow $39.9M, capital expenditures $45.1M. Management CUT full-year shipment guidance after high-wind storm damage in June destroyed a stacker reclaimer at the Dominion Terminal Associates export facility, and RAISED cost-of-coal-sales guidance on lower volumes and higher supply costs. FY2026 guidance: met segment shipments 14.2-15.4Mt, cost $103-107/ton, SG&A $53-59M, idle operations $24-32M, DD&A $160-174M, capital expenditures $148-168M, capital contributions to equity affiliates $35-45M (including DTA facility upgrades). As of 2026-07-30, 70% of 2026 met coal is committed and priced at an average of $128.17 per ton; thermal is fully committed at $75.94. Kingston Wildcat underground mine started production in Q2. Director Courtis bought roughly $2.9M, $3.2M and $1.1M of stock in open-market purchases on 2026-08-22 and 2026-08-25, taking his direct holding above 1.0 million shares. B. Riley cut its price target to $185 from $189 on 2026-08-20.

**Base case.** The FCFF base has to be replaced before any growth rate means anything. The engine uses $340.1M, the mean of $17.8M, $381.1M and $605.8M — a series that spans the 2022-23 met coal super-cycle and the collapse out of it, and whose mean corresponds to no state this business will plausibly occupy. The current reality is the opposite of what the model's trough flag implies: first-half 2026 operating cash flow was $68.9M against capital expenditures of $85.8M, so first-half free cash flow was NEGATIVE $16.9M, and full-year guidance is $148-168M of capex plus $35-45M of contributions to equity affiliates against first-half adjusted EBITDA of $55.6M. Alpha will burn cash in 2026. I have overridden the base to $150M, which is a deliberately mid-cycle figure: roughly 14 million tons at a realised margin near $30 per ton — between today's $15 (a $118.71 realisation against a $103.07 cost) and the $50+ of a genuine upcycle — less $56M of SG&A, $28M of idle expense, $158M of capex and $40M of affiliate contributions. Growth of 2% on top of that is just inflation; there is no volume growth story here, only a price story. I want the arithmetic of this on the record because it is the whole name: at the currently committed $128.17 per ton the mid-cycle figure is roughly $40M, at $135 it is roughly $138M, and at $155 it is over $400M. A $27 per ton move in realisation swings the base by an order of magnitude.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That a mid-cycle DCF is the wrong tool and I am using it to talk myself out of the best asymmetry on today's list. This is a debt-free company with $447.8M of liquidity, 14 million tons of premium met capacity, and 12.7 million shares outstanding after retiring roughly a third of the equity. Coal assets do not get built any more, so supply is structurally capped; demand from India is structurally growing. At the bottom of a commodity cycle the earnings-based valuation is always at its worst and that is exactly when the option is cheapest. A director with the best available information just spent $7.2 million of his own money over four days. My $150M mid-cycle base is a judgement about a coal price I have already admitted I cannot forecast, and by choosing the middle I am mechanically producing a middling answer.
- What would prove it: The realised price per ton over the next two to three quarters against the $103-107 cost guidance, and the uncommitted 30% of 2026 volume plus 2027 contracting — that is where a cycle turn would show up first and unambiguously.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it cuts both ways, which is why this is no_edge rather than a call. Against the bull case: 70% of 2026 is already committed and priced at $128.17 per ton, so most of this year is locked at a spread of roughly $23 per ton over guided cost — an upcycle cannot rescue 2026 even if it starts tomorrow. Also against: the June DTA storm damage is a real, dated operational impairment with no clarity yet on the stacker reclaimer replacement, and management cut shipment guidance because of it. For the bull case: the cost line is genuinely improving, $103.07 down from $107.98, and Kingston Wildcat came on in Q2. Critically, I could NOT identify a dated fundamental event behind the 48.2% twenty-one-day move that won this name its opportunistic slot. Q2 on 2026-08-07 was a miss with a guidance cut, and the stock rose 10.8% on it; the only other identifiable catalysts are the insider purchases and a price-target CUT. A 48% move on a guidance cut plus insider buying is a positioning and sentiment move, not new information about cash flows — and I am not willing to build a thesis on a re-rating I cannot explain.
- Left unresolved: I cannot forecast metallurgical coal prices, and on this name that is not a caveat, it is the entire valuation — my own scenario arithmetic swings the FCFF base from roughly $40M to over $400M across a $27 per ton range of realisations. I also could not determine what drove the 48.2% twenty-one-day move, and I did not read the August investor presentation or the earnings call transcript, either of which might contain a 2027 contracting or demand comment that would narrow the range.

**Key risks.** The valuation is a metallurgical coal price forecast wearing a DCF, and the base swings by an order of magnitude across a plausible price range; Free cash flow is currently negative: first-half operating cash flow $68.9M against $85.8M of capex, with FY26 capex guided to $148-168M plus $35-45M of affiliate contributions; 70% of 2026 volume is already committed at $128.17 per ton, so a cycle turn cannot rescue this year; The DTA stacker reclaimer destroyed by June storm damage has no disclosed replacement timeline and is already reducing shipments; The 48.2% twenty-one-day move that won this name a slot has no fundamental explanation I could find
**Watch for.** Realised price per ton in Q3 and the 2027 committed-and-priced disclosure — the first place a genuine cycle turn appears; Clarity on the DTA stacker reclaimer replacement and the insurance claim; Whether operating cash flow covers capex in any quarter of the next four; Continued open-market insider purchases, and continued buyback execution against the remaining $300M authorisation
