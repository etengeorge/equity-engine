**The case for the price.** The market is pricing a company that has finished its restructuring and is now a debt-free, Americas-focused gold producer sitting on $1.8B of cash in a strong gold tape. On 2026-06-24 SSR closed the sale of its 80% interest in Çöpler and related Türkiye properties for roughly $1.49B in cash, and on 2026-07-17 sold its 20% of Hod Maden for an uncapped 4.0% net smelter royalty — removing at a stroke the asset whose February 2024 heap-leach failure produced the -$103.4M free-cash-flow year sitting in the middle of the screen's three-year window. What is left is Marigold, Seabee, CC&V and Puna, guided to 450,000-535,000 gold-equivalent ounces for 2026, with the balance sheet carrying $1.78B of cash and no debt as of 6/30/2026. Capital is coming back fast: an additional $500M buyback authorised 2026-06-15, 12.9M shares repurchased year to date for $409.2M of capital returns — an effective yield near 8% — and the quarterly dividend reinstated at $0.03 on 2026-08-04. Q2 delivered $137.0M of net income, $115.6M of operating cash flow and $50.3M of free cash flow from continuing operations. A stock up 46% in 21 days and 105% in a year is a levered call on gold held by a company that now has the cash to buy back stock while it waits.

**What changed.** The company was reconstituted after the balance-sheet date the screen is priced off. (1) 2026-06-24: Çöpler 80% stake sold for approximately $1.49B cash. (2) 2026-07-17: Hod Maden 20% sold for an uncapped 4.0% NSR royalty. (3) 2026-06-15: an additional $500M share buyback authorised; $409.2M returned year to date across 12.9M shares. (4) 2026-08-04: Q2 results — net income $137.0M ($0.66 diluted), operating cash flow $115.6M, free cash flow $50.3M, 101,959 GEO produced, 2026 guidance of 450,000-535,000 GEO reaffirmed, $1.78B cash and debt-free, revolver upsized from $400M to $600M on a four-year term, quarterly dividend reinstated at $0.03 payable 2026-09-11. Costs were flagged as rising in the Q2 materials and the quarter was characterised as an earnings miss despite the cash position.

**Base case.** I decline to supply one, because both sides of the reverse DCF describe a company that no longer exists. The enterprise value of $8.08B is built from a 2025-12-31 balance sheet: $229.5M of debt, $534.8M of cash and $806.5M of non-controlling interest, that last item being the 20% of Çöpler that was sold outright in June. On the 6/30/2026 balance sheet the company holds roughly $1.78B of cash, is debt-free, and the Çöpler minority is gone, so enterprise value is closer to $5.9B than $8.1B — the screen overstates it by roughly 37%. The FCFF base of $125.0M is the mean of 2023, 2024 and 2025, a window whose middle year is the Çöpler disaster (-$103.4M) at an asset that has since been divested, and whose other two years include Çöpler's contribution. Correcting the enterprise value alone collapses the implied growth requirement from +42.1% toward roughly zero; correcting the base as well would move it again, in a direction I cannot compute from committed data because the extract has no continuing-operations split. Feeding a growth rate into that machinery would produce a fair value with two known-wrong inputs, and I will not do it.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The counter to refusing is that the corrected picture is computable enough to act on, and it says the stock is not cheap. On secondary reporting SSR trades near a 10.4x price-to-earnings ratio and roughly a 7% free-cash-flow yield, which on a corrected enterprise value near $5.9B is a healthy but unremarkable multiple — and critically, that yield is earned at what is very plausibly a cyclical peak in the gold price. The stock is up 105% in a year and 46% in 21 days; almost none of that is production growth, since guidance is unchanged at 450,000-535,000 GEO and Q2 materials flagged rising costs. A 7% free-cash-flow yield at peak gold on a company whose all-in costs are rising is exactly the trap LESSONS names: a stable-looking base that is stable at an elevated level. On that reading the honest verdict is not 'the model is broken', it is 'corrected for the model's errors, this is fairly-to-fully priced, and the 8% capital-return yield is being funded out of divestment proceeds rather than mine cash flow'.
- What would prove it: All-in sustaining cost per ounce for the continuing four-mine portfolio against the realised gold price, over the next two or three quarters, and the free cash flow those mines generate excluding divestment proceeds. If continuing-operations free cash flow annualises anywhere near the $50.3M posted in Q2 rather than the several hundred million the market seems to assume, the capital returns are a liquidation of the Çöpler proceeds rather than an earnings stream, and the stock is expensive. If mine free cash flow runs materially higher at the current gold price, the 7% yield is real and I have refused a live idea.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it is uncomfortable for my refusal. Q2 free cash flow from continuing operations was $50.3M against $115.6M of operating cash flow — roughly $200M annualised, which against a $7.6B market capitalisation is a 2.6% free-cash-flow yield, not 7%. That single figure is the strongest evidence in this write-up, and it points at the stock being expensive rather than merely unmodellable. What stops me converting it into a 'rich' verdict is that one quarter is not a run rate for a miner, Q2 straddles the divestment closings and so mixes stub periods, and I could not obtain the continuing-operations cash flow split from a primary source to check whether the $50.3M is comparable to a full-quarter run rate at all.
