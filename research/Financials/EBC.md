## 2026-09-16 — NO_EDGE (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $21.86 · fair value $18.60 · gap -14.9%
- **Sustainable ROTCE:** screen said +4.5%, analyst says +13.5%
- **P/TBV:** justified 1.40 vs actual 1.64 on tangible book of $13.30/share

**The case for the price.** At $21.86, or 1.64x tangible book, the market is paying a premium multiple for the largest independent bank headquartered in Boston following two acquisitions in eighteen months. The case for that multiple is operating performance that has already normalised: second-quarter 2026 operating return on average tangible common equity of 15.3%, operating net income up 20% linked quarter, an operating efficiency ratio of 49%, net interest margin expanding 3bp to 3.66%, tangible book value per share compounding at 7% annualised, and non-performing loans declining for a second consecutive quarter as the HarborOne credit mark seasons. A bank earning 15% on tangible equity with a 10.2% cost of equity is worth a premium to book; the buyer is underwriting that the merger integration is done and the earnings power is the current run rate rather than the three-year average.

**What changed.** The HarborOne Bancorp merger completed 2025-11-01 - Eastern issued 26.9 million shares and paid $74.6M cash - which is the second acquisition inside the cash-flow window after Cambridge Bancorp. Q2 2026 (2026-07-23): return on average tangible common equity 15.2% GAAP and 15.3% operating, return on average assets 1.37%, operating net income +20% linked quarter, operating efficiency ratio 49%, NIM 3.66%. Q1 2026 operating ROTCE was 12.8% and Q4 2025 13.8%. The Board approved a new 5% share repurchase programme. Management states it is on track to achieve the merger-related financial targets set at announcement.

**Base case.** I supply a sustainable ROTCE of 13.5%. The reported quarterly series runs 16.44%, 16.42%, 14.39%, 9.75%, 15.15% on a GAAP tangible basis, with operating ROTCE at 15.3% in the most recent quarter and 12.8% the quarter before. I deliberately underwrite below the 15.2-15.3% current run rate for two reasons: purchase-accounting accretion from the HarborOne and Cambridge marks is flattering net interest margin and runs off over the next several years, and the 49% efficiency ratio reflects cost saves that are fully realised rather than still compounding. 13.5% is the level I think Eastern holds through a cycle once accretion normalises, which is roughly the bank's own pre-merger through-cycle performance. That is three times the engine's 4.5% and still one to two points below what it is earning today.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That 1.64x tangible book is genuinely too much, and correcting the ROTCE upward does not rescue it. Even at my 13.5% the justified multiple is roughly 1.40 against an actual 1.64 - still about 15% overvalued - and the discount rate is not the usual excuse, because Eastern's beta of 0.95 carries an R-squared of 0.499, the best-fitted regression in today's ten and far above the level at which the low-R-squared bias operates. So this is not a measurement artifact: a well-measured cost of equity of 10.2% against a defensible 13.5% return still says the stock is dear. Worse, the bank is a serial acquirer whose reported returns have been flattered by accretion twice in two years, and the 15.3% operating figure excludes merger expenses that were real cash.
- What would prove it: The accretion disclosure in successive 10-Qs: how much of net interest margin is purchase-accounting accretion, and whether core NIM ex-accretion is rising or falling as it runs off. Also whether ROTCE holds above 13% in the quarters after the HarborOne cost saves have fully annualised and there is no new acquisition contributing.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it is why I did not call this cheap despite an 80-point correction to the engine's number. Supporting the counter: management attributes margin expansion to 'higher asset yields' rather than to core loan repricing, and a 7% annualised tangible book growth rate is respectable but not fast enough to grow into a 1.64x multiple quickly. Against it: the trajectory is the right way round - operating net income +20% linked quarter, NPLs falling for two consecutive quarters, positive operating leverage from both revenue growth AND lower expenses, and a new 5% buyback authorised, which is not the behaviour of a management team that thinks the multiple is stretched. The honest resolution is that the gap is modest and sits inside the range the model's own constants control: at the fixed 2.0% terminal growth the justified multiple is 1.40, but at 3.0% it is 1.46 and at 4.0% it is 1.53, so a -15% gap becomes -7% on a constant that is a choice rather than a fact. That is the ENVA bias operating on a name that is growing, and it is not a thesis.
- Left unresolved: The size of the purchase-accounting accretion contribution to net interest margin. It is the one number that decides whether 15.3% or 11% is the right through-cycle return, and I could not isolate it from the earnings release.

**Key risks.** Purchase-accounting accretion from two mergers flattering net interest margin with an undisclosed runoff profile; Massachusetts commercial real estate concentration in a book that is currently unusually clean; Serial acquirer: a third deal would reset the earnings base and the credit marks again; Goodwill and intangibles are 30% of book, so tangible book is a materially smaller denominator than reported equity; 1.64x tangible book leaves little room if the 15% return proves to be a post-merger peak
**Watch for.** Core net interest margin excluding purchase-accounting accretion in the Q3 2026 10-Q; Whether operating ROTCE holds above 13% once HarborOne cost saves fully annualise; Execution of the new 5% repurchase authorisation at 1.64x tangible book

**Data quality.** The flag unstable_rotce_0.9%_to_6.6% named the symptom and the model averaged the artifact in anyway - the AX/AUB/ENVA pattern, and the most extreme instance in today's ten. The engine's 'sustainable ROTCE' of 4.5% is a three-year GAAP average spanning the Cambridge Bancorp merger, an available-for-sale securities repositioning loss, and the HarborOne merger completed 2025-11-01 with its day-two CECL provision and merger expenses - three separate non-recurring events, none of which describes the bank's earning power. Eastern's own reported figures are operating ROTCE of 15.3% in Q2 2026, 12.8% in Q1 2026 and 13.8% in Q4 2025, with GAAP ROTCE of 15.2% most recently. The defect is worth 79 points of gap: at the engine's 4.5% the justified P/TBV is 0.31 against an actual 1.64 for a -81.4% gap, while at the current 15.2% it is roughly 1.61 and the gap is about -2%, and at my deliberately conservative 13.5% it is roughly 1.40 for a gap near -15%. Per the LESSONS rule for book-method names I also ran the two standing cross-checks and BOTH pass cleanly: equity_now agrees with equity_series[0] so there is no AII/BUR stale-tangible-book error, and tangible book per share of $13.30 is consistent with the company's disclosure. So the tangible book is right and the numerator alone is wrong - which is the AX signature precisely. The goodwill_and_intangibles_30%_of_book flag is real but is a consequence of two acquisitions rather than a modelling error. Recorded gap and fair value here reflect my 13.5% override, not the engine's 4.5%.
