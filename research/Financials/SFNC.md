## 2026-11-06 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $23.23 · fair value $20.47 · gap -11.9%

- **P/TBV:** justified 1.53 vs actual 1.73 on tangible book of $13.41/share

**The case for the price.** The market pays 1.72x tangible book for an Arkansas bank that has just finished repositioning its balance sheet and is now earning an adjusted 15.5% return on tangible common equity with a net interest margin expanding 8bp to 3.92%. The price is a bet that the clean-up is behind it: a $562.8M securities-repositioning loss taken in Q3 2025 swapped a low-yielding bond book for current coupons, and a branch and headcount restructuring announced in September 2026 is taking the cost base down. At 1.72x tangible book against a 15.5% adjusted return and a 9.5% cost of equity, the market is paying roughly fair value for a bank that has done the painful thing and is now earning its keep — and discounting the fact that roughly 39% of its equity is goodwill.

**What changed.** Nothing material since the Q3 print, but the engine's reading of the history is wrong by 16.8 percentage points of return and that is the whole of its -125.3% gap. The engine's 'sustainable ROTCE' of -1.3% is the mean of a three-year GAAP window whose newest observation is a FY2025 net LOSS of $397.6M. That loss is not operating: Q3 2025 alone was a net loss of $562,792k, and it is a securities-portfolio repositioning — tangible book per share was $13.45 at Q3 2025 and is $13.52 today, essentially unchanged across the year in which the loss was taken, because the unrealised loss already sat in accumulated other comprehensive income. Goodwill is flat at $1,320,799k across all three years, so there was no impairment. The company's own Q3 2026 figures: net income $47.1M, ROAA 0.77%, return on average common equity 5.37%, adjusted ROAA 1.29% and ADJUSTED ROTCE 15.50%. Nine-month 2026 net income is $182.3M against a nine-month 2025 LOSS of $475.6M. A separate, smaller restructuring was announced under item 2.05 on 2026-09-03 (branch and real-estate rightsizing, severance and early retirement), which is what the Q3 adjustments mostly are.

**Base case.** I underwrite a sustainable ROTCE of 13.5%, between the company's reported GAAP and adjusted figures and below both its own adjusted 15.50% and the 2026 annualised GAAP figure. The arithmetic: nine-month 2026 net income of $182.3M annualises to $243.1M against tangible common equity of about $1.95bn ($13.52 x 144.2M shares), i.e. a GAAP ROTCE near 12.5%. The company's adjusted 15.50% strips branch and real-estate rightsizing, severance, certain professional services, accelerated prepaid expenses, securities losses and debt-extinguishment losses. Some of that is genuinely discrete — the September 2026 item 2.05 programme is dated and finite — but the uplift is large and uneven: Q3 reported $47.1M against adjusted $79.0M, a $31.9M add-back in one quarter, where Q2 reported $66.7M against adjusted $72.2M, only $5.5M. Taking the cleaner Q2 adjusted figure annualised gives about 14.8%; discounting that for the recurring fraction of professional services and prepaid-expense acceleration, and for the fact that the NIM expansion is three quarters old, lands me at 13.5%. At 13.5% against a 9.5% cost of equity and the model's fixed 2% terminal growth, the justified multiple is 1.53x against an actual 1.72x — about 11% rich, which is inside this model's honest precision rather than a thesis.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I have corrected a defect and then quietly accepted management's own non-GAAP number, which is the AMSF mistake in a new place. GAAP return on average common equity is 5.37%, not 15.50%; the gap between them is management's judgement about what is non-recurring, and this is a bank that has taken a $562.8M securities loss, a branch restructuring, a severance programme and a debt extinguishment inside eighteen months. A company that restructures every year does not have non-recurring restructuring charges. On GAAP ROTCE of roughly 9.2% (5.37% grossed up for the goodwill in the denominator) the justified multiple is 1.0x against an actual 1.72x — about 42% rich, and the engine's direction, if not its magnitude, would be right.
- What would prove it: Whether Q4 2026 and the 2027 quarters carry further 'adjusted' items. A clean quarter with reported and adjusted converging settles it in management's favour; another $30M of add-backs settles it against them.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it is why I underwrite 13.5% rather than 15.5%. The evidence for management: Q2 2026 needed only $5.5M of adjustment against Q3's $31.9M, so the add-backs are lumpy and concentrated in the quarter the restructuring was announced, not uniform. The NIM expanded 8bp to 3.92% and the securities repositioning that cost $562.8M of accounting loss cost essentially nothing in tangible book — $13.45 to $13.52 — which is the point of the exercise and is verified. Against management: the item 2.05 filing is dated 2026-09-01, so the programme is live and its costs are not finished, and tangible book per share FELL from $14.42 to $13.52 in Q3 on an AOCI build from higher long-term rates, which is a reminder that this bank's book is rate-exposed in both directions. The counter does not win outright, but it moves my number down two points and it is why conviction is low.
- Left unresolved: Whether the 'certain professional services' and 'accelerated prepaid expenses' add-backs recur, which I cannot tell from one release. I also cannot verify the credit outlook: I did not reconcile non-performing loans or the allowance, and the company-news section held only seven items with nothing dated after 2026-09-04, so I have no independent read on asset quality.
