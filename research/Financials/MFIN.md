## 2026-09-01 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $11.82

**The case for the price.** At $11.82 against a genuine tangible book of $6.13 per share, Medallion Financial trades at 1.93x tangible common equity — not the 1.13x the screen believes. On the correct multiple the market is already paying a premium for a consumer lender earning high-teens to low-twenties returns on tangible common equity, and that premium is defensible but not obviously wrong: Medallion Bank's recreation and home-improvement lending books have performed, the legacy taxi medallion drag is largely behind the company, and net income has run $55.1M, $35.9M, $43.0M across the last three years. Nobody is getting a free lunch at roughly 2x tangible book for a subprime-adjacent consumer lender late in a credit cycle.

**What changed.** Nothing material found, and I want to be explicit that this is a statement about coverage rather than about the company. The brief holds no company news at all for the last 90 days, which means the name did not move enough, did not trade abnormal volume and did not file an 8-K to earn a per-company pull — it was picked on the gap alone. There is a 2026-08-05 10-Q and a 2026-07-29 8-K (item 2.02) with Q2 results, plus a cluster of June 8-Ks under items 5.02 and 8.01 suggesting board or officer changes. I did not read the Q2 release because the engine's exhibit extractor returns nothing and I judged the ninety-second fetch better spent on the names where a document decided a number; the balance-sheet decomposition below settles this name without it. I am recording that as a choice, not as an absence.

**Base case.** This is a book-method name, so no FCFF growth rate applies and record.py does not reprice it. The substantive finding is that the reported +213% gap is a known model defect, not an opportunity. LESSONS already names this exact bug: justified_pb computes tangible common equity as equity minus goodwill minus intangibles and never deducts non-controlling interests or preferred, even though both sit in the same extract and are correctly subtracted in enterprise_value. Medallion carries $99.4M of non-controlling interest — the preferred equity issued at Medallion Bank — inside the $408.6M of reported equity. Deducting goodwill of $150.8M and intangibles of $17.7M gives the model's tangible book of $10.46 per share on 22.96M shares; deducting the $99.4M of NCI as well gives $6.13. That is a 71% overstatement of tangible book, and it converts an apparent 1.13x P/TBV into an actual 1.93x. Against a justified multiple of 3.54x the gap does not vanish entirely, but the justified multiple is itself built on a sustainable ROTCE the model flags as unstable across a 17.9% to 32.1% range, and that ROTCE is computed on the same overstated equity denominator. I am not going to publish a fair value derived from two inputs I have just shown to be wrong in the same direction.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The counter is that I have over-corrected. Medallion Bank's preferred is genuinely subordinate to nothing the common holder cares about in a going concern — it is cheap permanent capital funding an earning asset, and the earnings attributable to common already sit in the net income series. If the $43.0M of net income is attributable to common shareholders AFTER the NCI preferred dividend, then deducting the NCI from the book denominator while leaving a common-only numerator is the correct treatment and my 1.93x stands. But if the model's net income series is total including NCI, then the ROTCE numerator and denominator are at least internally consistent as the model computes them, and my correction breaks that consistency rather than restoring it. On that reading the name might still be modestly cheap.
- What would prove it: The income statement's attribution line: whether the $43.0M net income series is 'net income attributable to Medallion Financial Corp.' or total consolidated net income before the non-controlling interest. That single line decides whether my correction is right or double-counts.
- Already visible today: Not to me. I did not fetch Medallion's filings and so I could not check the attribution line, which is the honest limit on this verdict. What IS visible and not in dispute is that the equity denominator includes $99.4M of non-controlling interest that a common holder does not own — that is arithmetic from the extract, and it means the reported tangible book of $10.46 per share overstates what the common shareholder has a claim on regardless of how the numerator is defined.
- Left unresolved: Whether the correct actual P/TBV is 1.93x or something between 1.13x and 1.93x, which depends on the income attribution line I did not read. Also unresolved: whether the 17.9%-32.1% ROTCE range reflects genuine credit-cycle variability or a mark-driven artifact of the kind LESSONS flags for JXN. Either way the +213% gap is not a finding.

**Key risks.** Tangible book per share is overstated 71% by the model, so the headline cheapness is largely a computation error; Subprime-adjacent consumer lending (recreation, home improvement) into a softening consumer; Goodwill and intangibles are 41% of reported book; $901K average daily dollar volume — thin enough that a position could not be exited quickly
**Watch for.** A rerun after justified_pb is corrected to deduct non-controlling interests and preferred — this name cannot be judged until then; Credit metrics in the recreation lending book: net charge-offs and 30+ day delinquency trend; Any change in Medallion Bank's preferred capital structure

**Data quality.** SUSPECTED MODEL ERROR, consistent with a defect already recorded in LESSONS. goodwill_and_intangibles_41%_of_book: confirmed, $150.8M goodwill plus $17.7M intangibles against $408.6M equity. The unflagged and more serious problem is that the same $408.6M includes $99.4M of non-controlling interest which justified_pb does not deduct, overstating tangible book per share at $10.46 against a corrected $6.13 and understating actual P/TBV at 1.13x against a corrected 1.93x. unstable_rotce_17.9%_to_32.1%: confirmed and unresolved — that spread is too wide for the average to be called 'sustainable', and it is computed on the same overstated denominator. I could not read the Q2 filing to check the net income attribution line, which is the one input that would settle how large the correction should be; that is a limit on this verdict and I am recording it rather than filling it. ## 2026-09-23 — NO_EDGE (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $11.95 · fair value $13.98 · gap +17.0%
- **Sustainable ROTCE:** screen said +22.6%, analyst says +10.5%
- **P/TBV:** justified 1.34 vs actual 1.14 on tangible book of $10.46/share

**The case for the price.** At $11.95 Medallion Financial trades at about 1.15x a tangible book of $10.38 a share, which is roughly what a subprime-adjacent consumer lender earning 10% on tangible common equity is worth against an equity cost anywhere north of 8%. The market is not refusing to pay for a 22.6% return — it is correctly pricing the return the company is actually producing NOW. Net income attributable to Medallion Financial fell 46% in the first half of 2026, to $12.4 million from $23.1 million, while originations hit records: the balance sheet is growing and the earnings on it are not, because provisions and operating expense are absorbing the spread. Recreation-lending net charge-offs are running in the 3.25%-3.94% range. A buyer at 1.15x tangible book is paying an ordinary multiple for an ordinary return in a deteriorating credit environment, and there is no free lunch in it.

**What changed.** Two things, and the first settles a question two previous passes on this name left open. (1) EARNINGS HAVE COLLAPSED. Q2 2026 net income attributable to Medallion Financial Corp. was $7,445 thousand against $11,069 thousand a year earlier (-33%), and H1 2026 was $12,398 thousand against $23,083 thousand (-46%) — this against RECORD originations, 7% sequential loan growth, total assets through $3.2 billion and net interest income of $57.2 million. The company's own framing is that net income dipped on 'higher provisions and operating expenses'. Recreation-lending net charge-offs are disclosed at 3.25% and 3.94% of average gross loans excluding loans held for sale. Cumulative taxi-medallion net charge-offs rose to $168.1 million from $161.5 million. (2) On 2026-09-22 — the day before this screen — Medallion Bank launched a REOPENING of its Series G non-cumulative perpetual preferred (Nasdaq: MBNKO), proceeds possibly to redeem the Series E. That is more non-controlling-interest capital ahead of the common, and it is what earned this name its opportunistic slot.

**Base case.** A book-method name, so the input is a sustainable return on tangible common equity, and I set it at 10.5%. H1 2026 net income attributable to the parent of $12.4 million annualises to $24.8 million; tangible common equity is $408.6 million of parent-only stockholders' equity less $150.8 million of goodwill and $17.0 million of intangibles, or $240.8 million. That is a 10.3% return, and I round to 10.5% to avoid pretending to precision on a half-year. The engine's 22.6% is the mean of three GAAP years ending 2025-12-31 — a window that closed before the deterioration began, since FY2025's own second half was already running at roughly $19.9 million against the $23.1 million of H1 2025. This is the CBT/CASH failure on a book-method name: the fiscal window is recent enough that no staleness flag fires, and the business inside it has changed. Note the direction of travel is still downward, so 10.5% is a current reading and not obviously a floor.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The bull case is the CECL argument above and it is genuinely strong, because it is the standard mechanical consequence of record origination growth: under CECL a lender books the lifetime expected loss on a new loan in the quarter it originates it, while the interest arrives over years. Medallion grew loans 7% sequentially to record levels; the provision that growth forces is not evidence of credit deterioration, it is evidence of growth. On that reading H1 2026 earnings are artificially depressed by the very thing that makes the franchise more valuable, and the 10.3% I am calling sustainable is a trough. This also happens to be the counter that the 2026-09-02 pass on this name explicitly could not resolve, and it deserves to be taken seriously rather than waved at.
- What would prove it: Separating the provision into the CECL build on new originations from the charge-offs on the existing book: if net charge-offs are flat and the provision is all growth-driven, the counter wins; if charge-offs are rising, it does not.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it does not win. Net charge-offs in recreation lending are disclosed at 3.25% and 3.94% of average gross loans — mid-to-high 3% is realised loss, not a forward reserve, and it is the single largest consumer book. Cumulative taxi-medallion charge-offs also rose $6.6 million year over year, in a portfolio that is supposed to be finished. And the decline is not one quarter of CECL noise: it runs from H2 2025 (roughly $19.9 million implied) through Q1 and Q2 2026, four consecutive periods. The 2026-09-02 pass wrote that its dismissal would fail 'if the ROTCE is real at even 18% — the LOW end of the observed range'. The actual print is 10.3%, which is BELOW the low end of the range, not at it. The test that pass nominated has now been run and it goes the other way.
- Left unresolved: The cost of equity, which the schema cannot override and which decides the sign. The 8.4% comes from a beta of 0.617 measured at R-squared 0.187 on a stock that trades $701 thousand a day; on that tape it is not a measurement. At 8.4% the corrected gap is +13%; at a beta of 1.0 it is -15%. I also could not size the Series G reopening, so I cannot quantify how much further the NCI dividend load rises.

**Key risks.** Net income attributable to the parent fell 46% year over year in H1 2026 and the trend is not yet arrested; Recreation-lending net charge-offs at 3.25%-3.94% into a softening consumer, on 2024-25 vintages that are only part-seasoned; Goodwill and intangibles are 41% of reported book; strip them and the equity cushion is $240.8M against $3.2B of assets; The Series G preferred reopening adds non-controlling-interest dividends ahead of the common, and the NCI already took $4.7M of H1 earnings; $701K average daily dollar volume — a position could not be exited quickly, and the 0.62 beta setting the 8.4% cost of equity is measured on that tape
