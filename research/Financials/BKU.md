## 2026-11-06 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $44.54 · fair value $41.03 · gap -7.9%
- **Sustainable ROTCE:** screen said +8.1%, analyst says +9.9%
- **P/TBV:** justified 0.96 vs actual 1.04 on tangible book of $42.89/share

**The case for the price.** The market pays 1.14x tangible book for a $34.6bn Florida and New York commercial bank earning a 9.6% return on average equity that has improved in each of the last four quarters (9.0%, 9.0%, 9.5%, 9.3%, 9.6%). Against a 10.3% cost of equity measured from a sound regression, a bank earning slightly less than its cost of capital should trade at or slightly below tangible book; it trades at a modest premium because the return is rising and the balance sheet is clean — $78M of goodwill on $2.82bn of equity, so tangible book is almost all of book. That is a coherent, unexciting price for a coherent, unexciting bank, and there is no mechanism visible that forces a re-rating in either direction.

**What changed.** Nothing material, and the absence is itself a data point: the news store held NO company-specific items for this name in the ninety-day window, which the brief correctly describes as a fact about coverage rather than about the company — BKU neither moved, traded abnormal volume, nor filed anything beyond routine earnings. What the Q3 filings do change is both inputs to the model, in opposite directions. Tangible book value per share is $39.10 at 2026-09-30, down from $40.48 in the prior quarter and 'essentially flat from a year ago', against the engine's $42.89 — the engine is a period stale, using FY2025 equity of $3,053,829k against the current $2,822,220k. Total stockholders' equity has fallen in each of the last three quarters ($3,053,829k at FY2025, then $3,015,537k, $3,003,405k, $2,822,220k), a $231.6M decline against positive earnings, on an AOCI build from higher long-term rates plus buybacks. And the return is higher than the engine's three-year average: 9.6% on average equity in Q3 2026, which grosses up to roughly 9.9% on tangible common equity given the small goodwill balance, against the engine's 8.06%.

**Base case.** I underwrite a sustainable ROTCE of 9.9%, which is the company's own Q3 2026 return on average equity of 9.6% adjusted for the modest goodwill in the denominator. The engine's 8.06% is the arithmetic mean of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 ratios of 7.00%, 8.35% and 8.83% — a monotonically rising series, which is the VLY condition: an average across a rising path describes a company that no longer exists. The path is now 7.00%, 8.35%, 8.83% and 9.6% current, and the improvement is coming from the liability side as higher-cost deposits and borrowings reprice down, not from credit releases or one-off gains. I do not underwrite more than 9.9% because that is where the company actually is, the trend has four quarters behind it rather than eight, and nothing in the disclosure promises a further leg. At 9.9% against a 10.3% cost of equity and the model's fixed 2% terminal growth, the justified multiple is 0.95x. Measured against the HONEST actual P/TBV of 1.139x (price $44.54 over the company's reported $39.10) the gap is about -16%; measured against the engine's stale $42.89 book it prints about -8%, and the difference is the defect, not a judgement.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I have corrected the return upward and the book downward and arrived at a fussy -16%, when the honest reading is that this is simply a fairly priced bank and the right answer is 'nothing here'. The stronger version is that the -16% is understated and the name is genuinely rich: equity has FALLEN $231.6M in three quarters while the market capitalisation holds, so the premium to tangible book is widening mechanically, and a bank earning 9.6% against a 10.3% cost of equity is destroying value at the margin — it should trade BELOW tangible book, not at 1.14x. On that reading fair value is nearer $39 than $42.
- What would prove it: Whether the return crosses the cost of equity. A ROTCE sustainably above 10.3% justifies a premium; one below it does not, and the gap is currently 0.4 points in the wrong direction.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it tightens the answer without reversing it. The return has risen in four consecutive quarters and is 0.4 points short of the cost of equity — so the market is paying 1.14x for the final step, which is a reasonable bet rather than an obvious error. But the counter is right that the book is going the wrong way: the tangible common equity ratio fell 45bp in the quarter and 49bp year on year, to 8.0%, and tangible book per share dropped $1.38 sequentially. A premium to a shrinking book needs the return to arrive, and it has not yet. That is enough to hold the verdict at no_edge rather than let the corrected ROTCE pull it to fair, and it is why I record the lean as modestly rich.
- Left unresolved: Credit. I did not reconcile non-performing assets, the allowance, or the composition of the New York commercial real estate book, and with no company news in the store I have no independent signal on asset quality at all. For a bank whose whole valuation question is 0.4 points of return against its cost of equity, a credit surprise would dominate everything above.

**Key risks.** The name sits 0.4 points on the wrong side of its own cost of equity — 9.9% ROTCE against 10.3% — so the 1.14x tangible book premium depends on an improvement that has not yet arrived; Total stockholders' equity has fallen $231.6M across three quarters on an AOCI build from higher long-term rates plus buybacks; tangible book per share fell $1.38 sequentially to $39.10 and the TCE ratio fell 45bp to 8.0%; Zero company-specific news in the ninety-day store, so I have no independent read on credit or on the New York commercial real estate book; The engine's tangible book of $42.89 is 9.7% above the company's reported $39.10, so any fair value the model prints is flattering by roughly that amount
**Watch for.** Return on average tangible common equity crossing 10.3%, which is what would justify the premium to book; Non-performing assets and the allowance, and specifically the New York commercial real estate concentration; Whether the AOCI drag reverses or the equity decline continues for a fourth quarter; Deposit cost trajectory — the source of the four-quarter return improvement
