**The case for the price.** Cathay General is a well-run Chinese-American commercial bank earning a return modestly above its cost of equity, and it trades at about 1.6x tangible book, which is what that is worth. Q2 2026 return on average stockholders' equity was 12.21% and return on average assets 1.52%, both improved from 11.88% and 1.47% in Q1; return on average TANGIBLE common equity was 13.98%, up from 13.64% in Q1 and 12.36% a year earlier. The stock is up 25.5% over twelve months. At a 9.84% cost of equity and a 2% terminal growth rate, a 13.98% sustainable return justifies 1.53x tangible book against an actual 1.61x. The market is paying a small premium to the arithmetic for a bank whose returns are rising rather than falling - which is a defensible price, not a mistake in either direction.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The best case against calling this fair is that a 13.98% ROATCE on a bank with Cathay's commercial real-estate concentration is a cyclical high, not a sustainable return, and I have taken the peak quarter of a rising series as the input. The engine's own three-year series runs 13.03%, 12.23%, 15.89% - a 3.7-point range with no trend - and picking the newest quarter from a noisy series is exactly the error the standing lesson warns about in the opposite direction. At the weakest year's 12.23% the justified multiple is 1.30 against an actual 1.61, and the stock is 19% RICH rather than 5%. The whole verdict sits inside that 3.7-point range.
- What would prove it: The path of non-performing loans and criticised commercial real-estate exposure over the next four quarters. A bank earning a genuine 14% on tangible equity does it with stable credit; a bank earning it on reserve releases does not. The specific test is provision expense against net charge-offs - releases flatter the return without improving the book.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it supports the fair reading rather than the bear. The return is rising monotonically over the last three reported quarters (12.36% -> 13.64% -> 13.98%) and return on average ASSETS is rising with it (1.47% -> 1.52%), which is harder to manufacture with a reserve release than a return on equity is. That is the VLY test - rising return together with a rising book is an operating improvement rather than an accounting one. But I did not obtain the credit-quality detail to run the provision-versus-charge-offs check directly.
- Left unresolved: Whether the $128.2M of preferred was outstanding for all three years in the ROTCE window. If it was issued recently, the older tangible-common-equity denominators are understated and the engine's 13.72% average is overstated by a few tenths - immaterial to the verdict but it is the one reconciliation I could not close. I also could not run the credit-quality test above from the documents I fetched.

**Key risks.** commercial real-estate concentration is the structural risk in this franchise and no return-on-equity model prices a credit cycle; the verdict moves from -5% to -19% across the engine's own three-year ROTCE range of 12.23% to 15.89%; the model's fixed 2% terminal growth is doing real work in the justified multiple - at 4% the justified multiple rises above the actual and the sign flips
