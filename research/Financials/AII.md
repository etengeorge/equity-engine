## 2026-09-04 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $26.16 · fair value $29.15 · gap +11.4%
- **Sustainable ROTCE:** screen said +28.9%, analyst says +16.0%
- **P/TBV:** justified 1.69 vs actual 1.52 on tangible book of $17.20/share

**The case for the price.** American Integrity is a Florida-concentrated homeowners insurer earning extraordinary returns in the best underwriting environment Florida property has offered in twenty years, and the market is paying 1.39x book for it rather than a multiple of the current return. Q2 2026: combined ratio 63.4% (from 72.9%), record pre-tax income $46.4M, ROE 38.7% and adjusted ROE 39.6%, gross premiums written +14% to $327M, record voluntary new business up 54% year on year, book value per share $18.86 (+22.3% YoY). The market's discount is not scepticism about those numbers — they are audited — it is a statement about their distribution. This is a single-state carrier whose reinsurance program is built to a 1-in-130-year probable maximum loss, which means it retains everything below that. Four benign years and one landfall is the shape of the business, and the arithmetic mean of that sequence is not what a shareholder compounds. A ~1.4x book multiple on a company that can lose a fifth of its book in a fortnight is a considered price, not an oversight.

**What changed.** Q2 2026, reported 2026-08-11 (8-K items 2.02/7.01, EX-99.1 and EX-99.2): combined ratio 63.4% versus 72.9% a year earlier, improvement driven primarily by a lower expense ratio and the ABSENCE OF CATASTROPHE LOSSES in the quarter. Record pre-tax income $46.4M. ROE 38.7%, adjusted ROE 39.6%. Book value per share $18.86, up 22.3% year on year and 10.1% versus 2026-03-31. Gross premiums written $326.6M (+$39.6M); gross premiums earned $242.3M (+$18.6M). Record voluntary new business policies, +54% year on year and +44% sequentially, concentrated in Tri-County and middle-aged homes. The 2026-2027 catastrophe excess-of-loss program was renewed effective 2026-06-01 with risk-adjusted rate reductions at the upper end of market declines, retaining the 1-in-130-year PML attachment and REDUCING aggregate retention from $95M. Ceded CAT XOL premium fell to 39.4% of premium from 42.2%. No company-specific news since 2026-08-19.

**Base case.** This is a book-method name, so my number is a sustainable ROTCE and it is in rotce_override. I use 16%, against the screen's 28.9% and the company's currently-reported 39.6% adjusted ROE. The bridge: start at ~39% in a zero-catastrophe year; subtract a normalised catastrophe load, which for a carrier retaining everything beneath a 1-in-130-year PML is worth on the order of 15-20 points of ROE averaged across the distribution rather than the zero booked in Q2; then subtract a few points more for the rate cycle. Florida's post-SB-2A economics are genuinely better — litigation frequency has fallen materially and that is a structural, legislated change rather than a weather outcome — but returns of this size attract capital, and the company's own disclosure already shows reinsurance pricing falling at the upper end of market declines, which is what capital inflow looks like from the buy side. Primary rate adequacy follows reinsurance cost down with a lag. 16% through the cycle is a return well above cost of equity and well below what is being printed today.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest case against my no_edge is that I am penalising the name for a risk the company has already sold. AII cedes ~39% of its premium to reinsurers, reduced its aggregate retention at the June 2026 renewal rather than raising it, and bought that protection more cheaply than last year. Its combined ratio improvement was driven primarily by the EXPENSE ratio, not by weather luck. Florida's reform is legislated, not cyclical. And on the corrected book value of $18.86 the stock trades at 1.39x, not the 1.52x the model used — at any sustainable ROTCE above roughly 14% the gap is positive, and the company is currently earning 39.6%. That is a wide margin of safety around my 16% estimate, and it argues the verdict should be cheap.
- What would prove it: The 2026 hurricane season closing without a Florida landfall that pierces the retention, followed by Q3 and Q4 combined ratios in the 60s and a 2027 June 1 renewal that again prices down; plus primary rate filings that hold rather than follow reinsurance costs lower. Four clean quarters of reported ROE above 30% would make 16% look too conservative and would justify revisiting at a 20-22% ROTCE.
- Already visible today: Partially, and honestly it favours the counter more than I expected when I started. The expense-ratio-driven improvement is real and is not weather. The retention reduction is real. The 22.3% book value growth is real. What is NOT visible is any observation of this company through a catastrophe as a public entity — it listed in 2025 and the engine has exactly ONE year of net income and equity in its extract, so the 'sustainable' return it computed is a single data point.
- Left unresolved: The one thing I cannot resolve is the only thing that matters: the size of the retained loss in a landfall year. I did not obtain the dollar attachment point and aggregate retention figures for the 2026-2027 tower beyond the disclosure that retention was reduced from $95M, so I cannot quantify the bear case against a $370M equity base. I also cannot assess reserve adequacy on pre-reform accident years with fifteen months of public reporting. Both are load-bearing and both are unanswered, which is why this is no_edge rather than cheap.

**Key risks.** Single-state Florida homeowners concentration at the statistical peak of hurricane season; The model prices an arithmetic-mean return; catastrophe earnings are not distributed that way; Fifteen months of public reporting and one fiscal year in the engine's extract — no observation through a loss year; Record new business (+54%) written into a softening primary rate environment is how Florida carriers have historically bought trouble; Reserve development on pre-SB-2A accident years; Stock is +59.9% over 63 days and +40.3% over twelve months — much of the reform thesis is priced
**Watch for.** Any named storm making Florida landfall before 30 November 2026, and the retained loss disclosed against it; Q3 2026 combined ratio — the first quarter of the season, and the test of whether 63.4% was weather or expense; Primary rate filings with the Florida OIR: reinsurance cost falling is good for AII only until primary rates follow it down; The 2027 June 1 CAT XOL renewal — a second consecutive risk-adjusted decrease would confirm capital inflow

**Data quality.** One flag, plus one error I found myself. (1) `beta_from_sector_median_0.87(unreliable_r2_0.008_raw_0.21)` — this is the fallback working CORRECTLY. AII listed in 2025 so its own regression has almost no history (R-squared 0.008) and the engine did the right thing by refusing it and substituting the Financials sector median, giving a 9.57% cost of equity. I accept it, with the caveat that the Financials median is built from banks and diversified financials and a single-state catastrophe insurer's true beta is not that. (2) An error the engine does not flag: tangible book per share is STALE. The model uses $17.20, which is the 2025-12-31 equity of $337.0M divided by current shares. The company reported book value per share of $18.86 at 2026-06-30, and the extract's own `equity_now` of $369.5M reproduces that figure exactly. So the book method is pricing today's share price against last year's book while the multiples table on the same name uses the current one (its p_tbv of 1.387 matches $18.86). Actual P/TBV is 1.39, not the 1.52 shown. The effect is to UNDERSTATE the gap by roughly ten percentage points: at my 16% ROTCE the record below will show +11.4% against a stale book, where the same assumption on the correct $18.86 book gives about +22%. I have not adjusted for it, because the schema has no field to do so, but the recorded fair value should be read as ~10% too low. (3) The engine's own 28.9% 'sustainable' ROTCE is a SINGLE observation — the extract holds exactly one year of net income ($97.4M) and one of equity ($337.0M). It is not an average of anything. That, not the beta, is what produced the +134% gap.

## 2026-09-11 — NO_EDGE (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $25.41 · fair value $28.52 · gap +12.2%
- **Sustainable ROTCE:** screen said +28.9%, analyst says +16.0%
- **P/TBV:** justified 1.66 vs actual 1.48 on tangible book of $17.20/share

**The case for the price.** At $25.41 against a corrected tangible book of $18.86, American Integrity trades at 1.35x book while reporting a 40% adjusted return on equity in the second quarter and 31% for the half. The market is therefore implying a sustainable return of roughly 12% — that returns fall by about two-thirds. The bear case for that is coherent: Florida primary rates are flattening while reinsurance costs fall faster, the company's own deck concedes 'reinsurance rates have been declining well in excess of primary rates', average gross written premium per policy already slipped from $2,178 to $2,156 year over year, and the 63.4% combined ratio is the best this business will ever look — a no-catastrophe quarter in a reformed market with capital flooding back in. A single-state Florida homeowners insurer at the statistical peak of hurricane season, up 49.5% in 63 days, priced at only 1.35x book is the market declining to capitalise a peak-cycle return.

**What changed.** I fetched the document the last devil's-advocate pass nominated and could not obtain, and it answers the question that capped that verdict. The 2026-09-08 investor deck (8-K Item 7.01, EX-99.1) discloses the finalised 2026/2027 catastrophe excess-of-loss programme in full: FIRST EVENT RETENTION OF $35.0 MILLION ($10M at the insurance entity, $25M at the captive), unchanged from last year despite 19% exposure growth; second event retention $20 million; third and fourth event retentions $10 million each; and AGGREGATE RETENTION IN A FOUR-EVENT SEASON REDUCED FROM $95 MILLION TO $75 MILLION. The first-event tower including retentions is $2.27 billion, a 1-in-130 year probable maximum loss, total third-party coverage for all occurrences is $3.0 billion (up $409 million, 15.8%), the programme cost $430-440 million, and it was placed at a risk-adjusted rate reduction at the high end of 15-20%. The tower is modelled to contain a repeat of the four-event 2004 Florida storm season. Separately: 1H 2026 gross premiums written $546.6M (+10%), net premiums earned $186.9M (+42%, on the quota share cut from 40% to 25%), pre-tax income $73.7M against $67.0M, policies in force +16% and voluntary +22%, policy retention up from 75.1% to 84.4%, voluntary new business gross written premium $35.1M to $87.7M. And the season itself: as of 11 September 2026 the Atlantic has produced five named storms and ZERO hurricanes — a record for the latest first hurricane — with accumulated cyclone energy of 4.4 against a normal 58.2, and no tropical development expected in the following seven days.

**Base case.** Not applicable — book method. My sustainable ROTCE is 16%, unchanged from the 2026-09-04 pass. The new information bounds the downside rather than raising the mean: knowing that a landfall costs $35 million and the worst modelled season costs $75 million tells me the distribution has a floor, not that the average is higher. Against that, 2026 is on track to be the quietest Atlantic season on record, which is the single best possible year for this business and is exactly the year being annualised into a 40% adjusted ROE. Through a cycle that includes landfall years, reserve development on pre-reform accident years, and primary rates following reinsurance costs down, 16% is a return well above the cost of equity and well below what is being printed today.
