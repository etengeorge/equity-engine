**The case for the price.** Slide is earning an extraordinary return and the market is paying about 5.8x this year's guided earnings for it. Q2 2026 net income was $134.9M (+92.4%), diluted EPS $1.06, the combined ratio improved 980bp to 57.6%, and return on equity was 11.7% IN THE QUARTER - roughly 47% annualised. The company reiterated full-year 2026 net income guidance of $455-470M against a $2.7bn market capitalisation and about $1.12bn of tangible common equity. The market's 2.42x price to tangible book is not scepticism about this year; it is a statement that a 57.6% combined ratio in a single-state Florida homeowners book is a no-hurricane number, that Florida rates are already falling as reformed-market capital returns, and that the tail risk in this equity is a distribution no multiple of a benign year can describe.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-28): the figures above, with guidance reiterated. Expansion outside Florida continued - an enhanced suite of property products launched across South Carolina, announced 2026-09-23. Morgan Stanley downgraded to Equalweight on valuation in July with a $20 target. The stock made a fresh 52-week high around 2026-09-15 and then fell 13.3% over the following five sessions on 1.7x average volume. I could NOT identify a specific driver for that fall: there is no company filing in the window, no 8-K, no named-storm Florida landfall I could find, and the only news item in the store for those five days is a Zacks value-screen listing. The move coincides with a broad financials pullback on rising Treasury yields visible in the sector feed, and follows a 31.8% 63-day run. I am recording that as unexplained rather than inventing a mechanism.

**Base case.** I supply a through-cycle sustainable ROTCE of 22%, against the engine's 40.0%. The engine's number is close to what Slide is actually earning right now - that is the surprise of this name - but it is the return of a Florida homeowners insurer in a year with no major landfall, a hard reinsurance market that is now softening, and post-SB-2A litigation reform still fully in the numbers. A 57.6% combined ratio implies a loss ratio near 30%, which no coastal property book sustains across a cat cycle. Blending the current earning power against (a) a cat load - one materially loss-making year in roughly eight - and (b) competitive normalisation of the combined ratio toward the mid-80s as capital returns to a market earning these returns, 22% is where I land. At 22% the justified multiple is 2.52x against an actual 2.42x, so the entire +97.8% gap disappears and the name is approximately fair.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: I went into this name expecting the AMSF pattern - an insurer's headline ROTCE inflated by prior-year reserve releases, with the stability of the series disguising it - and the filing killed that argument outright. Note 6 of the 2026-06-30 10-Q reads: 'During the three and six months ended June 30, 2026, the Company did not experience any prior year development. During the three and six months ended June 30, 2025, the Company recognized $14,363 and $21,620 of favorable loss development.' So the current 57.6% combined ratio contains ZERO releases while the comparison it beat by 980bp contained $14.4M of them. The AMSF detector - prior-year development as a share of the reported combined ratio - fires completely clean, in Slide's favour. This is current accident-year earning power, not a release cycle, and the 92% net income growth is understated by the comparison rather than flattered by it.
- What would prove it: Continued zero or adverse development alongside a combined ratio holding in the 60s through a 2027 season, plus Florida rate filings that do not fall.
- Already visible today: The clean-development evidence is already visible and it is the single strongest fact on this name. What is NOT visible is any test of the cat assumption: the 2026 season has been benign to date, so the reported return has never been stressed, and Slide has only traded publicly since June 2025.
- Left unresolved: The cost of equity, and it is the whole answer. The engine assigns 9.95% from the Financials SECTOR MEDIAN beta of 0.88, because Slide's own regression has an R-squared of 0.006 (raw beta 0.21). A single-state Florida catastrophe writer does not have a 9.95% cost of equity - its risk is a fat tail that a weekly-return beta cannot see. At a defensible 14% the SAME 22% ROTCE gives a justified 1.67x against an actual 2.42x, i.e. about 31% RICH rather than fair. The schema has no cost-of-equity override, so the recorded fair value of roughly $24 should be read as the answer to a question with the wrong discount rate in it. That is why this is no_edge and not fair, and certainly not cheap. I also could not explain the 13.3% five-day fall that won the slot.
