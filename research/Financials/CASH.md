## 2026-09-21 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $76.17 · fair value $76.43 · gap +0.3%
- **Sustainable ROTCE:** screen said +38.8%, analyst says +26.0%
- **P/TBV:** justified 2.90 vs actual 2.89 on tangible book of $26.36/share

**The case for the price.** Pathward is not a normal bank and the market knows it: it funds itself with essentially free deposits sourced from prepaid-card, tax-refund and BaaS partners, keeps its Durbin exemption, and earns a return on tangible equity no deposit-gathering community bank can approach — 34.8% for the nine months of fiscal 2025 and still 30.7% for the nine months of fiscal 2026. Paying 2.95x tangible book for a franchise that compounds tangible book per share from $22.09 to $25.83 in four quarters is not obviously wrong. What the buyer is also pricing, correctly, is that the commercial-finance book attached to that franchise has just broken: the stock is down 8.9% in 21 days and ~11.7% over six months, which is the market marking down the return rather than disputing that the deposit franchise is good.

**What changed.** The credit event, and it is large, recent and unresolved. Nonperforming loans and leases went from $71.3M (1.49% of gross loans) at 2025-06-30 to $99.1M (2.05%) at 2025-09-30 to $117.7M (2.39%) at 2026-03-31 to $275.1 MILLION (5.28%) at 2026-06-30 — more than doubling in a single quarter. The company attributes the increase to 'certain renewable energy construction projects with a common developer' and says it 'continues to work with other parties in these projects to bring them to completion', i.e. it is committing further capital, not exiting. Separately a working-capital loan was identified as 'sophisticated fraud within an otherwise viable operating company'. Provision for credit losses was $28.3M in the June quarter against $9.3M a year earlier, commercial finance accounting for $22.6M of the increase. Fiscal Q3 net income fell to $29.0M / $1.37 diluted from $42.1M / $1.81. Return on average tangible equity for the quarter was 21.61% against 26.72% a year earlier. An 8-K on 2026-09-17 reports an item 5.02 officer change. Plaintiff-firm 'securities fraud investigation' headlines from 2026-07-26 are boilerplate and I give them no weight.

**Base case.** The +53.9% gap is the CBT shape on a financial: the engine's 38.8% 'sustainable ROTCE' is the mean of GAAP returns for fiscal 2023, 2024 and 2025 — a window that ENDS at 2025-09-30, entirely before the credit event — and `fy_end` is 356 days old, so nothing flags it. The company publishes the current figures itself: return on average tangible equity of 30.65% for the nine months of fiscal 2026 and 21.61% for the June quarter. Holding the engine's 10.28% cost of equity and 2% terminal growth and pricing against the company's own reported tangible book of $25.83 (the engine uses $26.36, the fiscal 2025 year-end figure — the AII stale-book bug again, though only 2% here), the justified multiple and the gap run: 38.8% -> 4.45x, +50.8%; 30.65% -> 3.46x, +17.4%; 28% -> 3.14x, +6.5%; 26% -> 2.90x, -1.7%; 21.61% -> 2.37x, -19.7%. Seventy points of gap from one input, which is the JXN test verbatim. My own number is 26%: the nine-month 30.65% still contains the March tax-season quarter at 54.4% ROATE and books only the first instalment of the solar provisioning, while the 21.61% June quarter is the first with credit cost in it and the September quarter is seasonally the weakest. A through-cycle 26% on a franchise earning 2.5x its cost of equity is defensible — and at 26% the name is fairly priced, not 54% cheap.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest counter is that I am marking down the wrong thing. Pathward's earning power is a deposit franchise, not a loan book: it takes essentially zero-cost deposits from prepaid and tax partners and keeps a Durbin exemption most banks its size do not have. The commercial-finance portfolio is the part it bolted on, and it is a minority of assets. Even in the worst quarter it has ever printed on credit, the business earned 21.61% on tangible equity — more than twice its 10.28% cost of equity — and tangible book per share still ROSE, $25.41 to $25.83. On that reading the correct ROTCE is high-20s, the gap is a real +6% to +17%, and I am confusing a bad quarter with a broken franchise.
- What would prove it: Resolution of the renewable-energy nonperformers: the fiscal Q4 and Q1 filings will show whether the $275.1M migrates to charge-off or back to accrual, and what further capital Pathward put into 'bringing the projects to completion'. Net charge-off rate on the commercial finance book is the single number.
- Already visible today: Some of it, and it genuinely weakens my case: tangible book per share rose through the credit event ($22.09 -> $24.02 -> $24.54 -> $25.41 -> $25.83 across five quarters), which is not what a bank absorbing a real loss looks like, and the tax-services provision actually FELL $5.7M on better underwriting. Against that, the NPL ratio more than doubled in one quarter, specific reserves were taken on two loans, and the phrase 'continues to work with other parties ... to bring them to completion' means the exposure is still growing.
- Left unresolved: The loss content of the solar book. The 10-Q gives the nonperforming balance and says specific reserves 'reflect expected proceeds' but does not quantify expected recovery, so I cannot distinguish a timing problem from a capital loss. That distinction is the whole 70-point range and I could not close it from the filings.

**Key risks.** the engine's 38.8% ROTCE is measured over a window ending 2025-09-30, entirely before a credit event that took nonperformers from 2.05% to 5.28% of gross loans; loss content of the renewable-energy construction exposure is undisclosed and the company is still funding those projects; the fiscal Q4 (September) quarter is seasonally the weakest and will carry the credit cost without the tax-season offset; `rotce_39%_suggests_asset_light_financial` is the engine flagging its own model: Pathward's earning power is a deposit franchise, not tangible capital, so P/TBV is a strained frame even when the arithmetic works
**Watch for.** net charge-offs on the commercial finance portfolio in fiscal Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, and whether the $275.1M resolves or crystallises; fiscal 2027 guidance — management has pointed to a stronger year and that is the bull case stated; the item 5.02 officer change of 2026-09-17, four days before a further leg down

**Data quality.** Two flags raised, both substantive. `goodwill_and_intangibles_36%_of_book` is real ($308M against $851M of equity) and correctly handled — tangible book reconciles to the company's reported $25.83 within 2%. `rotce_39%_suggests_asset_light_financial_p_tbv_may_be_the_wrong_model_here` is the PIPR category-error flag; at 2.95x actual P/TBV it is below the ~4x threshold at which the model breaks down, so I priced it rather than refusing, but the flag is pointing at something real. NOT flagged and decisive: the ROTCE window (fiscal 2023-25) closes 356 days before the screen date and predates the entire credit event — the CBT defect on a book-method name. Minor and confirmed: `equity_series[0]` of $858.0M is the fiscal 2025 year-end figure while `equity_now` of $850.9M matches the filed $851,146k, so tangible book is one period stale (AII), worth about 2%. Beta is sound (0.96, R-squared 0.354, own regression) — unusually, the discount rate is not the problem here.
