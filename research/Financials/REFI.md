## 2026-09-21 — NO_MODEL (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $10.98

**The case for the price.** There is no standalone market opinion to steelman, because REFI has agreed to stop existing. The 8-K of 2026-07-13 (items 1.01, 3.02, 7.01, 9.01, filed as a Rule 425 merger communication) records a merger agreement under which 'the Company will merge with and into LIEN, with LIEN continuing as the surviving company' — Chicago Atlantic BDC, the affiliated business development company under the same manager — on an adjusted-NAV-for-NAV basis at an exchange ratio of roughly 1.085 LIEN shares per REFI share, struck just prior to closing, expected in the fourth quarter of 2026. That is why REFI trades at 0.91x its own tangible book: a NAV-for-NAV exchange hands holders shares in an entity that itself trades at a discount to NAV, so REFI's price converges on LIEN's discount, not on REFI's book. The price is an arbitrage relationship, not a valuation.

**What changed.** The merger, which the engine cannot see. Three of the last five 8-Ks are Rule 425 merger communications — the filenames say so outright (`ea0295037-8k425_chicago.htm` 2026-06-18, `ea0297476-8k425_chicago.htm` 2026-07-13, `ea0301176-8k425_chicago.htm` 2026-08-11) — carrying items 1.01 and 3.02. Q2 2026 (2026-08-11) reported distributable earnings of $0.44 per basic share against a $0.47 quarterly dividend, below consensus, which the company attributed to early loan repayments delaying redeployment; the Q2 call was described on the wire as 'a transformative quarter with a $649 million pipeline and a value-unlocking merger'. A $0.47 Q3 dividend was declared 2026-09-16. The same 8-K also records a private placement of second-lien promissory notes issued by Koach, secured by mortgages on 32 retail properties leased to cannabis operators and subordinate to senior first-lien debt.

**Base case.** No ROTCE is supplied and no fair value is recorded, for two independent reasons. First, the name is under a signed all-stock merger and a standalone justified-P/TBV is the wrong question — this is the fourth pending-merger blindness on this screen in thirteen sessions after BKH, LCII and PATK, and the cheapest detector yet: the SEC filename contains '425'. Second, even as a standalone the +95.8% gap is a discount-rate artifact. The justified multiple of 1.79 comes from a 12.6% ROTCE against a 7.95% cost of equity derived from a beta of 0.54. A senior secured lender to US cannabis operators — an industry with no federal banking access, where REFI's own collateral is described as second-lien notes subordinate to senior first-lien debt on cannabis-leased retail property — does not have a 7.95% cost of equity. The market's own number is visible on the page: a $0.47 quarterly dividend on a $10.98 share is a 17.1% yield, and a REIT distributing substantially all of its taxable income is priced almost entirely off its required return. Put 17% into the same Gordon form and the justified multiple is (0.126-0.02)/(0.17-0.02) = 0.71 against an actual 0.91 — the name is RICH, not 96% cheap, and the sign of the answer is set by an input the engine has no ability to measure on a name with this credit profile.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That a NAV-for-NAV merger is precisely the event that should make a sub-NAV price converge UP, so refusing here throws away the one case where the deal helps. If REFI holders receive 1.085 LIEN shares struck on adjusted NAV, and REFI trades at 0.91x NAV today, the exchange itself realises book — that is a 10% gross return plus the dividend, and I am calling it no_model.
- What would prove it: LIEN's own price-to-NAV at and after closing. The exchange is NAV-for-NAV between the two companies, so what REFI holders actually receive is worth REFI's NAV multiplied by LIEN's market discount — the arbitrage only works if LIEN trades at or above NAV.
- Already visible today: Not from anything in this brief or the filings fetched. I could not establish LIEN's price-to-NAV, which is the entire counter, and that is a fact about my confidence rather than an answer. The structure is a strong hint though: an internally-advised REIT merging into an affiliated BDC under a common manager, NAV-for-NAV, is not usually a transaction that creates value for the sub-NAV side.
- Left unresolved: LIEN's price/NAV, and therefore whether the exchange realises book or merely transfers the discount. Also unresolved: the Koach second-lien note placement, which adds subordinated cannabis real-estate credit to a book I could not otherwise stress.
