## 2026-09-11 — NO_MODEL (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $3.63

**The case for the price.** At $3.63 against a GAAP book value of $6.90 per share, Redwood Trust trades at 0.53x book — and the market has just been told why. On 10 September the company announced, and on 11 September priced, an UPSIZED $185.0 million offering of 7.00% convertible senior notes due 2030, conversion price approximately $4.90, a 35% premium to the 10 September close, with a $20 million greenshoe. A company trading at half of book that raises five-year money at a 7% coupon with equity attached 29% BELOW its own book value is telling you what its marginal cost of capital is, and it is far above what its legacy portfolio earns. That is the steelman for 0.53x: the mortgage banking platforms genuinely earn above 20% returns and production exceeded $8 billion for a second consecutive quarter, but the consolidated entity posted a GAAP net LOSS of $3 million in the second quarter and book value per share fell from $7.12 to $6.90. The market is capitalising the legacy investment portfolio at a discount and the origination platform at something, and the sum is half of stated book.

**What changed.** Three dated events in the two sessions before the screen, which together explain a 19.2% five-day fall on 2.4x normal volume. (1) 2026-09-10: announced an offering of convertible senior notes due 2030. (2) 2026-09-11: priced it, UPSIZED from $150.0 million to $185.0 million, at a 7.00% coupon, initial conversion rate 204.0608 shares per $1,000 (approximately $4.90 per share, a 35% premium to the 10 September close), maturing 15 September 2030, expected to close 15 September 2026, with an option for $20 million more. (3) 2026-09-10: declared third-quarter common and preferred dividends. Earlier: Q2 2026 reported 2026-07-28 — a GAAP net loss of $3 million or $0.03 per share, mortgage banking production above $8 billion for a second consecutive quarter with platform returns above 20%, revenue missing estimates by 26.2%, and GAAP book value per common share of $6.90 at 30 June 2026 against $7.12 at 31 March 2026.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest counter to refusing is that price to book is exactly the right frame for a balance-sheet lender — unlike PIPR, whose capital is a roster of bankers, Redwood's capital genuinely IS its balance sheet — so refusing to price it is a failure of nerve rather than of method. A company at 0.53x tangible book with an operating platform earning above 20% on $8 billion of quarterly production is, on its face, the sort of discount worth understanding. And the engine's 'richest 2% of 338 Financials' reading is so obviously wrong in DIRECTION that dismissing the whole model throws away the useful signal that the stock is cheap on book.
- What would prove it: Two consecutive quarters of GAAP profitability with book value per share stable or rising, and a disclosed split of return on equity between the mortgage banking segments and the legacy investment portfolio. If the platforms are earning 20% on a growing share of capital while the legacy book stops shrinking book value, the discount is an opportunity.
- Already visible today: The opposite is visible, and it is why I refuse rather than call it cheap. Book value per share fell 3% in a single quarter. The engine's own three-year net-income-to-common series is [-$77.0M, +$47.0M, -$9.0M] — two loss years in three, which is what produces the -1.7% average ROTCE and the range of -8.8% to +4.4% the brief flags. And the capital raise settles the question of how management sees its own cost of capital: a company that believed its shares were worth materially more than $3.63 does not sell five-year paper at 7% convertible at $4.90 when stated book is $6.90. That is an issuer accepting expensive money, which is evidence about the earning power of the assets, not about market mispricing.
- Left unresolved: I could not obtain the segment return on equity split between mortgage banking and the legacy investment portfolio, nor the composition and marks of the legacy book, so I cannot say what share of the 0.53x discount is a real impairment of the investment portfolio and what share is the market refusing to capitalise a genuinely profitable origination franchise.
