## 2026-09-14 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $35.62 · fair value $55.49 · gap +55.8%
- **Sustainable ROTCE:** screen said +13.0%, analyst says +11.0%

**The case for the price.** At $35.62 Radian trades at 0.99x its own reported book value per share of $36.00, and the market's position is that this is the right price for a mortgage insurer at this point in the cycle. Private mortgage insurance is a business that earns a high return for years and then gives a large part of it back in one credit event; the equity has traded near one times book for most of the post-2008 era precisely because that tail is real and unhedgeable. Radian is also, right now, at the good end of the cycle — the default rate declined again this quarter, credit trends are favourable, and the in-force book is full of low-rate mortgages with high persistency because nobody refinances a 3% loan at 7%. Paying book value for peak-of-cycle earnings from a levered credit insurer is a coherent thing to do, not an error.

**What changed.** THE COMPANY IS NOT WHAT THE MODEL IS PRICING. Radian consummated the acquisition of Inigo — a Lloyd's of London specialty insurer — on 2026-02-02, and the Q2 statements include only five months of it. The Specialty segment wrote $504M of gross premiums in Q2 against $162M, and it runs at a 97.7% combined ratio against Mortgage's 35.8%. Q2 2026 net income from continuing operations was $118M ($0.87/diluted share) against $154M ($1.11) a year earlier, down 23%. Return on equity from continuing operations ran 13.6% -> 10.8% -> 9.8% across Q2'25, Q1'26 and Q2'26; adjusted net operating ROE 13.5% -> 14.7% -> 12.9%. The Mortgage combined ratio deteriorated from 30.4% to 35.8%. Book value per share $36.00, +8.5% year over year.

**Base case.** My sustainable ROTCE is 11%, not the engine's 13.0%. The 13.0% is the mean of three ANNUAL ratios — 12.19%, 13.07%, 13.71% — every one of which belongs to a pure-play mortgage insurer that ceased to exist on 2026-02-02, and all three sit above the current quarterly run rate of 9.8% GAAP. The series is monotonically declining, which by the VLY rule inverted means the average is describing a company that no longer exists. Two mechanisms drive it down and both are structural rather than cyclical: the Inigo book is roughly a quarter of premium and earns essentially only investment income on float at a 97.7% combined ratio, and the 10-Q states that the rise in the provision for losses is driven by 'an increase in current period new defaults AND A REDUCTION IN FAVORABLE DEVELOPMENT on prior period defaults' — the reserve-release tailwind that flatters every year in the engine's window is explicitly shrinking. I land at 11% as the blend of a still-excellent mortgage book and a near-breakeven specialty book, above the 9.8% GAAP print because that print carries acquisition noise and VOBA amortisation, below the 12.9% adjusted figure because adjusted strips real costs.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That this is the cleanest set of inputs on the whole screen and I am explaining away a real signal. I checked every defect LESSONS.md predicts for a book-method financial and NONE of them is present: net_income_common_series equals net_income_series exactly, so there is no AX duration bug; equity_now ($4,808M) differs from equity_series[0] ($4,782M) by 0.56%, so there is no AII/BUR stale-tangible-book bug; goodwill, intangibles, preferred and minority interest are all zero, so there is no JXN decomposition question; the engine's tangible book of $36.14 reconciles to the company's own reported $36.00. The ROTCE series is stable, not volatile. If the inputs are clean and the name sits at the 96th percentile of 338 Financials, the burden is on me to explain why it is not cheap.
- What would prove it: Whether the gap survives defensible values for the two inputs the engine did not verify: the sustainable ROTCE, and the cost of equity.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it wins. The grid is the answer. At the engine's 13.0% ROTCE and 7.9% cost of equity the justified multiple is 1.86 and the gap is +89%. At my 11% ROTCE and the same 7.9% it is +55%. At the current 9.8% GAAP run rate and a cost of equity of 10.5% — which is what a beta of about 1.0 gives, and a mortgage insurer is not a 0.52-beta security — the justified multiple is 0.92 and the gap is MINUS 7%. One pair of defensible input choices moves the answer from +89% to -7%. That is the JXN test verbatim, and by the standard already written in LESSONS.md the output is not a valuation.
- Left unresolved: The cost of equity, which is the input doing most of the work and which the analyst schema has no field to override. The engine's 7.9% comes from a beta of 0.52 at R-squared 0.193 — a better fit than most names today but still in the bucket LESSONS.md measures as biased toward cheapness. I also could not size the dollar amount of favourable prior-year development in the Mortgage segment; the 10-Q describes the direction ('a reduction in favorable development') without quantifying it in the MD&A text I read, so I could not run the AMSF accident-year test properly on the mortgage book. I ran it on Specialty, where $24M of the quarter's $193M of current-period losses was offset by favourable prior-period development.

**Key risks.** Mortgage credit is a tail risk, not a normal distribution — 7% mortgage rates and falling affordability are the setup for the loss cycle, not for the release cycle; Reserve releases are explicitly shrinking and have supported every year in the engine's window; Inigo is a Lloyd's specialty book at a 97.7% combined ratio diluting a 35.8%-combined mortgage franchise, and it is only five months old in the accounts
**Watch for.** New notices of default and the Mortgage combined ratio in Q3 — the combined ratio has already moved 30.4% to 35.8%; Dollar quantification of favourable prior-year development in the Mortgage segment; Whether Specialty's combined ratio improves below 95% as Inigo integrates, or stays at breakeven
