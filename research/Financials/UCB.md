## 2026-09-23 — NO_EDGE (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $34.59 · fair value $30.56 · gap -11.7%
- **Sustainable ROTCE:** screen said +9.9%, analyst says +14.0%
- **P/TBV:** justified 1.38 vs actual 1.56 on tangible book of $22.15/share

**The case for the price.** At $34.59 United Community trades at about 1.62x the tangible book of $21.36 a share that the company's own 8 September deck projects for the end of Q3 2026, for a Southeastern relationship bank whose updated baseline operating earnings power is $0.78 a quarter — roughly a 14-15% return on that tangible equity. The market is paying a premium multiple for a franchise that has just deliberately simplified itself: it sold Navitas equipment finance for $2.0 billion in cash at a 7% premium to par, took a ~$300 million pre-tax loss to sell $2.6 billion of 2.20%-yield securities and redeploy at ~4.5%, closed Peach State, and is buying back stock. That is a bank trading at a fair-to-full multiple of a return it has just paid real tangible book to improve, and the price reflects the improvement rather than discounting it.

**What changed.** Almost everything, and all of it after the last 10-K. (1) Navitas Credit Corp and NLFC Reinsurance were sold to funds managed by Wafra and CLOSED 2026-09-01 for ~$2.0 billion cash, a 7% premium to par on the loan portfolio; Navitas was ~10% of loans and ~50% of the bank's net charge-offs. (2) A balance sheet repositioning COMPLETED 2026-09-02: $2.2 billion of HTM securities reclassified to AFS and $2.6 billion of long-dated securities yielding 2.20% sold, for an estimated ~$300 million pre-tax loss net of a ~$64 million gain on Navitas. United expects a NET LOSS in Q3 2026 while remaining profitable for the nine months. (3) Peach State Bancshares closed 2026-08-01, guided at +$0.12 to 2027 EPS. (4) $1.0 billion of ~3.80% borrowings repaid; $50 million of buybacks executed in Q3 to date, $87 million year to date (2% of shares), with the authorisation raised $100 million. (5) The company's own EPS and TBV walkforwards: baseline operating EPS $0.74 to $0.78 a quarter; 2Q26 TBVPS $23.31 to a 3Q26 proforma $21.36, i.e. a 8.4% hit to tangible book, of which $2.39 is the repositioning. Guidance: 3Q26 operating ROAA 1.25-1.30%, CET1 >13%, TCE/TA >9.0%.

**Base case.** A book-method name, so the input is sustainable return on tangible common equity, and I set it at 14.0%. Two independent routes agree. First, the company's own walkforward: an updated baseline operating EPS of $0.78 a quarter is about $3.12 a year, and against the proforma tangible book of $21.36 on roughly 118 million shares after buybacks that is a 14.6% return. Second, and independently, guided 3Q26 operating ROAA of 1.25-1.30% at guided TCE/TA above 9.0% implies roughly 14%. I take the lower end of the two because the $0.78 is management's illustrative full-quarter figure and is explicitly stated to assume a flat rate environment — an assumption the Federal Reserve's September hike has already violated. This is nowhere near the engine's 9.9%, which is a three-year GAAP average spanning the years in which United absorbed acquisitions, merger charges and a securities repositioning, and which describes a bank that no longer exists.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I have accepted management's own non-GAAP walkforward as the sustainable return, which is exactly the input a management team has the most incentive and the most latitude to flatter. 'Operating' excludes merger charges on a bank that has made small acquisitions repeatedly and will again, so the exclusion is of a recurring cost. The $0.78 baseline is illustrative, assumes a flat rate environment that has already broken, and rests on a $2.6 billion reinvestment spread not yet earned. Meanwhile the company has just destroyed $2.39 a share — 10% — of tangible book to buy it, and sold Navitas, which on its own disclosure generated $166 million of FY2025 gross revenue against $32 million of non-interest expense. On that reading the sustainable return is nearer 12% and the stock is 28% rich, not 11%.
- What would prove it: The Q3 and Q4 2026 operating ROTCE prints against the $0.78 baseline, and whether the reinvestment spread actually shows up in net interest margin rather than in a walkforward slide.
- Already visible today: Only partly, and the part that is visible cuts FOR the counter on book and AGAINST it on earnings. The tangible book destruction is not a forecast — the repositioning completed 2026-09-02 and the company has quantified it at $2.39 a share, so the denominator damage is banked and is already in my 1.62x. The earnings improvement is not banked. What supports the 14% is that it is corroborated by a second, differently-constructed disclosure (ROAA 1.25-1.30% over TCE/TA >9.0%), and that United's Q2 2026 net income available to common of $114.9 million against $76.7 million a year earlier is a real GAAP number, not a projection. I could not settle whether the reinvestment spread lands, so I took the bottom of my range rather than the top.
- Left unresolved: The Q3 2026 print itself, which will be a GAAP net loss and which I cannot see. Also unresolved: what the September Fed hike does to the AOCI on the securities that were NOT sold — the deck's TBV walkforward explicitly 'excludes impact of 3Q26 rate movements on unrealized losses', so the $21.36 proforma is a floor-less estimate in a rising-rate quarter.

**Key risks.** The $300M pre-tax repositioning loss is banked tangible-book destruction; the offsetting earnings improvement is a projection that assumes a flat rate environment the September Fed hike has already broken; 3Q26 will be a GAAP net loss, and the deck's $21.36 proforma TBVPS explicitly excludes the AOCI effect of Q3 rate moves on the securities NOT sold; Goodwill and intangibles are 27% of reported book and United keeps adding to them through small in-market acquisitions; Navitas was a high-yielding business as well as a high-charge-off one; replacing $2.0B of equipment-finance yield with 4.5% short securities and 'high single digit' promised loan growth is an execution bet on 42 newly hired lenders
**Watch for.** Q3 and Q4 2026 operating ROTCE against the company's own $0.78 quarterly baseline — the single number this verdict turns on; Actual 3Q26 tangible book per share against the $21.36 proforma, which will show what the September rate move did to the retained securities book; Net interest margin in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027: whether the reinvestment spread appears in the margin or only in the walkforward
