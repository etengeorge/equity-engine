- **Verdict:** no_model · price $11.43

**The case for the price.** At $11.43 nobody is valuing Intermex's cash flows. The stock is a probability on a contract. On 2025-08-10 the company signed an Agreement and Plan of Merger with The Western Union Company under which every share converts into the right to receive $16.00 IN CASH. The 2026-06-30 10-Q states the outside date is 2026-11-10 (automatically extended for certain regulatory approvals), that Western Union owes a $27.3M termination fee only where the failure relates to ANTITRUST law, and that the remaining closing conditions include consents with respect to 'the Company's or its subsidiaries' money transmitter licenses'. On 2026-08-13 the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation SUSPENDED the approval extension it had granted on 2026-07-31, 'based on a need to further review the transaction as a result of the intervening six months since approval was originally granted'. At $11.43 against $16.00 the market is paying roughly a coin flip: solving 11.43 = p x 16 + (1-p) x B gives p of about 52% at a $6.50 break price and 43% at $8.00. Given a seven-week outside date and a licensing regulator that has already reversed itself once, that is a reasonable price.

**What changed.** The deal broke down, and the business deteriorated underneath it. (i) NYDFS approved in August with commitments to maintain New York retail locations and limit certain price changes for three years, plus an Assurance of Discontinuance with the New York Attorney General. (ii) California DFPI suspended its approval extension on 2026-08-13; the parties said they would 'engage promptly' and close after reinstatement, and as of 2026-09-23 no reinstatement has been announced - six weeks of silence against a 2026-11-10 outside date. (iii) The stock fell 17.1% in five days and 19.1% in twenty-one. (iv) Underneath, the standalone business is contracting hard: Q2 2026 total revenues $131.2M against $161.1M (-18.6%), H1 $253.1M against $305.4M (-17.1%), Q2 operating income $8.8M against $19.5M (-55%), H1 net income $4.7M against $18.8M (-75%). Buybacks are suspended for the duration of the merger agreement.

**Base case.** No cash-flow base case is appropriate. This is a deal stock with a seven-week binary, and the engine's model is a category error on top of that.

**Devil's advocate.**

- Already visible today: The absence is what is visible: six weeks since the suspension and no announced reinstatement, with the outside date seven weeks away. I also cannot pin the break price precisely - my $5-8 range is annualised Q2 earnings at 8-12x, on a revenue line falling 18% a year with an immigration-policy-driven decline in the sending population and digital competitors taking share, and it could be materially lower.

**Key risks.** Binary outcome with a 2026-11-10 outside date and one unresolved state licensing regulator; The Western Union break fee of $27.3M is conditioned on ANTITRUST failure; a money-transmitter licensing failure appears not to trigger it; Standalone business declining 18% year over year in revenue and 75% in half-year net income; Buybacks suspended and capital allocation frozen by merger-agreement covenants for the duration

## 2026-11-13 — NO_MODEL (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $6.39

**The case for the price.** At $6.39 against a $16.00 cash deal price, the market has written the Western Union merger off almost entirely and is pricing Intermex as a standalone, shrinking remittance processor. That is defensible: Q3 revenue fell 18.4% to $126.4M and nine-month revenue 17.6% to $379.5M, nine-month operating income fell 51% to $21.5M and net income 62% to $9.0M, and the decline is structural - immigration enforcement is shrinking the sending population in the Latin America corridors while Remitly and other digital competitors take share from an agent network. On 30.2M shares the equity is $193M against $211.2M of cash and $171.4M of drawn revolver, so the market is paying roughly 4-5x EBITDA for a business losing a fifth of its revenue a year, with buybacks frozen since June 2025 by merger covenants. The litigation claim against Western Union is being valued at close to nothing, which is what the market usually does with contested contract claims.

**What changed.** Everything the 2026-09-24 pass nominated as its test arrived, and in an order nobody would have forecast. On the evening of 2026-11-10 - the outside date - the California DFPI, the sole remaining regulatory approval, APPROVED the merger subject to conditions. Shortly after receiving that approval Western Union delivered a notice purporting to terminate the merger agreement on the outside-date right. Intermex responded the same night, refused the termination, stated that all conditions had been satisfied or that their failure was caused by Western Union's breach of its reasonable-best-efforts obligations, demanded that Western Union rescind and close, and reserved the right to seek specific performance and damages for wrongful termination. On 2026-11-11 Western Union notified the DFPI of its purported termination and WITHDREW its application. The 8-K carrying this is dated 2026-11-12 under item 1.02. Separately, the Q3 10-Q of 2026-11-09 shows the standalone decline above, and cash flow flattered by a $48.0M release of prepaid wires (from $59.5M to $11.4M) - nine-month operating cash flow of $83.5M against $52.5M is mostly that working-capital liquidation, not earnings.

**Base case.** No cash-flow base case is appropriate and no book-value base case is admissible. The engine prices this as a financial on justified price-to-tangible-book, and that is a category error which the engine's own flag identifies correctly: Intermex is an agent-network remittance processor whose productive asset is a distribution network and whose $2.70 of tangible book per share is a working-capital balance, not regulatory capital constraining how much business it can write. Supplying a sustainable ROTCE would launder that category error into a fair value. Beyond the method, the price now contains a contested legal claim - specific performance or damages against Western Union for terminating minutes after the last regulator approved - whose expected value turns on a Delaware contract question about reasonable-best-efforts obligations that I cannot adjudicate and will not guess at.
