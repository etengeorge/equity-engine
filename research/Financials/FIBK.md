## 2026-10-23 — RICH (conviction: medium)

**The case for the price.** At $36.88 the market pays 1.74x the tangible book First Interstate itself reported at 2026-09-30 ($21.20/share) for a bank earning an 11.75% return on average tangible common equity, with a 14.36% CET1 ratio, a 5.0% dividend yield and a net interest margin that has expanded 19bp year over year to 3.53%. The buyer is underwriting a deliberate shrink-to-quality: management has sold the Arizona and Kansas branches (Q4 2025) and eleven Nebraska branches (Q2 2026), is running the loan book down, and has repurchased 10.02 million shares for $346.6 million since August 2025. On that reading 1.74x is the price of a clean, over-capitalised, mid-cap franchise whose returns are about to step up as the optimisation finishes, and the CEO says exactly that: 'confident in our ability to deliver improving returns over time.'

**What changed.** Q3 2026 was reported on 2026-10-22, the day before this screen priced the stock, and it resets both inputs. Net income $62.2 million ($0.65 diluted) against $83.9 million ($0.87) in Q2 2026 and $71.4 million ($0.69) in Q3 2025 - and the release states that Q2 'includes a $19.5 million gain related to the sale of the eleven Nebraska branches', so the Q2 strength was a divestiture gain. Reported ROATCE by quarter is 11.75% (Q3 2026), 15.43%, 10.77%, 18.79% (Q4 2025), 12.53%. Tangible book value per common share FELL to $21.20 from $22.47 at Q2. Credit is normalising off an unusually clean base: provision of $10.5 million against a $3.2 million RELEASE in Q2 and ZERO in Q3 2025, with net charge-offs of $9.9 million (0.28% annualised) against $2.3 million (0.06%) a year earlier - a 4.3x increase - while non-performing assets fell 16.5% and criticized loans fell from $1,164.1 million to $939.5 million. Deposits are down 5.4% year over year. Six separate item 5.02 officer-change 8-Ks since May 2026.

**Base case.** I supply a sustainable ROTCE of 12.0%, against the engine's 3.5%. The engine's figure is the AX numerator defect, confirmed directly in the extract: net_income_common_series reads [$84.401M, $86.136M, $54.924M] against net_income_series of [$302.1M, $226.0M, $257.5M] with preferred and minority interest both exactly zero, so income available to common MUST equal net income - and $84.4 million is simply the Q2 2026 quarter, accepted as an annual figure because edgar._annual never checks that a duration spans twelve months. Recomputed on net income the three-year mean ROTCE is 12.1%. The company's own quarterly prints corroborate that: the two highest (18.79% in Q4 2025, 15.43% in Q2 2026) are the quarters containing branch-sale gains, and stripping the disclosed $19.5 million from Q2 gives roughly 12.7%. The clean quarters are 11.75% (Q3 2026) and 10.77% (Q1 2026). 12.0% is the midpoint of the gain-free quarters and it is also where the corrected three-year average lands, which is the only reason I am willing to call it sustainable rather than a single print. The cost of equity is the one input I do not have to argue about: beta 1.033 at R-squared 0.534 from our own regression, the best fit in today's ten, so unlike most names in this cohort the 10.63% is a measurement and not an artefact.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I am pricing a 12% ROTCE against a tangible book that management is deliberately converting into shareholder cash, and that the conversion is worth more than the multiple says. First Interstate has 14.36% CET1 against a ~7% requirement, so a large part of tangible book is surplus capital earning nothing; the return on the capital actually needed to run the bank is materially above 12%, and a justified-P/TBV model built on total tangible equity therefore understates the franchise. On that view the right comparison is the 5.0% dividend yield plus a buyback retiring ~7% of the shares a year against an 11.75% ROTCE - a high-single-digit total return from a bank with falling non-performers and an expanding margin, which is not obviously a sell at 1.74x.
- What would prove it: Whether the capital return is accretive or destructive to tangible book per share. If the surplus-capital argument is right, buying stock back should still compound tangible book per share; if the stock is above intrinsic value, the buyback shrinks it.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it goes against the counter. Tangible book per share FELL from $22.47 to $21.20 in the third quarter, a 5.7% decline in one quarter. The arithmetic is visible: the company repurchased 2.04 million shares for $76.3 million, an average of $37.40, against a tangible book of $22.47 - so roughly $30 million of the $145 million decline in tangible common equity is the buyback paying 1.67x book for its own stock, and the dividend ($0.47 a share, roughly $44 million) exceeded retained earnings, with the remainder in AOCI as yields rose. A bank returning about $120 million in a quarter in which it earned $62.2 million, while AOCI erodes the rest, is shrinking tangible book per share rather than compounding it. That makes the standing fixed-2% terminal growth in the Gordon form GENEROUS here rather than punitive - the known bias (LESSONS, 2026-09-09) marks fast-compounding financials expensive and shrinking ones fair, and First Interstate is shrinking, so correcting it would make the name dearer, not cheaper. The counter loses.
- Left unresolved: Whether the 11.75% Q3 print is the floor or the trend. It contains a $10.5 million provision against a $3.2 million release in Q2 and nothing at all in Q3 2025, so provisioning is normalising from an unsustainably low base and I cannot tell from one quarter where it settles - at a mid-cycle charge-off rate the ROTCE could be a point or more below my 12%. I also cannot assess the six officer changes since May 2026: a new CEO, a new COO and four other 5.02 filings in five months is either a deliberate rebuild or instability, and nothing in the free record distinguishes them.

**Key risks.** Net charge-offs rose 4.3x year over year (0.06% to 0.28% annualised) and the provision swung from a $3.2M release to a $10.5M charge in one quarter - credit is normalising off an unsustainably clean base; Tangible book per share fell 5.7% in Q3 2026 ($22.47 to $21.20) because the dividend plus buyback exceeded earnings while AOCI eroded on rising yields; buying stock at 1.67x tangible book destroys tangible book per share; The balance sheet is shrinking: deposits down 5.4% year over year, loans down, three branch divestitures in four quarters - so two of the five quarterly ROATCE prints are divestiture gains rather than earning power; Six item 5.02 officer-change 8-Ks since 2026-05-13, including a new CEO and a new COO; Only $103.4M remains of the $450M buyback authorisation, so the ~7%-a-year share shrink that supports EPS is close to exhausted
**Watch for.** Q4 2026 ROATCE ex any further branch-sale gain: a print at or below the 11.75% of Q3 confirms 12% is the trend rather than a floor; The net charge-off ratio: whether it stabilises near 0.28% or continues toward a mid-cycle 0.40-0.50%; Tangible book value per share: a second consecutive quarterly decline would confirm the capital return is value-destructive at this price; Whether the board extends the buyback authorisation beyond the remaining $103.4M; Further officer departures
