**The case for the price.** Everforth — ASGN until it rebranded on 2026-04-24 — is the fourth-largest IT staffing firm in the US and the market is pricing the AI disruption of exactly that business. The company runs seven brands (Apex Systems, Creative Circle, CyberCoders, ECS, GlideFast, TopBloc, Quinnox) across a 70% commercial and 30% federal split. The bear case the price expresses is straightforward: contract IT labour is the most directly substitutable input in the enterprise technology stack, generative AI is substituting for it now, and the federal side is shrinking too — new contract awards of $0.9B on a trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill of 0.8 to 1. Against $1.5 billion of debt and a $1.3B market cap, a business whose revenue does not grow and whose margin is compressing is a levered stub. Q2 revenue was down 1.3% year over year with adjusted EBITDA margin at 9.6% against 10.6%, and free cash flow for the first half halved. A -18.6% five-year FCFF path is what the market thinks a shrinking, levered staffing book is worth.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 (2026-07-29) beat the high end of guidance on revenue, net income, adjusted EBITDA and margin — but the underlying numbers are worse than the headline. Revenue $1,007.0M against $1,020.6M, down 1.3%; Commercial $701.7M against $708.1M with four of five industries declining and only TMT up (+$7.7M, +5.6%); Federal $305.3M against $312.5M. Consolidated gross margin 28.3% against 28.7%; adjusted EBITDA $96.7M (9.6%) against $108.5M (10.6%). Commercial IT Consulting book-to-bill 1.2 to 1 on a trailing-twelve-month basis, but Federal book-to-bill only 0.8 to 1 on $0.9B of new awards. The decisive figures are in the 10-Q cash flow statement: first-half net cash provided by operating activities of $70.7M against $141.7M a year earlier — down 50% — and cash paid for acquisitions, net of cash acquired, of $283.6M in the first half of 2026 on top of $306.1M in the first half of 2025, funded by $370.0M of new long-term debt against $100.8M of repayments. On 2026-07-07, after quarter end, the company replaced its revolver and Term Loan A with a new five-year $600M revolving facility, extending maturity to 2031 and tightening the leverage covenant to 3.50x and then 3.25x. Debt at 2026-06-30 was approximately $1.5B carrying value (revolver, Term Loan A $97.5M, Term Loan B $486.3M, $550.0M of 4.625% unsecured senior notes due 2028) against $152.9M of cash. Shares outstanding 40.9M against 41.7M at year end.

**Base case.** The engine's $407.4M FCFF base is the mean of a declining series ($288.1M, $364.7M, $417.0M) and it describes a company two years bigger than this one. First-half 2026 operating cash flow was $70.7M against capex of $15.3M, so first-half free cash flow was $55.4M against $122.4M a year earlier. Even allowing for the usual second-half weighting, full-year 2026 free cash flow lands near $150M, and adding back roughly $55M of after-tax interest on $1.5B of debt gives an FCFF base near $205M — half what the model is using. That override is the first correction. The second is what the acquisition line does to the growth rate. Everforth spent $283.6M on acquisitions in the first half of 2026 and $306.1M in the first half of 2025 — roughly $590M in eighteen months — and revenue still went DOWN 1.3% year over year. That means organic revenue is declining at a mid-single-digit rate and the flat headline is bought, with borrowed money. My -3% is deliberately gentler than the -18.6% the market implies, because commercial book-to-bill of 1.2 is a real leading indicator of stabilisation and the company did beat its own guidance. But it is well below zero, because a business that needs $300M a year of acquisitions to stand still is not a flat business.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That this is a cheap, misunderstood business and the market has extrapolated a cyclical staffing trough into a secular death. Revenue is only down 1.3% and it BEAT guidance. Commercial book-to-bill is 1.2 to 1, which is a forward indicator pointing up, not down. Adjusted EBITDA annualises near $387M against a $2.65B enterprise value — 6.8x — for a business with a real federal franchise in defence, intelligence and national security that is not AI-substitutable, and a lender group that just handed the company a five-year $600M revolver out to 2031, which is not what banks do to a melting asset. The stock is down 37.9% over 252 days and up 61.7% over 63: the market has already been wrong once in both directions on this name.
- What would prove it: Whether the free cash flow is real and organic, or whether it is being manufactured by acquisitions that the free-cash-flow definition does not charge for. The test is cash paid for acquisitions against the change in revenue.
- Already visible today: Yes, and this is the pass that changed my answer. I started this name expecting to call it cheap: 6.8x EBITDA, flat revenue, improving commercial bookings, a big positive gap. The 10-Q cash flow statement killed it. Everforth spent $283.6M on acquisitions in the first half of 2026 and $306.1M in the first half of 2025 — and revenue was DOWN 1.3% year over year anyway. That is the EVI/roll-up pattern from the standing lessons: growth bought with cash that never appears in CFO less capex. It also borrowed to do it, $370.0M of new debt against $100.8M repaid. And first-half operating cash flow HALVED, from $141.7M to $70.7M, which no headline in the press release mentions. Once the acquisition spend is netted against the revenue it bought, the market's -18.6% stops looking like panic and starts looking like arithmetic. Correcting the base to the current run rate and applying -3% produces a fair value close to the current enterprise value — the +280% gap goes to roughly zero without my having to believe the AI bear case at all.
- Left unresolved: I could not separate organic from acquired revenue — the press release gives no organic growth disclosure and I did not read the acquisition footnote, so my 'organic revenue is declining mid-single-digits' is an inference from acquisition spend and flat headline revenue rather than a disclosed figure. I also cannot judge how much of the first-half operating cash flow decline is working capital timing rather than earnings, which matters because it is half my override.

**Key risks.** Roughly $1.5B of debt against a $1.3B market cap, with a covenant tightening to 3.25x in 2028; Revenue is flat only because of roughly $590M of acquisitions in eighteen months; organic revenue is declining; First-half operating cash flow halved year over year, from $141.7M to $70.7M; Federal book-to-bill of 0.8 to 1 means the government segment is contracting from backlog; Generative AI substitutes most directly for exactly the contract IT labour that is the majority of revenue
**Watch for.** Any disclosure of organic revenue growth separate from acquired — the number that would settle this; Whether second-half operating cash flow recovers toward the prior-year level or confirms the halving; Federal book-to-bill returning above 1.0; Whether the acquisition programme continues at ~$300M a year, and how it is funded as the leverage covenant steps down
