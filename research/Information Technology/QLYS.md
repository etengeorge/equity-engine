**The case for the price.** Qualys is a rare thing in security software: a genuinely profitable, net-cash, capital-light platform. Q2 2026 revenue of $182.2M grew 11%, GAAP net income was $52.4M and adjusted EBITDA $83.8M — an EBITDA margin above 45% that most of its faster-growing peers will never reach. Operating cash flow rose 77%. The composition of growth improved: channel revenue is now 54% of total against 49% a year ago and grew 22%, international grew 15% against 8% domestic, and net dollar expansion improved to 105% from 104% on upsell of ETM and patch management. Management raised full-year guidance to $732-738M of revenue and $7.74-7.88 of EPS, launched the AI-powered InstaScan vulnerability capability, and added new federal authorizations. The stock is +50.6% over 63 days on that re-rating. At an enterprise value of $5.5B the buyer is paying roughly 21x adjusted EBITDA for a business with no debt, high retention, and a widening moat as AI-driven attack surface expansion drives more scanning, not less.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 (4 Aug): revenue $182.2M, +11% year on year; GAAP net income $52.4M; non-GAAP net income $69.2M; adjusted EBITDA $83.8M; operating cash flow +77%. Full-year 2026 guidance raised to $732.0-738.0M of revenue and $7.74-7.88 of EPS. Net dollar expansion rate improved to 105% from 104%. Channel revenues +22% year on year to 54% of total while direct revenues were essentially unchanged. International +15%, domestic +8%. InstaScan AI vulnerability detection launched, plus new federal authorizations and AI security partnerships. A 11 Jun 8-K carried items 5.02 and 5.07 (officer/director change and annual meeting results). The stock rose sharply on 28 Aug alongside Rapid7, Tenable and other security names on AI-driven enterprise software commentary.

**Base case.** The decisive question on this name is stock compensation, and the brief puts it squarely: SBC is 29% of the reported FCFF base. Reported operating cash flow adds it back, so the engine's $257.3M base treats a third of employee pay as free. It is not — Qualys settles it in shares, and the dilution is real and permanent even when buybacks mask the share count. Expensing it takes the FCFF base to $182.9M and the growth the market requires from +15.4% to +24.4%. That is the number I have to underwrite, and I cannot. Revenue is growing 11% with guidance implying roughly 10% for the full year; net dollar expansion of 105% means the installed base contributes only 5 points and the rest must come from new logos; and the growth mix is shifting toward channel, which carries a lower realized price per dollar of end-customer spend. To compound free cash flow at 24% for five years on 10% revenue growth requires roughly 14 points of annual margin expansion off a base that is already above 45% EBITDA — there is not that much operating leverage left in a company this profitable. I underwrite +10% FCFF growth, matching revenue growth on an SBC-expensed basis, which is the honest statement that this is a good business growing at its revenue rate rather than a compounding machine.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest counter is that I am penalising Qualys twice for the same thing. If stock compensation is a real cost, then the correct comparison is Qualys's SBC-expensed cash generation against its peers' SBC-expensed cash generation — and on that basis Qualys is far and away cheaper than the security software cohort, which the engine confirms at the 63rd percentile of 124 Information Technology names with a gap 27.3 points better than the cohort median. Meanwhile a 29% SBC ratio is not high for security software; it is roughly average, and many of the peers trading at richer multiples are worse. Second, the required-growth arithmetic assumes the FCFF base is static, but Q2 operating cash flow grew 77% — if that is the new run rate rather than a timing artifact, the base is understated and the hurdle falls sharply. Third, the naive gap is only -8.3%, which is well inside the noise of any DCF; calling a name rich on an 8% gap is claiming a precision the model does not have.
- What would prove it: Net dollar expansion rate and the direct-versus-channel split over the next three quarters. If NDR keeps climbing past 107-108% while direct revenue resumes growing, the platform thesis is right and my 10% is too low. If NDR stalls around 105% while all growth continues to come through the channel, Qualys is being intermediated and the deceleration case is right. Separately, whether the 77% operating cash flow growth persists into H2 or reverses as a billings-timing effect.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it materially reduced my confidence without changing the direction. The cohort percentile is a real fact and it argues Qualys is the cheap one within its group — I accept that and it is why conviction here is low rather than medium. But the specific evidence on the deceleration side is also already visible and dated: direct revenue 'remained largely unchanged' year on year in Q2, with channel doing all the work, and NDR at 105% is only five points of expansion from an installed base. The bull needs those to inflect; they have not yet.
- Left unresolved: I could not establish how much of the 77% operating cash flow growth is durable versus billings timing, and that single figure is the difference between a $182.9M SBC-expensed base and a materially larger one. I read the Q2 press release but did not reconcile deferred revenue movement in the 10-Q, which is where the answer sits. If the cash flow step-up is real and sustained, my rich verdict is wrong.

**Data quality.** The single flag raised — stock compensation at 29% of the FCFF base — is the whole thesis and I have resolved it in the direction of expensing: I underwrite the $182.9M base and the +24.4% implied growth, not the reported $257.3M and +15.4%. Everything else on this name is clean: no debt, so the enterprise value is simply market cap less cash and there is no missing-debt exposure of the kind that has contaminated other names in this screen; no acquisition inside the window to make the history incomparable; the balance sheet is current at 30 Jun 2026; and no material 8-K items 1.01, 2.01 or 3.02 since. The one input I do not trust is beta: 0.99 at an R-squared of 0.148 is a weak regression, though it happens to land near 1.0 so the 10.25% cost of equity is not obviously distorted the way today's Energy names are. I did not use fcff_base_override because the brief already publishes the SBC-expensed figure and I have reasoned from it explicitly rather than substituting it. Note the honest caveat: this is a rich verdict on a naive gap of only -8.3%, and it rests entirely on the stock-compensation treatment — hence low conviction.
