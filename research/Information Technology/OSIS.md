**The case for the price.** At $208.17 against fiscal 2027 non-GAAP earnings per share guidance of $11.28-$11.64, OSI Systems trades on about 18.2x forward earnings — and the market is paying that for visibility rather than for growth. The first quarter of fiscal 2027, reported 2026-10-29, was a record on both lines: revenue $403.5M (+5%), GAAP diluted EPS $1.51 (+28%), non-GAAP $1.66 (+17%), a book-to-bill ratio of 1.4 and backlog at a record $2.1bn against $1.9bn at June 30 — an 11% increase in one quarter. Management reaffirmed revenue guidance and RAISED non-GAAP EPS guidance. The gap between GAAP and non-GAAP EPS is only $0.15, so this is not an adjusted-earnings story. Against that, three things justify restraint rather than enthusiasm: revenue growth is guided at only 5.0-8.1%, the Healthcare division is shrinking, and the Security division is described as delivering 'despite continued geopolitical headwinds'. A market asking roughly 18x for a business compounding earnings at 9-12.5% with two years of backlog is making a reasonable, unexciting judgment. The engine's claim that this is among the most EXPENSIVE 10% of 125 Information Technology names, requiring +41.8% cash-flow growth, is not what the market is doing.

**What changed.** The fiscal 2027 first-quarter 10-Q and earnings release were both filed 2026-10-29, the day before this pass, and the engine's extract predates them — its balance sheet is the 2026-06-30 10-K. The new quarter is materially better than the base the model is using: operating cash flow of $37.1M against $17.1M a year earlier, more than doubling, on capital expenditure of $7.7M. Revenue $403.5M (+5%), GAAP EPS +28%, non-GAAP EPS +17%, book-to-bill 1.4, backlog a record $2.1bn (from $1.9bn three months earlier), and fiscal 2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance raised to $11.28-$11.64 from $11.13-$11.49 while revenue guidance was reaffirmed at $1.875-1.930bn. The company repurchased 305,559 shares in the quarter — roughly 1.9% of the shares outstanding in three months — with 773,172 remaining under authorisation after the board added 1 million shares in August 2026. Also relevant and new to me from the fiscal 2026 10-K: management attributes the fiscal 2026 cash-flow surge explicitly to 'favorable changes in net working capital, largely from lower accounts receivable, an increase in deferred revenue and other liabilities, as well as higher net income', and names 'collections on large Security projects' as the driver — which identifies the three-year cash-flow series as one working-capital cycle rather than a trend.

**Base case.** I underwrite +10% real FCFF growth off an overridden through-cycle base of $190M rather than the engine's $74.1M. The base first, because it is the whole story. The engine's figure is the mean of free cash flow of $245.3M, $73.8M and -$109.6M (newest first), and I verified all three against the 10-K cash flow statement: operating cash flow was -$87,501k in fiscal 2024, $97,592k in fiscal 2025 and $275,904k in fiscal 2026, against property-and-equipment capex of $22.1M, $23.8M and about $30.6M. That series is not a cycle in the business, it is a single working-capital wave on large turnkey Security contracts — receivables built through fiscal 2024 and were collected through fiscal 2026 — so averaging the build year against the collection year understates normal generation badly. The newest year alone is 3.3x the mean. Building it from economics instead: fiscal 2027 guidance implies about $182M of non-GAAP net income (EPS midpoint $11.46 on roughly 15.9M shares), add back depreciation and amortisation, deduct true capital expenditure of about $50M (the $30.6M of property and equipment PLUS roughly $20M a year of capitalised software development the extractor omits — $5.6M in the first quarter alone), and charge the working-capital growth that a 1.4 book-to-bill on turnkey contracts genuinely consumes. That lands between $160M on a conservative through-cycle charge and $215M on an EBITDA-less-capex-less-tax construction; $190M is the midpoint. On the growth rate, +10% sits at the middle of the guided 9.0-12.5% non-GAAP EPS growth and above the guided 5.0-8.1% revenue growth, which is the right shape for a business whose backlog is growing faster than its revenue — but I hold it below the EPS figure because free cash flow has to fund the receivable build that record bookings create, and fiscal 2024 is the evidence that this can swallow a year's cash entirely.
