**The case for the price.** Agilysys is a vertical SaaS compounder in hospitality - property management, point of sale, booking and golf software for hotels, casinos, cruise lines and resorts - and it is executing. Fiscal 2027 Q2 revenue was a record $93.4M, up 17.7%; subscription revenue grew 32.6% and recurring revenue is now 66.6% of the total; gross margin improved to 64.6% from 61.7%; and management RAISED full-year guidance to $374-378M of revenue with subscription growth of at least 34% and adjusted EBITDA at 25% of revenue. The business is debt-free with $137.6M of cash and a long runway: it is still converting an installed base off perpetual licences onto subscription, and each conversion raises revenue per property. At 8.75x forward revenue for a company compounding high-teens with a Rule-of-40 near 43, the market is paying a normal premium-SaaS multiple, not a bubble one.

**What changed.** Fiscal 2027 second-quarter results, 2026-10-26. Revenue $93.4M +17.7%; recurring $62.2M; subscription +32.6%; GAAP net income $13.2M ($0.46 diluted) against $11.7M ($0.41); adjusted diluted EPS $0.67 against $0.40. Full-year guidance raised to $374-378M of revenue, subscription growth to at least 34%, adjusted EBITDA to 25% of revenue. Cash $137.6M against $116.9M at fiscal year-end, no debt. The one number that did not improve is the one that decides the valuation: FREE CASH FLOW IN THE QUARTER WAS $14.8M AGAINST $15.0M A YEAR EARLIER - flat, while revenue grew 17.7%. Alongside that, share-based compensation for the six months was $12,281k against $10,029k, up 22%, and basic weighted-average shares ROSE to 28,142k from 27,882k in the quarter and 28,122k from 27,838k for the half.

**Base case.** I underwrite a genuinely good business growing revenue in the high teens with meaningful operating leverage still to come: adjusted EBITDA margin at 25% can plausibly reach the low-to-mid thirties as the subscription mix matures, so free cash flow should compound faster than revenue. Twenty per cent a year for five years takes an SBC-expensed base of $36M to roughly $90M on revenue near $750M - a 12% free-cash-flow margin rising to the high teens. That is a demanding but achievable path and it is well above the company's own guided revenue growth of 17-18%, which is the right place for a software business converting mix. I will not underwrite more than that, because the evidence this quarter runs the other way: free cash flow was flat on revenue up 17.7%, so the margin is compressing, not expanding, and the growth is currently being funded rather than harvested.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I am applying a cash-flow model to a company deliberately trading near-term cash for a recurring base worth far more, and that the market is right to look through it. Subscription revenue growing 34% with 66.6% recurring mix is the strongest operating evidence in this session's ten; the company is debt-free with $137.6M of cash and just raised guidance on every line. Software buyers pay on revenue and ARR, not on a reverse DCF, and 8.75x forward sales with a Rule-of-40 near 43 is inside the band comparable names trade at. A model that calls this 50%-plus overvalued is the same model that would have called every quality compounder overvalued for a decade.
- What would prove it: Free cash flow re-accelerating in fiscal 2027 H2 and fiscal 2028 - specifically whether full-year FCF grows faster than the 17-18% revenue guide - and whether basic weighted-average shares stop rising.
- Already visible today: The opposite is visible. Q2 free cash flow was $14.8M against $15.0M, flat against revenue up 17.7%, and the share count rose 1.0% while SBC rose 22%. Both of the counter's load-bearing assumptions - that cash follows revenue, and that compensation is paid for - are contradicted by this quarter's own numbers.
- Left unresolved: Whether an 8.75x forward revenue multiple is simply what this quality of asset costs, in which case the DCF is the wrong lens rather than the wrong number. I cannot close the gap between a +46% required growth rate and a multiple that peers would call ordinary, and that is why conviction is medium rather than high.

**Key risks.** Free cash flow was FLAT year over year in Q2 while revenue grew 17.7% - the cash margin is compressing as the business scales; Basic weighted-average shares rose 1.0% year over year while share-based compensation rose 22% to about $25M annualised: the compensation is not being paid for; At 8.75x forward revenue and about 35x guided adjusted EBITDA, there is no valuation support if growth decelerates even modestly; Hospitality technology spend is discretionary and cyclical; the installed base is hotels, casinos and cruise lines
**Watch for.** Full-year fiscal 2027 free cash flow against the 17-18% revenue growth guide - it must grow faster, and this quarter it did not grow at all; Basic weighted-average share count: a flat or falling count would move the base from $36M back toward $54M and cut the required growth by 12 points; Any sign that capitalised software development is absorbing the cash the income statement is reporting

**Data quality.** The stock-compensation flag is the decisive input and the BOX rule settles it AGAINST the company. The brief offers two endpoints - $53.9M treating SBC as free (implied growth +34.2%) or $36.0M expensing it (+46.1%) - and the deciding number is the year-over-year BASIC weighted-average share count, which ROSE 1.0% (28,142k from 27,882k in the quarter, 28,122k from 27,838k for the half) while SBC rose 22% to roughly $25M annualised. A rising count means the compensation is not being paid for, so the expensed base is the honest one and I supplied $36.0M as fcff_base_override. The SECOND defect runs the SAME way, which is unusual and worth recording: beta is 0.518 at R-squared 0.041, barely above the engine's own 0.04 gate, producing a 7.95% cost of equity at a 100% equity weight for a small-cap vertical software company. A defensible beta of 1.0-1.1 gives a cost of equity near 11.2%, at which the market's requirement rises from +46.1% to +62.1%. Because the schema cannot override WACC, the recorded fair value is priced at 7.95% and therefore UNDERSTATES how rich this is - the error is conservative with respect to my own verdict, which is why the number is recorded rather than withheld (the FIBK rule). The 100% equity weight is CORRECT here: Agilysys genuinely has no debt and $137.6M of net cash, verified in the 10-Q. No other flags were raised and I found no others.
