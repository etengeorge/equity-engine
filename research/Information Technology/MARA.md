**The case for the price.** MARA is not a cash-flow business and is not priced as one; it is a levered bitcoin treasury with a power portfolio attached, and the market is paying for the second part. Bitcoin broke above $86,000 on 2026-09-21 for the first time in eight months and the stock rose 15.5% in five days. MARA held 35,577 bitcoin at 2026-06-30 - worth roughly $2.1 billion at the $58,524 quarter-end spot and roughly $3.1 billion at today's price - and bought a further 1,292 bitcoin for about $100 million in September. It has spent the first half of 2026 repairing the balance sheet, selling $1.63 billion of bitcoin to repurchase about $1.0 billion of principal of its zero-coupon convertible notes due 2030 and 2031, cutting convertible debt roughly 30% from about $3.3 billion to $2.3 billion. Against that, management expects the power portfolio to reach about 4.8 gigawatts once pending deals close, more than double today's capacity, and the bull case is that those megawatts are worth far more leased to artificial-intelligence tenants than used to mine. Net of about $3.1 billion of bitcoin and $0.42 billion of cash against $2.3 billion of converts, the $5.13 billion market capitalisation is paying roughly $3.8 billion for the mining fleet and the power pipeline. That is a price for an option, and options can be correctly priced.

**What changed.** H1 2026: $1.63 billion of bitcoin sold to fund a $912.8 million buyback of roughly $1 billion of principal across the 0.00% converts due 2030 and 2031; a Q2 net loss of $611.3 million (-$1.60 per diluted share) against net income of $808.2 million a year earlier, driven by bitcoin mark-to-market; adjusted EBITDA of negative $360.9 million against positive $1.2 billion. Q2 revenue was $174.9 million against $238.5 million. An 8-K on 2026-07-09 carries items 1.01 and 2.01 - a completed acquisition or disposition inside the current quarter, which post-dates the 2026-06-30 balance sheet the model uses. On 2026-09-14 J.P. Morgan double-downgraded the stock to Underweight from Overweight, citing the pivot to data centres; no AI leases have been signed.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The case against refusing is that MARA is one of the few companies in this universe whose asset value is directly observable, so a refusal throws away real information. Bitcoin is a marked, liquid, quoted asset: 36,900 coins at roughly $86,000 is about $3.17 billion, cash and short-term investments add $0.64 billion, and $2.3 billion of zero-coupon converts subtract. That is roughly $1.5 billion of net liquid value against a $5.13 billion market capitalisation, and the residual $3.6 billion is what the market pays for the fleet and the pipeline. Nothing about that computation requires a discounted cash flow, and it is a far more useful statement than 'no model'.
- What would prove it: A signed AI or hyperscaler lease with disclosed megawatts, term and rate. That is the single observable that converts the power pipeline from an option into a valuable contracted asset, and it is the thing every bull argument in the news flow is waiting for.
- Already visible today: No - and the absence is itself the most informative fact available. The 2026-09-21 Trefis piece is titled 'Is MARA Stock A Buy Today, Before Any AI Leases Are Signed?', and J.P. Morgan's double-downgrade to Underweight on 2026-09-14 cited precisely the data-centre pivot. So the market has been told about 4.8 gigawatts and has not been shown a lease. I accepted the counter's arithmetic and put it in the consensus case above rather than suppressing it, because it is right - but a net-asset computation whose residual is 74% of the market capitalisation is not a valuation of this company, it is a statement of what is left over.
- Left unresolved: The bitcoin count at today's date. I have 35,577 coins at 2026-06-30 plus a reported ~1,292 bought in September, but MARA both sells and buys continuously and I could not obtain a current figure, so my net-liquid-value estimate is approximate. I also could not read the 2026-07-09 item 1.01/2.01 8-K's consideration, which post-dates the balance sheet and may have changed both the asset side and the debt.
