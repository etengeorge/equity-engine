**The case for the price.** The market pays $2.74bn of enterprise value, about 10.2x guided 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $268.4M, for a debt-free supply-chain network with 300,000+ trading relationships, seven of the top ten US retailers as customers, and an adjusted EBITDA margin that rose 330 basis points to 34% this year. The price is a bet on operating leverage rather than growth: revenue is only growing about 5%, but adjusted EBITDA grew 17% in Q3 on 4% revenue because agentic customer onboarding is taking cost out of the hardest part of the business. At 10.2x EBITDA on a network asset nobody can replicate, with the share count shrinking 5.3% a year, that is a reasonable rather than a demanding price — and the market is right not to pay for the 19% revenue growth in the history, because that growth was bought.

**What changed.** Two things, in opposite directions, and together they move the verdict twice. AGAINST THE NAME: the engine's naive baseline of +19.2% five-year revenue CAGR is BOUGHT revenue, and the current organic rate is about a quarter of it. Q3 revenue was $197.6M against $189.9M, 4% growth, and RECURRING revenue also grew only 4%; full-year 2026 revenue is guided to $786.9-788.9M, 'approximately 5% growth over 2025'. Goodwill has gone from $249.2M to $541.7M over the window, and the largest piece is Carbon6 — $210.2M total consideration ($142.5M cash plus 378,100 shares worth $67.7M), closed 2025-02-04. That acquisition is now being unwound: SPSC DIVESTED the 3P Revenue Recovery business on 2026-06-30 (about $10.5M of H2 revenue, neutral to EBITDA), and effective 2026-09-01 it shortened the useful life of the remaining Carbon6 developed technology because it expects the acquired technology to CEASE BEING USED in mid-2027, adding $4.0M of Q4 amortisation and $6.9M in 2027. FOR THE NAME: the engine's FCFF base of $134.9M is about 45% below the current run rate. Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow is $185,664k against $132,919k, up 40%, on capex of $21,246k — nine-month free cash flow of $164.4M against a full-window base of $134.9M.

**Base case.** I underwrite 6% five-year FCFF growth on a corrected base of $190M. The base first: the engine's $134.9M is the mean of fiscal 2023-25 cfo-less-capex, and fiscal 2026 is running far above it — nine-month operating cash flow of $185.7M against $132.9M, which on last year's seasonality (FY2025 delivered $178.8M against $132.9M at nine months) implies roughly $250M of full-year operating cash flow, less about $29M of capex, i.e. ~$221M of free cash flow. I take $190M rather than $221M for two reasons: roughly half the year-over-year cash improvement is working capital and cash taxes rather than EBITDA (adjusted EBITDA grew about $25M over nine months while operating cash flow grew $53M), and the BOX construction charges the business for the cash needed to hold the share count flat — basic shares fell 1,995k while roughly 2,296k were retired with $196M annualised of buybacks, implying about 300k shares a year of net issuance, around $26M. Now the growth: revenue is growing 5% and recurring revenue 4%, so 6% total FCFF growth requires the margin to keep expanding, which it is — but 330 basis points a year is not a five-year rate. I model revenue at 5% with margin expansion decaying from 330bp toward zero over three years, giving roughly 7-8% FCFF growth early and 5% late, a five-year rate near 6%. That is deliberately BELOW the +12.7% the market requires on the engine's SBC-free base and far below the +19.2% bought baseline.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: Argued in both directions this pass, and it won both times. FIRST, AGAINST CHEAP: the engine's +26.6% gap and 78th-percentile rank rest entirely on a +19.2% revenue CAGR that is acquired — goodwill more than doubled over the window, Carbon6 alone cost $210.2M, and the organic rate is 4-5%. That is the EFOR artefact and it took the verdict off cheap. SECOND, AGAINST RICH: having corrected the growth I was about to record the name at roughly -16% on the engine's $134.9M base, and that base is wrong by 45% in the other direction — fiscal 2026 is generating about $221M of free cash flow, not $134M, which takes the market's requirement from +12.7% down to roughly +4%. Both corrections are large and they point opposite ways, so the name lands near fair.
- What would prove it: Whether the 34% adjusted EBITDA margin keeps rising once the agentic-onboarding savings are lapped, and whether recurring revenue growth recovers above 4% or settles there. Q4 and the 2027 guide settle both.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it is why I underwrite 6% rather than 9%. For the margin case: Q3 adjusted EBITDA grew 17% on 4% revenue and the full-year margin is up 330bp, which is genuine operating leverage and management attributes it specifically to agentic onboarding rather than to cost cuts. Against it: the same company is writing off a $210.2M acquisition's technology inside two years and has already sold part of it, which is evidence that capital allocation outside the core network has not worked — and the 4% recurring growth is the core network's own rate, not a depressed one. I also note that repurchases of $147.2M over nine months are running close to free cash flow of $164.4M, so the 5.3% share shrink that makes the SBC-free treatment defensible is not indefinitely fundable, which is the BOX caveat exactly.
- Left unresolved: How much of fiscal 2026's 40% jump in operating cash flow is durable. I attributed roughly half to working capital and cash taxes from the EBITDA-versus-cash bridge, but SPSC does not disclose the working-capital detail at a level that lets me verify the split, and that one judgement is worth about $30M on the base and several points on the fair value. I also cannot size what the mid-2027 retirement of the Carbon6 technology does to the revenue-recovery revenue line, only to amortisation.
