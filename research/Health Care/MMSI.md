**The case for the price.** Merit Medical is a well-run medical device manufacturer growing organic constant-currency revenue at 9%, raising guidance twice this year, and expanding non-GAAP operating margin 140bp with non-GAAP EPS up 18%. At $87.19 the market caps it at $5.2B, an enterprise value of $5.5B — about 3.4x guided fiscal 2026 revenue of $1.631-1.643B. That is an unremarkable multiple for medical technology, and the strongest case for the price is that a durable 8-9% organic grower with visible margin expansion and a diversified catheter and device portfolio deserves it. The market is not paying a bubble multiple; it is paying a quality multiple for a company that has been delivering.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 was reported 2026-07-30. Revenue $418.8M, up 10% reported and 9% organic constant currency, with US sales up 10% and results above the high end of management's own expectations. Full-year guidance was RAISED on both lines: revenue to $1.631-1.643B from $1.612-1.634B, non-GAAP EPS to $4.25-4.35 from $4.01-4.15, which is 11-14% growth against a prior 5-8%. Non-GAAP operating margin improved 140bp and non-GAAP EPS rose 18%. The fact that matters most for a discounted cash flow model, however, points the other way and is disclosed in the same release: FREE CASH FLOW GENERATION OF $76.6 MILLION YEAR TO DATE, DOWN 14%. Earnings are compounding at 18% while cash flow declines. Management notes the three-year 'Continued Growth Initiative' financial targets run only to 31 December 2026 and that a longer-term strategic plan is still being developed. The 8-K of 2026-08-20 is an officer change. There is no company-specific news in the store for the last 90 days, which lowers my confidence that nothing else happened rather than confirming it.

**Base case.** I underwrite 9% FCFF growth, against the 14.6% the price requires on reported cash flow and 19.3% with stock compensation expensed. The build: revenue growth of 7-8% is credible and roughly what the company guides (8% for 2026) and what its organic constant-currency rate has been running; on top of that some operating leverage, because the margin programme is demonstrably working. What I will not underwrite is 14.6% FCFF growth for five years, for two reasons. First, the margin programme that produces the excess over revenue growth is explicitly a three-year initiative ending 31 December 2026, and its successor plan does not exist yet — so the mechanism for years two through five of the DCF window is undefined. Second, and more concretely, free cash flow is falling 14% while non-GAAP EPS rises 18%. A discounted cash flow values cash, not adjusted earnings, and a company whose conversion is deteriorating during its best operating year is not the company that compounds cash at 14.6%. I also expense stock compensation, which is 18% of the FCFF base: on a device manufacturer competing for engineering and commercial talent that is a recurring cost of doing business, and on the expensed base of $151.3M the price requires 19.3%. At my 9% the fair value is $68.94 against $87.19 on the reported base and $56.32 on the expensed base — a gap of -21% to -35%.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: I am calling a quality compounder expensive on one soft cash-flow number and a calendar coincidence. The year-to-date free cash flow decline of 14% is a half-year figure on a company that is growing 9% organically and therefore necessarily building receivables and inventory; management explicitly guides to 'solid cash flow generation this year', and a working-capital build during accelerating growth is a sign of demand, not of deterioration. On the margin programme, the fact that a named three-year initiative ends in December 2026 says nothing about whether margins keep expanding — companies that have just delivered 140bp typically follow with another target, and management says a longer-term plan is being developed precisely for that. Meanwhile 3.4x enterprise value to revenue is not a demanding price for 9% organic growth in medical devices; at that multiple a modest re-rating is as likely as a de-rating, and a -21% DCF gap on a 7.7% WACC is well inside the noise of the discount rate. Note that a HIGHER, more defensible beta would make the name look worse, not better, so the WACC is not doing hidden work in my favour.
- What would prove it: Full-year 2026 free cash flow against 2025's. If it recovers to flat or better in the second half, the year-to-date decline was working-capital timing and my base case is too low. If it finishes the year down double digits while non-GAAP EPS lands inside the raised $4.25-4.35, the divergence between earnings and cash is structural and the DCF is right to discount the EPS story. The second observable is whatever margin target replaces the Continued Growth Initiative when the longer-term plan is published.
- Already visible today: Only half. The cash flow decline is visible and is a hard number the company chose to disclose; the explanation for it is not, because Merit does not break out the working capital and capital expenditure movements in the release, and I did not find a management explanation of the 14% decline anywhere in the materials I read. So I know the fact and not the cause, which is exactly why conviction is capped and why I did not go below 9%.
- Left unresolved: I could not determine why free cash flow fell 14% — receivables, inventory, capital expenditure and cash taxes are all candidates and the release does not decompose it. I also could not obtain any analyst or third-party view of the post-2026 margin plan, because web search and WebFetch were both unavailable this session. And with no company-specific news in the store for 90 days, I have no corroboration of the quarter beyond the company's own filings.
