**The case for the price.** At $6.45 the market prices Embecta's free cash flow to the firm to shrink about 6-9% a year for five years (my $165M base, WACC 8.35-9.35%, the engine's 2% terminal), and the evidence for a shrinking core is strong. FY2026 adjusted organic constant-currency revenue is guided down 8.8-10.2%; US revenue fell 20.6% in the first nine months and 24.6% in Q3 after a large US retailer moved to a lower-cost pen needle. Q3 alone lost $20.1M to price and $19.9M to volume year on year. Management said the customer and payer-mix hit to net price was larger in Q3 than in Q2, and the year-on-year decline in retail insulin-pen prescriptions was slightly faster. Gross-to-net deductions rose to $157.2M on roughly $429M of Q3 gross sales (36.7%, vs 32.6% a year earlier). Adjusted gross margin fell from 67.2% to 58.2%. Two once-weekly basal insulins are now FDA-approved: Novo's Awiqli on 2026-03-26, with US launch in 2H 2026, and Lilly's Onswik (efsitora) on 2026-09-24. Each cuts a basal patient from 365 needles a year to 52. Oral GLP-1s (Wegovy pill, Foundayo) and automated insulin delivery for Type 2 keep delaying or replacing injections. The guide implies Q4 adjusted operating margin of about 16-18%, against 25.5% in Q3, which was flattered by distributor orders normalizing after a Q2 destock. On the balance sheet: $1,469M of principal debt, 3.7x net leverage, and covenants that step down to 3.75x first-lien net leverage and 3.0x interest cover in 2028. The revolver was cut from $500M to $310M, with $100M still maturing 2027-03-31, and the lenders now require monthly liquidity forecasts. The $639M term loan (Mar-2029) and $700M of notes (Feb-2030) have to be refinanced off a smaller EBITDA. Equity is about 20% of enterprise value, so one turn of EBITDA multiple moves the stock by roughly $5-6 a share. The stock has already doubled from about $3.2 since the Q3 beat, so the stabilization re-rating has happened.

**Base case.** Base case: FCFF falls 5% a year from a normalized base of about $165M. That base is unlevered, treats stock compensation as a cost, and is my own construction, not the engine's.

Building the base from the FY26 guide: FCF of about $100M plus after-tax interest ($95M x 0.75 = $71M) gives $171M. Expensing stock compensation (about $21M) leaves $150M. Adding back about $26M of after-tax stand-up, brand-transition and Owen Mumford deal costs gives about $176M. Two adjustments bring that to about $165M: Q1 FY26 earned above the post-shock run rate (adjusted operating income of $79M, against a Q2-Q4 average of about $55M), which takes off about $18M; a full year of Owen Mumford plus the cost-review savings adds back about $5-7M.

Why -5% a year: US pen needles keep falling at roughly 6-8% a year, driven by the two weekly insulins, oral GLP-1s, automated insulin delivery for Type 2, and net-price resets to low-cost competitors. International, about 55% of revenue, grows low single digits in constant currency, helped by Latin America and Asia, held back by China. Owen Mumford (about $92M of annual revenue per the user's Q&A pack) grows high single digits. GLP-1 co-packaging and US small packs are real but small. The cost review offsets part of the negative operating leverage, and capex normalizes near $20M.

Where this differs from the screen: the naive baseline (3-year revenue CAGR of -1.5%) predates the FY26 step-down of about 9.5% organically. The engine's $81M FCFF base omits about $80M of after-tax interest (see data quality) and averages in the FY24 TSA-exit year.

Fair value in my base case: $12.33 with the engine's 2% terminal at its own corrected WACC of 8.35%. It falls to $6.16 with a 0% terminal, and to $7.85 or $3.26 at a 9.35% WACC with 2% or 0% terminal (subtract about $1.47 a share for claims the extract leaves out: $30.9M of finance leases, $14.2M of issuance costs netted against debt, and the $38.1M Owen Mumford earn-out liability). That range brackets $6.45. Once corrected, the whole gap rests on two choices the cash flows cannot settle: whether a pen-needle franchise whose drug partners are actively removing injections deserves a 2% perpetual growth rate, and a 3-point-a-year difference in FCFF decline that FY27 guidance has not yet tested. That is not an edge.
