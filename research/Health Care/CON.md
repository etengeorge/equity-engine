**The case for the price.** At $34.77 Concentra is a $5.8 billion enterprise valued at roughly 12x the $485-495 million of adjusted EBITDA it guides for 2026, for the largest occupational health platform in the United States — 633 occupational health centres, 415 onsite clinics, ~54,000 patient visits a day across 46 states. The market's case is that this is a defensive, fragmented, GDP-plus end market with a genuine consolidator at the front of it: Q2 revenue rose 10.0% to $606.0 million, patient visits per day 2.6%, and adjusted EBITDA 22.5% to $140.9 million as the fixed-cost base levered. Workers' compensation volumes do not cycle the way discretionary healthcare does, the payor mix is employers rather than Medicare, and the 2024 spin from Select Medical left a levered balance sheet ($1.56 billion of debt) that now delevers into growing cash flow. The stock is up 22.3% in three months and 67.1% in a year, so the re-rating has already happened.

**What changed.** Nothing that invalidates the model, which is itself worth recording. Q2 2026 (2026-08-06): revenue $606.0 million (+10.0%), adjusted EBITDA $140.9 million (+22.5%), patient visits per day 56,421 (+2.6%), with the increase attributed 'primarily to organic increases in both volume of patient visits and revenue per visit' plus the June 2025 onsite acquisition. Full-year 2026 guidance: adjusted EBITDA $485-495 million and FREE CASH FLOW $220-240 million. The acquisition machine has stopped for now — net cash used in investing was $29.0 million in H1 2026 against $374.3 million in H1 2025, with zero occupational health centres acquired in Q2 — so the free cash flow guided is available rather than pre-spent. Governance: a $34.65 million buyback of 1,000,000 shares (0.78%) from chairman Robert Ortenzio and related entities at a 1% discount, closed 2026-08-24, described as portfolio diversification; Matt DiCanio becomes CEO 2026-11-01 with Tanner Newton as CFO, the next step of a pre-announced succession.

**Base case.** Eight percent five-year FCFF growth. The building blocks are visible and modest: patient visits per day grew 2.6% organically, revenue per visit is rising, and H1 revenue grew 11.8% of which roughly 5.8 points were organic once the $28.7 million from Pivot and $22.7 million from the onsite acquisition are stripped out. So call organic revenue growth 5-6%. On top of that Concentra is levering a fixed clinic cost base — Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin went to 23.3% from 20.9%, 240 basis points in a year — and is deleveraging $1.56 billion of debt, which converts EBITDA growth into faster growth in cash available to the firm. I will not extrapolate 22.5% EBITDA growth: 240bp of annual margin expansion cannot repeat five times, and the roll-up has paused. Mid-single-digit organic revenue growth plus decaying margin leverage plus selective tuck-ins gets to roughly 8%, and that is close to what the market is asking for once the enterprise value is discounted properly.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That Concentra is a roll-up whose headline growth is bought, and that I have corrected the cash-flow base upward without checking whether the cash flow is real. This is the EFOR test from LESSONS: a serial acquirer's flat or growing revenue is purchased revenue, cash paid for acquisitions sits in investing activities where CFO-less-capex never charges for it, and a base built that way always reads as cheapness. Concentra acquired 72 occupational health centres in H1 2025 and over 240 onsite locations in June 2025, and 2025 revenue of $2,163 million against $1,900 million would then be mostly bought. If so, adding $77 million of after-tax interest to a purchased cash-flow stream compounds one error with another, and the +8% growth I am underwriting is not organic at all.
- What would prove it: Cash paid for acquisitions against the change in revenue, and the company's own organic-versus-acquired revenue split.
- Already visible today: Yes, and it clears Concentra — which is why the verdict is no_edge rather than rich. Net cash used in investing activities was $29.0 million in H1 2026 against $374.3 million in H1 2025, so acquisition spend has essentially stopped this year; zero centres were acquired in Q2. And the company gives the split directly: of the 11.8% H1 revenue increase, $28.7 million came from Pivot and $22.7 million from the onsite deal, i.e. about $51 million of the $124 million increase, leaving roughly 5.8 points organic. Volume is independently corroborated at +2.6% visits per day. The detector was the right one to run and the answer is that this is a 5-6% organic grower with real operating leverage, not a purchased top line.
- Left unresolved: The discount rate, which the schema cannot override and which decides the sign. Concentra's beta of 0.573 is measured at R-squared 0.145 on barely two years of post-spin price history, and per the LESSONS bucket study a weak regression attenuates beta toward zero and manufactures cheapness. At the engine's 7.12% WACC the market requires only +2.2% on my base; at a beta of 1.0 it requires +9.2%, which is where I actually sit. That is the difference between a large discount and roughly fair, and I could not settle it.
