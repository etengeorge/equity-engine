For a homebuilder, operating cash flow is dominated by investment in land and inventory, so it is large and positive when the builder is shrinking and negative when it is growing — the $1.08B in the oldest year of the series is the 2023 inventory-liquidation year, not earnings power. **The case for the price.** At $47.86 KB Home trades at roughly 0.76x a book value of about $62.80 a share, and the market is pricing the rate environment rather than mispricing the company. Fiscal 2026 is guided to $4.90-5.10 billion of housing revenue against $6.24 billion delivered in fiscal 2025 — deliveries down to 10,500-11,000 — with a housing gross margin of 16.0-16.2% and SG&A of 11.5-11.7%, i.e. a homebuilding operating margin around 4.5% and full-year net income near $190 million on $3.80 billion of equity. Meanwhile notes payable have risen to $2.11 billion from $1.69 billion a year ago and the debt-to-capital ratio to 35.7% from 30.3%, so the company is levering into the downturn to hold community count and buy back stock. 0.76x book for a 5% return is not a discount; it is arithmetic.
