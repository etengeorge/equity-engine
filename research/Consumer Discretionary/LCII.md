## 2026-10-16 — NO_MODEL (conviction: high)

**The case for the price.** LCII's price is not an opinion about LCI Industries' cash flows. It is Patrick Industries' price multiplied by a contracted exchange ratio. Under the Agreement and Plan of Merger signed 2026-06-30, each LCI share converts into 1.2440 Patrick shares; at Patrick's $68.13 that is $84.75, and LCII closed at $84.02 - a spread of -0.87%. The two securities are the same claim: their return series are nearly identical (5d +4.78% vs +4.70%, 21d -3.55% vs -3.24%, 63d -21.47% vs -22.22%). So the market is paying 99.1% of merger consideration, which is a statement about deal probability and timing, not about recreational-vehicle component demand. The reverse DCF's -15.3% implied growth and the +243.2% baseline gap describe a standalone company that has contracted to stop existing.

**What changed.** A MATERIAL, DATED REGULATORY ESCALATION THAT THE 2026-09-15 PATK PASS PREDICTED. The 8-K filed 2026-10-13 (event date 2026-10-09) states: 'On October 9, 2026, the Company and Patrick each received a request for additional information and documentary material (the "Second Request") from the DOJ in connection with the DOJ's review of the Mergers. Issuance of the Second Request extends the waiting period under the HSR Act until 30 days after both the Company and Patrick have substantially complied.' The sequence is exactly what the PATK entry forecast from the pull-and-refile: HSR filed 2026-08-05, both parties voluntarily withdrew 2026-09-04 and refiled 2026-09-09 (restarting the clock), and the Second Request landed thirty days later. That entry wrote that a withdraw-and-refile is 'the standard pull-and-refile when the agencies are preparing a Second Request'; it was, and it arrived. Also new: Patrick filed the Form S-4 on 2026-09-23 (File No. 333-299100) containing the preliminary joint proxy statement/prospectus, and it 'has not yet become effective', so no shareholder vote has been scheduled. Structure confirmed: a double merger (First Merger Sub into LCI, LCI surviving as a Patrick subsidiary, then LCI into Second Merger Sub).

**Base case.** No standalone growth rate is meaningful on a name trading at 99.1% of a stock-for-stock merger consideration, and this is the SECOND slot this exact transaction has consumed on this ticker (researched 2026-09-11) plus one on the acquirer (PATK, 2026-09-15) - three slots on one deal, with both halves of it sitting in today's screen simultaneously at +243.2% and +129.3% gaps. The valuable output is therefore a detector, and this filing supplies a better one than the file currently holds. THE RULE 425 FILENAME TEST FAILS HERE. The 2026-09-21 REFI entry nominated a substring test for '425' in the primary-document filename, having found 'ea0295037-8k425_chicago.htm' and siblings; LCII's merger-communication 8-K is named 'lcii-20261009.htm' with no '425' anywhere, and the same is true of the 2026-08-07, 2026-08-10 and 2026-09-10 filings. WHAT DOES WORK IS THE COVER-PAGE CHECKBOX: every one of these 8-Ks carries, on the face of the form, a checked box reading 'Written communications pursuant to Rule 425 under the Securities Act (17 CFR 230.425)'. That is a required, structured disclosure on every merger-communication 8-K regardless of how the filer names the file, so it catches BKH, LCII, PATK and REFI alike at zero cost. I also tested the obvious pairwise idea - flagging two names in the universe whose 5d/21d/63d return vectors are nearly identical - and IT DOES NOT WORK: at a threshold loose enough to capture LCII/PATK (0.75 percentage points of maximum absolute difference) the scan returns 1,212 pairs across 1,687 liquid names, overwhelmingly coincidental bank-and-bank matches, and the real pair does not even rank in the top twenty-five. Three return observations carry no discriminating power in a quiet tape. That negative result is worth recording so the next analyst does not build it. The test that does generalise is textual (the 425 checkbox) or anchored on a ratio read from the filing (price_target / price_acquirer against the contracted exchange ratio, here 1.2332 against 1.2440).
