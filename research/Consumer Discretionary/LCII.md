**The case for the price.** There is no standalone consensus to steelman, because LCI Industries has agreed to be acquired. On 30 June 2026 LCII entered an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Patrick Industries under which each LCI share converts into 1.2440 Patrick shares, with legacy Patrick holders owning about 52% of the combined company and LCI holders about 48%. Closing is expected in the first half of 2027, subject to both shareholder votes and regulatory approval, with more than $150 million of run-rate cost synergies targeted within three years. At $93.40 the shares imply a Patrick price of about $75.08, so what the market is 'assuming' about LCI's cash flows is only its assumption about Patrick's, plus a deal-completion probability.

**What changed.** Everything the model does not know. (1) The merger: announced 2026-06-30, confirmed in the 8-K filed 2026-09-10 under Item 8.01 reporting an event dated 2026-09-04, which recites the Merger Agreement with Patrick Industries, Planet First Merger Sub Inc. and Planet Second Merger Sub LLC — a two-step structure with LCI surviving first as a Patrick subsidiary and then merging into the second sub. The cluster of Item 8.01/9.01 8-Ks on 2026-07-22, 2026-08-07, 2026-08-10 and 2026-09-10 with no 2.02 among them is the merger-communications pattern. (2) The business, which is in a genuine downturn: Q2 2026 adjusted net sales fell 4.5% to $1,057.5 million; the company LOWERED 2026 North American RV wholesale shipment guidance to 280,000-300,000 units from 315,000-330,000; July net sales were approximately $315 million, down 4%. Operating margin guidance of 7.5-8.0% was reaffirmed on self-help cost actions, and adjusted EPS rose 13% to $2.70 on a 4% sales decline. The company also paid off the remaining $92.0 million of its 2026 convertible notes at maturity in the first half.

**Base case.** I will not supply one. A five-year FCFF growth rate for a standalone LCI Industries is a number about a company that has contracted to stop existing in the first half of 2027. ## 2026-10-16 — NO_MODEL (conviction: high)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $84.02
- **Not repriced:** not a repriceable fcff name or no growth supplied

**The case for the price.** LCII's price is not an opinion about LCI Industries' cash flows. It is Patrick Industries' price multiplied by a contracted exchange ratio. Under the Agreement and Plan of Merger signed 2026-06-30, each LCI share converts into 1.2440 Patrick shares; at Patrick's $68.13 that is $84.75, and LCII closed at $84.02 - a spread of -0.87%. The two securities are the same claim: their return series are nearly identical (5d +4.78% vs +4.70%, 21d -3.55% vs -3.24%, 63d -21.47% vs -22.22%). So the market is paying 99.1% of merger consideration, which is a statement about deal probability and timing, not about recreational-vehicle component demand. The reverse DCF's -15.3% implied growth and the +243.2% baseline gap describe a standalone company that has contracted to stop existing.

**What changed.** A MATERIAL, DATED REGULATORY ESCALATION THAT THE 2026-09-15 PATK PASS PREDICTED. The 8-K filed 2026-10-13 (event date 2026-10-09) states: 'On October 9, 2026, the Company and Patrick each received a request for additional information and documentary material (the "Second Request") from the DOJ in connection with the DOJ's review of the Mergers. Issuance of the Second Request extends the waiting period under the HSR Act until 30 days after both the Company and Patrick have substantially complied.' The sequence is exactly what the PATK entry forecast from the pull-and-refile: HSR filed 2026-08-05, both parties voluntarily withdrew 2026-09-04 and refiled 2026-09-09 (restarting the clock), and the Second Request landed thirty days later. That entry wrote that a withdraw-and-refile is 'the standard pull-and-refile when the agencies are preparing a Second Request'; it was, and it arrived. Also new: Patrick filed the Form S-4 on 2026-09-23 (File No. 333-299100) containing the preliminary joint proxy statement/prospectus, and it 'has not yet become effective', so no shareholder vote has been scheduled. Structure confirmed: a double merger (First Merger Sub into LCI, LCI surviving as a Patrick subsidiary, then LCI into Second Merger Sub).

**Base case.** No standalone growth rate is meaningful on a name trading at 99.1% of a stock-for-stock merger consideration, and this is the SECOND slot this exact transaction has consumed on this ticker (researched 2026-09-11) plus one on the acquirer (PATK, 2026-09-15) - three slots on one deal, with both halves of it sitting in today's screen simultaneously at +243.2% and +129.3% gaps. The valuable output is therefore a detector, and this filing supplies a better one than the file currently holds. THE RULE 425 FILENAME TEST FAILS HERE. The 2026-09-21 REFI entry nominated a substring test for '425' in the primary-document filename, having found 'ea0295037-8k425_chicago.htm' and siblings; LCII's merger-communication 8-K is named 'lcii-20261009.htm' with no '425' anywhere, and the same is true of the 2026-08-07, 2026-08-10 and 2026-09-10 filings. WHAT DOES WORK IS THE COVER-PAGE CHECKBOX: every one of these 8-Ks carries, on the face of the form, a checked box reading 'Written communications pursuant to Rule 425 under the Securities Act (17 CFR 230.425)'. That is a required, structured disclosure on every merger-communication 8-K regardless of how the filer names the file, so it catches BKH, LCII, PATK and REFI alike at zero cost. I also tested the obvious pairwise idea - flagging two names in the universe whose 5d/21d/63d return vectors are nearly identical - and IT DOES NOT WORK: at a threshold loose enough to capture LCII/PATK (0.75 percentage points of maximum absolute difference) the scan returns 1,212 pairs across 1,687 liquid names, overwhelmingly coincidental bank-and-bank matches, and the real pair does not even rank in the top twenty-five. Three return observations carry no discriminating power in a quiet tape. That negative result is worth recording so the next analyst does not build it. The test that does generalise is textual (the 425 checkbox) or anchored on a ratio read from the filing (price_target / price_acquirer against the contracted exchange ratio, here 1.2332 against 1.2440).

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That the -0.87% spread is too tight for a deal that just drew a Second Request, and therefore that there IS a tradable opinion embedded here - just a merger-arbitrage one rather than a valuation one. If the market is pricing ~99% completion against a genuine antitrust challenge to a 1-versus-2 horizontal combination, the risk is mispriced and the correct output is a view on deal probability rather than a refusal.
- What would prove it: The standalone downside. A credible break price for LCII - what the shares are worth absent the deal - against the 1.2440-ratio value, which would let the implied completion probability be solved for. That requires a standalone valuation of a cyclical RV supplier in a declining shipment year, which is exactly what the engine's inputs cannot deliver here (the FCFF series is falling and `possible_trough_cycle_base` is firing on it).
- Already visible today: Only partially, and it is why I am not making the arbitrage call. The spread itself is visible and so is the Second Request; what is not visible is the break price. Note the asymmetry the IMXI entry teaches: the condition most at risk is the one to read, and here it is squarely antitrust - so unlike IMXI, where the break risk sat outside the antitrust termination-fee trigger, any reciprocal break fee (the PATK entry recorded $94.2M each way) is at least aimed at the right risk. But a $94.2M fee against a $2.0bn market capitalisation is under 5% of value and does not protect the equity.
- Left unresolved: The implied completion probability, because I did not establish a standalone break price. I also did not read the S-4 itself, so I have not verified the outside date or the full condition set from the primary document; the 2026-09-11 pass recorded an expected close in the first half of 2027 and a 2027-03-30 outside date was recorded on the PATK pass, neither of which I re-confirmed here. And I cannot assess how likely the DOJ is to demand divestitures, which is the whole question.

**Key risks.** The price is PATK x 1.2440, not a cash-flow opinion: 1.2332 actual ratio against 1.2440 contracted, a -0.87% spread; DOJ Second Request issued 2026-10-09 to both parties - a material antitrust escalation on a 1-versus-2 horizontal combination in RV components; S-4 (File No. 333-299100) filed 2026-09-23 and NOT yet effective; no shareholder vote scheduled; The engine runs standalone reverse DCFs on BOTH sides of this merger in the same screen (LCII +243.2%, PATK +129.3%) and has now spent three slots on one transaction; Underlying RV cycle is contracting: 2026 North American wholesale shipment guidance cut to 280,000-300,000 from 315,000-330,000; standalone FCF series falling $465.0M -> $328.0M -> $278.3M; `possible_trough_cycle_base` fires on a genuine downturn, so an analyst working only the flag would debate a trough base on a company that has contracted to stop existing
