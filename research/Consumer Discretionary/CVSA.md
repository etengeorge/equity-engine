**The case for the price.** At $126.28 the market implies roughly zero five-year FCFF growth for a company that has just guided fiscal 2027 revenue up 5-7% and adjusted EPS up 8-11%. Those two facts only reconcile through dated legal events the model cannot see, and Covista's own 10-K names three. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act eliminated or restricted Grad PLUS and imposed new annual, aggregate and lifetime federal borrowing limits effective 2026-07-01 - the first day of Covista's fiscal 2027 - applying prospectively to new borrowers. The Act's 'Do No Harm' provisions, with implementing regulations published 2026-07-01 and generally effective 2027-07-01, strip Title IV eligibility from any graduate or professional programme whose completers do not out-earn a bachelor's-degree population in two years of three. And ED has placed BOTH Chamberlain and Walden - the two largest institutions - on PROVISIONAL certification through 2028-12-31, a status under which ED may revoke participation without advance notice. The market is not being pessimistic about a healthcare educator; it is pricing a stack of regulatory cliffs that bite in fiscal 2028-2030 as grandfathering runs off.

**What changed.** The company renamed itself from Adtalem Global Education to Covista on 2026-02-05 and began trading as CVSA on 2026-02-24, which is why the filing history switches prefix mid-stream. Fiscal 2026 results (2026-08-06, year ended 2026-06-30): revenue +9.3%, Q4 revenue +9.7% and total enrollment +8.4%, diluted EPS $7.04, adjusted EPS $8.25 (+23.7%), net leverage 0.5x. Walden total enrollment up 14% to nearly 55,000. Chamberlain total enrollment growth accelerated to 1.6%. Fiscal 2027 guidance initiated: revenue $2,050-2,090M (+5-7%), adjusted EPS $8.90-9.15 (+8-11%). Management states that loan caps and Grad PLUS elimination are not dampening demand, citing alternative financing and grandfathering.

**Base case.** I underwrite +2.5%, which is two years of the guided high-single-digit growth followed by three years of erosion as the grandfathering on Grad PLUS runs off and the Do No Harm earnings test begins removing programmes. The exposure is not evenly distributed and that is what makes the middle of the range right rather than either end. Chamberlain is predominantly undergraduate nursing funded by Direct Loans, which Grad PLUS elimination does not touch, and it is the institution with accelerating enrollment. Walden, at nearly 55,000 students, is overwhelmingly graduate and concentrated in exactly the low-earning fields - education, counselling, social work - that the Do No Harm test is designed to catch. The medical and veterinary schools are the sharpest exposure per student: a Caribbean MD costs well above the new professional lifetime cap, so those students face a funding gap federal loans will no longer close. Management's answer is private lending and grandfathering; grandfathering is a fact and defers the problem, private lending at that scale to those borrowers is a claim about a market that may not exist.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I have been captured by a risk-factor section and am ignoring the operating record. Covista just EXCEEDED its own guidance, grew revenue 9.3%, grew adjusted EPS 23.7%, guided a further 8-11% into the first year of the new regime, and carries net leverage of 0.5x with the balance sheet to buy back stock aggressively. Every for-profit education regulatory scare of the last fifteen years - gainful employment, the 90/10 rule, borrower defense - was priced as terminal and most were litigated, delayed, softened or repealed before they bit; 'Do No Harm' regulations effective 2027-07-01 with a two-of-three-year measurement window do not remove a single dollar of revenue before 2030, by which time the political composition of ED may have changed twice. On that reading a 13x multiple of owner earnings for a business compounding high single digits is simply cheap, and the +72.2% baseline gap is real.
- What would prove it: Walden's new-enrollment growth in the fiscal 2027 quarters - specifically the first cohorts who enroll AFTER 2026-07-01 and are therefore not grandfathered. That is the cleanest available read on whether the funding gap suppresses demand, and it arrives quarterly starting with the November 2026 report.
- Already visible today: Not yet, and that is the honest state of it - the first non-grandfathered cohort has barely enrolled, so the decisive evidence does not exist at the date of this verdict. What IS visible cuts both ways. For the counter: Chamberlain's enrollment growth ACCELERATED to 1.6% and Walden's rose 14%, both measured into the announcement of these changes, so there is no demand damage in the data yet. Against it: those are grandfathered students, so their behaviour tells us nothing about the cohorts that matter, and the provisional certification of BOTH Chamberlain and Walden through 2028-12-31 is a present fact rather than a future risk - it is a status in which ED 'may seek to revoke the institution's certification without advance notice', and it applies to essentially the whole company. The counter is strong enough that I will not call this rich; it is not strong enough to make a company facing three dated regulatory events a buy at a price that already implies near-zero growth. That is a no_edge, and I would rather own the question than the answer.
- Left unresolved: The share of revenue attributable to graduate and professional students dependent on Grad PLUS. I could not find a disclosure breaking revenue by loan programme, and without it I cannot size the cliff - I can only establish that it exists, is dated, and is concentrated in Walden and the medical/veterinary schools. That is the single most valuable missing number on this name.
