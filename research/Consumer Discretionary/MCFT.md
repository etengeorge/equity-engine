**The case for the price.** The market is pricing a company roughly 50% larger than the one the engine priced. On 2026-05-15 MasterCraft completed its combination with Marine Products Corporation and, per Note 4 of the FY2026 10-K, ISSUED 8,088,171 shares at a $24.64 closing price as $199.3M of stock consideration plus $84.8M of cash, for total merger consideration of $284.2M. The engine's share count is 16,279,890 as of 2026-05-01 — two weeks before the close — so it is missing every one of those shares. Market capitalisation is therefore roughly $489M, not the $326.7M in the brief, and enterprise value roughly $445M against the brief's $242.3M. Against the same $43.0M base, that moves the growth the market requires from -8.9% to about +6%, and on the run rate implied by the company's own transition-period guidance to about +13%. The +13.8% gap does not shrink; it inverts.

**What changed.** FY2026 results and 10-K, both 2026-09-10, for the year ended 2026-06-30 — the engine's balance sheet is dated 2026-03-29 and so predates the entire transaction. Q4 net sales $129.9M, +63.4%, or +21.5% excluding the Marine Products contribution; FY net sales $348.9M, +22.8%, or +11.0% ex-transaction. FY adjusted EBITDA $45.6M against $24.4M, margin 13.1% against 8.6%; Q4 adjusted EBITDA $20.5M against $9.5M. Dealer inventory down 30%. Cash $43.9M at year end against $28.9M, with $50.5M of short-term investments liquidated to fund the cash consideration. The company is ALSO changing its fiscal year end: guidance is given for a six-month 'Transition Period' of $287-291M of net sales, $29-32M of adjusted EBITDA, roughly $0.66-0.76 of adjusted EPS and about $9M of capex, with the first quarter of that period at roughly $147M of sales and $16M of adjusted EBITDA. A registration rights agreement was signed with LOR, Inc., now a holder of more than 10% of the stock, allowing up to two underwritten shelf takedowns a year.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The correction I have made kills the recorded gap, but it may prove the opposite of a refusal — it may prove the name is interesting on the other side. MasterCraft just doubled adjusted EBITDA margin on an 11% organic sales increase with dealer inventory down 30%, which is the classic shape of a marine cycle bottoming: destocking is complete, so retail demand now flows to wholesale shipments one-for-one. The transition-period guide of $29-32M of adjusted EBITDA on $287-291M of sales annualises to roughly $60M against a corrected enterprise value of $445M — about 7.4x — for a business with net cash and two newly acquired brands to integrate.
- What would prove it: Whether the Chaparral and Robalo brands hold the legacy margin structure through a full year rather than a six-week stub, and whether the transition-period adjusted EBITDA guide is met without a further dealer inventory drawdown. The first full combined quarter, guided to roughly $16M of adjusted EBITDA on $147M of sales, is the test.
- Already visible today: Only partially, and it is genuinely ambiguous. The Q4 margin expansion is real and the organic 21.5% sales growth is real. But the first Transition Period quarter is guided to a 10.9% adjusted EBITDA margin ($16M on $147M) against the 15.8% Q4 just delivered, which is a substantial sequential step DOWN and is the company's own signal that the acquired mix is dilutive to margin. The counter therefore does not yet win, and I record explicitly that refusing here is NOT a judgment that MasterCraft is expensive — at roughly 7.4x EV/EBITDA on corrected numbers it may well be cheap, and I cannot say so with a defensible per-share number.
- Left unresolved: I could not establish the post-transaction debt balance. The extract reads total_debt of zero, cash went from $28.9M to $43.9M while $50.5M of short-term investments were consumed and $84.8M of cash consideration was paid, which implies borrowing that I could not size from the sections I read. Any drawn revolver raises my corrected enterprise value further and makes the implied growth higher still — so the direction of my correction is safe, but its magnitude is a floor rather than a point.
