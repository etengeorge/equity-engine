**The case for the price.** Sonic fell 15.7% in five days into this screen, and the reason is dated and external rather than company-specific: on 2026-09-17 AutoNation guided down on parts-and-service growth and EV demand and the whole franchise-dealer group repriced (SAH -9.3% that day, a further -3.5% on 09-18). UBS had already published a downside 2027 earnings scenario for the group on 2026-08-17. The bear case is coherent: new-vehicle gross profit per unit is still normalising down from the 2021-22 shortage peak, affordability is stretched, and the parts-and-service annuity that supports the multiple is exactly what AutoNation flagged. Against that the market is paying roughly 8.9x annualised first-half diluted EPS of $3.58 for a business retiring about 7% of its shares a year, which is not an obviously stupid price in either direction.

**What changed.** No company-specific event. The Q2 10-Q was filed 2026-07-30; since then Sonic bought Porsche Walnut Creek (2026-08-27) and set a Sturgis powersports sales record (2026-09-09), neither material. The move is the AutoNation-led sector repricing of 2026-09-17/18. What HAS changed is my reading of the inputs: the +282.3% gap is an engine defect, not a mispricing.

**Base case.** The recorded +282.3% gap at the 99.2nd percentile of 128 Consumer Discretionary names is manufactured by a SIGN-FLIPPED CAPEX SERIES. `capex_series` reads [-$149.9M, -$187.3M, -$203.6M] — negative, because Sonic files 'Purchases of land, property and equipment' in parentheses and the extractor kept the sign — so `cfo - capex` ADDS capex instead of subtracting it. The brief's FCF series of ['$717.3M', '$296.5M', '$187.9M'] is cfo+|capex|; on cfo-|capex| it is [$417.5M, -$78.1M, -$219.3M], a mean of $40.0M against the recorded base of $400.9M. A universe scan found only six names with any negative capex year, and SAH is the ONLY modelled fcff name among them, which is why this has not been seen before. Two further errors compound it. `interest_expense` reads $0.5M against $1,758.8M of debt, an implied 0.03% (the PATK shape); the 10-Q reports non-floor-plan interest of $58.7M for six months, about $117M a year, so the after-tax add-back is understated by roughly $92M. And CFO is not owner earnings for a floor-plan-financed dealer at all — inventory investment runs through operating activities while non-trade floor-plan borrowing runs through financing, the KBH homebuilder shape. Sonic discloses the correction itself: 'if all changes in floor plan notes payable were classified as an operating activity ... net cash provided by operating activities [would be] approximately $235.7 million and $632.1 million in the six months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025'. The resulting grid, at the engine's 7.47% WACC and $3.95bn enterprise value: on the sign-corrected engine series alone the market implies +45.3%; with the real interest expense, +13.9%; on normalised owner earnings of $150M/$200M/$250M plus the real after-tax add-back (FCFF bases of $238M/$288M/$338M), -0.6% / -4.8% / -8.2%. The honest span is roughly -8% to +14%, which is not a valuation. My own view of the business is that Sonic's FCFF is broadly flat over five years — parts, service and F&I are a genuine annuity and Powersports is compounding through acquisition, but new-vehicle GPU normalisation and a doubled capex programme absorb it.
