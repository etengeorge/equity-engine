## 2026-11-13 — NO_MODEL (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $257.29

**The case for the price.** Group 1 Automotive is a Fortune 250 franchised auto retailer with 249 dealerships in the US and UK, and the market is paying about 8x earnings for it because franchise dealer economics have normalised off the 2021-22 gross-margin bubble: new-vehicle gross profit per unit has been compressing for three years, UBS has publicly modelled a downside case in which 2027 dealer earnings take a further hit, and the group has just levered up to buy more of the same business. The stock is down 31.8% over twelve months against a flat index. A reverse DCF that reports the market requires only +0.9% growth is describing a business the market thinks is at a cyclical plateau with rising capital intensity, not a bargain.

**What changed.** A $1.3 billion acquisition closed nineteen days after the balance sheet this model is built on. Group 1 completed the HENNESSY ACQUISITION on 2026-10-19 - ten dealerships and a collision centre in greater Atlanta plus related real estate - for approximately $1.3 billion, funded with the net proceeds of a 2026-09-22 private placement of $625.0M of 6.250% senior notes due 2032 and $625.0M of 6.625% senior notes due 2035, together with cash on hand. The 10-Q states plainly that 'the initial accounting for the combination, including the allocation of the purchase price to the assets acquired and limited liabilities assumed, the amount of goodwill and the relevant pro forma information was INCOMPLETE as of the date of this Form 10-Q.' Also: a 2026-11-10 8-K under items 5.02/5.03/8.01, and nine-month GAAP operating cash flow down $223.4M year over year, or down $149.7M on the company's own adjusted basis.

**Base case.** No defensible base case exists on these inputs. The enterprise value is dated 2026-09-30, when the balance sheet carried $881.5 million of cash against $32.5 million at year end - that cash is the senior-note proceeds, and it had already been spent on Hennessy by the time the stock was priced. So the model deducts, as shareholders' cash, $881.5M that now sits in ten Atlanta dealerships whose earnings appear nowhere in the cash-flow history. Enterprise value is therefore understated by roughly $0.85 billion against a $5.8 billion figure. Separately, the free-cash-flow series of [$424.5M, $341.2M, $4.8M] is a floor-plan artefact rather than a cash-flow history: Group 1 reports manufacturer-affiliated floorplan inside operating activities and revolver-financed floorplan inside financing activities, and says in terms that it considers all floorplan financing of inventory 'should correspond with the related inventory activity and be classified as an operating activity', publishing 'Adjusted net cash provided by operating activities' of $350.4M for the nine months against $500.0M a year earlier. The engine reads neither adjustment. With enterprise value wrong by a known amount, the base an artefact of inventory financing, and the company itself stating that pro forma information is not yet available, pricing this name in either direction would be a guess.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That I am refusing a name that is genuinely cheap. Even correcting enterprise value to roughly $6.65 billion and using the company's own adjusted operating cash flow rather than the GAAP figure, a franchised dealer at these levels is not expensive on any ordinary multiple, and parts and service - roughly half of gross profit and almost entirely recession-resistant - is the part the cycle does not touch.
- What would prove it: The Form 8-K/A with Hennessy's audited financial statements and the ASC 805 pro forma, due within 71 days of the 2026-10-21 filing. That is a FILED number and it will settle both the base and the purchase-price allocation.
- Already visible today: No. The company states the pro forma is incomplete, so the one document that would resolve this does not yet exist. Refusing today is a statement about the calendar, not about the business.
- Left unresolved: The size and margin of the acquired earnings, and therefore the whole valuation. Per the HNI rule the right move is to read the pro forma rather than estimate it - and here it has not been filed yet.

**Key risks.** $1.3 billion acquisition closed 2026-10-19 with purchase accounting and pro forma information explicitly incomplete; $1.25 billion of new senior notes at 6.250% and 6.625% added in September against a normalising earnings base; Nine-month adjusted operating cash flow fell to $350.4M from $500.0M; Franchise dealer new-vehicle gross profit per unit is still normalising off the 2021-22 peak
**Watch for.** The Form 8-K/A carrying Hennessy's audited statements and the ASC 805 pro forma - due within 71 days of 2026-10-21 and the number that makes this name modellable; The first balance sheet that is post-Hennessy, which will show true post-deal net debt against the $5.8B enterprise value used here; Whether adjusted operating cash flow stabilises after a $149.7M nine-month decline

**Data quality.** Three independent defects, and the detector that fires is one already on file. (1) The MCFT/PRGS test - an item 2.01 8-K dated AFTER `balance_sheet_asof` - fires at 19 days: the Hennessy acquisition completed 2026-10-19 against a 2026-09-30 balance sheet. Here it runs in an unusual direction: because the $1.25 billion of financing closed BEFORE the balance-sheet date and the spending after it, the 2026-09-30 cash of $881.5M (against $32.5M at year end) is deducted from enterprise value as though it were shareholders' money when it has already been converted into dealerships, so EV is understated by roughly $0.85B rather than by the full purchase price. (2) The SAH floor-plan defect: `cfo − capex` is not owner earnings for a floor-plan-financed dealer, and Group 1 publishes the corrected figure itself - adjusted operating cash flow of $350.4M for nine months against $500.0M - which the engine does not read. The resulting series [$424.5M, $341.2M, $4.8M] triggers `possible_peak_cycle_base_newest_fcf_88.4x_oldest`, a ratio computed on inventory financing rather than on earnings. (3) The 6.378% WACC at a 54% debt weight treats floorplan notes payable ($767.3M to manufacturer affiliates plus the credit facility) as permanent capital when it is inventory financing, which drags the discount rate toward the cost of debt.
