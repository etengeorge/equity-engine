# SABR — SABRE
*Consumer Discretionary · Russell 2000*

Append-only research log. Newest entries at the bottom.

## 2026-09-18 — NO_MODEL (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $2.32
- **Not repriced:** not a repriceable fcff name or no growth supplied

**The case for the price.** Sabre's equity is a stub option on a travel-technology franchise whose GDS and IT Solutions businesses still process a large share of the world's airline bookings. Between 2026-09-14 and 2026-09-16 the company priced an upsized $1.35 billion of 9.875% senior secured notes due 2032 and launched tender offers for its 11.125% 2029 notes (any and all of $1.0bn) and up to $250M of its 10.750%/11.125% 2029 and 2030 paper. That pushes the nearest wall from 2029 to 2032 and lowers the coupon on the most expensive tranche. On $3.7bn of net debt against a $936M market capitalisation, a refinancing that buys three years of runway is worth far more to the equity than to the enterprise — which is exactly why the stock rose 9.6% on 2026-09-16 on twice its normal volume. The market is not repricing cash flows; it is repricing survival odds.

**What changed.** The September refinancing, and nothing operational. 8-K 2026-09-15 (item 8.01) with four exhibits: the $1.1bn offering announcement (upsized to $1.35bn at pricing), the Sabre Financial tender and consent solicitation for the 11.125% 2029 notes at $1,092.50 per $1,000 including a $50 early-tender premium, and the Sabre GLBL tender for the 10.750% 2029s ($445.7M outstanding, at $992.50), 10.750% 2030s ($469.8M, at $980.00) and 11.125% 2030s, capped at a $250M aggregate purchase price. An investor day is scheduled for 2026-11-17. The coupons in that table — 9.875%, 10.750%, 11.125% — are the most informative numbers on the name.

**Base case.** There is no base to grow. Cash from operations less capital expenditure is NEGATIVE in every year of the model's window: [-$191.8M, -$13.6M, -$31.2M], a three-year mean of -$78.9M. The $257.1M 'normalized FCFF base' is therefore composed of MORE than 100% of the after-tax interest add-back on $3.7bn of debt priced between 9.875% and 11.125%. The reverse DCF built on it is solving for the growth rate that services the lenders, not the growth rate that accrues to the equity.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: Refusing here throws away the one thing LESSONS says a thesis actually needs: a specific, dated event. The refinancing is exactly that — the 2029 wall is gone, the most expensive coupon is retired, and on a stub this levered a change in the discount rate applied to survival is worth more than any change in the cash flows. Sabre also sold Hospitality Solutions for ~$1.1bn in 2025, which is real deleveraging the three-year window does not reflect. no_model is a refusal to engage with a genuine recapitalisation story.
- What would prove it: A year of positive cash from operations less capital expenditure — any single year — or a management free-cash-flow bridge showing how EBITDA covers cash interest, capitalised software and working capital. The 2026-11-17 investor day is the scheduled place for it.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it argues against the counter rather than for it. The company is paying $1,092.50 per $1,000 to retire the 11.125% 2029s — a premium, so the refinancing consumes cash rather than releasing it — and the new money costs 9.875%. A borrower that must pay a 9.875% coupon to extend by three years is not a borrower whose cost of capital has fallen. The 9.6% equity move is consistent with a stub repricing, not with cash flows improving.
- Left unresolved: Whether Sabre's underlying EBITDA is genuinely rising fast enough to outrun the interest burden. I could not establish it: the three-year window in the extract is contaminated by the Hospitality Solutions disposal and contains no clean year. This is the reason for the refusal rather than an argument against it.

**Key risks.** The -110.5% gap and the 6th-percentile 'richest' rank are both artifacts: the +15.7% revenue-CAGR baseline is measured off the COVID trough and across the 2025 Hospitality Solutions disposal, so it describes recovery and divestiture rather than growth; The entire $257.1M FCFF base is the after-tax interest add-back; the operating business consumed cash in all three years of the window; WACC of 14.5% at an 82% debt weight means the discount rate is essentially the cost of debt — the equity assumption barely enters the answer; At 7.4x net leverage a small change in the EBITDA multiple moves the equity stub by multiples of its current price, in either direction
**Watch for.** The 2026-11-17 investor day — the first scheduled opportunity for a free-cash-flow bridge that would make this name modellable; Final tender participation and the post-refinancing maturity schedule in the Q3 10-Q; Any single quarter of positive cash from operations less capital expenditure

**Data quality.** Flags raised were `negative_fcf_year_in_window` and `lumpy_fcff_spread_2.3x_of_mean`; both are understatements — ALL THREE years are negative, not one. Unlike BIPC and ALTG the debt here is visible and correctly in enterprise value, so this is not a missing-debt bug; it is the rarer case where the FCFF identity is arithmetically correct and still produces a number that cannot be grown. Recording no_model is explicitly NOT a judgment that the equity is worthless: the refinancing is real, dated and favourable to the stub, and I have said so above rather than burying it.

*Horizon: 12 months — re-evaluate no earlier than that unless something on the watch list fires.*
