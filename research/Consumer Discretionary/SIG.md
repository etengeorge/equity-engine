**The case for the price.** Signet is the largest specialty jewellery retailer in the US — Kay, Zales, Jared — with roughly 2,300 stores, $6.7-6.9B of guided fiscal 2027 sales, zero long-term debt and $526.8M of cash. At $102.48 the market caps it at $4.0B, an enterprise value of $3.47B against a $464.5M normalised FCFF base: a 13.6% FCFF yield, and a reverse DCF that says the price requires FCFF to DECLINE 9.5% a year for five years. The strongest case for that price is not that Signet is badly run, it is that the category is deflating. Lab-grown diamonds have collapsed the cost of the central input in bridal, which is roughly half of Signet's sales; unit volumes are already falling — Q2 sales fell 0.5% in absolute dollars while average unit retail rose about 6%, which arithmetically means units fell around 6%. Signet is shrinking to fit: net square footage down low single digit for the year, the James Allen brand being transitioned out at a cost of $60-80M of revenue, Rocksbox decommissioned, and $31.3M of inventory write-downs taken against those two closures in the first half. Gold at record highs is compressing merchandise margin in the same period. A market pricing permanent single-digit decline in a mall-based jeweller losing 6% of its units a year is not obviously wrong.

**What changed.** Q2 fiscal 2027 was reported 2026-09-09 with the 10-Q, and the stock rose 26% in five days on 2.2x average volume. Sales $1,528.1M, down 0.5% year over year, on same-store sales of +2.2% and AUR up ~6%. GAAP operating income $87.5M against $2.8M in a heavily-impaired prior-year quarter; adjusted operating income $107.2M against $85.4M; adjusted diluted EPS $2.19 against $1.61, +36%. Full-year guidance was raised for the second time this year: adjusted operating income to $535-605M from $480-560M, adjusted EBITDA to $730-800M from $665-745M, adjusted diluted EPS to $10.45-12.15 from $9.20-11.00. TOTAL SALES GUIDANCE WAS NOT CHANGED, at $6.7-6.9B. The quality of the raise is the story. The guidance assumptions name 'Refunds of tariffs previously paid of approximately $30 million' and '$30 to $40 million of non-comp revenue and gross margin from the new consumer credit agreement' — $60-70M of stated non-operating contribution against an adjusted EBITDA raise of $55-65M. Signet has received ~$20.4M of IEEPA refunds to date, of which $14.9M was booked as a reduction to cost of sales; the Q2 gross-margin improvement of 80bp was built on approximately $15M of tariff refunds, which is about 98bp of margin on $1,528M of sales, so EX-REFUND Q2 GROSS MARGIN WAS DOWN ROUGHLY 18bp YEAR OVER YEAR. Year to date, gross margin was 37.6% against 38.7%, down 110bp, on gold prices, accelerated melt and the James Allen/Rocksbox write-downs. Separately, Signet extended its consumer credit partnership with Bread Financial through December 2035, with a profit-sharing arrangement and a signing bonus recognised over the life of the agreement (8-K item 1.01, 2026-09-09), and announced a $125M accelerated share repurchase with the authorisation expanded to $700M.

**Base case.** I underwrite -3% FCFF growth: better than the -9.5% the price requires, worse than flat. The engine's $464.5M base reconciles to the company's own forward numbers and needs no correction — guided adjusted EBITDA of $730-800M less guided capex of $150-180M less cash taxes lands within a few percent of it — but roughly $30M of that FY2027 EBITDA is the non-recurring tariff refund, so I take a slightly conservative $430M base. From there the honest arithmetic is: sales flat with a low-single-digit square-footage runoff underneath, merchandise margin under persistent pressure from gold (cited as a drag in both the quarter and the year-to-date discussion) and from unit deflation, offset by genuine SG&A leverage (32.3% of sales from 32.9%, driven by operating model changes rather than by one-offs) and by $30-40M a year of high-margin credit revenue that is now contracted for a decade. AUR up 6% in both Bridal and Fashion says pricing is holding, which is the single fact that most contradicts a lab-grown collapse thesis. But units are falling about 6%, and a category that loses units while raising price is mixing up, not growing. -3% respects that; -9.5% assumes the pricing holds nowhere.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: I am reading a cyclical and promotional peak as a durable base, and the comparison quarter flatters everything. Q2 fiscal 2026 had GAAP operating income of $2.8M — a quarter loaded with impairments and restructuring — so 'operating income up from $2.8M to $87.5M' measures the absence of charges, not operating improvement. Signet's own decomposition of the +36% adjusted EPS names 'higher adjusted operating income, LOWER DILUTED SHARE COUNT, and HIGHER INTEREST INCOME'; strip the ~$15M tariff refund from adjusted operating income and it went $85.4M to roughly $92M, up about 8%, not 26%. Sales have now been flat in absolute dollars for two years ($3,081.7M against $3,076.7M year to date) while the company closes brands. And there is an unflagged cash-flow-quality item: deferred revenue is $371.6M current plus $905.6M non-current, $1.28B of extended service plans, so cash arrives years before revenue and a slowdown in plan sales would cut cash flow faster than earnings.
- What would prove it: Q3 and Q4 fiscal 2027 same-store sales against the guided -1.0% to +2.0%, and specifically whether AUR growth survives once the holiday promotional season starts. Gross margin ex-tariff-refund is the cleaner series: the company now discloses the refund amounts, so the underlying merchandise margin can be tracked directly against gold. Also the deferred revenue balance — if it stops growing, the cash conversion I am relying on weakens.
- Already visible today: Yes, on both sides, which is why I moved to -3% rather than to flat. Against me: year-to-date gross margin is DOWN 110bp even including the refunds; sales are down 0.5% in the quarter; the company's own Q3 comp guidance steps down to -1.0% to +2.0%; and the entire guidance raise is accounted for by the tariff refund and the credit agreement on Signet's own stated assumptions. For me: same-store sales have been positive in five of the last six quarters, AUR is up 6% in both categories, SG&A leverage of 60bp is from operating model changes, the balance sheet carries zero debt and $526.8M of cash, and the credit partnership runs to 2035.
- Left unresolved: I could not separate AUR growth into price versus mix. A 6% AUR increase with units down 6% is consistent with genuine pricing power AND with a shrinking business selling to a wealthier residual customer, and those have opposite five-year implications. Nothing in the release or the 10-Q decomposes it. I also could not size the Bread Financial signing bonus separately from the profit-share, so I cannot say how much of the $30-40M is a one-decade amortisation of a fixed payment versus recurring economics.
