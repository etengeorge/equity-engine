# CRI — CARTERS
*Consumer Discretionary · Russell 2000*

Append-only research log. Newest entries at the bottom.

## 2026-09-01 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_edge · price $32.39 · fair value $41.92 · gap +29.4%
- **Growth:** market implies -25.2%, analyst says -4.0% (delta +21.2%)
- **FCFF base overridden** by the analyst to $130.0M

**The case for the price.** At $32.39 the market treats Carter's as a structurally shrinking business, and the reasoning is respectable. The end market is the US birth cohort, which has been declining for over a decade and is not a cycle. Roughly a third of sales go through wholesale customers who have just cut second-half inventory commitments. Q2 gross margin fell 180bp to 46.3% on tariffs, product investment and clearance. Management's own FY26 guidance is sales +2-3% with adjusted EPS DOWN high-single to low-double digits from $3.47. Owners at this price are paying about 10x a declining earnings stream for a brand whose customer is disappearing, and are declining to capitalise a trough they do not believe is a trough.

**What changed.** The 2026-07-31 8-K (item 2.02) and 10-Q. Q2 FY26 net sales $615.5M (+5.2%), adjusted EPS $0.26 against $0.17. FY26 guidance narrowed: sales +2-3% off $2.90B, adjusted operating income up low-to-mid single digits, adjusted EPS down high-single to low-double digits. The material discovery is in the 10-Q liquidity section, which I read directly: H1 FY26 net cash from operations was $202.3M against NEGATIVE $8.3M a year earlier, but that swing is dominated by $132M of IEEPA tariff refund recoveries including interest, and the income taxes on those recoveries are payable in Q3. Ex-refund H1 operating cash flow is roughly $70M. Capex was $12.8M in H1 with about $50M planned for the full year. This is the second name in today's ten carrying a large one-time IEEPA tariff refund, the other being ANF — a cohort-level event, not a company-specific one.

**Base case.** The engine's $286.5M FCFF base is not usable: it is the mean of $469.3M, $242.6M and $68.6M, where the oldest year is a post-COVID inventory unwind that released working capital and the newest is depressed by first-year tariff costs. Neither end is normal. I rebuild the base from the current year instead: H1 FY26 operating cash flow ex-refund was $70.3M; H2 FY25 ran $130.6M (FY25 full year $122.3M less H1's negative $8.3M); netting the Q3 tax payment on the refund and subtracting ~$50M of capex puts normalized free cash flow near $130M, which is what I use as the override. On growth, I take management's guidance at face value rather than arguing with it: sales +2-3% but earnings declining, with tariffs a permanent level shift in gross margin rather than a one-off. That is a mildly declining FCFF stream, and I use −4%. I differ from the naive −0.8% baseline by being more negative, because the baseline is a revenue CAGR and revenue is holding up better than margin.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The red team's case here is not that Carter's is worse than I think — it is that my fair value is manufactured almost entirely by a model constant I did not choose and cannot defend for this business. The engine's terminal value assumes 2.5% nominal growth in perpetuity. For a company whose addressable market is the number of American babies born each year, a cohort in secular decline, a perpetual +2.5% nominal growth rate is not conservative, it is wrong. Carter's cannot grow with nominal GDP forever if its end market shrinks in real terms and it has no pricing power against Amazon, Walmart, Shein and Temu. Every dollar of my apparent upside is sitting in a terminal value that assumes the opposite of the thesis the bears are underwriting.
- What would prove it: Re-run the same base case at a terminal growth rate appropriate to a shrinking end market and see whether the gap survives. If it does not, there is no thesis, only a constant.
- Already visible today: Yes — I ran it, and the counter wins outright. Holding my $130M base and −4% explicit growth, fair value is $41.92 at the model's 2.5% terminal growth, $37.23 at 1.5%, $33.68 at 0.5% and $32.22 at 0.0%. The stock trades at $32.39. So at any terminal growth rate at or below about 0.5% — which is what I actually believe is right for a declining birth cohort — Carter's is worth exactly what it costs. The entire +29% gap at the model's default is a terminal-value artifact. This changed my answer: I came into the reconciliation intending to write 'cheap, low conviction' on a 10% free cash flow yield, and I am not going to, because I cannot defend the only assumption that produces the gap.
- Left unresolved: I could not establish the durability of the tariff cost. If the IEEPA refunds signal that the duties are being unwound rather than merely rebated, gross margin recovers 180bp and the earnings decline guided for FY26 reverses, which would lift the base enough to matter even at low terminal growth. I also could not verify the refinancing terms on the $567M of debt against $487M of cash.

**Key risks.** Secular decline in the US birth cohort is the end market and is not cyclical — the model's 2.5% perpetual growth assumption is inconsistent with it; Wholesale is roughly a third of sales and customers have cut second-half inventory commitments; Tariffs cost 180bp of gross margin in Q2 and guidance treats them as ongoing; H1 operating cash flow flattered by $132M of one-time refunds, with the tax on them payable in Q3
**Watch for.** Q3 FY26 operating cash flow ex-refund and net of the refund tax payment — the cleanest read yet on the true normalized base; Any FY27 guidance that breaks the pattern of sales up / earnings down; Wholesale order book commentary for spring 2027; Evidence that IEEPA duties are being permanently removed rather than rebated

**Data quality.** Three flags, all engaged. (1) lumpy_fcff_spread_1.5x_of_mean and (2) possible_trough_cycle_base — both real and both resolved by rebuilding the base from current-year filings rather than the three-year mean; I override to $130M, between the $68.6M trough and the $286.5M mean. The name is extremely base-sensitive: implied growth on the model's own EV runs from +5.0% at a $68.6M base to −25.2% at $286.5M, which is exactly the LESSONS warning that when one defensible input choice moves the answer that far the output is not a valuation — hence low conviction. (3) operating_leases_40%_of_EV_kept_as_opex: I initially treated this as an understatement of EV, then corrected myself — rent is already expensed inside CFO, so the FCFF base is net of it, and adding the $508M lease liability to EV without adding rent back to FCFF would double-count. The model's treatment is internally consistent and this flag resolves in its favour. Share count 36.7M as of 2026-07-24 is current. No item 1.01/2.01/3.02 8-K in the last two quarters, so EV is not invalidated by a transaction.

*Horizon: 24 months — re-evaluate no earlier than that unless something on the watch list fires.*
