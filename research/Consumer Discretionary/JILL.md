**The case for the price.** J.Jill is a 255-store women's apparel retailer with $600M or so of annual sales, about 47% of it direct-to-consumer, carrying $71.2M of debt against a $325M market capitalisation. At $21.74 the market is pricing FCFF to decline. The case for that is on the face of the company's own numbers once the one-time items come out: year-to-date net sales are DOWN 2.7% and year-to-date comparable sales are DOWN 4.2%; SG&A has risen 320bp as a share of sales on eight net new stores, lease renewals, marketing and shipping; and full-year comparable sales are guided at down 1% to up 1%. This is a mature specialty brand spending more to hold its customer file flat, with 30% of its enterprise value in operating leases that are kept as opex.

**What changed.** Q2 fiscal 2026 was reported 2026-09-09 with the 10-Q, and the stock rose 12% in five days on 3.2x average volume against a headline beat and a raised full-year outlook. The quarter was almost entirely a tariff refund. Net sales $154.8M, up 0.5%; comparable sales up 0.5%. GROSS MARGIN WAS 76.8% AGAINST 68.4%, UP 840bp — AND THE COMPANY DISCLOSES THAT EXCLUDING $13.3M OF NET IEEPA TARIFF REFUNDS IT WAS 68.3%, i.e. DOWN 10bp YEAR OVER YEAR. Operating income rose $7.5M to $24.3M while the refund alone was $13.3M, so ex-refund operating income fell from $16.8M to roughly $11.0M. J.Jill gives the cleanest version itself: 'Adjusted EBITDA for the second quarter of fiscal 2026 excluding net tariff refunds and deployment of these refunds toward strategic initiatives and elevated fuel costs during the period was $20.1 million' against $25.6M a year earlier — DOWN 21%. The raised full-year outlook 'includes net tariff refunds as well as the deployment of these refunds toward investments primarily focused on marketing', so the windfall is being spent, not banked. Full-year guidance: net sales flat to +2%, comparable sales -1% to +1%. Q3: net sales +3-5%, comps +1-3%, gross margin about flat, adjusted EBITDA $20.0-22.0M. This is the third name in today's ten carrying an IEEPA refund, alongside SIG and BBW.

**Base case.** The engine's $54.9M FCFF base is roughly twice the current run rate and must be overridden. Its series is $65.2M, $50.8M, $25.2M — monotonically declining, newest 0.39x oldest — and the `possible_trough_cycle_base` flag fires with the advisory sentence that 'growth applied to a trough understates value'. As LESSONS.md records, that sentence is backwards: the base is a three-year MEAN, the newest year IS the trough, and the mean overstates current cash generation by 2.2x. The current half is worse than it looks, not better. Reported first-half operating cash flow was $48.0M against $24.7M, but the 10-Q attributes the $23.3M increase to $25.0M of operating asset and liability changes, including '$16.0 million [of] accrued expenses and other current liabilities, WHICH INCLUDES LIABILITIES TO VENDORS RESULTING FROM TARIFF RECOVERIES' — money J.Jill is holding and owes on. Net income actually FELL $0.7M. Strip the vendor float and the $13.3M refund and underlying first-half cash generation is close to the prior year's. I take a $30M FCFF base, roughly FY2025's $25.2M plus after-tax interest, and underwrite 0% growth: comps guided to roughly zero, gross margin ex-refund flat, and SG&A rising 320bp of sales, against a genuine offset in accelerating new-to-brand customer acquisition and a stabilising customer file that management describes and I cannot verify.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: I may be overcorrecting the base and mistaking an investment year for decline. Q2 comps were POSITIVE (+0.5%) after a negative first quarter, the direct-to-consumer channel grew 1.9%, Q3 is guided to +1% to +3% comps on +3% to +5% net sales, and management says new-to-brand acquisition is accelerating and the customer file is stabilising — that is an inflection, and the tariff refund being deployed into marketing is precisely how a retailer buys one. On that reading my $30M base is too harsh: the correct base is the earnings power after the marketing investment pays back, not the trough during it. And at a $30M base the market implies only +1.0% growth, so if J.Jill can grow at all the stock is not expensive.
- What would prove it: Q3 and Q4 fiscal 2026 comparable sales against the guided +1-3%, and then fiscal 2027 SG&A as a percentage of sales once the refund-funded marketing lapses. The decisive series is comps holding positive WHILE SG&A percentage falls back — either one alone proves nothing.
- Already visible today: Only the first half of it, and it is genuinely split. For the bull: Q2 comps turned positive and Q3 is guided positive. For me: the first half as a whole ran comps of -4.2%, so one positive quarter against an easy comparison is not a trend, and the company itself guides the FULL YEAR to -1% to +1%, which implies the back half does not accelerate much beyond flat. The Q3 adjusted EBITDA guide of $20.0-22.0M is also almost exactly the $20.1M ex-refund figure Q2 produced, so the company is not guiding to an underlying step up.
- Left unresolved: I could not determine how much of the $16.0M accrued-liability increase is specifically the vendor tariff obligation as opposed to ordinary accruals — the 10-Q says the line 'includes' it without sizing it — so my reconstruction of underlying first-half cash flow is approximate, and the $30M base is my estimate rather than a disclosed figure. I also have no visibility on how the tariff refund is split between J.Jill and its vendors.

**Key risks.** The +61% baseline gap is a trough-flag artifact: on a base corrected to the ~$30M run rate the market implies +1.0% growth, not -12.7%, and the gap disappears; The quarter that moved the stock 12% on 3.2x volume was a $13.3M one-time IEEPA tariff refund; ex-refund gross margin fell 10bp and the company's own ex-refund adjusted EBITDA was $20.1M against $25.6M, down 21%; Year-to-date comparable sales are -4.2% and full-year comps are guided at -1% to +1%; First-half operating cash flow of $48.0M is inflated by $16.0M of accrued liabilities that the 10-Q says include amounts owed to vendors from tariff recoveries — a float that reverses; SG&A rose 320bp as a share of sales; the refund is explicitly being spent on marketing rather than retained; Operating leases are 30% of enterprise value and kept as opex, on 255 stores; $1.6M average daily dollar volume on a $325M company
**Watch for.** Q3 and Q4 comparable sales against the guided +1% to +3%, which is the test of whether the refund-funded marketing bought anything durable; Fiscal 2027 SG&A as a percentage of sales once the tariff refund lapses — the number that says whether this was an investment year or a new cost base; The customer file and new-to-brand acquisition metrics management cited qualitatively but did not quantify; Whether the vendor tariff liabilities are paid out and reverse the first-half working capital benefit
