## 2026-09-03 — NO_MODEL (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $29.97

**The case for the price.** Essential Properties is a net-lease REIT built on sale-leasebacks with middle-market, service-oriented and experience-based tenants — car washes, quick-service restaurants, early childhood education, medical and automotive service — where cap rates are wider than for investment-grade retail net lease and unit-level coverage is actually reported. Its investment spread over cost of capital is the whole business model, and it has historically been one of the widest in the sector. At $29.97 against roughly 1.5x tangible book, and having gone nowhere for a year (-0.3% over 252 days, +0.5% over 63 days) while the sector de-rated on rates, the buyer is paying for a growing, well-covered rent stream with an internal growth escalator plus accretive external growth funded at a positive spread. That is a rate story more than a credit story, and it prices as one.

**What changed.** No company-specific news in the store for the last 90 days, which for this name means the tape was quiet and the wire was quiet — it did not move enough or trade abnormally enough to earn a company news pull. The filing record is routine and consistent with a REIT executing a normal acquisition programme: Q2 results and an investor presentation on 22 Jul (items 2.02, 7.01, 9.01), a 24 Jul 8-K under item 8.01, a 15 Jun 8-K carrying item 1.01 (a material agreement), a 1 Jun dividend declaration, and a June investor presentation. Nothing in that set is a transaction large enough to invalidate the balance sheet, which is current at 30 Jun 2026. I found no guidance revision, no management change and no credit event.

**Base case.** I decline to state a growth rate. The engine classifies EPRT as not_modelled with the flag `sector_has_no_defensible_free_model`, and that classification is correct rather than a gap to be filled. A REIT's free cash flow to the firm is not a meaningful construct: depreciation on real estate is a non-economic charge that dominates GAAP earnings, acquisitions of income-producing property sit in investing cash flow and are indistinguishable from maintenance capital in the extract, and the capital structure is the business rather than a financing decision. The metrics that actually value this company — AFFO per share, the investment spread between acquisition cap rate and weighted average cost of capital, unit-level rent coverage, weighted average lease term and the debt maturity ladder — are none of them available from the free XBRL fields this engine reads. Producing a growth rate would mean producing a fair value, and any fair value I produced would be an artifact of the wrong model rather than a view about the company.

Those four numbers value a net-lease REIT and they are all in the quarterly supplemental. None of them is in this engine's extract.
- Already visible today: The counter loses on the arithmetic in its own table. The three multiples disagree violently: EV/EBITDA gives $26.63 at the cohort median, p_tbv gives $32.00 — ABOVE the current price — and EV/sales gives $5.57, a number that is not merely low but meaningless, because a REIT's 'sales' are rental revenue and comparing 16.6x rental revenue against a cohort median of 7.2x is comparing REITs to operating companies mixed into the same Real Estate bucket. The blended midpoint of $21.40 is an average of one plausible number, one high number and one nonsense number, and the brief itself says the cohort is 'not ranked — too few comparable Real Estate names to define a distribution honestly'. Averaging away a nonsense input does not produce a valuation. So the refusal stands, and this is the second name today where the multiples fallback should be actively disregarded rather than deferred to.
