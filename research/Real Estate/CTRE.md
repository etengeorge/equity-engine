## 2026-08-31 — NO_MODEL (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $39.03

**The case for the price.** CareTrust is a triple-net landlord to skilled nursing and senior housing operators, and it is currently the best-capitalised acquirer in a fragmented, demographically favoured asset class. Q2 2026 normalised FFO was $119.7M, or $0.51 per diluted share, up 19% year over year, with normalised FAD also $0.51 and up 19%. It closed $899.6M of investment in the quarter at a blended stabilised yield of 8.9%, taking year-to-date investment to roughly $1.5B at 8.7%, and raised full-year 2026 guidance to $2.03-2.06 of normalised FFO per share and $2.01-2.04 of FAD per share — about 16% FFO per share growth at the midpoint. At $39.03 that is roughly 19x FFO, a premium to healthcare REIT peers, and the premium is the point: a REIT that can deploy $1.5B a year at an 8.9% yield against a low cost of capital converts external growth into per-share growth mechanically, and the market is paying for the spread machine rather than the in-place rent. Underwriting quality supports it — 42% of the stabilised portfolio by rent covers at 3.00x EBITDARM or better, and only 10% sits in the 1.40-1.79x band.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 (2026-08-06): normalised FFO $119.7M / $0.51 per share (+19%), normalised FAD $118.5M / $0.51 per share (+19%), $899.6M invested at an 8.9% blended stabilised yield, roughly $1.5B year to date at 8.7%, and full-year guidance raised to $2.03-2.06 FFO per share. Guidance had already been raised at Q1. Portfolio activity through the year included a Mid-Atlantic skilled nursing acquisition of roughly $142M and a disclosed pipeline including a $500M Southeastern portfolio. Tenant concentration stands at Ensign Group at about 21% of annualised rent and PACS Group at about 10%. I found no adverse operator news, no guidance cut and no management change since the last 10-K.

**Base case.** None supplied. A triple-net REIT's value is not a discounted FCFF stream — depreciation is non-cash and larger than maintenance capex, acquisitions are the growth engine and are funded with issued equity, and the per-share outcome depends on the spread between issuance cost and acquisition yield rather than on organic cash-flow growth. The engine correctly declines to model this sector and I am not going to manufacture a fair value to fill the gap.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The counter to leaving this at 'no model, no view' is that the qualitative read is not neutral and I should say so. Nineteen times FFO for a skilled nursing landlord is a large premium to the peer set — Omega and Sabra trade nearer 11-13x — and the premium exists entirely because CareTrust keeps issuing equity at a high multiple to buy assets at an 8.9% yield. That is a self-reinforcing loop that runs in reverse the moment the multiple compresses: if the cost of equity rises, the accretion disappears, external growth stops, and a REIT valued on external growth re-rates to the value of its in-place rent. Skilled nursing also carries a policy risk unlike any other property type, since the tenants' revenue is Medicaid and Medicare, and Medicaid funding is under active federal scrutiny. On that reading the honest verdict is 'rich', not 'no view'.
- What would prove it: The pace and pricing of equity issuance against acquisition yields over the next several quarters, and any Medicaid rate or eligibility change that moves operator EBITDARM coverage. A quarter in which CareTrust stops issuing, or issues at a materially lower multiple, would show the loop tightening.
- Already visible today: Not yet. Coverage is strong (42% of stabilised rent above 3.00x EBITDARM), guidance has been raised twice this year rather than cut, the investment pace is accelerating rather than slowing, and the stock is only -6.3% over 21 sessions and still +17.3% over 252. Nothing in the observable data says the loop is breaking. The one durable soft spot is concentration: Ensign at 21% and PACS at 10% is 31% of rent in two tenants, and PACS was the subject of Medicare billing fraud allegations from a short seller in 2024.
- Left unresolved: Whether 19x FFO is right. I am not equipped to answer it here — the engine has no REIT model, I could not read the 10-Q, and a proper answer needs a cap-rate and cost-of-capital analysis this system does not do. I record the premium and the mechanism that sustains it as an observation, not as a verdict.

**Key risks.** Tenant concentration: Ensign at ~21% and PACS at ~10% of annualised rent, and PACS carries unresolved reputational and billing-allegation history from 2024; Medicaid and Medicare reimbursement policy is the operators' revenue and therefore the landlord's rent coverage; The valuation depends on continued accretive equity issuance; multiple compression stops external growth and re-rates the whole thesis; This engine has no defensible free model for REITs, so nothing here is a quantitative conclusion
**Watch for.** Any change in Medicaid reimbursement or eligibility rules affecting skilled nursing operators; EBITDARM coverage trend on the stabilised portfolio, particularly the sub-1.80x cohort; Whether the investment pace holds without dilutive issuance if the multiple compresses; Operator-level distress at Ensign or PACS
