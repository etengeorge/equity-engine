## 2026-10-16 — NO_MODEL (conviction: medium)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $65.67

**The case for the price.** National Health Investors is a healthcare REIT, and on the measure REITs are actually priced on it is unremarkable. Q2 2026 NAREIT FFO was $1.19 per diluted share ($2.42 for the half, up 3.4%), normalized FFO $1.19 (down 2.5% year over year in the quarter, up 2.1% for the half), and normalized Funds Available for Distribution rose $5.7M to $61.6M in the quarter and $12.1M to $124.1M for the half, driven by acquisitions. Full-year 2026 NAREIT FFO guidance is $232.3-234.9M. Against roughly 48.7M shares that is about $4.80 of FFO per share, so at $65.67 the stock trades near 13.7x FFO - squarely inside the normal band for a healthcare REIT and not obviously cheap or expensive. The market is paying a routine multiple for a landlord with modest per-share FFO growth, and that is a coherent price.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 reported 2026-08-10 (8-K items 2.02/9.01 with EX-99.1 and EX-99.2, 10-Q same day). NAREIT FFO per diluted share flat year over year at $1.19 for the quarter; the prior-year comparison contained $1.3M of proxy-contest expenses and, for the half, $1.2M of transaction costs on a SHOP acquisition that did not materialise, so the clean comparison is weaker than the headline 3.4% half-year increase suggests. Normalized FFO per share FELL 2.5% in the quarter, and the 2026 figures carry $1.1M of CFO-transition compensation and $0.7M of deferred income tax expense. Normalized FAD rose on acquisition activity. Two item 7.01 8-Ks on 2026-08-10, an item 5.02 officer change on 2026-08-27, and an item 8-K on 2026-07-01. No item 1.01, 2.01, 3.02 or 4.02 in the recent list, so nothing invalidates the balance sheet.

**Base case.** No FCFF growth rate is applicable and the engine correctly declines to compute one: method is `none` and status is `not_modelled`, on the standing and correct ground that a free-cash-flow-to-the-firm model cannot value a REIT, where depreciation is a non-cash charge against appreciating real estate and where the cash flow that matters is FFO rather than CFO minus capex. The engine's own mechanical picture confirms the refusal is not arbitrary - cfo minus capex runs [-$57.5M, $49.8M, $134.9M] newest first, i.e. it swings by nearly $200M across three years on a landlord with stable rents, because property acquisitions and dispositions run through those lines - and the after-tax interest add-back is 50% of what a base would be, which is near the SABR threshold on its own. MORE IMPORTANTLY, THE MULTIPLES TABLE MUST ALSO BE DISREGARDED, and the brief's -32.2% blended gap should not be read as a valuation. It fails the RIOT/EPRT refusal test twice over: the ev_sales row returns a NEGATIVE value at the 25th percentile (-$0.33 a share), and the cohort is explicitly 'not ranked - too few comparable Real Estate names to define a distribution honestly'. It also fails the EPRT test specifically: an EV/sales median of 7.4x built from a Real Estate bucket that mixes REITs with operating companies is being applied to a REIT's rental revenue, which is not the same quantity at all. The honest output is a refusal on the DCF plus the observation that the right tool - FFO - says the name is ordinary. That distinction matters: 'unmodellable' is not 'cheap' and it is not 'expensive' either, and recording the FFO multiple is how this verdict avoids leaving the reader with the multiples table's -32% as the only number on the page.
