# COMP — COMPASS INC CLASS A
*Real Estate · Russell 2000*

Append-only research log. Newest entries at the bottom.

## 2026-09-01 — NO_MODEL (conviction: low)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $10.56

**The case for the price.** At $10.56 and an $8.0B market cap, Compass is being priced as the consolidator of US residential brokerage. Having closed the Anywhere Real Estate merger on 9 January 2026, it now owns Coldwell Banker, Century 21, Sotheby's International Realty and Corcoran alongside the Compass platform, and the combined entity transacts a very large share of US residential volume. The bull case is operating leverage on a fixed technology and brand cost base plus merger synergies against a housing market that has been transaction-depressed for three years by mortgage rates — so volumes are near a cyclical floor and any rate relief flows straight to a business whose costs are largely agent commissions that scale with revenue. Compass has also turned cash generative: operating cash flow of $216.7M in 2025 against negative $25.9M two years earlier.

**What changed.** The Anywhere Real Estate merger closed on 9 January 2026, which is after the 31 December 2025 balance sheet the engine's extract is built on. The 2026-08-06 10-Q confirms it directly, disclosing H1 2026 operating cash flow of $34 million driven by $114 million of net income adjusted for $316 million of depreciation and amortisation and $149 million of stock-based compensation, offset by a $401 million deferred income tax benefit and a $142 million working-capital outflow that included cash payments for Anywhere merger transaction and integration expenses and payments on liabilities assumed in the merger. Note the shape of that: reported net income is positive but is carried by a deferred tax benefit and enormous non-cash amortisation, while actual operating cash generation fell to $34 million in H1 from $96 million a year earlier. There is also a 2026-04-17 8-K under item 1.01 (a material agreement) which I did not read.

**Base case.** The engine assigns method 'none' to this name because its GICS sector is Real Estate, and Real Estate is configured as unmodellable on the reasoning that REIT accounting defeats an FCFF model. I am recording the verdict the engine's configuration produces, but I want to be explicit that the reason given is not the reason that applies. Compass is not a REIT — it is an asset-light brokerage whose economics are commission revenue against agent splits and platform cost, and there is nothing about its accounting that makes free cash flow to the firm meaningless the way it is for a REIT or a bank. On the face of it, it is closer to an operating company than several names the engine happily models. So the sector rule reaches the right answer here by the wrong route. It does reach the right answer, though, and for a reason specific to today: the extract predates the Anywhere merger entirely. It records total debt of $0.0 for a company that has just absorbed Anywhere's capital structure, and a revenue series topping out at $6.96B that describes only standalone Compass. Any enterprise value computed from those inputs would be badly wrong, and the merger sits inside what would be the cash-flow window, making the history non-comparable. Both the configured reason and the situational reason point to no_model, so that is what I record.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The counter is that 'no_model' here is a configuration artifact dressed up as judgment, and that by accepting it I am letting the engine skip a name it could actually value. Compass has three years of operating cash flow ($216.7M, $121.5M, negative $25.9M), modest capex of $13.4M, and a clean asset-light model. Feed those into the same FCFF machinery the engine uses for any other operating company and you would get a number. Worse, because the sector rule blackballs the whole of Real Estate, the engine will never surface a residential brokerage, a title insurer or a property services firm no matter how mispriced, and that is a systematic blind spot rather than an honest refusal.
- What would prove it: Whether a post-merger FCFF valuation of Compass is computable from free data — specifically whether the combined entity's debt, share count and normalized cash flow can be established from the 10-Q.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it splits the difference. The counter is right about the sector rule being too blunt: Compass genuinely is not a REIT and the Real Estate blanket exclusion is over-broad. But it is wrong that Compass is valuable today, because the specific inputs are wrecked — total_debt of $0.0 post-Anywhere is not a small error, and the cash-flow series describes a company that no longer exists. Note too that H1 2026 operating cash flow of $34 million against $149 million of stock compensation means owner earnings are negative once stock comp is expensed, so even a repaired model would not obviously find value. The counter changes what I think should be fixed in the engine; it does not change today's verdict.
- Left unresolved: The combined entity's debt, normalized post-synergy cash flow, and whether the deferred tax benefit driving reported net income recurs. I did not read the 2026-04-17 item 1.01 8-K, which may bear on financing. I also could not establish the post-merger share count from what I read.
