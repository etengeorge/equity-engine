**The case for the price.** The market pays $2.77bn of equity plus $0.81bn of net debt for a consulting firm that has just raised guidance: record Q3 revenues before reimbursable expenses of $482.5M, up 11.6%, adjusted EBITDA margin at a sixteen per cent eight-year high, Healthcare managed services revenue nearly doubled, full-year RBR narrowed to $1.855-1.885bn and adjusted diluted EPS guidance raised to $9.20-9.40. On $9.30 of adjusted EPS the stock is on roughly 19x, which for a double-digit grower expanding margins is an ordinary price, not a cheap one. What the market is also pricing, and what the engine cannot see, is that none of this year's growth has turned into cash: nine-month operating cash flow is $15.3M against $67.0M a year earlier, and after $14.7M of capex nine-month free cash flow is $0.7M. The stock fell 8.9% in five days on 3.5x normal volume into a raised guide, which is the market reading the cash flow statement rather than the press release.

**What changed.** The Q3 10-Q of 2026-11-03 supplies three facts the engine has none of. First, NINE-MONTH OPERATING CASH FLOW COLLAPSED: $15,329k against $66,987k, and capex rose to $14,673k from $7,859k, so nine-month free cash flow is $656k against $59.1M — management framed it on the call as collections slipping into next year. Fourth quarter is seasonally dominant for this business (FY2025 operating cash flow was $193.4M against $67.0M at nine months, so Q4 2025 alone was $126.4M), so nine-month cash is not an annualising base; but a $51.7M year-over-year deterioration on higher revenue and better margins is real. Second, the interest expense in the extract is wrong by a factor of thirteen: `interest_expense` reads $3,463k against the 10-Q's $33,048k for nine months (about $44M annualised) on $813,886k of debt, and the filing STATES the weighted average rate — 5.5% at 2026-09-30, up from 5.3%. Third, the BOX test passes decisively: basic weighted-average shares are 15,916k against 17,340k in Q3 2025, down 8.2%, and 16,365k against 17,492k for nine months, down 6.4% — so $46.5M a year of stock compensation is more than paid for and the shares are shrinking net of all of it.

**Base case.** No number is recordable, because the single input that sets the sign of the answer is one the schema cannot reach. The engine's WACC is 4.994%, built from a BETA OF 0.0715 with an R-squared of 0.033, marked `yahoo_rescaled`, on a company with a 23% debt weight. A beta of 0.07 is not a low-beta business, it is a failed measurement — this is the ISBA condition on an fcff name. Correct the two things that can be corrected and the picture changes completely. The base: mean(cfo-less-capex) of $167.2M plus a correct after-tax interest add-back of about $33M (not $2.6M) gives roughly $200M, which cross-checks against the company's own guidance — adjusted EBITDA at 14.5-15.0% of $1.87bn of RBR is about $276M, less $20M of capex and $44M of interest, taxed, plus the add-back back, lands near $192M. The discount rate: a consulting firm with Huron's leverage supports an equity beta near 1.1, giving a cost of equity of about 10.6% and, at 23% debt costing 5.5% pre-tax, a WACC near 9.05%. At that base and that WACC the market requires roughly +7% five-year FCFF growth — against RBR guided to double-digit growth and margins at an eight-year high. But the required growth runs +1% at an 8.0% WACC and +11% at a 10.0% WACC, so a two-point choice I am making up spans the whole answer, and the engine's own figure of -8.0% required (the source of the +230.1% gap and the 97th-percentile rank) is simply the beta.
