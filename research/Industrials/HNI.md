**The case for the price.** The market is pricing HNI as a cyclical office-furniture manufacturer that has just doubled its size into a structurally impaired end market, and that case is coherent. At $46.91 the enterprise is about $4.65 billion against roughly $630 million of pro forma EBITDA — about 7.4x — which is almost exactly the multiple at which furniture assets change hands: HNI itself bought Steelcase at approximately 5.8x trailing adjusted EBITDA inclusive of $120 million of run-rate synergies. So the market is paying a modest premium to the private-market clearing price for the asset class, which is not an error. The bear logic underneath it is real: hybrid work permanently reduced office square footage; Steelcase's revenue was flat to declining for years before the deal; GAAP operating income for H1 2026 FELL to $55.7 million from $92.6 million a year earlier on purchase-accounting and restructuring charges; and operating cash flow for the half was NEGATIVE $32.0 million against positive $43.7 million, while capital expenditure doubled to $64.7 million. An investor looking at a 2x-larger company generating negative operating cash flow, with a new leadership team not yet complete, two retained headquarters and 113 years of acquired-company management just ended, can reasonably decline to pay for synergies that are still a forecast. Finally, part of the 2026 earnings growth is a windfall: net tariff impacts added 150 basis points to operating margin in Q2 and 40-45 basis points is expected for the full year, which is a benefit that can reverse.

**What changed.** HNI is a different company and the engine's cash-flow base does not know it. On 2025-12-10 HNI completed the acquisition of Steelcase Inc. — a roughly $2.2 billion transaction creating a business with approximately $5.8 billion of pro forma annual revenue, with HNI holders owning about 64% and Steelcase holders about 36%, funded with approximately $1,012.5 million of stock issued plus $390.3 million of net cash and debt. HNI's fiscal 2025 ended 2026-01-03, so its newest reported year contains only about three and a half weeks of Steelcase: the 10-K's own supplemental table shows Steelcase contributing just $187.5 million of net sales and a $30.7 million net loss to fiscal 2025. Meanwhile H1 2026 net sales were $2,819.9 million against $1,266.8 million — 2.2x. Q2 2026: net sales $1,472.4 million, GAAP diluted EPS $0.70, non-GAAP diluted EPS $1.27 (+14%). Guidance: full-year 2026 non-GAAP diluted EPS growth of 20-25% including net tariff impacts, a fifth consecutive year of double-digit growth; Q3 non-GAAP EPS growth in the mid-to-high 20s; double-digit growth again in 2027; and cumulative savings from Steelcase synergies, cost actions and legacy network optimisation exceeding $70 million in 2027 and more than $150 million when fully mature. Demand is turning: Workplace Furnishings Q2 orders and quarter-ending backlog each +5% year over year, with legacy Workplace Furnishings Q3 sales guided to high-single-digit growth.

**Base case.** The base is the whole question, so I replaced it with HNI's own filed ASC 805 pro forma rather than an estimate of my own. The 10-K discloses combined HNI-plus-Steelcase results as though the acquisition had occurred at the start of fiscal 2024: net sales $5,932.6 million and NET INCOME $176.1 MILLION for fiscal 2025, and $5,687.8 million / $119.9 million for fiscal 2024 — and that pro forma already charges full-year purchase-accounting depreciation and amortisation, full-year interest on the acquisition borrowings, and the non-recurring transaction costs ($41.6 million pre-tax). Building the engine's FCFF convention from it: pro forma net income $176.1M, add back transaction costs after tax (+$31M), add post-deal D&A at the H1 2026 run rate of $141.4M for the half (+$283M), add stock compensation (+$55M), deduct capital expenditure including capitalised software at the historical ~2.4% of sales (-$140M) and working-capital growth (-$25M), and add back after-tax interest on the H1 2026 run rate of $44.4M for the half (+$67M). That gives roughly $448 million. A cross-check from the cash-flow side agrees: the two companies' standalone free cash flow (HNI's three-year mean of $190.2M, which I tied to the 10-K line by line, plus Steelcase at roughly $140M implied by the deal's own 5.8x-including-synergies metric) plus the same interest add-back gives about $412 million. I take $410 million — the low end of a $410-450 million range — because 2026 capital expenditure is running above the historical rate and I want the haircut on my side. Against $410 million the market implies HNI's FCFF DECLINES about 4.5% a year for five years. I underwrite +3%: below the company's own guided trajectory, because synergy promises decay and the net tariff benefit is a windfall I do not capitalise, but above the decline the market requires, because orders and backlog are both growing 5% and $150 million of identified savings has not yet landed.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest counter is a multiple, not a model, and I cannot fully answer it. My base case fair value of $68.58 implies an enterprise value of about $6.07 billion, which is roughly 9.6x pro forma EBITDA. HNI itself paid approximately 5.8x for Steelcase — INCLUDING the $120 million of synergies it is now asking the market to pay for separately — and office-furniture comparables have traded at 6-8x for years. So my DCF is asserting a re-rating to a multiple the asset class has not supported, on the strength of a 9.02% WACC and a 2% perpetuity applied to a cyclical manufacturer in an end market that lost structural square footage to hybrid work and is not getting it back. Put differently: the market may not be mispricing HNI's cash flow at all; it may be correctly refusing to capitalise furniture cash flow at 15x. A second, sharper counter: the only post-deal cash-flow evidence that exists is H1 2026, and it is NEGATIVE $32 million of operating cash flow. I am overriding the base to $410 million on the strength of a pro forma and a cross-check while the one period of actual combined reporting shows cash going out the door.
- What would prove it: Two clean quarters of combined operating cash flow once the acquisition-related expense accruals are settled — H2 2026 and Q1 2027 — read against the guided 20-25% full-year non-GAAP EPS growth; and the 2027 realisation of the '>$70 million in 2027' savings figure, which is the first checkpoint on the $150 million.
- Already visible today: Some of it, and the balance of it favours my case rather than the counter's. Against the counter: the H1 negative operating cash flow is explained in the 10-Q's own words — 'higher working capital usage, driven by the settlement of certain acquisition related expense accruals' — which is a one-time integration cost, not a run-rate; and Workplace Furnishings orders and backlog both +5% with Q3 legacy sales guided up high-single-digit is direct evidence against secular decline right now. I also tested and DISMISSED the obvious objection that the free-cash-flow base over-adds D&A: combined capital expenditure at ~$140 million is 2.4% of sales, which is exactly what BOTH companies historically spent, so the excess of D&A over capex is acquisition-intangible amortisation rather than under-investment. For the counter and unanswered: nothing visible tells me whether 7.4x or 9.6x is the right multiple for post-hybrid-work office furniture.
- Left unresolved: The multiple question above — whether a 9.02% WACC and a 2% perpetuity are defensible for this end market — which is the difference between a +46% gap and no gap at all. I also could not obtain Steelcase's standalone free cash flow from a filing I read; my $140 million is inferred from the transaction's disclosed 5.8x-inclusive-of-synergies metric, so one leg of my cross-check is a derivation rather than a disclosure. This is why conviction is medium and not high.

**Key risks.** Secular contraction in office square footage — the end market, not the company, is the bear case; Integration execution on a merger of near-equals: two headquarters retained, leadership team incomplete as of July 2026, and $150 million of savings still a forecast; The net tariff benefit (+150bp of operating margin in Q2, +40-45bp guided for the year) is a windfall that can reverse and is inside the guided EPS growth; Residential Building Products is housing-levered with new-construction orders declining; My $410 million base is built from a pro forma and a derivation, not from a reported combined cash flow statement
**Watch for.** H2 2026 and Q1 2027 combined operating cash flow — whether it normalises once acquisition accruals are settled, which is the single observable my base rests on; The 2027 realisation of '>$70 million' of cumulative savings, the first checkpoint on the $150 million; Workplace Furnishings organic revenue and backlog: a return to negative orders would take this to no_edge; Any quantification of the net tariff benefit in dollars, so it can be stripped from the earnings base
