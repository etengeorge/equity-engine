**The case for the price.** Franklin Electric is a high-quality, 34-year dividend grower in water and energy flow control, and the market is paying for that quality plus a consolidating sector. Q2 2026 net sales were $622.9M (+6.0%) with operating income $93.6M (+6.2%) and adjusted EPS up 18%; the company raised full-year guidance to $2.21-2.29bn of sales and $4.50-4.70 of adjusted EPS. On 2026-09-04 it closed Cat Pumps for $350M cash - roughly 7.8x the $45M of 2026 adjusted EBITDA on ~$115M of revenue, a 39% margin business with recurring aftermarket content - expected to be accretive in the first full year, taking net leverage to about 1.5x. Sector M&A is repricing the whole group after ABB paid $5.5bn for Rotork. A buyer at $96.30 is underwriting mid-single-digit organic growth compounded by disciplined bolt-ons bought at single-digit EBITDA multiples and funded at roughly 5.5%.

**What changed.** Three things after the extract's 2026-03-31 balance sheet. (i) Q2 results and a raised FY26 guide (sales $2.21-2.29bn, adjusted EPS $4.50-4.70). (ii) The 2026-09-08 8-K reports completion of the Cat Pumps acquisition on 2026-09-04 for ~$350M cash plus performance RSUs with a $25M target value (up to $50M in stock per the press release), funded with cash and revolver borrowings - filed under items 1.01, 2.01 AND 3.02. (iii) The 2026-06-30 10-Q shows total debt of $242.8M and cash of $97.3M, i.e. net debt of $145.5M, against roughly $100M in the extract's period.

**Base case.** I use a standalone FY26 free-cash-flow base of about $215M against the engine's enterprise value, which predates the Cat Pumps debt - the only internally consistent pairing available, since the schema cannot override enterprise value. The engine's $237.1M is the mean of [$193.5M, $219.7M, $274.3M] and that series is monotonically DECLINING, so unlike almost every peak/trough case in this file the mean OVERSTATES the current level. FY2023's $274.3M was about 125% of net income - a post-supply-chain working-capital release - while FY2025's $193.5M was 106%. Conversion is not the problem; the 2024-25 water and agriculture cycle was. FY26 is recovering: H1 sales +7.7%, H1 operating income +7.1%, adjusted EPS guided up 13-18%, and H1 free cash flow of $38.3M against $13.6M a year ago on a business whose cash flow is heavily second-half weighted. That puts FY26 standalone free cash flow near $215-225M. Forward, I underwrite 5-6%: Q2's +6% was described by the CEO as 'balanced between organic and inorganic', so organic is roughly 3%, and price realisation is being fought by the 'inflationary price pressures in sourced materials and logistics lanes' management flagged in the same release. I deliberately do NOT credit future acquisitions to growth: cfo-less-capex never charges for the acquisition cash, so counting bought growth as free is the EFOR error.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: Two arguments, and both are serious. First, Cat Pumps is genuinely value-creating arithmetic, not a story: $350M for $45M of adjusted EBITDA is 7.8x for a business with 39% margins and recurring aftermarket revenue, funded at roughly 5.5% - instantly and substantially accretive to free cash flow, and the company says accretive to adjusted EPS in 2027. If I include it, the base rises to about $247M and the required growth falls from +10.8% to roughly +8.9%. Second, this is a $4.3bn flow-control asset with 35%+ gross margins in a sector where ABB just paid $5.5bn for Rotork, and FELE has been named in print as a plausible target. A strategic bid ignores a reverse DCF entirely.
- What would prove it: Full-year 2026 free cash flow at or above $240M standalone, or FY27 guidance that shows Cat Pumps plus organic growth compounding FCFF above 8%; alternatively, a bid.
- Already visible today: Partly. The earnings recovery is visible and real - H1 operating income +7.1%, adjusted EPS guided +13-18%, guidance raised twice. What is NOT visible is FCFF growing at the rate the price requires: the free-cash-flow series has fallen three years running, and H1 2026 free cash flow of $38.3M is still only 18% of a full-year $215M, so the whole FY26 number depends on a second half I cannot observe.
- Left unresolved: The takeout argument. I cannot price an acquisition premium and I am not trying to; if FELE is bought, this verdict is simply irrelevant rather than wrong. I also cannot override enterprise value in the schema, and the recorded fair value therefore uses net debt of roughly $100M rather than the ~$600M implied by management's post-close 1.5x net leverage - so the recorded number UNDERSTATES how expensive the stock is. Both framings agree on the sign: standalone base $215M against engine net debt gives $74-80, and a Cat-Pumps-inclusive base of $247M against ~$600M of net debt gives $77-85. Every version is below $96.30.
