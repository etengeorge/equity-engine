**The case for the price.** Alta is an equipment dealership roll-up with a $187M equity value sitting underneath roughly $730M of debt plus floor-plan payables. At 4.2x leverage on 2026 guided Adjusted EBITDA of $167.5-177.5M, and with $500M of second-lien notes costing 9.00%, the equity is a thin option on the construction and material-handling cycle turning. The market's -18% 21-day move is not irrational: Q2 was the seasonally strong quarter and the company still reported a net loss available to common of $(8.2)M, or $(0.25) per share. Revenue fell year over year ($475.5M, -$5.7M; -0.2% organic) and Adjusted EBITDA was flat at $48.6M. A levered dealer that cannot grow revenue in a recovering market, while paying 9% on half a billion of notes, is worth very little in equity terms, and that is what the price says.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 (2026-08-06): sequential improvement across all three segments, revenue up $65.0M versus Q1, Adjusted EBITDA up $20.5M sequentially to $48.6M, Material Handling backlog $143M — the highest since 2023 — and bookings up 12.3% year to date. Management describes 'signs of market recovery emerging' and tightened full-year Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $167.5-177.5M. Tariff-related disruption in Master Distribution eased.

**Base case.** The operating recovery looks real: backlog at a three-year high, bookings up, margins improving, and tariff disruption easing. But the FCFF this model discounts is nearly two-thirds interest add-back on a company that is not covering its interest with earnings, and the underlying cash generation — CFO less capex of $23.8M, $41.6M and $51.4M and falling — is small and shrinking. In a genuine dealership cycle recovery I would expect that to grow, but from a base this thin and against a fixed 9% coupon, low-single-digit growth in unlevered free cash flow is as far as I can honestly go. I am well below the naive +16.0% baseline, which is a revenue CAGR produced by acquiring dealerships, not by the existing ones growing.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The counter is that a correctly-levered equity stub in a turning cycle is exactly where the biggest returns are, and my correcting the debt downward-biases me into dismissing it. If Adjusted EBITDA recovers to $200M and the multiple holds, enterprise value rises roughly $270M on my corrected $907M base — a 30% move in EV against a $187M equity stub is more than a double in the shares. Backlog at a three-year high and bookings up 12.3% is the leading indicator of exactly that, and it is visible now, not hoped for.
- What would prove it: Sequential Adjusted EBITDA in Q3 and Q4 against the $167.5-177.5M full-year guide, and whether net debt plus floor plan actually falls. Deleveraging, not EBITDA alone, is what transfers value to this equity.
- Already visible today: The leading indicators are visible and genuinely positive — $143M material handling backlog, bookings +12.3% year to date, Adjusted EBITDA up $20.5M sequentially. What is NOT visible is any deleveraging: the ABL balance was $211.6M at 2026-06-30 against $213.6M at year-end 2025, essentially unchanged, and the $500M notes are untouched. Six months of 'recovery' has not reduced debt at all.
- Left unresolved: I could not extract the new-equipment floor plan payable balance, so I do not know total obligations precisely — I know they exceed $730.6M of debt and finance leases plus $85.2M of used and rental floor plan. I also could not separate maintenance from growth rental fleet spending. The verdict does not turn on either: it turns on the fact that the screen's inputs are wrong, which I have established.

**Key risks.** Total debt is at least $730.6M against a $187M equity value; a modest fall in enterprise value eliminates the equity; $500M of second-lien notes at 9.00% due June 2029 against guided Adjusted EBITDA of ~$172M — 4.2x leverage before floor plan; Six months of operating recovery produced no debt reduction whatsoever; Beta 2.26 (R-squared 0.441) gives a 17.1% WACC, so the discount rate itself dominates the valuation
**Watch for.** Any reduction in the ABL balance or total floor plan — the first real evidence of deleveraging; Q3/Q4 Adjusted EBITDA against the $167.5-177.5M guide; Used equipment residual values, which drive both earnings and the ABL borrowing base

**Data quality.** The +281% gap is manufactured by missing debt, and I verified this against the filing rather than inferring it. The extract records total_debt of $497.2M. The 2026-06-30 10-Q reports total debt and finance leases of $730.6M — $500.0M of senior secured second lien notes plus a $209.6M line of credit plus finance leases — and separately $85.2M of floor plan payable on used and rental equipment, plus a further new-equipment floor plan balance. The $209.6M ABL line of credit has been dropped entirely. Missing debt understates enterprise value ($663.2M recorded against at least ~$907M), which understates the growth the market appears to imply and manufactures a positive gap every time, exactly as the convertible-notes bug did. The cheap detector named in LESSONS catches it instantly: interest_expense of $88.4M against recorded total_debt of $497.2M is an implied interest rate of 17.8%, which is impossible for a company whose stated coupons are 9.00% and 5.3%. Second, ALTG's capex_series is [$9.2M, $15.4M, $12.4M], which cannot cover a rental fleet — the same rental-capex omission found on CTOS today. The trough-cycle flag the brief did raise is real and points the other way, but it is not what produced this gap. I have deliberately NOT set an fcff_base_override, because with the enterprise value understated by roughly a third any repriced fair value will be too high regardless of the base; read the recorded number as an upper bound, not an estimate.
