**The case for the price.** GEO is in the steepest expansion of its history and the market is paying for the run rate rather than the trailing year. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA rose 20% to $142.0M; H1 adjusted EBITDA was $273.4M against $218.4M; and management RAISED full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $550-560M. Two ICE facility activations are contracted and funded - a five-year support-services contract effective 2026-08-01 for the 1,320-bed Rivers Facility expected to generate approximately $80M of annual revenue in its first full year, plus Big Horn - with both expected to be complete by end-2026 and to reach normalized earnings in early 2027. Critically, ICE reimburses GEO for the reactivation capital expenditure and funds start-up costs, so the growth is being financed by the customer. Net leverage is below 3x trailing adjusted EBITDA. At $5.44B of enterprise value that is 9.8x guided 2026 EBITDA for a business whose EBITDA is growing 25%+ with contracted visibility into 2027.

**What changed.** Q2 2026 (filed 2026-08-06): FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance raised to $550-560M; total UNREIMBURSED capital expenditures guided to $135-145M; effective tax rate ~30%; Q3 adjusted EBITDA guided $140-145M and Q4 $137-142M on quarterly revenue of $755-808M. The Rivers Facility support-services contract was signed effective 2026-08-01. The 10-Q also discloses that the federal government shutdown ENDED on 2026-04-30 - which is the fact that explains the engine's entire cash-flow history.

**Base case.** The engine's newest free-cash-flow year of MINUS $124.9M is an artifact of two events, neither of which is an owner cost, and the correction is large. First, the FY2025 10-K states that operating cash flow of $72.6M was reduced by accounts receivable and other assets increasing 'a net of $233.0 million... primarily driven by the timing of billings and collections along with DELAYS IN PAYMENT DUE TO THE GOVERNMENT SHUT DOWN during the fourth quarter of 2025'. That is a receivable build, and it has already reversed: H1 2026 CFO was $236.5M against $110.4M a year earlier, helped by a $68.9M decrease in receivables. Second, FY2025 capital expenditure of $197.5M includes the Big Horn and Rivers reactivation spend that ICE contractually reimburses; GEO's own guidance for the capex that actually costs shareholders is $135-145M. So the model charged GEO for a customer-funded expansion and for a shutdown-driven working-capital swing in the same year, and then applied a growth rate to what was left. Normalising FY2025 CFO for the shutdown and capex to the guided unreimbursed level gives a three-year mean free cash flow near $180M; adding back after-tax interest at the guided 30% rate puts the base near $295-315M against the recorded $203.9M. On that base the market requires +16.5% five-year FCFF growth. I underwrite 8%: the two activations are real and lift 2027, but they are a step, not a compounding rate - there is a finite stock of idle beds, and beyond the current wave GEO's revenue history is a 2.3% five-year CAGR.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest case against my 'rich' verdict is that I am extrapolating a 2.3% historical revenue CAGR across a structural break. GEO's revenue history is the history of a company with a third of its beds empty; the company trading today has contracted, funded activations and a customer with an expanding appropriation. If detention capacity demand keeps growing, the correct comparison is not the five-year CAGR but the 25% EBITDA growth being delivered right now, and +16.5% for five years is then achievable rather than heroic. There is also a structural argument I cannot dismiss: because ICE reimburses the activation capex, GEO grows revenue without consuming shareholder capital, which is precisely the condition under which high FCFF growth rates ARE sustainable.
- What would prove it: The 2027 guidance, and specifically whether a third and fourth activation are announced. If GEO names further facility reactivations at Rivers-like economics during 2027, my step-not-a-rate framing is wrong. The cheaper interim test is the FY2026 cash flow statement: whether full-year CFO less unreimbursed capex lands near my $200M assumption once the shutdown receivable is fully unwound.
- Already visible today: Partly, and it cuts toward the bull. Q3 and Q4 2026 adjusted EBITDA are guided at $140-145M and $137-142M against Q2's $142.0M, which is a PLATEAU rather than continued sequential growth - that supports my step framing. But the company says both facilities reach 'normalized operations and earnings contribution in early 2027', so the guided Q4 deliberately excludes the contribution the bull case is buying. I cannot separate those two readings from the 2026 guidance alone, which is the main reason conviction is low rather than medium.
- Left unresolved: How much of the $233.0M FY2025 receivable build is genuinely shutdown-related versus ordinary growth in receivables on revenue that rose from $2.42B to $2.63B. I used the full amount in one scenario and $180M in another; the base moves about $18M between them, which is not decisive, but I could not pin it. I also could not obtain the split of the $197.5M of FY2025 capex between reimbursable activation spend and ordinary maintenance.

**Key risks.** the entire thesis is a bet on federal detention policy and appropriations, which no cash-flow model prices; the recorded +28.2% implied growth and 9th-percentile rank are built on a cash-flow year created by a government shutdown and by customer-reimbursed capex; the true requirement is nearer +16.5%; my base is hand-built from a normalised FY2025 - at the engine's own base the name looks far more expensive and at the FY2026 frame far less; adjusted EBITDA is guided to plateau at $137-145M a quarter through Q4 2026, so the 2027 step has to arrive to justify the price
**Watch for.** any announcement of a third or fourth facility activation at Rivers-like economics - that is the fact that would overturn this verdict; FY2026 full-year CFO less unreimbursed capex, the first clean read on the base; 2027 guidance, and whether Rivers and Big Horn contribute the full ~$80M+ each as promised
