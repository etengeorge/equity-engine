**The case for the price.** Terex is a different company than it was a year ago and the market is pricing the new one. It closed the $9B REV Group merger on 2026-02-02, issuing 47.9 million shares and paying $426M of cash, and now runs roughly $8 billion of net sales across Environmental Solutions, Materials Processing, Specialty Vehicles and Aerials. Q2 2026 delivered Adjusted EBITDA of $269M at a 12.0% margin, up 10.7% on a pro forma basis, with free cash flow of $101M and revenue growth in every segment. Management raised full-year guidance to $7.9-8.2B of sales, $960M-$1.0B of Adjusted EBITDA and $4.70-5.10 of adjusted EPS. At $59.29 the stock is on about 12x adjusted EPS and 9.3x Adjusted EBITDA — an ordinary industrial multiple for a business that has just diversified away from pure aerial-platform cyclicality into fire apparatus, ambulances and waste, with synergies still to come. That is a coherent case for the price.

**What changed.** Everything that matters. The REV Group merger closed 2026-02-02 (announced 2025-10-29); the pro forma 8-K filed 2026-07-31 shows combined FY2025 net sales of $7,885M against Terex standalone $5,421M, and combined interest expense of $202M against $177M standalone. Q2 2026 results (2026-07-30) raised 2026 guidance: sales $7.9-8.2B (+7% pro forma), Adjusted EBITDA $960M-$1.0B (+14.5% pro forma), adjusted EPS $4.70-5.10, free cash flow $300-350M, full-year average share count ~110M rising to ~114M in Q3-Q4. Adjusted EPS included roughly $8M of IEEPA tariff refunds net of a one-time customs accrual — the same 2026 cohort event that showed up on ANF and CRI in a previous session.

**Base case.** The combined business is less cyclical than old Terex but it is still an equipment manufacturer with a $2.0B Aerials segment guided down low-double-digits and a Specialty Vehicles book whose demand comes from municipal budgets. Management guides 2026 free cash flow of $300-350M; adding back after-tax interest of roughly $158M on $202M of pro forma interest gives an unlevered FCFF base near $483M, which is what I have used. Growing that at mid-single digits assumes the merger synergies land and end markets grow roughly with nominal GDP plus a point of share. I am below the naive +12.0% revenue-CAGR baseline because that CAGR was produced by buying ESG and REV, not by the underlying businesses growing — you cannot apply an acquisition-driven revenue history to the cash flow of the company that resulted from the acquisitions.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The counter is that my corrected base case makes TEX look expensive, and I should follow that to a 'rich' verdict rather than retreating to no_edge. On an FCFF base of $483M growing 4.5% at a 10.6% WACC, the two-stage model gives an enterprise value near $6.4B against an actual $9.1B — implying roughly $35 per share against $59.29, or -40%. That is not a small disagreement, and if the DCF is right the stock is materially overvalued.
- What would prove it: Whether the combined company converts its guided Adjusted EBITDA into cash. $960M-$1.0B of Adjusted EBITDA producing only $300-350M of free cash flow is a 33% conversion rate; if that is structural — because purchase-accounting amortisation of $215M a year reflects real economic wear on acquired intangibles — the DCF is right and the adjusted P/E is a fiction. If conversion improves toward 50% as integration costs roll off, the multiples are right.
- Already visible today: Partly. The pro forma FY2025 statement is the uncomfortable evidence: combined GAAP net income of just $58M and diluted EPS of $0.50, against adjusted EPS guidance of $4.70-5.10 for 2026. That is a very large gap between adjusted and GAAP earnings, and $215M a year of purchase-intangible amortisation is most of it. On the other side, Q2 free cash flow of $101M was up $23M year over year and the CFO explicitly attributed it to 'the lower capital intensity of our new portfolio', with quarterly capex of only $27M — which supports better conversion ahead.
- Left unresolved: I could not settle the cash-conversion question, and it is what separates -40% from fairly valued. The DCF and the multiples disagree by more than a factor of one and a half on this name, and per the standing lesson from JXN, when one defensible input choice moves the answer that far the output is not a valuation. That is why this is no_edge rather than rich: I do not have an edge, I have two methods that disagree.

**Key risks.** Aerials segment guided low-double-digit down; the cyclical exposure did not disappear with diversification; $2.69B of debt against $407M cash while integrating a transformational merger; Adjusted EPS of $4.70-5.10 sits against pro forma GAAP EPS of $0.50 for FY2025 — $215M a year of purchase-intangible amortisation is the bridge, and whether to expense it is the whole valuation argument; Integration and synergy execution risk on a deal that added 42% to the share count
**Watch for.** Full-year free cash flow against the $300-350M guide — the single number that resolves the DCF-versus-multiples disagreement; Aerials segment orders and backlog for evidence the cycle has troughed; Realised synergy disclosure against plan; Net leverage trajectory through 2027

**Data quality.** Enterprise value and share count are CORRECT and I verified both against the 10-Q — total debt $2,687M less cash $407M gives net debt of $2,280M, which with a $6.8B market cap reproduces the model's $9.1B enterprise value, and the 114.3M share count as of 2026-07-28 is the post-merger figure matching the 114.2M pro forma diluted count. The defect is the other side of the ratio. The normalised FCFF base of $413.8M is the mean of CFO less capex for FY2023-FY2025, all of which are STANDALONE Terex years — REV closed 2026-02-02, so none of the merger is in the cash-flow history, while all of it is in the enterprise value. The reverse DCF is therefore asking what growth in pre-merger cash flow justifies a post-merger enterprise value, and its answer of +17.2% is not a number about this company. This is the acquisition-inside-the-window failure named in LESSONS, and nothing in the brief flagged it; the tell was an EX-99.2 titled 'REV GROUP, INC. Q1 2026 FINANCIALS' in the exhibit list, which the newly-fixed exhibit lookup surfaced. I have set fcff_base_override to $483M, derived from management's own 2026 free cash flow guidance of $300-350M plus after-tax interest on $202M of pro forma interest expense; that is an estimate built on guidance, not a filed figure, and should be read as such. Note also that the naive +12.0% baseline is acquisition-driven revenue growth and carries no information about this business.
