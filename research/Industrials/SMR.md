## 2026-11-20 — NO_MODEL (conviction: high)

- **Verdict:** no_model · price $3.81
- **Not repriced:** not a repriceable fcff name or no growth supplied

**The case for the price.** At $3.81 — down 82.3% in a year and 59.5% in three months, at a 52-week low — the market is pricing NuScale as an option that has moved a long way out of the money rather than as a going concern. The remaining bull case rests on two things that are real: NuScale holds the only small modular reactor design certified by the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, which is a regulatory moat no competitor can buy quickly; and the company has roughly $933M of cash and short-term investments after raising about $2.05bn of equity through its 2025 and 2026 at-the-market programmes, so it can fund several more years of development without a financing emergency. Against a true market capitalisation of about $1.64bn and stockholders' equity of $2.02bn, the shares trade below book — which is what the market does to a developer whose first commercial deployment keeps moving right.

**What changed.** The 63-day collapse is identified and dated: Canada's first SMR project is running late, which took the stock down 22% in the week to 2026-11-18 to a 52-week low, and the coverage that followed frames the question as liquidity against delays. Operationally the quarter brought incremental process news rather than a commercial order — NuScale implemented Everstar's Gordian AI platform to accelerate commercial reactor deployment (2026-11-18). The financial direction is the thing that matters and it is in the 10-Q: operating cash outflow was $459.6M in the newest fiscal year against $108.7M and $183.3M in the two prior years, so the burn has roughly quadrupled, and EBIT is negative $689.6M. The company has funded that by issuing roughly 120.7 million Class A shares across three tranches disclosed in the Q3 10-Q — 89,727,165 shares for $1,000.0M gross, 17,764,936 for $577.6M gross and 13,216,809 for $475.2M gross, about $2.05bn in total.

**Base case.** No base case is admissible, and no fair value either. There is no positive free-cash-flow year in the window: operating cash flow is negative in all three years and accelerating away from zero, revenue is $31.5M against an EBIT of negative $689.6M, `dep_amort_series` is empty and capital expenditure is trivial, so `normalized_fcff` cannot be computed and the engine correctly returned `model_failed`. A discounted cash flow cannot value a pre-commercial reactor developer whose first plant is not built, and the multiples path is disqualified separately (see the data-quality note) because every per-share and per-dollar figure on the brief divides by a share count that is 1,662 days stale and 45% too low. This is the honest form of refusal: 'a DCF cannot value this' is a different claim from 'this is worthless', and I am making only the first.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: That an 82% fall in a company with the only NRC-certified SMR design, $933M of liquidity and shares below book value is where asymmetric returns come from, and that refusing to put a number on it is refusing to do the job. The specific version: stockholders' equity is $2.02bn against a true market capitalisation of $1.64bn, so the market is valuing the certified design, the order book and the Romanian and other project positions at less than nothing.
- What would prove it: A firm commercial order with committed financing and milestone payments, or a credible statement of the capital still required to reach first power — which would let a buyer judge whether the $933M is a runway or a down payment.
- Already visible today: No, and the price-to-book argument does not survive inspection. Of the $2.02bn of equity, about $933M is cash and short-term investments; the balance is largely capitalised project and development assets on a company that has not delivered a commercial reactor, and those carry nothing like the certainty of cash. More importantly the composition of the discount is the tell: the burn is $459.6M a year and quadrupling, so book value is being consumed at roughly 23% a year, and the market is marking that forward rather than mispricing the present. I also cannot test the book-value claim properly, because the engine's own equity series runs $93.5M, $618.7M, $1,168.8M against an `equity_now` of $2,024.1M — equity has grown 21x across the window purely through issuance, so there is no stable denominator to anchor on. And the counter leans on a share count that is wrong: the real count is 429.9 million, not 236.8 million, so the market capitalisation is $1.64bn rather than $902M and the discount to book is 19%, not 55%. A gap that shrinks by two thirds when the denominator is corrected is not a thesis. The counter fails, and it fails on a number that is on the cover page of the 10-Q.
- Left unresolved: The capital required to reach first commercial power, which no filing quantifies and which determines whether the remaining cash is sufficient. The status and economics of the Romanian RoPower project and the Canadian project whose delay caused the fall — I could identify the delay from the news feed but not its contractual or financial consequence. And the carrying basis of the roughly $1.1bn of non-cash assets inside stockholders' equity, which I did not reconcile line by line.
