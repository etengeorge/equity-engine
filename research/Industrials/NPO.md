**The case for the price.** EnPro has stopped being an industrial conglomerate and is being repriced as a semiconductor and advanced-materials compounder. Advanced Surface Technologies grew sales 21.8% organically in Q2 on leading-edge precision cleaning and semiconductor capital equipment demand; total sales rose 17.6% to $338.8M; adjusted EBITDA rose 22.2% to $86.9M at a 25.6% margin (+90bp); and management RAISED full-year 2026 guidance to 14-16% revenue growth, $330-340M of adjusted EBITDA and $9.30-9.80 of adjusted EPS. Sealing Technologies underneath it is a genuinely high-quality aftermarket business with pricing power and low cyclicality. The market pays 20x forward EBITDA because it is underwriting AST becoming the majority of the earnings mix at semiconductor-equipment growth rates and semiconductor-equipment multiples, with Sealing's cash flow funding it. That is a coherent thesis, and the Q2 print is evidence for it, not against it.

**What changed.** Q2 2026, reported 2026-08-04 (8-K item 2.02, EX-99.1). Sales $338.8M, +17.6% total and +10.9% organic. Adjusted EBITDA $86.9M versus $71.1M, margin 25.6% versus 24.7%. Advanced Surface Technologies sales +21.8% ORGANIC on leading-edge precision cleaning and semiconductor capital equipment. Sealing Technologies sales +15.3%, or +5.0% excluding FX and the AlpHa Measurement Solutions and Overlook Industries acquisitions completed in Q4 2025 — so most of Sealing's headline growth was bought. H1 free cash flow $61.4M on $90.9M of operating cash flow after $29.5M of capex, versus $52.8M and $73.2M last year. Full-year 2026 guidance RAISED to revenue growth of 14-16%, adjusted EBITDA $330-340M, adjusted EPS $9.30-9.80. No company-specific news in the store for 90 days; the sector feed carries a soft August ISM print and 'why industrial stocks could keep losing steam', which is consistent with the stock's -10.0% 21-day move.

**Base case.** I underwrite 9% five-year FCFF growth. The mix argument is real: if AST is roughly 40% of EBITDA growing at 15-20% and Sealing is 60% growing at 4-5% organically, blended EBITDA growth is 9-11% before any margin expansion, and EnPro converts EBITDA to free cash flow at a reasonable rate (H1: $61.4M of FCF against $163.3M of adjusted EBITDA, seasonally understated). I decline to extrapolate the headline 14-16% because guidance itself discloses that a large part of it is the AlpHa and Overlook acquisitions annualising, and organic Sealing growth is 5.0%. I also note the five-year revenue CAGR is 1.3% — this company has not compounded before, and the semiconductor cycle it is now levered to is the most violent capex cycle in industrials. 9% is my estimate of what a good outcome looks like without assuming the up-cycle never ends.

**Devil's advocate.**
- Strongest counter: The strongest counter is that my DCF is the wrong instrument, not that my growth number is too low. A two-stage FCFF model with a 2% terminal growth rate and a 10.8% WACC systematically undervalues a business whose value is in a long compounding runway — it forces almost all of the value into an explicit five-year window and then caps the rest at inflation. The market is not underwriting 31% FCFF growth for five years; it is underwriting a durable 10-12% compounder that deserves a 20x terminal EBITDA multiple, and those are very different statements that this model cannot distinguish. And the bull evidence is partly VISIBLE, not hypothetical: AST grew 21.8% organically last quarter, margins expanded 90bp, and guidance was raised.
- What would prove it: Four to six quarters of AST organic growth sustained above 20% with total adjusted EBITDA margin above 27%, and Sealing organic growth holding at 5%+ through a soft industrial tape. That would establish the mix shift as durable rather than cyclical.
- Already visible today: Yes, in part — and this is the strongest evidence against my own verdict. Q2 AST organic growth of 21.8%, margin +90bp, guidance raised twice. I take it seriously.
- Left unresolved: I cannot resolve whether the correct terminal multiple for EnPro is an industrial one or a semiconductor one, and that single choice moves the fair value more than any growth assumption I could make. What I CAN say is that the gap does not close under the bull case within this model's frame: at 15% growth the fair value is $151, at 20% it is $188, and only at 31.3% does it reach $295. Even granting AST 20% growth for five years and Sealing 4%, blended FCFF growth lands well short of 31%. So the price requires either a growth rate I cannot construct from the segment arithmetic, or a terminal multiple this model does not offer. I record rich on that basis, with the terminal-multiple objection unresolved and stated.

**Key risks.** Semiconductor capital-equipment cycle: AST is levered to fab tool builds, not installed base; A large share of headline 2026 growth is the AlpHa and Overlook acquisitions annualising, not organic; Five-year revenue CAGR is 1.3% — the compounding record is short; At 20x forward EBITDA against an Industrials cohort median of 14.3x, multiple compression is the dominant risk to the equity, not earnings; Legacy asbestos and environmental liabilities in the sealing business
**Watch for.** AST organic growth falling below the high teens in any quarter — the first sign the mix thesis is cyclical; Full-year adjusted EBITDA landing outside the raised $330-340M range; Any further acquisition that inflates headline growth while diluting organic disclosure
