 Report of Independent Auditors 

To the Management of Marathon Petroleum Corporation 

 Opinion 
 
We have audited the accompanying combined
financial statements of The Mandan and Saint Paul Park Refining Business of Marathon Petroleum Corporation (the “Business”), which comprise the combined balance sheets as of December 31, 2025 and 2024, and the related combined
statements of income (loss), of changes in net parent investment and of cash flows for the years then ended, including the related notes (collectively referred to as the “combined financial statements”). 

In our opinion, the accompanying combined financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position of the Business as of
December 31, 2025 and 2024, and the results of its operations and its cash flows for the years then ended in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America. 

 Basis for Opinion 
 
We conducted our audit in
accordance with auditing standards generally accepted in the United States of America (US GAAS). Our responsibilities under those standards are further described in the Auditors’ Responsibilities for the Audit of the Combined Financial
Statements section of our report. We are required to be independent of the Business and to meet our other ethical responsibilities, in accordance with the relevant ethical requirements relating to our audit. We believe that the audit evidence we
have obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for our audit opinion. 
 
 Responsibilities of Management for the Combined Financial
Statements 
 
Management is responsible for the preparation and fair presentation of the combined financial statements in accordance with accounting
principles generally accepted in the United States of America, and for the design, implementation, and maintenance of internal control relevant to the preparation and fair presentation of combined financial statements that are free from material
misstatement, whether due to fraud or error. 
 
In preparing the combined financial statements, management is required to evaluate whether there are
conditions or events, considered in the aggregate, that raise substantial doubt about the Business’s ability to continue as a going concern for one year after the date the combined financial statements are available to be issued. 

 Auditors’ Responsibilities for the Audit of the Combined Financial Statements 

Our objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the combined financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due
to fraud or error, and to issue an auditors’ report that includes our opinion. Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not absolute assurance and therefore is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with US GAAS
will always detect a material misstatement when it exists. The risk of not detecting a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error, as fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions,
misrepresentations, or the override of internal control. Misstatements are considered material if there is a substantial likelihood that, individually or in the aggregate, they would influence the judgment made by a reasonable user based on the
combined financial statements.

 Notes to Combined Financial Statements 

 1. Description of the Business and Basis of Presentation 

 Description of the Business 
 
The accompanying Combined
Financial Statements and notes present the Combined Statements of Income (Loss), Balance Sheets, Cash Flows, and Changes in Net Parent Investment of the Mandan and Saint Paul Park (“SPP”) refineries and accompanying refining logistics
assets (“the Business,” “we,” or “our”) of Marathon Petroleum Corporation (“MPC” or “Parent”) and its midstream subsidiary MPLX LP (“MPLX”). 

The Business operates as crude oil refineries within MPC’s mid-continent refining system. SPP, located in St.
Paul Park, Minnesota, has a crude oil refining capacity of approximately 105,000 barrels per calendar day (“bpcd”) and processes sweet and heavy sour crude oils into gasoline, distillates, asphalt, propane, heavy fuel oil, natural gas
liquids (“NGLs”), and petrochemicals, with distribution by truck, rail, and barge. The Mandan Refinery, located in Mandan, North Dakota, has a crude oil refining capacity of approximately 72,000 bpcd and processes primarily sweet
domestic crude oil from North Dakota into gasoline, distillates, propane, heavy fuel oil, NGLs, and petrochemicals, with distribution by truck and rail. The combined crude oil refining capacity is approximately 177,000 bpcd. 

The Business also owns a 17% interest in the Minnesota Pipe Line Company (“MPL”) along with Flint Hills Resources and Trof, Inc. MPL, transports
crude oil to the Business’ SPP and Flint Hills Resources’ Pine Bend refineries. 
 
 Basis of Presentation 

These Combined Financial Statements have been derived from the MPC consolidated financial statements and accounting records of MPC. These Combined Financial
Statements include all majority-owned and controlled subsidiaries and reflect the combined historical results of operations, financial position and cash flows of the Business for the periods presented as historically managed within MPC in conformity
with generally accepted accounting principles in the United States (“GAAP”). The Combined Financial Statements may not be indicative of the Business’ future performance and do not necessarily reflect what the financial position,
results of operations and cash flows would have been had it operated as an independent company during the periods presented. 
 
Transactions among
components included within the Business have been eliminated and transactions with MPC, MPLX, and other affiliates outside of the Business have been retained as related party transactions in these Combined Financial Statements. Related party
transactions between the Business and Parent have been settled through Net parent investment with the net effect reflected in the Combined Statements of Cash Flows as a financing activity and in the Combined Balance Sheets as Net parent investment.
Related party transactions between the Business and MPLX historically settled in cash have been reflected in the Combined Balance Sheets as Related party receivables and payables with the net effect reflected in the Combined Statements of Cash Flows
as operating activities. See Note 4 for additional information. The Business’ investments in which the Business exercises significant influence but does not control and does not have a controlling financial interest are accounted for using the
equity method. 
 
Historically, MPC provided certain corporate functions to the Business and costs associated with these functions were allocated to the
Business. These functions include, but are not limited to, executive management, legal, human resources, treasury, finance, accounting, internal audit, information technology, and the related benefit costs associated with such functions, such as
stock-based compensation. The costs of such services were allocated to the Business based on direct usage when identifiable, with the remainder allocated on a pro rata basis using an applicable measure of income from operations, total assets,
headcount, or other relative drivers applied to the relevant cost pools. The charges for these functions are deemed settled in cash by the Business to MPC in the period in which the costs were recorded within Selling, general and administrative
expenses and Cost of revenues in the Combined Statements of Income (Loss). Management believes the basis on which the expenses have been allocated is a reasonable reflection of the utilization of services provided to, or the benefit received by, the
Business during the periods presented; however, they may not be indicative of the actual expense that would have been incurred had the Business been operating as an independent company for the periods presented. Actual costs that may have been
incurred if the Business had been an independent company could materially differ from the amounts allocated as the costs would depend on a number of factors, including 

9 

the organizational structure, whether functions were outsourced or performed by employees, and strategic
decisions made in areas such as information technology and infrastructure. Management does not believe, however, that it is practicable to estimate what these expenses would have been for the periods presented had the Business operated as an
independent company, including any expenses associated with obtaining any of these services from unaffiliated entities. 
 
MPC utilizes a centralized
treasury management function for financing its operations, with cash from MPC’s operating companies, including the Business, swept to shared MPC cash pooling accounts. The cash and cash equivalents held by MPC at the corporate level are not
specifically identifiable to the Business and therefore have not been reflected in the Business’ Combined Balance Sheets. Cash transfers between the Parent and the Business are accounted for through Net parent investment. 

The Combined Financial Statements include certain assets and liabilities that have historically been held at the Parent corporate level but are specifically
identifiable or otherwise attributable to the Business. MPC’s third-party long-term debt and the related interest expense have not been allocated to the Business for any of the periods presented as the Business was not the legal obligor of
such debt and the MPC borrowings were not directly attributable to the Business. 
 
Current and deferred income taxes have been determined based on the
stand-alone results of the Business; however, because the Business filed as part of MPC’s tax group in certain jurisdictions, the Business’ actual tax balances may differ from those reported. The Business’ portion of income tax
expense is deemed to have been settled in the period the related tax expense was recorded.

 Summary of Principal Accounting Policies 

 Use of Estimates 
 
The preparation of financial
statements in accordance with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities as of the date of the financial statements
and the reported amounts of revenues and expenses during the respective reporting periods. Actual results could differ materially from those estimates. Estimates are subject to uncertainties due to the levels of subjectivity and judgment necessary
to account for highly uncertain matters or the susceptibility of such matters to change and affect items such as valuing identified intangible assets; evaluating impairments of long-lived assets and equity investments; establishing estimated useful
lives for long-lived assets; estimating revenues, expense accruals and capital expenditures; valuing asset retirement obligations; recognizing equity-based compensation expense; and determining liabilities, if any, for environmental and legal
contingencies. 
 
 Revenue Recognition 
 
Revenue
is measured based on consideration specified in a contract with a customer. The Business recognizes revenue when it satisfies a performance obligation by transferring control over a product or providing services to a customer. 

The vast majority of the Business’ contracts contain pricing that is based on the market price for the product at the time of delivery. The
Business’ obligations to deliver product volumes are typically satisfied and revenue is recognized when control of the product transfers to its customers. Concurrent with the transfer of control, the Business typically receives the right to
payment for the delivered product, the customer accepts the product, and the customer has significant risks and rewards of ownership of the product. Payment terms require customers to pay shortly after delivery and do not contain significant
financing components. 
 
Revenues from services are recognized over time when the performance obligation is satisfied as services are provided in a series.
The Business has elected to use the output measure of progress to recognize revenue based on the units delivered, processed or transported. 

10 

Taxes collected from customers and remitted to the appropriate taxing authority are excluded from revenue.
See Note 10 for further information on Revenue. 
 
 Revenue and Expense Accruals 

The Business routinely makes accruals based on estimates for both revenues and expenses due to the timing of compiling billing information, receiving certain
third-party information and reconciling the Business’ records with those of third parties. The delayed information from third parties includes, among other things, actual volumes purchased, transported or sold, invoices for purchases, and
other operating expenses. The Business makes accruals to reflect estimates for these items based on its internal records and information from third parties. Estimated accruals are adjusted when actual information is received from third parties and
the Business’ internal records have been reconciled. 
 
 Cost Classifications 

Cost of revenues consists of the cost of crude oil, other feedstocks, purchased refined products, the related in-bound 
freight and transportation costs, direct costs of labor, maintenance and services, utilities, property taxes, environmental compliance costs and other direct operating costs incurred in connection with the Business’ refining operations and
logistics assets. These expenses exclude depreciation related to refining and logistics assets that are integral to the refinery production process, which is presented separately as Depreciation and amortization expense as a component of Cost and
expenses on the Business’ Combined Statements of Income (Loss). 
 
 Accounts Receivables and Allowance for Expected Credit Loss 

Receivables primarily consist of customer accounts receivable, which are recorded at the invoiced amount and generally do not bear interest. Allowances for
expected credit losses are estimated and recognized at each reporting period and are recorded as provision for credit losses. The allowance for expected credit loss is the best estimate of the amount of expected credit losses over the contractual
life of the receivables in customer accounts receivable. The Business reviews the allowance quarterly and past-due balances over 150 days are reviewed individually for collectability. Balances that remain
outstanding after reasonable collection efforts have been unsuccessful are written off against the allowance when deemed uncollectible and a credit to accounts receivable. The Business does not have an allowance for expected credit loss as of
December 31, 2025 or December 31, 2024. 
 
 Leases 

Contracts with a term greater than one year that convey the right to direct the use of and obtain substantially all of the economic benefit of an asset are
accounted for as right-of-use (“ROU”) assets and lease liabilities. 

ROU asset and lease liability balances are recorded at the commencement date at present value of the fixed lease payments using a secured incremental
borrowing rate with a maturity similar to the lease term because our leases do not provide implicit rates. The Business has elected to include both lease and non-lease components in the present value of the
lease payments for all lessee asset classes with the exception of third-party contractor service and equipment leases. The lease component of the payment for the equipment asset classes is determined using a relative standalone selling price.
Operating lease expense is recognized on a straight-line basis over the lease term. 
 
Other noncurrent assets include right-of-use assets of $5 million and $6 million as of December 31, 2025 and 2024, respectively. Other current liabilities include current operating lease liabilities of $2 million and
$2 million, and other long-term liabilities include noncurrent operating lease liabilities of $3 million and $4 million, as of December 31, 2025 and 2024, respectively. 

 Inventories 
 
Inventories are carried at the lower
of cost or net realizable value. Cost of inventories is determined primarily under the FIFO method. The method differs to that of the parent who uses LIFO. Management believes that this method is preferable as it improves comparability with a
majority of the Business’ small and midsize refinery peers. Additionally, utilizing FIFO also reflects a more appropriate valuation of the assets as the users of the financial statements place a greater emphasis on the balance sheet and FIFO
results in an inventory value that is more indicative of current market prices compared to LIFO. 

11 

No prior periods have previously been presented, and all periods reflect the application of FIFO. Because
LIFO is applied at a consolidated level by MPC, and not at each location, it is not possible to determine what the amount of inventory would be on a LIFO basis for the combined entities and operations without undue cost and effort. See Note 6 for
further information. 
 
 Investments 
 
The
Business holds an investment in an equity method investee. An equity investment is an investment in which the Business exercises significant influence but does not control and is not the primary beneficiary, are accounted for using the equity method
and are reported in equity method investment on the accompanying Combined Balance Sheets. Differences in the basis of the investment and the net asset value of the investee, if any, are amortized into net income over the remaining useful lives of
the underlying assets and liabilities, except for the excess related to goodwill. The equity method investment is evaluated for impairment whenever changes in the facts and circumstances indicate an other than temporary loss in value has occurred.
When the loss is deemed to be other than temporary, the carrying value of the equity method investment is written down to fair value. See Note 5 for further information on equity method investment held by the Business. 

An equity investment without a readily determinable fair value includes ownership rights that do not provide the Business with control or significant
influence. An equity investment without a readily determinable fair value is recorded at cost and adjusted for subsequent observable price changes as of the date that an observable transaction takes place. 

Equity securities without a readily determinable fair value are measured at cost minus impairment and are reported within Other noncurrent assets on the
Combined Balance Sheets and approximated $19 million and $19 million as of December 31, 2025 and 2024, respectively. 
 
 Property, Plant
and Equipment 
 
Property, plant and equipment are recorded at cost and depreciated on a straight-line basis over the estimated useful lives of the
assets, generally 4 to 40 years for buildings, refinery, machinery and other equipment, and 4 to 30 years for other fixed assets. Such assets are reviewed for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount
of an asset group may not be recoverable. If the sum of the expected undiscounted future cash flows from the use of the asset group and its eventual disposition is less than the carrying amount of the asset group, an impairment assessment is
performed and the excess of the book value over the fair value of the asset group is recorded as an impairment loss. 
 
When items of property, plant and
equipment are sold or otherwise disposed of, any gains or losses are reported in net income. Gains on the disposal of property, plant and equipment are recognized when earned, which is generally at the time of closing. If a loss on disposal is
expected, such losses are recognized when the assets are classified as held for sale. 
 
Interest expense is capitalized for qualifying assets under
construction. Capitalized interest costs are included in property, plant and equipment and are depreciated over the useful life of the related asset. See Note 7 for further information on property, plant and equipment. 

 Intangibles 
 
Amortization of intangibles with
definite lives is calculated using the straight-line method, which is reflective of the benefit pattern in which the estimated economic benefit is expected to be received over the estimated useful life of the intangible asset. Intangibles subject to
amortization are reviewed for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of the intangible may not be recoverable. If the sum of the expected undiscounted future cash flows related to the asset is less
than the carrying amount of the asset, an impairment loss is recognized based on the fair value of the asset. See Note 8 for additional disclosures about our intangible assets.

 Major Maintenance Activities 

Costs for planned turnaround and other major maintenance activities are expensed in the period incurred. These types of costs include contractor repair
services, materials and supplies, equipment rentals and our labor costs. 
 
 Pension and Other Postretirement Benefits 

 MPC-sponsored defined benefit plans 

Many of the Business’ employees participate in defined benefit pension plans administered and sponsored by MPC. The Business does not record assets or
liabilities to recognize the funded status of the plan because the Combined Financial Statements reflect the cost for these plans as if they were multi-employer plans. Costs are allocated to the Business. These costs reflect the Business’
employees’ proportionate share of total cost in MPC plans in which they participate as well as an allocation of MPC’s corporate cost for these plans. The Business was allocated service costs of $8 million and $8 million for the
years ended December 31, 2025 and 2024, respectively, recorded within Cost of revenues and Selling, general and administrative expenses in the Combined Statements of Income (Loss). 

 Defined contribution plans 
 
The Business also contributes
to a defined contribution plan for eligible employees. Contributions to this plan totaled $7 million and $6 million in 2025 and 2024, respectively. 

 Environmental Costs 
 
Environmental expenditures
for additional equipment that mitigates or prevents future contamination or improves environmental safety or efficiency of the existing assets are capitalized. The Business recognizes remediation costs and penalties when the responsibility to
remediate is probable and the amount of associated costs can be reasonably estimated. The timing of remediation accruals coincides with the completion of a feasibility study or the commitment to a formal plan of action. Remediation liabilities are
accrued based on estimates of known environmental exposure and are discounted when the estimated amounts are reasonably fixed and determinable. If recoveries of remediation costs from third parties are probable, a receivable is recorded and is
discounted when the estimated amount is reasonably fixed and determinable. 
 
 Asset Retirement Obligations 

The fair value of asset retirement obligations is recognized in the period in which the obligations are incurred if a reasonable estimate of fair value can be
made. The majority of the recognized asset retirement liability relates to conditional asset retirement obligations for removal and disposal of fire-retardant material from certain refining facilities. The remaining recognized asset retirement
liability relates to other refining assets, certain pipelines and processing facilities and other related pipeline assets. The fair values recorded for such obligations are based on the most probable current cost projections. See Note 12 for further
information on asset retirement obligations. 
 
 Income Taxes 

Deferred tax assets and liabilities are recognized for the estimated future tax consequences attributable to differences between the financial statement
carrying amounts of assets and liabilities and their tax bases. Deferred tax assets are recorded when it is more likely than not that they will be realized. The realization of deferred tax assets is assessed periodically based on several factors,
primarily our expectation to generate sufficient future taxable income. See Note 9 for further information on income taxes. 
 
 Accrued Taxes

The Business is subject to federal, state, or local excise taxes and other transactional taxes based on the sale of products. The liabilities for
such taxes are recognized in the period in which the related taxable transaction occurs and are included in Accrued taxes on the Combined Balance Sheets until remitted to the applicable taxing authority.

 Share-Based Compensation 

The fair value of restricted stock awards granted to our employees is determined based on the fair market value of our common stock on the date of grant. The
fair value of performance awards granted to our employees is determined using a Monte Carlo valuation model, which is updated quarterly, with appropriate mark-to-market 
adjustments made. 
 
Our share-based compensation expense is recognized based on management’s estimate of the awards that are expected to vest, using
the straight-line attribution method for all service-based awards with a graded vesting feature. Awards expected to vest are estimated using the historical data of our own employees. If actual forfeiture results are different than expected,
adjustments to recognized compensation expense may be required in future periods. Unearned share-based compensation is charged to equity when restricted stock awards are granted. Compensation expense is recognized over the requisite service period
and is adjusted if conditions of the restricted stock award are not met. See Note 11 for further information on share-based compensation. 

 Concentrations of Risk 
 
The Business has a
concentration of transactions with customers in the same industry. For the periods ended December 31, 2025 and December 31, 2024, one customer accounted for approximately 12% and 10%, respectively, of the Business’ total revenues and
other income. The remaining customers account for less than 10% of the Business’ total revenues and other income. 
 
 Environmental Credits and
Obligations 
 
In order to comply with certain regulations, specifically the Renewable Fuel Standards (“RFS”) requirements implemented by
the Environmental Protection Agency (“EPA”) and the cap-and-trade emission reduction program and low carbon fuel standard implemented by state programs, the
Business is required to reduce its emissions, blend certain levels of biofuels or obtain allowances or credits to offset the obligations created by the Business’ operations. In regard to each program, the Business records an asset to
Environmental credits on the Combined Balance Sheets, for allowances or credits owned in excess of our anticipated current period compliance requirements. The asset value is based on the product of the excess allowances or credits as of the balance
sheet date, if any, and the weighted average cost of those allowances or credits. The Business did not have environmental credits as of December 31, 2025 or December 31, 2024. The Business records a liability to Environment obligations on
the Combined Balance Sheets, when there are deficient allowances or credits based on the product of the deficient amount as of the balance sheet date, if any, and either the fixed contract price or the market price of the allowances or credits as of
the balance sheet date. The cost of allowances or credits used for compliance is reflected in Cost of revenues in the Combined Statements of Income (Loss). Any gains or losses on the sale or expiration of allowances or credits are classified as
Other income on the Combined Statements of Income (Loss). The Business did not sell allowances or credits for the twelve months ended December 31, 2025 or December 31, 2024. 

Environmental obligations on the Combined Balance Sheets approximated $46 million and $114 million as of December 31, 2025 and 2024,
respectively. The environmental obligation value in each period is primarily impacted by the timing of receipt of exemption credits, the settlement timing of obligations and changes in renewable fuel standards requirements. The decrease was
primarily due to the receipt of the Mandan small refinery exemption (SRE) credit for $114 million which was offset by an increase in obligations. The EPA approved the SRE for the 2023 and 2024 compliance periods in August 2025. The Business
recognized the $114 million compliance credit in Cost of revenues for the year ended December 31, 2025 and reduced the Environmental obligation by $114 million as of December 31, 2025 in the Combined Statements of Income (Loss) and
Combined Balance Sheets, respectively.

Business adopted this ASU and it did not have a material impact on our financial statements or disclosures.

in 2025 and applied the amendments on a retrospective basis. The enhanced income tax disclosures are presented in Note 9.

 Related Party Transactions 

 Transactions with MPC 
 
 Cash Management Agreement

The Business participates in MPC’s centralized treasury management function for financing its operations. MPC utilizes a centralized treasury
management function for financing its operations, with all cash from its operating companies, including the Business, swept to shared MPC cash pooling accounts. Our net cash balance with MPC is included in Net parent investment on the Combined
Balance Sheets, and the net cash activity is classified under financing activities on the Combined Statements of Cash Flows. 
 
 Trade Receivables Program

MPC participates in a trade receivables securitization program with a group of lenders. The trade receivables facility consists of certain of
MPC’s wholly owned subsidiaries (“Originators”), including the Business, selling or contributing on an on-going basis all of the trade receivables generated by them (the “Pool
Receivables”), together with all related security and interests in the proceeds thereof, without recourse, to another wholly owned, bankruptcy-remote special purpose entity (“BRSPE”), MPC Trade Receivables Company I LLC
(“TRC”), in exchange for a combination of cash, equity and/or borrowings under a subordinated note issued by TRC to one or more of the Originators. MPC accounts for transfers under these securitization arrangements as sales because full
title and ownership in the underlying receivables and control of the receivables is considered transferred and its assets are not available to MPC creditors. MPC wholly owns and therefore consolidates the BRSPE in its consolidated financial
statements. 
 
As the BRSPE is not owned or controlled by the Business, the activities of the BRSPE is not presented in the Combined Financial Statements of
the Business. The Business receivables included within MPC’s trade receivables securitization program, and transferred to the BRSPE, was $206 million and $202 million as of December 31, 2025 and 2024, respectively. These receivables
have been derecognized and removed from our Combined Balance Sheets at the date of transfer.

 Environmental Matters 

The Business is subject to federal, state and local laws and regulations relating to the environment. These laws generally provide for control of pollutants
released into the environment and require responsible parties to undertake remediation of hazardous waste disposal sites. Penalties may be imposed for non-compliance. 

As of December 31, 2025, and December 31, 2024, accrued liabilities for remediation totaled $5 million and $6 million, respectively. It is
not presently possible to estimate the ultimate amount of all remediation costs that might be incurred or the penalties, if any, that may be imposed. 
 
The
Business is involved in environmental enforcement matters arising in the ordinary course of business. While the outcome and impact to the Business cannot be predicted with certainty, management believes the resolution of these environmental matters
will not, individually or collectively, have a material adverse effect on its results of operations, financial position or cash flows. The pending environmental matters are: United States EPA v. St. Paul Park Refinery (date instituted
January 9, 2024) and Department of Justice Environment and Natural Resources Division (DOJ) v. Mandan Refinery ( date instituted March 5, 2025). At this stage, the ultimate outcome of these matters remains uncertain, and neither the
likelihood of an unfavorable outcome nor the ultimate liability, if any, can be determined.
