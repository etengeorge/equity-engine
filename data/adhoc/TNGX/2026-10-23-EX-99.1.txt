 Tango Presents Promising Results from Phase 1/2 Study of Vopimetostat and Daraxonrasib in Pancreatic Cancer and Provides Updates to
Development Strategy 
 
 82% objective response rate, median progression-free survival (PFS) not yet reached, and 9-month PFS-rate of 88% in patients with 2L/3L pancreatic cancer treated with vopimetostat plus daraxonrasib 

 Vopimetostat combinations with RAS(ON) inhibitors daraxonrasib or zoldonrasib continued to be generally well-tolerated 

 Registrational Phase 3 study of vopimetostat plus daraxonrasib in front-line patients with MTAP-deleted pancreatic cancer expected to be
initiated in 1H 2027 
 
 Investor webcast focused on PDAC to be held today at 7:30 a.m. ET 

BOSTON, October 23, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) — Tango Therapeutics, Inc. (NASDAQ: TNGX), a clinical-stage biotechnology company committed to delivering
breakthrough targeted therapies that extend and improve the lives of patients with cancer, today announced promising results from its ongoing Phase 1/2 study of vopimetostat, an investigational PRMT5 inhibitor with first- and best-in-class potential, in combination with Revolution Medicines’ RAS(ON) inhibitors in patients with MTAP-deleted and
 RAS-mutant metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The results are being presented at the European Society for Medical
Oncology (ESMO) 2026 Congress being held in Madrid, Spain on October 23-27, 2026. 
 
As of the data cutoff date of
August 20, 2026, updated data from the ongoing Phase 1/2 study demonstrated an 82% objective response rate (ORR), median PFS not yet reached, and a 9-month PFS rate of 88%, in 22 patients with 2L/3L MTAP-deleted/RAS-mutant PDAC who were efficacy-evaluable. The combination was generally well-tolerated in the 30 PDAC and NSCLC patients who received dose level 1. 

“It is encouraging to see that the clinical activity and tolerability observed in our initial June 2026 dataset has been confirmed with longer follow-up and in a larger patient population, affirming our belief that the response rate and durability observed with vopimetostat plus daraxonrasib surpass any published PDAC datasets, even in front-line
settings,” said Malte Peters, M.D., Chief Executive Officer and President of Tango Therapeutics. “Following discussions with the U.S. FDA, we are focused on initiating a planned registrational Phase 3 trial of vopimetostat with
daraxonrasib in front-line patients with MTAP-deleted pancreatic cancer in the first half of 2027. Given the data generated to date, we view PRMT5 inhibition as an emerging backbone in combination treatment strategies in PDAC and beyond. As a leader
in the development of PRMT5-targeted therapies, we are also encouraged by the potential combinations of vopimetostat with RAS inhibitors in MTAP wild-type tumors, which we believe could further expand the impact of PRMT5 inhibition across a broader
population of patients with PDAC.” 

“For patients with pancreatic cancer, a highly aggressive malignancy that often progresses despite
prior treatment, achieving durable disease control remains a significant challenge,” said Alexander Spira, M.D., Co-Director of the Virginia Cancer Specialists Research Institute, NEXT Oncology, an
Avacare business, and a principal investigator of the study. “In this updated analysis, the large majority of patients remained progression-free at nine months despite being heavily pretreated, with more than half receiving the combination as
third-line therapy. Achieving this level of durability with an oral, chemotherapy-free regimen is particularly meaningful for patients and highlights the potential of a vopimetostat combination to redefine the standard of care in a disease that has
been notoriously difficult to treat.” 
 
“The depth and durability of responses reported with the vopimetostat and daraxonrasib combination are
encouraging, particularly for patients who had already received multiple lines of treatment. We are excited by the potential of this biomarker-driven, targeted therapy approach and believe these results warrant rapid evaluation in a larger,
randomized Phase 3 trial,” said Anna Berkenblit, M.D., MMSc, Chief Scientific & Medical Officer of the Pancreatic Cancer Action Network (PanCAN). “The approval of daraxonrasib marked the beginning of a new era for the pancreatic
cancer community after a long wait, and we are looking forward to the next major advance that will translate into meaningful progress for patients quickly.” 

 Regulatory Update 
 
Following recent interactions with the
U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), Tango plans to initiate a global, randomized registrational trial evaluating the combination of vopimetostat with daraxonrasib in front-line patients with MTAP-deleted PDAC. The planned study will randomize
patients 1:1 to receive either vopimetostat (200 mg once daily) plus daraxonrasib (100 mg once daily) or standard-of-care chemotherapy. The dual primary endpoints of the
planned study are ORR and PFS, with overall survival as a key secondary endpoint. The Company anticipates initiating this study in the first half of 2027. 

In addition, the Company intends to discuss the possibility of pursuing approval of this combination in patients with second-line PDAC with regulatory
authorities.

 About Vopimetostat 

Vopimetostat is an investigational, potentially first- and best-in-class oral, once-daily, PRMT5 inhibitor that is being evaluated as a monotherapy and as a combination therapy in multiple indications. In ongoing clinical studies, vopimetostat has demonstrated a
favorable safety and tolerability profile and shown durable activity in multiple tumor types with MTAP (methylthioadenosine phosphorylase) deletion, including pancreatic cancer and lung cancer. 

 About Phase 1/2 Study of Vopimetostat Combinations with Daraxonrasib or Zoldonrasib 

The ongoing Phase 1/2 open-label, dose-escalation and expansion trial is evaluating vopimetostat in combination with either daraxonrasib or zoldonrasib in
patients with RAS-mutant, MTAP-deleted PDAC or NSCLC. The primary objective of the dose-escalation portion of the study was to determine the maximum tolerated dose of the combination regimens. Secondary
objectives include assessment of antitumor activity, pharmacokinetics, safety and tolerability. Patients are treated until disease progression and may continue treatment beyond progression if, in the opinion of the investigator, they are deriving
ongoing clinical benefit from therapy. To learn more about this study, visit clinicaltrials.gov (NCT06922591).
