 Item 5.02. Departure of Directors or Certain Officers; Election of Directors; Appointment of Certain Officers; Compensatory Arrangements of Certain Officers 

 ​ 

As announced in the press release filed as Exhibit 99.1 hereto, on November 12, 2026, the Board of Directors (the “Board”) of RPC, Inc. (the “Company”) appointed Robert James Saltiel, Jr., age 63, as President and Chief Executive Officer of the Company, effective November 20, 2026 (the “Start Date”). Mr. Saltiel will report to the Board. The Board also appointed Mr. Saltiel as a director of the Company, and as a member of the Board’s Executive Committee, effective as of the Start Date. Mr. Saltiel will not receive additional compensation for his service on the Board or the Executive Committee.

 ​ 

Mr. Saltiel, age 63, served as President and Chief Executive Officer and a director of MRC Global Inc., a global industrial distributor serving energy and process-industry customers, from 2021 to 2025. Previously, he served as President and Chief Executive Officer and a director of Key Energy Services, Inc. from 2018 to 2019 and Atwood Oceanics, Inc. from 2009 to 2017. From 2003 to 2009, Mr. Saltiel held various senior leadership positions at Transocean Ltd., ultimately serving as Chief Operating Officer. He holds a bachelor’s degree in chemical engineering from Princeton University and an MBA from Northwestern University’s Kellogg School of Management.

 ​ 

There are no arrangements or understandings between Mr. Saltiel and any other person pursuant to which he was selected as Chief Executive Officer or director, no family relationships between Mr. Saltiel and any director or executive officer of the Company, and no transactions involving Mr. Saltiel requiring disclosure under Item 404(a) of Regulation S-K. 

 ​ 

 Offer Letter with Mr. Saltiel 

 ​ 

In connection with his appointment, the Company entered into an offer letter with Mr. Saltiel dated November 17, 2026 (the “Offer Letter”). Under the Offer Letter, Mr. Saltiel will receive an annual base salary of $860,000 and will be eligible to participate in the Company’s annual cash incentive program with a target annual bonus opportunity equal to 125% of his base salary. His actual annual bonus, if any, will be determined by the Human Capital Management and Compensation Committee of the Board (the “Compensation Committee”) based on achievement of applicable performance goals to be set by the Company and subject to the terms of the applicable program. His target bonus for 2026 will be prorated based on the portion of the year during which he is employed by the Company.

 ​ 

Beginning in 2027, Mr. Saltiel will be eligible to participate in the Company’s long-term equity incentive program on the same basis as other senior executives. His first annual long-term equity incentive award will have a target grant-date value of $4.3 million, with 50% delivered in time-based restricted stock awards and 50% delivered in performance stock units. These awards generally are expected to be subject to the same vesting schedule and performance goals, as applicable, as the 2027 annual long-term incentive awards granted to the Company’s other senior executives. Subsequent annual awards will be determined by the Compensation Committee in the ordinary course and are intended to have a target grant-date value of 500% of Mr. Saltiel’s base salary, but in no event will have a value less than 450% of his base salary.

 ​ 

Mr. Saltiel also will receive a sign-on award of 50,000 fully vested, unrestricted shares of the Company’s common stock on the Start Date. 

 ​ 

All equity awards contemplated by the Offer Letter will be granted under the Company’s 2024 Stock Incentive Plan and will be governed by the plan and the applicable award agreements.

 ​ 

Mr. Saltiel will also be eligible to participate as a Tier 1 participant in the Company’s Change of Control and Severance Plan described below (the “Severance Plan”), subject to the terms and conditions of the Severance Plan, including execution of the required participation agreement containing restrictive covenants.

 ​ 

In addition, upon a “qualifying termination” outside the “Protection Period” (in each case, as defined below), Mr. Saltiel will receive immediate vesting of the portion of his outstanding time-based restricted stock awards that otherwise would have vested during the 24 months following termination, and his outstanding performance stock units and other long-term equity awards will continue to vest for 24 months following termination as though he remained employed during that period. These protections override contrary award-agreement provisions, but settlement remains subject to the applicable award agreements and Section 409A of the Internal Revenue Code.

 ​ 

Mr. Saltiel’s principal place of employment will be Houston, Texas. The Company will reimburse his reasonable travel expenses between Houston and its headquarters in Atlanta, Georgia in accordance with its travel and expense reimbursement policies. Mr. Saltiel will be eligible to participate in the employee benefit plans and programs generally available to the Company’s senior executives, subject to their applicable terms. He will be subject to the Company’s stock ownership guidelines, clawback policy and other policies applicable to senior executives. Upon termination of his employment for any reason, he will be deemed to have resigned from the Board and all other officer and director positions with the Company and its subsidiaries.

 ​ 

 Adoption of Change of Control and Severance Plan 

 ​ 

On November 17, 2026, the Board adopted the Severance Plan, effective November 17, 2026. The Severance Plan provides severance payments and benefits to officers and other key employees selected by the Compensation Committee who execute participation agreements. Unless otherwise specified in a participant’s participation agreement, the Chief Executive Officer participates in Tier 1 and other participants participate in Tier 2. The initial participants include Mr. Saltiel and, subject to his execution of a participation agreement, Michael Schmit, the Company’s Chief Financial Officer. 

 ​ 

Under the Severance Plan, a participant is eligible for severance payments and benefits if the Company terminates the participant’s employment other than for cause, disability or death, or the participant resigns for good reason, in each case as defined in the Severance Plan (a “qualifying termination”). The benefits depend on the participant’s tier and whether the qualifying termination occurs during the period beginning upon the occurrence of a change of control, as defined in the Severance Plan, and ending on its second anniversary (the “Protection Period”).

 ​ 

For a qualifying termination during the Protection Period, a participant is entitled to (i) a lump-sum cash severance payment equal to three times, for Tier 1 participants, or two times, for Tier 2 participants, the sum of annual base salary and target annual cash bonus; (ii) a prorated annual cash bonus for the year of termination based on target performance; and (iii) reimbursement of a specified portion of COBRA medical coverage premiums for up to 18 months, subject to earlier cessation upon enrollment in another employer’s or any other medical plan. The cash severance payment and prorated target bonus generally are payable within 74 days following termination, subject to the release requirements and applicable tax provisions of the Severance Plan.

 ​ 

For a qualifying termination outside the Protection Period, a participant is entitled to (i) cash severance equal to two times, for Tier 1 participants, or one times, for Tier 2 participants, the sum of annual base salary and target annual cash bonus, payable in substantially equal installments over 24 months for Tier 1 participants or 12 months for Tier 2 participants; (ii) a prorated annual cash bonus for the year of termination based on actual performance, payable when annual bonuses otherwise are paid; and (iii) reimbursement of a specified portion of COBRA medical coverage premiums for up to 18 months for Tier 1 participants or 12 months for Tier 2 participants, subject to earlier cessation upon enrollment in other medical coverage as described above.

 ​ 

Participants also are entitled upon a qualifying termination to earned but unpaid base salary and any earned but unpaid annual cash bonus for the preceding fiscal year. Rights under equity awards and other benefit arrangements remain governed by the applicable arrangements, and payments under the Severance Plan are in lieu of other severance, separation or salary-continuation payments arising from the termination.

 ​ 

Good reason generally includes specified material reductions in duties, authority, responsibilities, base salary or target bonus; a material adverse change in title or reporting relationship; relocation of the participant’s primary workplace beyond a 50-mile radius; or a material breach of the Severance Plan by the Company, subject to applicable exceptions and notice, cure and resignation requirements. For Mr. Saltiel, good reason also includes (i) any reduction in base salary or target bonus (even if it is not material) and (ii) a reduction in his target annual long-term incentive opportunity to a value less than 450% of his base salary.

 ​ 

Payment of severance, the prorated annual bonus and COBRA benefits is conditioned on timely execution and nonrevocation of a release of claims and compliance with the Severance Plan and the participant’s participation agreement. Participation agreements will contain restrictive covenants, including confidentiality, noncompetition, nonsolicitation and

A material breach of the participation agreement may result in forfeiture of remaining unpaid severance payments and benefits other than earned but unpaid base salary and the preceding fiscal year’s earned but unpaid bonus. Payments and benefits also are subject to applicable Company clawback policies.

 ​ 

The Severance Plan provides for a reduction in payments and benefits that would otherwise constitute excess parachute payments under Section 280G of the Internal Revenue Code, but only if the reduction would result in a greater after-tax benefit to the participant. Materially adverse amendments or termination of the Severance Plan generally require either the affected participant’s prior written consent or 12 months’ written notice, and may not become effective without such consent during the Protection Period or while a transaction that would constitute a change of control is pending. Under Mr. Saltiel’s participation agreement, however, no amendment or termination materially adversely affecting him may be made at any time without his prior written consent.

 Transition Employment Arrangement with Ben M. Palmer 

 ​ 

On November 12, 2026, the Board approved a transition agreement with Mr. Palmer in connection with his previously announced retirement. Mr. Palmer’s resignation as President and Chief Executive Officer, as a member of the Board and from all officer and director positions he holds with the Company’s subsidiaries will become effective upon Mr. Saltiel’s commencement of employment as President and Chief Executive Officer.

 ​ 

Pursuant to the transition agreement, when Mr. Saltiel succeeds to office as President and CEO, Mr. Palmer will remain employed by the Company as a non-officer Senior Advisor, and remain so employed through December 31, 2026. During this transitional period, Mr. Palmer will provide reasonable transition and advisory assistance to Mr. Saltiel and management, including transferring institutional knowledge, assisting with significant business relationships and providing historical context regarding Company matters. The position will involve no significant policymaking authority, and Mr. Palmer will cease to serve as an executive officer of the Company effective upon Mr. Saltiel’s succession.

 ​ 

As consideration for these services, Mr. Palmer will receive a base salary equal to the base salary he otherwise would have received for the remainder of 2026 had he remained President and Chief Executive Officer, calculated using an annual base salary of $750,000. In addition, the transition agreement provides that he will remain eligible to receive an annual cash bonus under the Company’s 2026 cash performance bonus plan and to receive a payout under his performance stock unit award granted in 2024, if the relevant performance criteria are met, to the same extent he would have had he remained in office through the end of the year; provides for certain calculations thereunder; clarifies that he will be entitled to proportionate vesting for his restricted stock awards granted in the years 2021, 2024, 2025 and 2026, based upon the date of his departure as a non-officer Senior Advisor; and provides for an additional payment of $75,000 in exchange for his execution of a release of claims (as described below). 

 ​ 

The transition agreement also includes releases of claims and related execution, revocation and effectiveness provisions; restrictions on solicitation of Company and affiliate employees and contractors and direct solicitation of sales from established Company customers for six months following separation from employment; non-disparagement obligations for two years following his execution of the transition agreement; continuing confidentiality and trade secret protections; cooperation obligations relating to his prior employment, with reimbursement of reasonable expenses; and provisions concerning the return and protection of Company property and records. 

 ​ 

Mr. Palmer will not participate in the Company’s newly approved Change of Control and Severance Plan.
