How to read this, and where it is weak

Method
Each name is priced twice with the same two-stage model. Reverse DCF: solve for the 5-year free-cash-flow growth that makes the model reproduce today's enterprise value — that is what the market is assuming. Forward DCF: price an assumption of our own and compare. On this page the forward number is a mechanical baseline (the company's own revenue history), which is why it carries no judgment. The ten researched names each week replace that baseline with a real one.

Financials get justified price-to-tangible-book from sustainable return on tangible equity instead — free cash flow to the firm is meaningless when debt is raw material. REITs and companies with negative normalized cash flow get no number at all.

% of 52w high is the current price divided by the highest close in the trailing 252 sessions.
Assumptions that move every number on this page
Equity risk premium 5.5% · terminal growth 2.0% · explicit forecast 5y · tax 25%. These are choices, not facts, and they shift every absolute gap in the same direction. That is exactly why the cohort percentile column exists: it compares a name to its own sector under identical assumptions, so a whole sector cannot look cheap because of a constant chosen here. Implied growth is also reported at ±1pt of WACC on each brief, because it is far more sensitive to the discount rate than to anything else.
What this screen cannot do
It cannot value pre-revenue biotech, bitcoin miners, or anything with negative normalized free cash flow — those are refused rather than guessed at. It cannot see off-balance-sheet obligations, segment detail, or anything not tagged in XBRL. A single lumpy year still distorts a three-year cash-flow base, which is why briefs carry dispersion flags. Prices are end-of-day. Fundamentals are as of the last annual filing and can be up to a year stale.
