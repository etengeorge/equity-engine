Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The strongest case against my 'rich' verdict is that I am extrapolating a 2.3% historical revenue CAGR across a structural break. GEO's revenue history is the history of a company with a third of its beds empty; the company trading today has contracted, funded activations and a customer with an expanding appropriation. If detention capacity demand keeps growing, the correct comparison is not the five-year CAGR but the 25% EBITDA growth being delivered right now, and +16.5% for five years is then achievable rather than heroic. There is also a structural argument I cannot dismiss: because ICE reimburses the activation capex, GEO grows revenue without consuming shareholder capital, which is precisely the condition under which high FCFF growth rates ARE sustainable.

If GEO names further facility reactivations at Rivers-like economics during 2027, my step-not-a-rate framing is wrong. The cheaper interim test is the FY2026 cash flow statement: whether full-year CFO less unreimbursed capex lands near my $200M assumption once the shutdown receivable is fully unwound.
