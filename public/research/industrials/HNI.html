The case for the price. The market is pricing HNI as a cyclical office-furniture manufacturer that has just doubled its size into a structurally impaired end market, and that case is coherent. At $46.91 the enterprise is about $4.65 billion against roughly $630 million of pro forma EBITDA — about 7.4x — which is almost exactly the multiple at which furniture assets change hands: HNI itself bought Steelcase at approximately 5.8x trailing adjusted EBITDA inclusive of $120 million of run-rate synergies. So the market is paying a modest premium to the private-market clearing price for the asset class, which is not an error. The bear logic underneath it is real: hybrid work permanently reduced office square footage; Steelcase's revenue was flat to declining for years before the deal; GAAP operating income for H1 2026 FELL to $55.7 million from $92.6 million a year earlier on purchase-accounting and restructuring charges; and operating cash flow for the half was NEGATIVE $32.0 million against positive $43.7 million, while capital expenditure doubled to $64.7 million. An investor looking at a 2x-larger company generating negative operating cash flow, with a new leadership team not yet complete, two retained headquarters and 113 years of acquired-company management just ended, can reasonably decline to pay for synergies that are still a forecast. Finally, part of the 2026 earnings growth is a windfall: net tariff impacts added 150 basis points to operating margin in Q2 and 40-45 basis points is expected for the full year, which is a benefit that can reverse.

What changed. HNI is a different company and the engine's cash-flow base does not know it. On 2025-12-10 HNI completed the acquisition of Steelcase Inc. — a roughly $2.2 billion transaction creating a business with approximately $5.8 billion of pro forma annual revenue, with HNI holders owning about 64% and Steelcase holders about 36%, funded with approximately $1,012.5 million of stock issued plus $390.3 million of net cash and debt. HNI's fiscal 2025 ended 2026-01-03, so its newest reported year contains only about three and a half weeks of Steelcase: the 10-K's own supplemental table shows Steelcase contributing just $187.5 million of net sales and a $30.7 million net loss to fiscal 2025. Meanwhile H1 2026 net sales were $2,819.9 million against $1,266.8 million — 2.2x. Q2 2026: net sales $1,472.4 million, GAAP diluted EPS $0.70, non-GAAP diluted EPS $1.27 (+14%). Guidance: full-year 2026 non-GAAP diluted EPS growth of 20-25% including net tariff impacts, a fifth consecutive year of double-digit growth; Q3 non-GAAP EPS growth in the mid-to-high 20s; double-digit growth again in 2027; and cumulative savings from Steelcase synergies, cost actions and legacy network optimisation exceeding $70 million in 2027 and more than $150 million when fully mature. Demand is turning: Workplace Furnishings Q2 orders and quarter-ending backlog each +5% year over year, with legacy Workplace Furnishings Q3 sales guided to high-single-digit growth.
