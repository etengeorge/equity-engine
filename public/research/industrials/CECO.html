The case for the price. CECO is being priced as a pure-play on power generation, grid and data-centre build-out rather than as an industrial. The Thermon acquisition ($2.1bn, closed 2026-06-01) added industrial process heating to an air-quality and water platform, and management reports a sales pipeline 'over $8.5 billion' with a record backlog, Q2 revenue +54% to $285.0M, adjusted EBITDA +73% to $40.2M at a 14.1% margin, synergies 'outpacing the targets set prior to the transaction close', and a raised full-year outlook. Buyers of infrastructure compounders in the hottest industrial end-market of the cycle have paid 22-25x EBITDA and been right for three years running. At $71.87 the market requires about +22-25% five-year FCFF growth on the combined business, which is roughly what management means by 'long-term, double-digit growth' plus continued bolt-ons funded out of a 55%-conversion cash flow.

What changed. The whole company changed and the model did not see it. CECO completed the acquisition of Thermon Group Holdings on 2026-06-01 for $2.1bn (agreed 2026-02-23; Thermon holders electing $63.89 all cash, 0.8110 CECO shares, or $10 cash plus 0.6840 shares), filed under 8-K items 2.01, 2.03, 5.02 and 5.03. Q2 2026 (filed 2026-08-10): revenue $285.0M +54%, adjusted EBITDA $40.2M +73%, GAAP operating LOSS $(33.2)M and net loss $(34.8)M on acquisition and purchase-accounting charges, non-GAAP net income $21.5M, free cash flow $(24.3)M with adjusted free cash flow $53.2M excluding Thermon transaction payments. Raised FY2026 outlook: adjusted EBITDA $200-225M (from $195-225M) with free cash flow conversion of at least 55% of adjusted EBITDA, i.e. roughly $110-124M of FCF.

Base case. The engine's +78.3% required growth is an artifact of pairing a POST-deal enterprise value with a PRE-deal cash-flow base - the SM/HOG defect. Enterprise value of $4,881M is built from the 2026-06-30 balance sheet, which is post-Thermon (equity $2,075.9M against an equity_series[0] of $317.5M, goodwill $1,501.2M against $288.2M, intangibles $999.4M against $97.0M). The FCFF base of $28.4M is the mean of standalone cfo-capex of [-5.5, 7.5, 36.3] for the three years to 2025-12-31, a company roughly a third of the current size. Pairing them is meaningless in either direction. Rebuilt on the combined company: FY2026 guided FCF is ~$117M at the midpoint, but FY2026 contains only seven months of Thermon; a full-year combined run rate is nearer $135M of FCF, and post-deal debt of $727.7M at ~6.5% adds roughly $35M of after-tax interest, giving a base near $170M. At that base the market requires +22.0%. My own assumption is +11% - management's 'double-digit' - because that is what the business plus ordinary bolt-ons should deliver, and because applying growth to a base already struck after total capex, on a roll-up whose growth is bought with acquisition cash that never enters free cash flow, is already generous (the EFOR/AROC point). At +11% the corrected fair value is roughly $43 against $71.87.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: Calling a 22-24x EV/EBITDA industrial 'rich' into the strongest power and data-centre capex cycle in forty years is the classic early short. CECO has grown revenue from $316M to $774M standalone in five years and has just doubled again; a company genuinely compounding 20%+ deserves a multiple that looks absurd on trailing numbers, and the +25.4% hurdle is only two or three good bolt-ons away. I also have exactly ONE quarter of combined financials, the integration is four months old, and the FY2026 guide spans only seven months of Thermon - so the base I corrected to is itself an estimate.
  • What would prove it: FY2027 guidance: adjusted EBITDA materially above ~$260M with free cash flow conversion holding at 55%+ would show the combined entity compounding at the rate the price requires. Conversely, organic (ex-Thermon) revenue growth disclosed below ~10%, or conversion falling below 50%, would confirm the bear.
  • Already visible today: Partly for the bull: Q2 adjusted EBITDA +73% and synergies ahead of plan are real and recent. Against it, GAAP operating income was a $(33.2)M LOSS and reported free cash flow was $(24.3)M; the positive numbers are all non-GAAP and all adjusted for the transaction. And the standalone three-year cash flow record the engine holds - cfo-capex of [-5.5, 7.5, 36.3] - is a business that generated almost no free cash before the deal.
  • Left unresolved: I cannot separate organic from acquired growth on the combined entity: CECO does not disclose an organic revenue figure, and per the EFOR rule a roll-up's headline growth is meaningless without one. That is the input that decides whether +11% or +22% is the right base case, and one quarter of combined reporting does not settle it. My 'rich' is therefore low conviction and rests on the arithmetic of the hurdle, not on a judgment that the end-market is overrated.
