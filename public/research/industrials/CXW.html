The case for the price. CoreCivic at $31.08 is up 69.2% over twelve months because the United States government has started buying its real estate, at prices that validate the book value, while leaving CoreCivic to operate the same facilities under management contract. In 2026 it sold FOUR detention facilities to the Department of Homeland Security: California City (2,560 beds) for $732.6M and Otay Mesa (1,994 beds) for $739.2M, both closed 2026-07-02 for $1.5bn gross and about $1.1bn net of roughly $0.4bn of taxes; the Midwest Regional Reception Center in Leavenworth (1,033 beds) for $238.4M on 2026-08-04; and the Prairie Facility. That is roughly $1.71bn of gross proceeds against a $3.1bn market capitalisation — 55% of the equity — and management states it 'expects to continue to manage' all of them under the existing ICE contracts. The proceeds repaid $270.0M of revolver, a $100.0M incremental term loan and $238.5M of 4.75% senior notes, and the company has opened unlimited restricted-payment capacity for buybacks once secured leverage is at or below 1.50x. The revised full-year 2026 guidance is the cleanest statement of what the market is buying: adjusted diluted EPS RAISED to $1.62-$1.70 from $1.53-$1.63 and adjusted net income raised to $161.5-169.5M, while adjusted EBITDA was CUT to $440.5-445.5M from $453.8-461.8M. In other words the sales cost about 3% of EBITDA and added to per-share earnings, because the interest saved and the shares retired more than cover the lost ownership margin. On top of that, management discloses it 'has been in discussions with ICE about the potential acquisition of additional detention facilities'. The market is paying for a company converting owned real estate into cash at good prices with a buyer that wants more.

What changed. Everything that matters, and all of it after the 2026-06-30 balance sheet the engine is using. The four facility sales closed on 2026-07-02 and 2026-08-04, i.e. two days and five weeks after the balance-sheet date, for roughly $1.71bn gross. The Q2 10-Q's own subsequent-events note records the uses: the revolving credit facility and incremental term loan repaid in July for $270.0M and $100.0M, and an irrevocable notice delivered 2026-07-13 to redeem the full $238.5M of 4.75% senior notes on 2026-08-12 at par plus make-whole. So $608.5M of debt has been retired and roughly $1.28bn of net proceeds received, none of which is in the enterprise value on this brief. Remaining debt is the $500.0M of 8.25% senior notes due April 2029, about $107.8M of initial term loan amortising to October 2028, and $131.2M of non-recourse Kansas Notes — roughly $739M against the roughly $1.35bn the extract carries. Full-year 2026 guidance was revised to reflect the sales (GAAP net income to $1.497-1.516bn from $147.8-157.8M, the difference being the gain). Also new: the acquisition of Clinical Solutions Pharmacy closed 2026-04-01 for $142.8M of cash, which is inside the cash-flow window but not the revenue history; five previously idle facilities have been activated; a new ICE management agreement was signed for the Prairie Facility, idle since 2010, running to August 2031; and a 2026-09-25 8-K reports an officer change.
