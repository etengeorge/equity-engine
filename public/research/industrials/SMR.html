The case for the price. At $3.81 — down 82.3% in a year and 59.5% in three months, at a 52-week low — the market is pricing NuScale as an option that has moved a long way out of the money rather than as a going concern. The remaining bull case rests on two things that are real: NuScale holds the only small modular reactor design certified by the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, which is a regulatory moat no competitor can buy quickly; and the company has roughly $933M of cash and short-term investments after raising about $2.05bn of equity through its 2025 and 2026 at-the-market programmes, so it can fund several more years of development without a financing emergency. Against a true market capitalisation of about $1.64bn and stockholders' equity of $2.02bn, the shares trade below book — which is what the market does to a developer whose first commercial deployment keeps moving right.

What changed. The 63-day collapse is identified and dated: Canada's first SMR project is running late, which took the stock down 22% in the week to 2026-11-18 to a 52-week low, and the coverage that followed frames the question as liquidity against delays. Operationally the quarter brought incremental process news rather than a commercial order — NuScale implemented Everstar's Gordian AI platform to accelerate commercial reactor deployment (2026-11-18). The financial direction is the thing that matters and it is in the 10-Q: operating cash outflow was $459.6M in the newest fiscal year against $108.7M and $183.3M in the two prior years, so the burn has roughly quadrupled, and EBIT is negative $689.6M. The company has funded that by issuing roughly 120.7 million Class A shares across three tranches disclosed in the Q3 10-Q — 89,727,165 shares for $1,000.0M gross, 17,764,936 for $577.6M gross and 13,216,809 for $475.2M gross, about $2.05bn in total.
