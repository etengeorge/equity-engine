Base case. I underwrite 9% five-year FCFF growth. The mix argument is real: if AST is roughly 40% of EBITDA growing at 15-20% and Sealing is 60% growing at 4-5% organically, blended EBITDA growth is 9-11% before any margin expansion, and EnPro converts EBITDA to free cash flow at a reasonable rate (H1: $61.4M of FCF against $163.3M of adjusted EBITDA, seasonally understated). I decline to extrapolate the headline 14-16% because guidance itself discloses that a large part of it is the AlpHa and Overlook acquisitions annualising, and organic Sealing growth is 5.0%. I also note the five-year revenue CAGR is 1.3% — this company has not compounded before, and the semiconductor cycle it is now levered to is the most violent capex cycle in industrials. 9% is my estimate of what a good outcome looks like without assuming the up-cycle never ends.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The strongest counter is that my DCF is the wrong instrument, not that my growth number is too low. A two-stage FCFF model with a 2% terminal growth rate and a 10.8% WACC systematically undervalues a business whose value is in a long compounding runway — it forces almost all of the value into an explicit five-year window and then caps the rest at inflation. The market is not underwriting 31% FCFF growth for five years; it is underwriting a durable 10-12% compounder that deserves a 20x terminal EBITDA multiple, and those are very different statements that this model cannot distinguish. And the bull evidence is partly VISIBLE, not hypothetical: AST grew 21.8% organically last quarter, margins expanded 90bp, and guidance was raised.
  • What would prove it: Four to six quarters of AST organic growth sustained above 20% with total adjusted EBITDA margin above 27%, and Sealing organic growth holding at 5%+ through a soft industrial tape. That would establish the mix shift as durable rather than cyclical.

  • Left unresolved: I cannot resolve whether the correct terminal multiple for EnPro is an industrial one or a semiconductor one, and that single choice moves the fair value more than any growth assumption I could make.
