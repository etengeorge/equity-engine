The case for the price. Pathward is not a normal bank and the market knows it: it funds itself with essentially free deposits sourced from prepaid-card, tax-refund and BaaS partners, keeps its Durbin exemption, and earns a return on tangible equity no deposit-gathering community bank can approach — 34.8% for the nine months of fiscal 2025 and still 30.7% for the nine months of fiscal 2026. Paying 2.95x tangible book for a franchise that compounds tangible book per share from $22.09 to $25.83 in four quarters is not obviously wrong. What the buyer is also pricing, correctly, is that the commercial-finance book attached to that franchise has just broken: the stock is down 8.9% in 21 days and ~11.7% over six months, which is the market marking down the return rather than disputing that the deposit franchise is good.

What changed. The credit event, and it is large, recent and unresolved. Nonperforming loans and leases went from $71.3M (1.49% of gross loans) at 2025-06-30 to $99.1M (2.05%) at 2025-09-30 to $117.7M (2.39%) at 2026-03-31 to $275.1 MILLION (5.28%) at 2026-06-30 — more than doubling in a single quarter. The company attributes the increase to 'certain renewable energy construction projects with a common developer' and says it 'continues to work with other parties in these projects to bring them to completion', i.e. it is committing further capital, not exiting. Separately a working-capital loan was identified as 'sophisticated fraud within an otherwise viable operating company'. Provision for credit losses was $28.3M in the June quarter against $9.3M a year earlier, commercial finance accounting for $22.6M of the increase. Fiscal Q3 net income fell to $29.0M / $1.37 diluted from $42.1M / $1.81. Return on average tangible equity for the quarter was 21.61% against 26.72% a year earlier. An 8-K on 2026-09-17 reports an item 5.02 officer change. Plaintiff-firm 'securities fraud investigation' headlines from 2026-07-26 are boilerplate and I give them no weight.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The strongest counter is that I am marking down the wrong thing. Pathward's earning power is a deposit franchise, not a loan book: it takes essentially zero-cost deposits from prepaid and tax partners and keeps a Durbin exemption most banks its size do not have. The commercial-finance portfolio is the part it bolted on, and it is a minority of assets. Even in the worst quarter it has ever printed on credit, the business earned 21.61% on tangible equity — more than twice its 10.28% cost of equity — and tangible book per share still ROSE, $25.41 to $25.83. On that reading the correct ROTCE is high-20s, the gap is a real +6% to +17%, and I am confusing a bad quarter with a broken franchise.
  • What would prove it: Resolution of the renewable-energy nonperformers: the fiscal Q4 and Q1 filings will show whether the $275.1M migrates to charge-off or back to accrual, and what further capital Pathward put into 'bringing the projects to completion'. Net charge-off rate on the commercial finance book is the single number.
  • Already visible today: Some of it, and it genuinely weakens my case: tangible book per share rose through the credit event ($22.09 -> $24.02 -> $24.54 -> $25.41 -> $25.83 across five quarters), which is not what a bank absorbing a real loss looks like, and the tax-services provision actually FELL $5.7M on better underwriting. The 10-Q gives the nonperforming balance and says specific reserves 'reflect expected proceeds' but does not quantify expected recovery, so I cannot distinguish a timing problem from a capital loss. That distinction is the whole 70-point range and I could not close it from the filings.
