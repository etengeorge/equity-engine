The case for the price. At $11.82 against a genuine tangible book of $6.13 per share, Medallion Financial trades at 1.93x tangible common equity — not the 1.13x the screen believes. On the correct multiple the market is already paying a premium for a consumer lender earning high-teens to low-twenties returns on tangible common equity, and that premium is defensible but not obviously wrong: Medallion Bank's recreation and home-improvement lending books have performed, the legacy taxi medallion drag is largely behind the company, and net income has run $55.1M, $35.9M, $43.0M across the last three years. Nobody is getting a free lunch at roughly 2x tangible book for a subprime-adjacent consumer lender late in a credit cycle.

Base case. This is a book-method name, so no FCFF growth rate applies and record.py does not reprice it. The substantive finding is that the reported +213% gap is a known model defect, not an opportunity. LESSONS already names this exact bug: justified_pb computes tangible common equity as equity minus goodwill minus intangibles and never deducts non-controlling interests or preferred, even though both sit in the same extract and are correctly subtracted in enterprise_value. Medallion carries $99.4M of non-controlling interest — the preferred equity issued at Medallion Bank — inside the $408.6M of reported equity. Deducting goodwill of $150.8M and intangibles of $17.7M gives the model's tangible book of $10.46 per share on 22.96M shares; deducting the $99.4M of NCI as well gives $6.13. That is a 71% overstatement of tangible book, and it converts an apparent 1.13x P/TBV into an actual 1.93x. Against a justified multiple of 3.54x the gap does not vanish entirely, but the justified multiple is itself built on a sustainable ROTCE the model flags as unstable across a 17.9% to 32.1% range, and that ROTCE is computed on the same overstated equity denominator. I am not going to publish a fair value derived from two inputs I have just shown to be wrong in the same direction.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The counter is that I have over-corrected. Medallion Bank's preferred is genuinely subordinate to nothing the common holder cares about in a going concern — it is cheap permanent capital funding an earning asset, and the earnings attributable to common already sit in the net income series. If the $43.0M of net income is attributable to common shareholders AFTER the NCI preferred dividend, then deducting the NCI from the book denominator while leaving a common-only numerator is the correct treatment and my 1.93x stands. But if the model's net income series is total including NCI, then the ROTCE numerator and denominator are at least internally consistent as the model computes them, and my correction breaks that consistency rather than restoring it. On that reading the name might still be modestly cheap.
  • What would prove it: The income statement's attribution line: whether the $43.0M net income series is 'net income attributable to Medallion Financial Corp.' or total consolidated net income before the non-controlling interest. That single line decides whether my correction is right or double-counts.
  • Already visible today: Not to me. I did not fetch Medallion's filings and so I could not check the attribution line, which is the honest limit on this verdict. What IS visible and not in dispute is that the equity denominator includes $99.4M of non-controlling interest that a common holder does not own — that is arithmetic from the extract, and it means the reported tangible book of $10.46 per share overstates what the common shareholder has a claim on regardless of how the numerator is defined.
  • Left unresolved: Whether the correct actual P/TBV is 1.93x or something between 1.13x and 1.93x, which depends on the income attribution line I did not read. Also unresolved: whether the 17.9%-32.1% ROTCE range reflects genuine credit-cycle variability or a mark-driven artifact of the kind LESSONS flags for JXN. Either way the +213% gap is not a finding.
