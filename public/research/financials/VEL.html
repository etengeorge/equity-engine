2026-08-31 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

  • Verdict: no_edge · price $18.01

The case for the price. Velocity originates and holds small-balance investor real-estate loans — business-purpose lending to landlords and small commercial owners, a segment banks largely exited. It is a spread book: the portfolio reached $7.0B of UPB in Q2 2026, up 19% year over year, NIM held above target at 3.66%, and core pre-tax ROE was 21.8%. Diluted book value per share compounded 18% year over year to $18.43. At $18.01 the market is paying roughly one times tangible book — which is exactly what the market has always paid for a levered, non-bank, non-prime real-estate lender, no matter how good the reported ROE looks, because the equity is a thin slice under a lot of securitised debt and the loss content of the book is only revealed in a downturn. Non-performing loans at 9.6% are the reason: they are improving, but a book with near-double-digit NPLs does not get a franchise multiple.

What changed. Two things since the last 10-K. Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-05): portfolio $7.0B UPB (+19% YoY), NIM 3.66%, core pre-tax ROE 21.8%, diluted BVPS $18.43 (+18% YoY, +3.8% sequentially), adjusted diluted BVPS $20.59, NPLs improved to 9.6%, net income $25.2M. Then, materially, on 2026-08-26 Velocity Commercial Capital signed an Equity Purchase Agreement to acquire the operating platform of Toorak Capital (KKR-backed, ~280 employees, Tampa), disclosed 2026-08-27 in an 8-K under items 1.01/7.01. Reported as an all-cash purchase of the platform, with roughly $3B UPB of Toorak loans being sold to a third-party investment firm that Velocity will then manage, plus forward-flow arrangements to sell future Toorak originations. Toorak's founder-CEO John Beacham stays and becomes an EVP of Velocity Commercial Capital. Expected to close in Q4 2026.

Base case. Sustainable ROTCE, not FCFF growth, is the operative number here, and I put it at roughly 14% — essentially where the screen has it (13.8%), and I did not need to move it. Three years of net income ($105.1M, $68.4M, $52.3M) against tangible equity averaging out to a 13-14% return is a consistent, unglamorous spread return, and the 21.8% figure management quotes is a core PRE-TAX return on equity, which is not the same measure and should not be substituted for it. Where I differ sharply from the screen is not the return but the discount rate applied to it: at the model's 6.5% cost of equity the justified multiple is 2.82x tangible book, and that is the entire source of the +163.7% gap.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The counter to my dismissal is that the cost of equity is not obviously wrong just because it is low, and that even at a defensible 11-13% cost of equity the justified multiple is 1.1-1.3x tangible book against an actual multiple near 1.0x — a real if unspectacular discount — and that the Toorak deal is a genuine re-rating mechanism I am waving away. Buying an origination and asset-management platform while a third party takes the balance-sheet risk converts part of Velocity's earnings from spread income (which deserves a book multiple) into fee and servicing income (which deserves an earnings multiple). If that mix shift is real, the right comparison is not price-to-book at all, and the stock is cheap.
  • What would prove it: The purchase price and funding of the Toorak platform, and the disclosed run-rate fee and servicing revenue it carries. If Velocity is paying a modest cash sum for a platform that adds durable capital-light fee income, the counter wins. If it is paying up, or funding it with equity, or if the 'management' arrangement is thin-margin subservicing, it does not. The Q3 or Q4 2026 filings and the closing 8-K will say.
  • Already visible today: Not yet, and that is the point. The deal was announced four days before this brief and the platform purchase price was not in any source I could reach; the widely repeated '$3.2 billion' figure refers to the portfolio changing hands, not to what Velocity is paying. The market's own reaction is close to nil — the stock is -1.8% over five sessions — which is either the market ignoring it or the market judging it immaterial to Velocity's equity. I cannot tell which.
  • Left unresolved: The purchase price, the funding, and whether Velocity's balance sheet takes any of the Toorak credit. Also unresolved: whether 9.6% NPLs on investor real-estate loans are, as management frames them, low-LTV positions that resolve at or near par, or the leading edge of a credit cycle. I could not read the 10-Q.

Key risks. The headline gap is a discount-rate artifact, not a mispricing — anyone acting on the 97th-percentile cohort rank is acting on a 6.5% cost of equity for a levered non-prime lender; Toorak integration: 280 employees, a founder-CEO retained, and an undisclosed price, closing into Q4 2026; 9.6% non-performing loans; investor real-estate credit is the first thing to crack if small-landlord cash flows deteriorate; Securitisation market access is the funding model — a closed ABS window is an existential rather than a cyclical problem; $1.8M average daily dollar volume: a $711M company that cannot be traded in size Watch for.
