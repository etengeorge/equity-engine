The case for the price. Axos is a $30B-asset branchless bank compounding tangible book per share at 17.6% a year ($55.81 book value per share at 30 June 2026 from $47.46) while earning a return on average common equity of 16.3% in fiscal 2026, 17.3% in 2025 and 21.6% in 2024 — the company's own figures from the 10-K. Total assets grew 20.9% to $30.0 billion on $4.5 billion of net loan growth, deposits grew $3.7 billion, and fiscal 2026 net income was $490.4 million against $432.9 million. A buyer at 1.82x tangible book is paying roughly ten times earnings for a bank that has out-earned its cost of equity every year of the cycle, funds itself with low-cost digital deposits, and has repeatedly bought distressed loan books (FDIC portfolios, Verdant) at wide spreads. At a 17-18% sustainable return on tangible common equity and an 11.3% cost of equity, 1.8x tangible book is close to arithmetically correct, not a premium.

  • Strongest counter: The strongest case against my no_edge is that Axos is genuinely cheap once the engine's error is removed. On the corrected three-year ROTCE of 18.2% the justified multiple is 1.75 against an actual 1.82 — but the justified-P/TBV form uses a fixed 2.0% terminal growth for a bank whose tangible book per share is compounding 17.6% a year and whose assets grew 20.9%. That is the ENVA bias in this file, and it marks fast-compounding lenders expensive by construction. A grower this fast arguably deserves more than a Gordon model with g pinned at 2%.

Data quality. The brief raised NO flags and both of the engine's inputs to the gap were wrong in the same direction. (1) NEW DEFECT, and it is universe-wide, not specific to Axos. justified_pb builds sustainable ROTCE from net_income_common_series, which for Axos reads [$307.2M, $240.7M, $215.5M]. The company's own audited income statement in the fiscal 2026 10-K reads NET INCOME $490,372 / $432,908 / $450,008 thousand, Axos has no preferred stock outstanding, and the figures $307,165 / $240,716 / $215,518 do not appear anywhere in the 10-K. The equity_series the engine pairs them with ($3,166.4M / $2,680.7M / $2,290.6M) matches the 10-K's common stockholders' equity EXACTLY — so the numerator and the denominator are describing different periods. The result is a 'sustainable' ROTCE of 10.0% against a company reporting return on average common equity of 16.32%, 17.30% and 21.64%. Recomputing with net_income_series over the same tangible equity gives 16.8% / 17.0% / 20.9%, mean 18.2%. That alone moves the justified multiple from 0.86 to 1.75 and the gap from -52.7% to roughly -4%: the entire 'rich' reading is the defect. Root cause is in edgar._annual, which filters on fp == 'FY' and an annual form but never checks that the fact's duration actually spans a year, so a shorter-duration or stale-concept observation filed under fp='FY' is accepted as the annual value. I scanned all 400 book-method names: 86 have a newest net-income-to-common that disagrees with net income by more than 5% with negligible preferred, and of the 79 where the ROTCE could be recomputed, 69 are UNDERSTATED by the defect, median 5.2 percentage points. Like the ENVA and PIPR biases this manufactures false SELLS, which is why it has gone unnoticed. The worst are ordinary profitable community banks reported as earning 2-4% on tangible equity: TBBK 4.5% -> 28.2%, PEBO 2.3% -> 15.8%, PLBC 2.6% -> 16.9%, PLMR 3.3% -> 19.1%, QCRH, FIBK, NIC, MPB, BHB, STC. Those rows also sit inside the cohort distribution every other Financial is percentile-ranked against. (2) Tangible book of $51.42 is very close to the company's own $50.96, which additionally deducts servicing rights — a 0.9% difference, not material, and NOT the AII/BUR stale-book bug: the equity is the 30 June 2026 figure and reconciles exactly. (3) Beta 1.15 with R-squared 0.453 is one of the better-fitted regressions in the index, so unlike HASI and AII in today's ten the 11.3% cost of equity here is trustworthy and the failed-beta bias does not apply. What remains open is the composition of earnings, not the arithmetic.
