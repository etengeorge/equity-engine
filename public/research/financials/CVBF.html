The case for the price. At $22.30 CVB Financial trades at roughly 1.9-2.0x its CURRENT tangible book for a bank that has paid a dividend for 197 consecutive quarters, funds itself with 52% noninterest-bearing deposits at an 0.86% cost of deposits, runs an NPA ratio of 0.08% and zero net charge-offs, and has just integrated a $5.7B acquisition 187 days from announcement to core-system conversion. A ~2x tangible book multiple on a 14-15% return on tangible equity with that deposit franchise is close to what the arithmetic supports; the market is not making an obvious error here. The bear reading inside the same price is that ROATCE has fallen every year - 18.48% (2023), 14.95% (2024), 14.28% (2025), 13.38% (Q1 26) - and that CRE is 75% of a $12.0B loan book in California.

What changed. The Heritage Bank of Commerce acquisition closed on 2026-04-17 (announced 2025-12-17, shareholder approval 2026-03-26, regulatory approval 2026-04-01, core conversion 2026-06-22) and it rewrites both inputs to this model. Acquired net assets $845M, adding $334M of goodwill and $117M of core deposit intangible, 16 business financial centres and Bay Area presence; total assets now >$21B. Q2 2026 carried $31.4M of acquisition expense and a $4.25M provision for unfunded commitments, taking GAAP net income to $48.3M and EPS to $0.29 from $0.38, against core net income of $73.4M. NIM rose to 3.72% from 3.36% helped by $2.7M of Heritage fair-value discount accretion. Organic loan growth was $477M in the quarter. Deal underwriting was IRR 20%, EPS accretion 13%, ROATCE 17%.

Base case. A sustainable ROATCE of roughly 14-15% is defensible and it is, within a few basis points, what the engine already computed - the company's own disclosed ROATCE was 14.95% in 2024 and 14.28% in 2025, so the numerator is not the defect here. I am deliberately NOT supplying it as an override, because the model would price it against a tangible book that predates the acquisition (see data_quality_note), and a correct return divided by a stale book produces a confidently wrong fair value. That is the WLFC rule: when one arm of the calculation cannot be corrected through the schema, publish no number rather than half a correction.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The declining ROATCE trend is the real signal and I am explaining it away as acquisition noise. 18.48% -> 14.95% -> 14.28% -> 13.38% (Q1 2026, BEFORE any Heritage expense) is four consecutive years of deterioration in a period when this bank made no large acquisition until 2026, so the trend is organic, not accounting. On the VLY rule - a monotonic quarterly path means the three-year average describes a company that no longer exists - the direction here is DOWN, so the engine's 14.9% overstates the forward return and the gap is wider than my correction implies, not narrower.
  • What would prove it: Q3 and Q4 2026 ROATCE with the $31.4M of acquisition expense out of the run rate. If core ROATCE settles near 15-17% the decline was mix and rate, and Heritage is accretive as underwritten; if it settles near 12-13% the trend is the franchise and the justified multiple falls below the current one.
  • Already visible today: Partly. Q1 2026 ROATCE of 13.38% is a clean pre-acquisition quarter and it continues the decline, which supports the counter. Against it, Q2 core net income of $73.4M annualised against roughly $1.95-2.08B of tangible common equity is about 14-15%, and NIM expanded 36bp to 3.72% - so the core earning power in the quarter does not corroborate further deterioration. Net charge-offs were 0.00% in every period shown.
  • Left unresolved: Whether the Heritage fair-value discount accretion ($2.7M in Q2) is flattering the NIM expansion enough to matter, and how much of it runs off. I also could not separate how much of the Q2 NIM gain is Heritage's balance-sheet restructuring - $815M of securities and loans-held-for-sale were repositioned into cash at close - from underlying spread. Neither changes the verdict, because the verdict is that the headline gap is an artifact, not that the bank is cheap.

Data quality. The -38.5% gap is mostly a stale-tangible-book artifact and this is the cleanest instance of that defect recorded so far, because the acquisition makes it self-evident. justified_pb computes its denominator as _tce_at(0) = equity_series[0] - goodwill_series[0] - intangibles_series[0] = $2,295,224k - $765,822k - $5,774k = $1,523,628k, and then divides by the CURRENT share count of 175,997,435 (cover page, 2026-08-03) to get $8.66 per share. Those are pre-Heritage equity and pre-Heritage goodwill against a POST-Heritage share count. The docstring above the function warns against exactly this using UMBF as the example ('averaging book levels across an acquisition prices the bank against a company that no longer exists') and the implementation reproduces the error one period later. Corrected: equity_now $3,169,689k - goodwill $1,099,936k - intangibles $117,927k = $1,951,826k, or $11.09 per share, which cross-checks against the company's disclosed 9.8% tangible common equity ratio on >$21B of assets ($2.08B, $11.81/share). Actual P/TBV is therefore 1.89-2.01x, not 2.58x, and the gap against the unchanged justified 1.583 is -16% to -21%, not -38.5%. A second, separate consequence: the flag goodwill_and_intangibles_53%_of_book divides CURRENT goodwill+intangibles ($1,217,863k) by STALE equity ($2,295,224k); the true ratio is 38.4%, so the flag itself overstates by 15 points on any name that has closed an acquisition since its last annual period.
