The case for the price. At $34.59 United Community trades at about 1.62x the tangible book of $21.36 a share that the company's own 8 September deck projects for the end of Q3 2026, for a Southeastern relationship bank whose updated baseline operating earnings power is $0.78 a quarter — roughly a 14-15% return on that tangible equity. The market is paying a premium multiple for a franchise that has just deliberately simplified itself: it sold Navitas equipment finance for $2.0 billion in cash at a 7% premium to par, took a ~$300 million pre-tax loss to sell $2.6 billion of 2.20%-yield securities and redeploy at ~4.5%, closed Peach State, and is buying back stock. That is a bank trading at a fair-to-full multiple of a return it has just paid real tangible book to improve, and the price reflects the improvement rather than discounting it.

What changed. Almost everything, and all of it after the last 10-K. (1) Navitas Credit Corp and NLFC Reinsurance were sold to funds managed by Wafra and CLOSED 2026-09-01 for ~$2.0 billion cash, a 7% premium to par on the loan portfolio; Navitas was ~10% of loans and ~50% of the bank's net charge-offs. (2) A balance sheet repositioning COMPLETED 2026-09-02: $2.2 billion of HTM securities reclassified to AFS and $2.6 billion of long-dated securities yielding 2.20% sold, for an estimated ~$300 million pre-tax loss net of a ~$64 million gain on Navitas. United expects a NET LOSS in Q3 2026 while remaining profitable for the nine months. (3) Peach State Bancshares closed 2026-08-01, guided at +$0.12 to 2027 EPS. (4) $1.0 billion of ~3.80% borrowings repaid; $50 million of buybacks executed in Q3 to date, $87 million year to date (2% of shares), with the authorisation raised $100 million. (5) The company's own EPS and TBV walkforwards: baseline operating EPS $0.74 to $0.78 a quarter; 2Q26 TBVPS $23.31 to a 3Q26 proforma $21.36, i.e. a 8.4% hit to tangible book, of which $2.39 is the repositioning. First, the company's own walkforward: an updated baseline operating EPS of $0.78 a quarter is about $3.12 a year, and against the proforma tangible book of $21.36 on roughly 118 million shares after buybacks that is a 14.6% return. Second, and independently, guided 3Q26 operating ROAA of 1.25-1.30% at guided TCE/TA above 9.0% implies roughly 14%. I take the lower end of the two because the $0.78 is management's illustrative full-quarter figure and is explicitly stated to assume a flat rate environment — an assumption the Federal Reserve's September hike has already violated. This is nowhere near the engine's 9.9%, which is a three-year GAAP average spanning the years in which United absorbed acquisitions, merger charges and a securities repositioning, and which describes a bank that no longer exists.
