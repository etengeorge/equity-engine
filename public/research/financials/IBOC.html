The case for the price. At $70.16 International Bancshares trades at roughly 1.41x its true tangible book of $49.73 a share and about 11x annualised earnings, for a bank earning about 13% on tangible common equity and falling. IBC is extraordinarily overcapitalised — CET1 23.56%, Tier 1 leverage 20.27%, equity 19.9% of $17.0B of assets — and it does not deploy or return that capital fast enough to stop the return decaying: ROTCE on the engine's own equity series runs 19.0% (FY2023) to 16.3% (FY2024) to 13.9% (FY2025) to roughly 13.1% annualising H1 2026, because equity compounds faster than earnings. A buyer at 1.41x book is paying a fair multiple for a durable, low-cost Texas/Oklahoma deposit franchise whose returns are structurally drifting toward its cost of equity, and is now also absorbing a credit event: non-accrual loans roughly doubled in six months.

What changed. H1 2026 net income was $198.0 million ($3.18 diluted), up just 0.5% year over year, and Q2 alone FELL 4.3% to $95.8 million from $100.1 million. The reason is credit: total non-accrual loans went from roughly $140.3 million at 2025-12-31 to $297.1 million at 2026-06-30 — about 3.06% of $9.7 billion of net loans against 1.51% — which the 10-Q attributes primarily to 'a relationship consisting of several loans secured by interests in affordable housing projects that were placed on non-accrual in the second quarter of 2026'. Provision expense rose accordingly. Management's framing in the release is promotional ('no actual losses on those loans have been, or may ever be, realized') and should be read as an assertion, not a fact. Total assets grew to $17.0B from $16.6B and loans to $9.7B from $9.3B.

Base case. This is a book-method name, so the input is a sustainable return on tangible common equity, and I set it at 13.0%. That is the current run rate, not the three-year mean. The engine's three-year window on the CORRECT numerator gives 16.4%, but the series is monotonically declining (19.0% / 16.3% / 13.9%) and H1 2026 annualises to about 13.1%, so the mean is describing a company that no longer exists — the VLY rule in reverse. The decline is mechanical rather than operational: IBC retains almost everything it earns into a balance sheet already carrying 23.6% CET1, so tangible common equity compounded from $2.17B to $3.09B over three years while net income sat flat at $409-412 million. Nothing in the franchise is deteriorating — net interest income is holding up on a redistribution of deposit rates — but nothing is arresting the denominator either, and the new non-accruals argue the next move in the numerator is down, not up.
