2026-09-01 — NO_EDGE (conviction: low)

  • Verdict: no_edge · price $68.48

The case for the price. At $68.48 against $13.66 of tangible book, StoneX trades at 5.01x tangible common equity, and the market pays that because StoneX is a compounder, not a balance-sheet lender. Net income has run $238.5M, $260.8M and $305.9M across the last three years and book value has compounded at a high-teens to twenty percent rate for a decade. The business is a fee-and-spread franchise — clearing, execution, market-making across commodities, FX, securities and payments — where the balance sheet is a means of carrying client float rather than the source of earnings. Having just closed R.J. O'Brien, StoneX is the largest non-bank futures commission merchant in the United States and has added nearly $6 billion of client float. A buyer paying 5x tangible book for a franchise that grows book at 15-20% and earns high-teens returns is paying roughly 20x earnings for a business with scale advantages, which is not obviously rich.

What changed. The R.J. O'Brien acquisition closed on 31 July 2025, inside StoneX's fiscal 2025 (year end 30 September 2025) and therefore inside the three-year ROTCE window the model averages. Equity value was approximately $900 million: about $610.6 million of cash consideration reflecting RJO's tangible book above an agreed benchmark, plus 3,085,554 shares reissued from treasury against a pre-close target value of $275.0 million, with up to $143 million of RJO debt assumed. StoneX priced $625 million of secured notes to fund the cash portion. The 2026-08-05 8-K (item 2.02) carried fiscal Q3 results and there is a 2026-08-28 8-K under item 7.01. The extract shows the footprint of the deal clearly: equity jumped from $1,709.1M to $2,377.4M in a single year while goodwill and intangibles reached $730.2M, or 31% of book.

Base case. This is a book-method name, so no FCFF growth applies and record.py does not reprice it. On the substance, I do not accept the model's −63.2% 'expensive' reading as a finding. The justified P/TBV of 1.85x comes from a Gordon-style relationship between a sustainable ROTCE of 17.6%, a cost of equity of 10.7% and the engine's fixed 2.5% terminal growth constant. That construction structurally undervalues any firm that reinvests at high returns and grows book value far faster than 2.5%: it capitalises the spread between return and cost of equity while assuming the capital base is nearly static. StoneX has grown book at roughly 15-20% a year. Feed a 2.5% growth constant into a business compounding equity at 15% and the model will call it expensive at almost any price, which is what has happened here. Separately, the 17.6% 'sustainable' ROTCE averages three years that straddle the RJO close — pre-deal years with a smaller, cleaner equity base against a post-deal year carrying $730M of goodwill and intangibles and a $625M notes issuance. That is the acquisition-inside-the-window problem LESSONS records for UMBF, in the ROTCE numerator rather than the book denominator. Neither the multiple nor the return input is trustworthy enough to support a directional call.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The counter is that I am explaining away a real warning. 5.01x tangible book is genuinely expensive for a business whose earnings are cyclical, transaction-volume dependent and levered to volatility that will not stay elevated forever. StoneX's returns are flattered by a period of exceptional commodity and rates volatility; normalise volatility and the 17.6% ROTCE falls toward the low teens, at which point a 1.85x-2.5x justified multiple is about right and the stock is worth a third of its price. The 'compounder deserves a premium' argument is exactly what people say at the top of a cycle, and the stock is already down 10.6% over 21 days, which may be the market starting to agree.
  • What would prove it: ROTCE through a low-volatility quarter. If StoneX's returns hold in the high teens when commodity and rates volatility normalises, the compounder case is intact; if they compress into the low teens, the model's justified multiple is closer to right than my objection to it.
  • Already visible today: Only ambiguously. Net income has risen in each of the last three years, which supports the compounder reading, but those three years were all high-volatility years, so the series does not test the counter at all. The 21-day 10.6% drawdown is price action, not evidence. I could not find a low-volatility period inside the window to observe, which means the strongest bear argument here is untested rather than refuted.
  • Left unresolved: Whether StoneX's ROTCE survives a normal-volatility environment — the single question that decides whether 5x tangible book is a premium for compounding or a peak-cycle multiple. I also could not separate RJO's contribution from the legacy business in the fiscal 2026 results, so I cannot say whether the acquisition is accretive to returns or diluting them.

Key risks. 5.01x tangible book leaves no margin for a normalisation in commodity, rates and FX volatility; Earnings are transaction-volume dependent and the three-year record spans only high-volatility years; Goodwill and intangibles are 31% of book after RJO, so reported book quality has fallen; The RJO integration is roughly one year old and its return contribution is not yet separable Watch for. ROTCE in a quarter with normalised volatility — the observable that settles the bull and bear cases; Segment disclosure separating RJO's contribution from the legacy FCM business; Client float and interest income sensitivity if short rates fall

Data quality. goodwill_and_intangibles_31%_of_book: confirmed at $298.3M goodwill plus $431.9M intangibles against $2,377.4M equity, and it is a direct consequence of the RJO acquisition rather than an accounting oddity. Unlike MFIN, the equity line here is clean — minority interest and preferred are both zero, so the model's $13.66 tangible book per share is correct and the LESSONS defect about undeducted NCI and preferred does not bite on this name. What does bite is that the three-year ROTCE average straddles the 31 July 2025 RJO close, blending pre- and post-acquisition capital structures, so the 17.6% 'sustainable' figure describes no actual configuration of the business. Combined with a justified-P/TBV construction that assumes 2.5% terminal growth for a firm compounding book at 15-20%, the −63.2% gap is a model artifact and I would not act on it in either direction.
