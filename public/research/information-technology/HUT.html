The case for the price. Nobody buying this stock is buying cash flow, and the market knows it. Hut 8 is being priced as an energy-infrastructure developer: a portfolio of scarce interconnected power that it intends to commercialise through AI data centres, with bitcoin mining as the interim monetisation of megawatts that are not yet contracted. The Q2 2026 print supports that framing — compute revenue of $72.5M against $34.3M a year earlier as Vega and the re-energised Drumheller site came online, roughly 935 bitcoin mined versus about 308, adjusted revenue $105.2M, and gross margin expanding to 64% from 47% on operating leverage. Most importantly the company closed $7.5B of project-level financing in the quarter and capitalised $5.7M of interest on assets under construction, which is what a developer's balance sheet looks like mid-build, not what a distressed miner's looks like. At a $9.5B market capitalisation the equity is a claim on contracted power and future AI leases, discounted for the fact that the operating company currently burns cash.

What changed. Base case. There is no base case to state, because there is no cash flow to grow. The committed extract shows operating cash flow of -$139.2M and -$68.5M in the two available years against capex of $14.0M and $39.6M, so normalised FCFF is negative and the screen correctly refused to model it. That refusal is the right answer and I am not going to work around it. A discounted-cash-flow model prices a stream of cash; Hut 8 is a construction project financed with $7.5B of project-level debt whose value depends on lease contracts that do not yet exist, at capitalisation rates that do not yet exist, on power assets whose value is set by an AI-datacentre demand cycle. Nothing in a two-stage FCFF model addresses any of that. Neither does the alternative lens the engine has available: this is not a financial, so justified price-to-tangible-book is equally inapplicable, and in any case book value here is construction in progress plus a bitcoin position marked to a volatile price.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The counter to refusing is that 'no model' is being used here to avoid a judgement that is actually available. A $9.5B market capitalisation is a specific, falsifiable claim about megawatts: the market is paying some number of dollars per contracted or contractable megawatt, and that number can be compared with what comparable AI-datacentre developers trade at and what recent power-asset transactions have cleared at. That is a real valuation method, it is the method the actual holders of this stock are using, and declining to attempt it means the engine will permanently mark every energy-infrastructure name in the index as unanalysable rather than merely outside its current toolkit. Furthermore, the negative operating cash flow is partly a choice — the company mines and holds bitcoin rather than selling it, so reported operating cash flow understates the economics of the compute segment, which posted a 64% gross margin.
  • What would prove it: A megawatt-denominated disclosure set: energised capacity, contracted capacity, capacity under development with dated energisation, and signed lease terms with counterparties and durations. With those, an enterprise-value-per-megawatt comparison against contracted AI-datacentre peers is a defensible valuation and the refusal becomes lazy. Without them, any per-megawatt number is an assumption dressed as a method.
  • Already visible today: Partly, and it does not get me there. The $7.5B of project-level financing is strong evidence that lenders have underwritten specific projects at specific values, and the gross margin expansion to 64% is evidence the compute segment has real unit economics. But I could not obtain the contracted-versus-speculative megawatt split, the lease terms, or the equity-versus-debt split of that $7.5B — and without the split, the $7.5B tells me the projects are financed, not who owns the residual. A GAAP loss of $177.1M with $138M of it a bitcoin mark also tells me the equity carries a large, unhedged commodity position that no operating model captures.
  • Left unresolved: Whether an enterprise-value-per-megawatt framework would find this stock cheap or dear. I could not read the Q2 10-Q — SEC egress is blocked from this runtime — so I have no capacity table, no lease disclosures and no breakdown of the $7.5B. I am recording that the refusal is a limit of this engine's toolkit and of my access, not a demonstration that the equity is unvaluable in principle.
