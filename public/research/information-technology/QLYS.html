Base case. The decisive question on this name is stock compensation, and the brief puts it squarely: SBC is 29% of the reported FCFF base. Reported operating cash flow adds it back, so the engine's $257.3M base treats a third of employee pay as free. It is not — Qualys settles it in shares, and the dilution is real and permanent even when buybacks mask the share count. Expensing it takes the FCFF base to $182.9M and the growth the market requires from +15.4% to +24.4%. That is the number I have to underwrite, and I cannot. Revenue is growing 11% with guidance implying roughly 10% for the full year; net dollar expansion of 105% means the installed base contributes only 5 points and the rest must come from new logos; and the growth mix is shifting toward channel, which carries a lower realized price per dollar of end-customer spend. To compound free cash flow at 24% for five years on 10% revenue growth requires roughly 14 points of annual margin expansion off a base that is already above 45% EBITDA — there is not that much operating leverage left in a company this profitable. I underwrite +10% FCFF growth, matching revenue growth on an SBC-expensed basis, which is the honest statement that this is a good business growing at its revenue rate rather than a compounding machine.
