The case for the price. At $41.17 the market is paying roughly $2.76bn of enterprise value — a $4.51bn market capitalisation less $1.74bn of cash and marketable securities — for two cardiovascular assets and no debt. The case is that Edgewise sold its original franchise at the top and kept the proceeds: Servier acquired sevasemten and the whole muscular dystrophy programme on 2026-07-10 for $1,396.1 million in upfront cash, and what remains is a cardiovascular company with a differentiated mechanism. EDG-7500 is a first-in-class oral cardiac sarcomere modulator acting on the regulatory light chain to slow early contraction velocity and improve impaired relaxation; positive top-line Phase 2 CIRRUS-HCM Part D results were presented as late-breaking science at the European Society of Cardiology Congress in August 2026, the preclinical profile was published in JCI Insight, and a Phase 3 in obstructive AND non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is on track to initiate by year-end 2026. The second asset, EDG-15400, has dosed its first patient in the Phase 2 EQUINOX-HFpEF trial — heart failure with preserved ejection fraction is one of the largest untreated cardiovascular populations.

What changed. The most consequential pair of facts on any name in today's ten, and they are in the same filing. (1) ON 2026-07-10 SERVIER ACQUIRED SEVASEMTEN AND THE MUSCULAR DYSTROPHY PROGRAMME — including related assets, intellectual property, know-how, select employees, contracts, regulatory filings and clinical data — FOR $1,396.1 MILLION OF UPFRONT CASH, with aggregate potential consideration of up to $2,650.0 million; $7.8M of closing consideration was deferred against transition services. (2) AFTER THAT SALE CLOSED, 'SERVIER ANNOUNCED THAT THE PIVOTAL GRAND CANYON STUDY OF SEVASEMTEN IN BECKER MUSCULAR DYSTROPHY DID NOT MEET ITS PRIMARY OR SECONDARY ENDPOINTS', and Servier is conducting further analysis. So Edgewise sold its lead asset for $1.4bn in cash and the buyer's pivotal trial of that asset then failed. Two consequences follow directly: the roughly $1,254M of contingent milestone consideration beyond the upfront is now very unlikely to be earned and should be valued near zero, and the company's entire reported cash-flow history describes a business that no longer exists. On that reading the market is paying $2.76bn above net cash for two unproven assets from a platform whose only pivotal readout to date was negative, and the $1.4bn of cash is a one-off that flatters a company whose research has not yet produced an approval.
