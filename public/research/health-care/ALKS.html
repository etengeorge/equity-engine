The case for the price. The market is paying for a commercial base that funds a genuinely large orexin option. Alkermes closed the $2.3B acquisition of Avadel on 2026-02-12 — roughly $775M of balance-sheet cash plus $1.525B of term loans due 2031 — bringing LUMRYZ, the once-nightly sodium oxybate for narcolepsy, into a portfolio that already contains VIVITROL (about $468M of annual net sales, up 2%) and LYBALVI (about $347M, up 24%). That deal replaces the high-margin INVEGA SUSTENNA US royalty stream that expired in August 2024 and turned 2025 into a revenue decline; 2026 revenue is guided to grow roughly 21%, with LUMRYZ contributing $315-335M for the remainder of the year and Q3 total revenue guided to $450-470M. Behind the commercial base sits the actual reason the stock is up 62% in a year: alixorexton, an oral orexin-2 agonist in Phase 3 for narcolepsy with the Brilliance NT1 and NT2 studies enrolling and completion expected in 2027, plus ALKS 7290 in adult ADHD with topline results due by the end of Q3 2026 and a Vibrance-3 idiopathic hypersomnia readout expected in Q4. Owning LUMRYZ gives Alkermes the sleep-medicine commercial infrastructure to launch alixorexton into a franchise it already sells to. The implied +5.9% FCFF growth is what you pay when a solid base business is worth roughly the market price and the pipeline is thrown in.

What changed. The company was reshaped after the balance-sheet date the screen prices off. (1) 2026-02-12: acquisition of Avadel Pharmaceuticals completed following Irish High Court approval, funded with approximately $775M of cash and $1.525B of term loans due 2031, after a competing bid from Lundbeck forced a sweetened offer; roughly $1.5B of LUMRYZ intellectual property is being recorded and amortised over an expected 13-year life, and net interest expense is guided to $75-85M in 2026. (2) Q2 2026, reported 2026-07-28: proprietary product net sales up 34% year over year, first full quarter of LUMRYZ revenue, LYBALVI formulary coverage above 80%, no VIVITROL generic expected in 2027; most 2026 guidance reaffirmed with Q3 total revenue of $450-470M and proprietary net sales of $390-410M. (3) A CEO transition is under way alongside the orexin readouts. (4) Context for the base: 2025 revenue of $1,475.9M was down from $1,557.6M in 2024, primarily because the US INVEGA SUSTENNA royalty expired in August 2024; the Trinza and Hafyera royalties continue to be paid by J&J into May 2030.

Base case. I decline to supply one, and the reason is mechanical rather than a shrug at uncertainty. The forward DCF in this repo computes equity value as enterprise value minus debt plus cash, using the same committed extract the screen used — and that extract is the 2025-12-31 balance sheet, which shows $290.7M of total debt and $588.2M of cash and short-term investments. Six weeks after that date Alkermes drew $1.525B of term loans and spent about $775M of cash to buy Avadel. Any fair value I generate would therefore be overstated by roughly $1.5B of missing debt spread over 167.8M shares — on the order of $9 per share on a $46.91 stock — no matter how careful the growth rate. Supplying a number I know is wrong by that much, and letting it appear on a dashboard as a fair value, is precisely the failure mode this project exists to prevent. The reported +5.9% gap is not a signal either: it is the coincidence of a pre-acquisition cash-flow base compared against a post-acquisition market capitalisation using a pre-acquisition balance sheet. Two large errors happen to point in opposite directions — the FCFF base excludes LUMRYZ entirely, which understates cash flow, while the enterprise value excludes $1.5B of acquisition debt, which understates the claim on it — and the near-zero gap they produce is arithmetic luck, not a measurement.
