The case for the price. At $6.45 the market prices Embecta's free cash flow to the firm to shrink about 6-9% a year for five years (my $165M base, WACC 8.35-9.35%, the engine's 2% terminal), and the evidence for a shrinking core is strong. FY2026 adjusted organic constant-currency revenue is guided down 8.8-10.2%; US revenue fell 20.6% in the first nine months and 24.6% in Q3 after a large US retailer moved to a lower-cost pen needle. Q3 alone lost $20.1M to price and $19.9M to volume year on year. Management said the customer and payer-mix hit to net price was larger in Q3 than in Q2, and the year-on-year decline in retail insulin-pen prescriptions was slightly faster. Gross-to-net deductions rose to $157.2M on roughly $429M of Q3 gross sales (36.7%, vs 32.6% a year earlier). Adjusted gross margin fell from 67.2% to 58.2%. Two once-weekly basal insulins are now FDA-approved: Novo's Awiqli on 2026-03-26, with US launch in 2H 2026, and Lilly's Onswik (efsitora) on 2026-09-24. Each cuts a basal patient from 365 needles a year to 52. Oral GLP-1s (Wegovy pill, Foundayo) and automated insulin delivery for Type 2 keep delaying or replacing injections. The guide implies Q4 adjusted operating margin of about 16-18%, against 25.5% in Q3, which was flattered by distributor orders normalizing after a Q2 destock. On the balance sheet: $1,469M of principal debt, 3.7x net leverage, and covenants that step down to 3.75x first-lien net leverage and 3.0x interest cover in 2028. The revolver was cut from $500M to $310M, with $100M still maturing 2027-03-31, and the lenders now require monthly liquidity forecasts. The $639M term loan (Mar-2029) and $700M of notes (Feb-2030) have to be refinanced off a smaller EBITDA. Equity is about 20% of enterprise value, so one turn of EBITDA multiple moves the stock by roughly $5-6 a share. The stock has already doubled from about $3.2 since the Q3 beat, so the stabilization re-rating has happened.
