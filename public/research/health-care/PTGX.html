The case for the price. Protagonist has stopped being a clinical-stage biotech and become a royalty holder on two approved drugs, without taking on a commercial infrastructure. Icotrokinra, partnered with Johnson & Johnson, was approved by the FDA in March 2026 for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis and launched in the US the same month, triggering a $50M milestone and carrying tiered royalties of 6-10% plus up to $580M of further milestones, with Phase 3 programmes running in psoriatic arthritis, ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease. Rusfertide was approved on 2026-08-28 as Takeda's MIMRYLO for erythrocytosis in polycythemia vera, on VERIFY data showing 76.9% of patients phlebotomy-free over 32 weeks against 32.9% on placebo. Protagonist opted out of the Takeda collaboration in April 2026, which converts it into a pure royalty position: $200M received on opt-out, a further $200M opt-out fee and a $75M milestone triggered by approval, up to $775M of sales milestones, and worldwide royalties of 14-29%. That royalty rate is unusually high, it is worldwide, and it costs Protagonist nothing to earn. Against $849.5M of cash at 30 June plus roughly $275M more now triggered, a $9.3B market capitalisation is the market's estimate of the net present value of two royalty streams — one on a first-in-class drug for a disease whose standard of care is repeated bloodletting.

What changed. Almost everything, and most of it after the last 10-K. Icotrokinra FDA approval and US launch, March 2026, $50M milestone. Rusfertide opt-out exercised 2026-04-28, triggering a $200M fee. NDA accepted with priority review, PDUFA in Q3 2026. Four presentations of Phase 3 VERIFY and long-term REVIVE/THRIVE data at EHA in June 2026. Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-05): net income of $162.8M against a $34.8M loss a year earlier, on $213.5M of licence and collaboration revenue that included $192.4M of the Takeda opt-out payment; cash and marketable securities $849.5M. Then, three days before this brief, FDA approval of MIMRYLO (rusfertide) on 2026-08-28, which triggers the second $200M opt-out fee and the $75M approval milestone. The stock is down 6.5% over the five sessions spanning that approval — a sell-the-news reaction to the single largest de-risking event in the company's history.
