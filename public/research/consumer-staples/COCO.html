The case for the price. At $65.18 the market is paying $3.57bn of enterprise value — about 4.4x sales and roughly 21x guided adjusted EBITDA — for the brand leader in what its own management calls the fastest-growing category in the beverage aisle. The case is strong and mostly in the numbers: nine-month net sales up 32.7% to $639.7M, gross margin up 530bp to 42.2%, income from operations up 96.5% to $142.2M, full-year guidance RAISED to $810-820M of net sales and $170-175M of adjusted EBITDA, no debt, $152M of cash, and a July acquisition of Copra that buys entry into the super-premium Thai Nam Hom segment with a factory, a 48% three-year sales CAGR and more than $100M of 2026 net sales. A debt-free category leader compounding sales at 30% with a long international runway is the kind of asset the market pays 4x sales for.

What changed. Two things, in opposite directions. (1) THE ACQUISITION: Vita Coco closed the purchase of Copra, Inc. on 2026-07-22 for $175M upfront — 80% cash on hand, the balance in Vita Coco stock — plus EARNOUT CONSIDERATION PAYABLE IN 2029 BASED ON 2028 PERFORMANCE WITH A FLOOR OF $45 MILLION AND A CAP OF $100 MILLION (8-K items 1.01/2.01/3.02, 2026-07-22). Copra expects full-year 2026 net sales above $100M, predominantly in the Americas, with 'a very strong private label business'. The cash flow statement shows $139.3M paid net of $8.3M of cash acquired, and goodwill and intangibles now stand at $110.2M and $107.1M. (2) THE MARGIN IS ALREADY REVERSING, AND MANAGEMENT HAS DATED IT: gross margin was 44.7% in the first half, 38.0% in Q3, and FOURTH-QUARTER GROSS MARGIN IS GUIDED TO APPROXIMATELY 33% 'due to increases in cost of goods and no offsetting price actions expected until early 2027'. That is a decline of roughly 1,170 basis points from H1 to Q4 in a single year, disclosed by the company.

Base case. The engine's $62.5M base is far too low — it is the mean of fiscal 2023-2025 cfo-less-capex against a company whose nine-month 2026 free cash flow was already $151.1M and whose guided full-year adjusted EBITDA is $170-175M, so the HOG and JBI cross-check fires in the UNUSUAL direction. But the right correction is not to the current year either, because the current year is a margin peak the company has told me is ending. I normalise to the margin structure management has guided rather than the one just earned: roughly $850M of 2027 net sales at the guided ~33% gross margin is about $280M of gross profit, less SG&A annualising toward $175M from the nine-month $127.6M, gives operating income near $105M; taxed at 25% and adding back trivial D&A against modest capex (the nine-month capex line is only $0.8M, though the Thailand factory will raise it) gives owner earnings of roughly $95-110M. I use $110M, the generous end, and pair it with +8% growth — above the category's pricing but below the 30% top-line pace, because the volume runway is real while the margin tailwind has turned into a headwind.
