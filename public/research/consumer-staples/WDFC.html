The case for the price. WD-40 is a 70-year-old single-brand franchise with pricing power, a globally decentralised supply chain that management credits with absorbing tariff and input volatility, and a maintenance-products business that grew at the top of its own long-term target range in fiscal 2026. Net sales grew 11% to $688.0M, non-GAAP operating income improved 6% to $110.2M and non-GAAP EPS 6% to $6.18. Management states that the fiscal-2026 fourth quarter 'marked the low point for gross margin' and guides fiscal 2027 to 4-7% sales growth, 54.0-55.5% gross margin and $114-120M of operating income (+5% to +10%). The bull case for a 37x earnings multiple is that this is a consumer-staple compounder with a 2-point beta, a tiny capital base ($7.4M of capex on $688M of sales), 80%+ returns on tangible capital, and a product with no credible substitute - so the right discount rate really is low and the right terminal growth really is perpetual. Base case. WD-40's cash generation is remarkably stable and remarkably un-levered to reinvestment: CFO less capex of $97.6M, $83.4M and $87.8M across three years on capex of $7.4M, $4.5M and $4.2M, so essentially all of operating cash flow is free. The naive baseline of +7.3% is a four-year revenue CAGR that includes the 11% fiscal 2026, itself helped by price; I am below it because gross margin is at a cyclical low that management attributes to specialty-chemical input costs, and recovering it is in the guided 5-10% operating income growth rather than additive to it.
