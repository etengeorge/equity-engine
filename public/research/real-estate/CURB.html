The case for the price. Curbline is a two-year-old REIT spun off from SITE Centers on 2024-10-01 to own convenience centres in high-household-income suburbs, and it is the rare REIT that was spun with net cash and has been deploying it. At $29.33 against raised 2026 Operating FFO guidance of $1.25-1.27 per diluted share, the market is paying about 23x Operating FFO - a clear premium to shopping-centre REITs, which typically trade in the low-to-mid teens. What the premium buys: same-property net operating income up 3.4% in the third quarter 'driven by an acceleration in base rent growth from rent commencements', a 96.4% leased rate, and $734.9 million of cash and capital commitments available for future acquisitions, of which $612.1 million is expected gross proceeds from unsettled forward equity sales. So the market is capitalising external growth that is funded and contracted rather than hoped for, on a balance sheet carrying only $596.1M of debt against $2.27bn of equity. That is a defensible reason to pay a premium multiple and it is why the shares are up 28.2% over twelve months.
