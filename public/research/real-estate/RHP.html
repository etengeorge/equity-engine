The case for the price. Ryman owns the Gaylord group-convention hotels — very large, self-contained meeting properties with a booking model that is nothing like transient lodging: group business is contracted years ahead, which gives unusual forward visibility into occupancy and rate, and the properties are effectively irreplaceable because nobody is building 2,000-room convention resorts at current construction costs. Q2 2026 was an all-time record: consolidated revenue $749.0M, up 13.6%, net income $102.1M, adjusted EBITDAre $258.3M. Same-store hospitality RevPAR rose 5.2% to about $202 and total RevPAR 6.5% to about $524, each roughly 2.5 points ahead of expectations, and management raised the same-store hospitality adjusted EBITDAre guidance midpoint by $10M while guiding low-to-mid single-digit RevPAR growth for Q3 and Q4. Alongside that sits a second business the market values separately: Opry Entertainment Group did a record $144.0M of quarterly revenue, and management continues to discuss a path to greater independence for it with potential outside investors — an unpriced separation option. The market is paying for contracted forward group revenue, an irreplaceable asset base, and that optionality.

What changed. The balance sheet changed materially in three weeks. Q2 2026 (2026-08-07): record revenue $749.0M (+13.6%), net income $102.1M, adjusted EBITDAre $258.3M, same-store RevPAR +5.2%, total RevPAR +6.5%, Entertainment revenue a record $144.0M; unrestricted cash of $366.1M and total debt of $3,969.5M net of unamortised deferred financing costs at 30 June; 2026 capex guided to $400-500M. Then on 2026-08-10 a subsidiary agreed to purchase the JW Marriott Orlando Grande Lakes and the Ritz-Carlton Orlando Grande Lakes plus related assets for approximately $1.38B. It was funded with 5,100,000 shares at $117.00 plus a fully exercised 765,000-share option — about $658M of net proceeds, closed 2026-08-12 — together with a private placement of $700M of 6.250% senior notes due 2035 raising roughly $689M net, plus cash on hand. On Opry Entertainment, discussions with select potential investors continue and management states no agreement has been entered into.

Base case. None supplied. Ryman is a hotel REIT whose reported cash flow is dominated by non-cash depreciation on an irreplaceable asset base and by very large discretionary capex ($400-500M guided for 2026 against $749M of quarterly revenue). A two-stage FCFF model cannot separate maintenance from expansion capital here, and the engine is right to decline. I will not manufacture a fair value.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The counter to a neutral qualitative note is that a specific, dated, financeable event just happened and it deserves a view. Ryman has taken pro-forma debt from roughly $3.97B toward $4.67B and issued 5.865M shares — about 7% of the pre-deal count — to buy two Orlando luxury resorts for $1.38B. That is a large, levered, late-cycle acquisition of TRANSIENT and leisure-luxury hotels by a company whose entire competitive advantage is the CONTRACTED GROUP model. Grande Lakes is a different business with a different demand curve, and it is exactly the kind of diversification that historically ends REIT re-ratings. The equity is also trading at $129.33 against the $117.00 the company just issued at, so the market has marked the deal up by 10% before a single quarter of ownership.
  • What would prove it: The Grande Lakes properties' group-versus-transient booking mix and their contribution to same-store metrics once they enter the comparable set, together with pro-forma net-debt-to-EBITDAre after a full year. If the Orlando assets dilute the forward booking visibility that justifies Ryman's premium, the multiple should compress regardless of how accretive the deal looks on day one.
  • Already visible today: No — and that is the honest answer rather than a comfortable one. The deal is three weeks old, the notes priced at 6.250% which is unremarkable rather than distressed, the equity raise cleared at a 9% discount to today's price, and the core business is putting up records with raised guidance. There is no visible stress. But there is also no data yet on how the acquired assets perform inside this portfolio, so the counter is unfalsified rather than refuted.
  • Left unresolved: Whether $1.38B is the right price for the Grande Lakes assets, which I cannot judge without the purchase cap rate — it was not in any source I could reach, and I could not open the 2026-08-10 or 2026-08-25 8-Ks. Also unresolved: the Opry Entertainment separation, which is a real and potentially large source of value that is impossible to price while management says only that discussions continue and no agreement exists.
