The case for the price. MSG Entertainment owns or controls an irreplaceable set of New York venues - Madison Square Garden, Radio City Music Hall, the Beacon Theatre and the Chicago Theatre - plus the Christmas Spectacular, and fiscal 2027 has started strongly. Q1 revenue was $217.1M, up 37%, with adjusted operating income of $34.3M against $7.1M, driven by the start of Harry Styles' 30-show Garden residency and higher revenue per concert; food, beverage and merchandise alone rose 56%. Fiscal 2026 adjusted operating income was $262.2M, up 18%, on revenue of $1,060.8M, up 13%, and the Christmas Spectacular is running 231 performances this season against 215. At roughly 16x adjusted operating income for scarce live-entertainment real estate with demonstrated pricing power, a controlling family with a track record of surfacing asset value, and an open buyback, the market is paying for an asset base rather than a cash-flow multiple. That is a coherent case.

What changed. Fiscal 2027 first-quarter results, 2026-11-04. Revenue $217.1M +37%, operating income $9.7M (+$39.5M, helped by the absence of a prior-year impairment), adjusted operating income $34.3M (+$27.2M). But the cash statement inverts the picture, and the company explains it in its own words. Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was $351.4M against $115.3M, and the 10-K states the $236.1M increase was 'primarily due to an increase in cash flows from changes in working capital of $224,675 and an increase in net income adjusted for non-cash items of $11,463' - so 95% of it was timing, specifically 'lower payments to promoters driven by the timing of event settlements'. It reversed immediately: Q1 fiscal 2027 operating cash flow was NEGATIVE $26.2M against POSITIVE $19.8M, which the 10-Q attributes to '$78,306' of additional working-capital outflow 'primarily due to an increase in payments to promoters'. Unrestricted cash fell from $294M to $194M in the quarter. Management also flags that Garden concert volume, while up in the December quarter, 'is currently pacing lower in the second half of fiscal 2027 due to event timing'.

Base case. This is a fixed portfolio of seats. The Garden holds about 20,000 and runs roughly 200 events a year; Radio City holds 6,000. Growth therefore comes from utilisation and price, both of which have ceilings, and from the residency model, which is a way of raising utilisation at one venue with one artist. I underwrite about 6% a year - mid-single-digit price and per-capita spend growth plus modest incremental event count, which is roughly double nominal inflation and generous for an asset whose capacity cannot expand. I differ from the naive +10.8% revenue baseline because that is measured over the post-spin recovery years, and I differ sharply from the engine's base, which capitalises a one-year working-capital wave the company has already disclosed and already reversed.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: That a reverse DCF is simply the wrong lens on trophy real estate. Madison Square Garden is unreplicable; the controlling family has already split MSG Networks, Sphere and MSG Sports out of this entity precisely to surface asset value; and a buyer would pay for the arena, the air rights and the Christmas Spectacular annuity rather than for five years of discounted free cash flow. On that view 16x adjusted operating income is unremarkable and my 'required +20% growth' is an artefact of capitalising cash flow instead of assets.
  • What would prove it: A transaction - a sale, a sale-leaseback, a naming-rights or air-rights deal - or a permanent special permit that removes the 2028 cliff and lets the asset be financed as real estate.
  • Already visible today: The counter is weakened by what the assets actually are. Radio City and the Beacon are LEASED, not owned; only the Garden and the Chicago Theatre are owned. And the Garden's operating permit is not permanent: in September 2023 the New York City Council granted it a FIVE-year special permit to 2028 - the shortest in its history, explicitly tied to Penn Station redevelopment - and I found no reporting of a 2026 renewal, so the expiry sits INSIDE the five-year valuation window. An asset whose right to operate must be re-litigated in 2028 does not support an unconditional asset-value premium.
  • Left unresolved: I cannot value the real estate independently, and I cannot rule out that a control transaction clears well above my cash-flow number. That is why conviction is low despite a large recorded gap.
