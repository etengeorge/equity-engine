The case for the price. At $23.13 the market requires about +16.0% five-year FCFF growth on reported cash flow, and Magnite is currently delivering more than that on every operating measure: second-quarter contribution ex-TAC grew 17% to $189.6 million, beating the top of its own guidance, with CTV contribution ex-TAC up 36% to $97.1 million, and adjusted EBITDA up 30% to $70.6 million at a 37% margin against 34% a year earlier. Management raised 2026 free-cash-flow growth guidance to the high-40% range from the mid-30s. On top of the operating case sits a free option: on 2026-09-17 Judge Brinkema's remedies decision in the Google ad-tech case ordered the publisher ad server separated from AdX with real-time bidding access and six years of compliance monitoring, which is why StoneX went to $43, Bank of America to $38 and Craig-Hallum to $32. A buyer at $23.13 is paying roughly 11x forward adjusted EBITDA for the largest independent sell-side platform in a market whose dominant player has just been ordered to open its auction.

What changed. The Google remedies decision was unsealed around 2026-09-17: AdX was NOT divested, but the court ordered parts of the publisher ad server separated from AdX, real-time bidding access, a 60-day implementation window and six years of compliance monitoring, with a joint final judgment due from Google and the DOJ in early October. Magnite rose about 5% on the day, PubMatic 6%, while The Trade Desk fell - the market read it as a supply-side win. Magnite also has its own damages suit against Google. Operationally: Q2 2026 contribution ex-TAC $189.6 million (+17%), CTV $97.1 million (+36%), DV+ $92.5 million (+2%), adjusted EBITDA $70.6 million (+30%, 37% margin); revenue $192.8 million (+11.2%) and H1 $357.2 million (+8.5%). HP TV+ adopted Magnite's full stack as primary video ad server (2026-09-23). Brian Gephart was promoted to CFO on 2026-09-21, succeeding the retiring David Day. On 2026-10-07 Magnite repriced its $358 million Term Loan B 50bp lower to SOFR+2.50% and cut the revolver margin 100bp, worth about $1.8 million a year. The 0.75% convertible notes matured 2026-03-15 and none remain outstanding.

Base case. I underwrite +16% five-year FCFF growth, which is what a bottom-up build of the actual segments produces and which happens to be almost exactly what the market already requires. Contribution ex-TAC splits roughly half and half: CTV is $97.1 million of $189.6 million growing 36%, DV+ is $92.5 million growing 2%. Hold CTV at a decelerating 30% and DV+ at 2% and the blend is 51% x 30% + 49% x 2% = about 16.3%. Margin expansion (37% from 34%) argues FCFF could grow a little faster than the top line; against that, free cash flow has NOT grown - operating cash flow is nearly flat across three years at $214.4 million, $235.2 million and $236.2 million while adjusted EBITDA compounded 30%, and cfo-less-capex actually fell to $165.6 million from $202.4 million because fiscal 2025 capex more than doubled to $70.5 million. That capex is rolling off: H1 2026 property and equipment of $21.5 million plus capitalised software of $8.1 million is $29.6 million against $33.3 million in H1 2025, so roughly $59 million annualised. I differ from the naive +25% baseline because that figure is a five-year revenue CAGR measured from 2021's $221.6 million - the year Magnite ACQUIRED SpotX, having merged with Telaria in 2020 - so it is bought revenue, and the organic rate is the +17% contribution ex-TAC the company actually reports.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: That the stock-compensation treatment is the whole valuation and it should be settled against the company, in which case Magnite is substantially overvalued rather than fair. Stock compensation is 41% of the reported FCFF base - $75.2 million of $208.9 million - and expensing it moves the growth the market requires from +16.0% to +28.4%. A 28.4% five-year FCFF CAGR is above even the CURRENT adjusted-EBITDA growth rate and far above a business half of which (DV+) grows 2%. On the expensed base, even granting +16% growth the fair value is about $14 against a $23.13 price, roughly -40%.
  • What would prove it: The BOX test, run on BASIC weighted-average shares: whether the compensation is being paid for in cash. If the count is flat or falling net of buybacks, the compensation is bought and treating it as free is defensible; if the count is rising, shareholders are funding it through dilution and the expensed base is the honest one.
  • Already visible today: Yes, and IT GOES AGAINST THE COMPANY, which is why I will not call this fair with any confidence. Basic weighted-average shares were 143,024 thousand in Q2 2026 against 141,654 thousand in Q2 2025, and 143,281 thousand against 141,752 thousand for the six months - UP 1.0% - despite $35.5 million of treasury purchases in H1 2026 against $22.9 million a year earlier. So roughly $71 million a year of buybacks did not even hold the count flat. The cash construction is worse than the accounting charge: about 3.1 million shares were retired and the count still rose 1.37 million, implying roughly 4.5 million shares issued, so the cash cost of holding the count flat is nearer $100 million a year than the $75.2 million of reported expense - which would put owner earnings around $105 million, BELOW the expensed $133.7 million. I note the QTWO trap and avoided it: the DILUTED count fell (147,172 from 148,260) and that is not a buyback - the 0.75% convertible notes matured on 2026-03-15 and their if-converted shares simply dropped out, which the 10-Q states. So the counter wins on its own terms and the verdict is no_edge rather than fair: the market's requirement is +16% on a treatment the share count says is too generous, and +28.4% on the treatment the share count supports. A 12-point spread on one choice, straddling my own build, is not an edge in either direction.
  • Left unresolved: How much of the Google remedy reaches revenue, and when. The decision is behavioural rather than structural - AdX was not divested - and the clearest analysis I found says nothing shifts for Magnite until Google builds the required integrations, against a six-year compliance horizon. I cannot date or size it, and the stock is already up 92% in six months and 25% in 63 days partly on it, so some unknown fraction is in the price. I also cannot resolve whether fiscal 2025's doubled capex was a one-off CTV build or the new run rate; H1 2026 says it is rolling off, but two quarters is not a cycle.
