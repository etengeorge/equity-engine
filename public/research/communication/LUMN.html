The case for the price. Lumen is a levered bet on being the physical backbone of AI-era enterprise networking. Management is executing the pivot laid out at Investor Day: strategic revenue rose to approximately 53% of total business revenue from 51% in Q1, there are more than 3,000 NaaS customers with quarter-over-quarter growth in usage metrics, the Multi-Cloud Gateway now reaches more than 10 million US business locations including sites beyond Lumen's own fibre footprint, and the Alkira acquisition closed in the quarter to add software-defined networking. Q2 2026 revenue was $2.805B with adjusted EBITDA of $802M, and the company says it is on track for full-year guidance. The bull case is that the legacy copper and voice base declines predictably while the Private Connectivity Fabric deals with hyperscalers are pre-funded, capex peaks and then falls, and an equity stub with a $7.0B market cap against roughly $13B of net debt re-rates violently once free cash flow inflects. The tape is behaving like that argument is being made: +12.1% over five days and +14.7% over twenty-one, against -24.0% over sixty-three.

What changed. Q2 2026 (2026-08-04): revenue $2.805B; adjusted EBITDA $802M against $877M a year earlier, excluding special items of $204M and $152M respectively — so EBITDA is still declining year over year. Strategic revenue 53% of business revenue, up from 51%. Alkira acquisition closed in the quarter. Management reiterated the full-year guidance and long-term framework from Investor Day. A dense run of 8-Ks in May and June 2026 carries items 1.01 and 2.03 (2026-05-14 and 2026-05-21) — new or amended debt obligations — plus several 8.01 disclosures, consistent with continued liability-management activity after the earlier restructuring. Base case. I am not supplying a growth rate. Normalized FCFF is not positive — Lumen is spending through a multi-year fibre and PCF build that is designed to consume cash now and return it later — so the reverse DCF returns n/a at every discount rate and there is nothing to compare a forward case against. Even if there were, the discount rate would not survive contact with the balance sheet: the engine puts a 65% debt weight on a speculative, guessed cost of debt and stamps the result 13.5%, and its own flag says so. On a company where debt is roughly two-thirds of enterprise value and trades well away from par, a guessed cost of debt at that weight is not a WACC, it is a placeholder. Refusing is the correct answer here, and it is not the same as saying the equity is worthless.
