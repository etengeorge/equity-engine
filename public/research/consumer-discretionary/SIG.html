The case for the price. Signet is the largest specialty jewellery retailer in the US — Kay, Zales, Jared — with roughly 2,300 stores, $6.7-6.9B of guided fiscal 2027 sales, zero long-term debt and $526.8M of cash. At $102.48 the market caps it at $4.0B, an enterprise value of $3.47B against a $464.5M normalised FCFF base: a 13.6% FCFF yield, and a reverse DCF that says the price requires FCFF to DECLINE 9.5% a year for five years. The strongest case for that price is not that Signet is badly run, it is that the category is deflating. Lab-grown diamonds have collapsed the cost of the central input in bridal, which is roughly half of Signet's sales; unit volumes are already falling — Q2 sales fell 0.5% in absolute dollars while average unit retail rose about 6%, which arithmetically means units fell around 6%. Signet is shrinking to fit: net square footage down low single digit for the year, the James Allen brand being transitioned out at a cost of $60-80M of revenue, Rocksbox decommissioned, and $31.3M of inventory write-downs taken against those two closures in the first half. Gold at record highs is compressing merchandise margin in the same period. A market pricing permanent single-digit decline in a mall-based jeweller losing 6% of its units a year is not obviously wrong.

What changed. Q2 fiscal 2027 was reported 2026-09-09 with the 10-Q, and the stock rose 26% in five days on 2.2x average volume. Sales $1,528.1M, down 0.5% year over year, on same-store sales of +2.2% and AUR up ~6%. GAAP operating income $87.5M against $2.8M in a heavily-impaired prior-year quarter; adjusted operating income $107.2M against $85.4M; adjusted diluted EPS $2.19 against $1.61, +36%. Full-year guidance was raised for the second time this year: adjusted operating income to $535-605M from $480-560M, adjusted EBITDA to $730-800M from $665-745M, adjusted diluted EPS to $10.45-12.15 from $9.20-11.00. TOTAL SALES GUIDANCE WAS NOT CHANGED, at $6.7-6.9B. The quality of the raise is the story. The guidance assumptions name 'Refunds of tariffs previously paid of approximately $30 million' and '$30 to $40 million of non-comp revenue and gross margin from the new consumer credit agreement' — $60-70M of stated non-operating contribution against an adjusted EBITDA raise of $55-65M. Signet has received ~$20.4M of IEEPA refunds to date, of which $14.9M was booked as a reduction to cost of sales; the Q2 gross-margin improvement of 80bp was built on approximately $15M of tariff refunds, which is about 98bp of margin on $1,528M of sales, so EX-REFUND Q2 GROSS MARGIN WAS DOWN ROUGHLY 18bp YEAR OVER YEAR. Year to date, gross margin was 37.6% against 38.7%, down 110bp, on gold prices, accelerated melt and the James Allen/Rocksbox write-downs. Separately, Signet extended its consumer credit partnership with Bread Financial through December 2035, with a profit-sharing arrangement and a signing bonus recognised over the life of the agreement (8-K item 1.01, 2026-09-09), and announced a $125M accelerated share repurchase with the authorisation expanded to $700M.

From there the honest arithmetic is: sales flat with a low-single-digit square-footage runoff underneath, merchandise margin under persistent pressure from gold (cited as a drag in both the quarter and the year-to-date discussion) and from unit deflation, offset by genuine SG&A leverage (32.3% of sales from 32.9%, driven by operating model changes rather than by one-offs) and by $30-40M a year of high-margin credit revenue that is now contracted for a decade. AUR up 6% in both Bridal and Fashion says pricing is holding, which is the single fact that most contradicts a lab-grown collapse thesis. But units are falling about 6%, and a category that loses units while raising price is mixing up, not growing. -3% respects that; -9.5% assumes the pricing holds nowhere.
