The case for the price. Strattec makes locks, latches, keys, power access and security systems for vehicles, selling into a customer list dominated by GM, Ford and Stellantis. The market pays 4.3x EV/EBITDA and requires FCFF to fall 23.6% a year for five years, and it does so for structural reasons that have nothing to do with this year's results being good. Auto suppliers of this size are price-takers: annual give-backs are contractual, tariff costs are largely passed back to the supplier under customer agreements, and the customer base is the three most volume-exposed North American OEMs. Management guided fiscal 2027 to lower North American production — down about 2% overall, and nearly 6% at Ford, Stellantis and GM specifically — with the Mexican peso at 16.90 against an 18.00 average, worth roughly 100bp of gross margin. Cash generation is also demonstrably unstable: operating cash flow was $12.3M two years ago, $71.7M last year, $46.3M this year. A market that has watched that series does not capitalise the good years.

What changed. Fiscal 2026 results (announced 2026-08-25, 10-K filed 2026-08-28) were a record: revenue $579.4M, up 2.5% on $565.1M, gross margin up 150bp to 16.5%, adjusted EBITDA up 15% to $50.5M, EPS up 9% to $5.00, operating cash flow $46.3M, and a year-end balance sheet of $108.2M cash and no debt. Q4 net sales of $151.8M were flat year over year but above management's own expectations, with Q4 gross margin 15.6% and net income $8.4M ($2.06 per diluted share). Pricing actions added 2% to revenue, offsetting tariffs and cancelled EV programmes. Management said cancelled EV programmes should stop being an incremental headwind, targets 18-20% gross margin over the next several years, and guided fiscal 2027 capex to about $12M for automation and process modernisation. The stock is nonetheless down 15% over 21 sessions and 8% over five.

Base case. I assume FCFF declines about 2% a year for five years off a base I have cut to $33M. The screen's $52.0M base is the average of only two years, and those happen to be the two good ones — the extract holds three years of operating cash flow ($46.3M, $71.7M, $12.3M) but only two years of capex, so the model silently dropped the $12.3M year and averaged the survivors. Rebuilding from the income statement instead: adjusted EBITDA of $50.5M, less the $12M of capex management has guided for fiscal 2027, less roughly $6-7M of cash tax, against essentially no interest on a debt-free balance sheet, gives $32-34M of maintainable free cash flow. Net income of $20.6M plus D&A less that capex lands in the same place. On the growth rate: fiscal 2027 is a genuine down year — Big Three production off roughly 6% and a 100bp peso headwind — but the transformation is delivering measurably rather than aspirationally (gross margin +150bp, EBITDA +15% on 2.5% revenue growth), and the 18-20% gross margin target on $570M of revenue is worth roughly $9-20M of incremental gross profit against a $50M EBITDA base. A down year followed by margin-led recovery averages out slightly negative. That is far below the naive +3.6% revenue CAGR, which mechanically applies top-line growth to a cash-flow base that has nothing to do with it.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The gap that survives my base-year correction is not a mispricing, it is a disagreement about terminal multiple, and the market is the one with the evidence. This model discounts at 9.85% to a 2.5% perpetuity, which is a 13.5x multiple on terminal free cash flow. Auto suppliers do not get 13.5x and never have — the sector trades at 3.5-5x EV/EBITDA, and Strattec's 4.3x sits squarely inside that. The reason is not sentiment: the terminal value of a mechanical-lock-and-latch supplier to three North American OEMs is genuinely uncertain, because vehicle architecture is being redesigned around digital access, and a supplier whose product is a physical key has a real chance of not having a perpetuity at all. Every dollar of the gap I am reporting comes from assuming it does.
  • What would prove it: Content-per-vehicle on new programme awards, and the mix between legacy mechanical products and the power-access and electronic security lines. If new awards are replacing mechanical content one-for-one or better with electronic content, the perpetuity is safe and the multiple is wrong. If Strattec's content is shrinking per vehicle as architectures change, the market's implied decay is the honest number and the terminal value should not be there.
  • Already visible today: Ambiguous, and I have to say so. Cancelled EV programmes have already cost Strattec real revenue — that is a live example of architecture change destroying content, and management flagged it explicitly. Against it, the same management says cancelled EV programmes are no longer an incremental headwind and is spending $12M a year automating, which is not what you do to a business you expect to run off. I could not read the 10-K's programme-award or content-per-vehicle disclosure, so I cannot resolve which pattern dominates.
  • Left unresolved: The terminal multiple, which is the whole disagreement, and I could not close it. I also could not reconcile the $71.7M operating cash flow year against $18.7M of net income in the same year — a working-capital release of that size matters to what 'maintainable' means, and it is the reason I cut the base to $33M rather than trusting any single year. And there is no dated catalyst here: LESSONS is explicit that a small-cap value gap without an event that forces re-pricing is usually early, and this one has none.

Key risks. Terminal value is the whole gap — a mechanical access supplier may not have a perpetuity as vehicle architectures go digital; Customer concentration in GM, Ford and Stellantis, whose 2027 production is guided down about 6%; Peso strength worth roughly 100bp of gross margin, and tariff costs that customer agreements push back onto the supplier; Operating cash flow has swung $12.3M / $71.7M / $46.3M across three years — 'maintainable' is a judgement, not a measurement; No dated catalyst; 3.99M shares and $7.4M average daily dollar volume make this genuinely hard to own in size Watch for.
