The case for the price. At $28.30 Harley-Davidson is a $2.9B equity on a company that will earn roughly $150-180M this year, i.e. about 17x, with $1.9B of cash on the balance sheet and an aggressive buyback ($158M / 7.9M shares in Q2 alone). The bull argument the market is paying for is the 'Back to the Bricks' reset under Artie Starrs: North American retail up 3% to 29,751 units in Q2, market share gains, dealer inventory under control, and a raised FY26 guide that moved HDMC operating income from a $40M loss-to-$10M profit range up to $10-50M. The bear side of the same price is what the guide actually says: HDMC $10-50M plus HDFS $55-65M less LiveWire $70-80M sums to consolidated segment operating income of roughly -$5M to +$35M for the full year. The market is paying 17x for a company whose operating income this year rounds to zero and whose cash generation now depends on a motorcycle business, not on a finance book.

What changed. The company changed shape, and it happened after the fiscal year the model prices. HDFS has moved to a capital-light model: the retail loan assets were sold in the second half of 2025, and total quarter-end net finance receivables were $2.6B at 2026-06-30, a 64% decline year over year. HDFS revenue fell 55% and HDFS operating income fell 69% to $22M in Q2 from $70M; the FY26 guide is $55-65M against $133.8M earned in the FIRST HALF of 2025 alone. Consolidated debt fell from $6,854.8M at 2025-06-30 to $2,242.4M at 2026-06-30. Consolidated operating cash flow for the six months to 2026-06-30 was NEGATIVE $59.7M, against positive $509.5M in the same period of 2025. Q2 net income attributable to HDI was $80M, down 26%, and diluted EPS $0.75, down 15%.

Base case. The forward business is a motorcycle manufacturer with a small finance arm and a loss-making EV subsidiary, not the levered lender the FCFF history describes. Building the base from the segments: HDMC adjusted EBITDA was $189.1M in the first half and the second half is seasonally weaker, so call it ~$300M for the year; LiveWire runs at about -$60M of adjusted EBITDA; HDFS contributes $55-65M of operating income; consolidated capital investment is guided at $175-200M; cash taxes on ~$190M of pre-tax income are ~$40M. That triangulates to roughly $130-150M of FCFF, and the net income route agrees ($150M net income plus ~$161M of HDMC D&A less ~$190M capex plus ~$11M after-tax interest = ~$132M). I use $140M. On growth, I assume the reset works partially: HDMC operating income recovers from a near-zero base toward mid-single-digit margins over five years while global unit volumes stay roughly flat to slightly down and LiveWire's drag shrinks. That is a low-single-digit compounding of a small base, not a recovery to the pre-2020 earnings power, because the finance book that produced a third of profit has been sold and is not coming back. I differ from the naive +2.0% baseline not in the rate but in the base: the baseline applies revenue history to an FCFF number that belongs to a different company.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The strongest case that I am too cautious: the capital-light shift is a GOOD thing that the cash-flow optics disguise. Selling a $7B retail loan book removed a low-return, capital-hungry, credit-exposed balance sheet and left $1.9B of cash against $2.2B of debt. Consolidated CFO is negative in the half precisely because HDFS interest and collections no longer flow through operating; that is a change in the shape of the cash flows, not a deterioration in the business. On that reading, 17x this year's depressed earnings for a debt-free-ish brand buying back 5% of itself annually is not expensive, and the reverse-DCF's -27.7% implied growth is the market failing to see through the transition.
  • What would prove it: Full-year 2026 consolidated free cash flow, and whether HDMC operating income lands at the top or the bottom of the $10-50M guide. If HDMC clears $50M with the second half seasonally weak, the margin recovery is real.
  • Already visible today: Some of it. HDMC operating income was $72.3M in Q2 against $61.3M a year earlier — up 18% while the consolidated number fell — and HDMC adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 10.4% from 9.3%. That is genuine evidence for the counter-case and it is why my base case is +3% rather than negative. What is not visible is any half in which the new shape generates cash: H1 2026 operating cash flow is -$59.7M.
  • Left unresolved: I could not determine how much of the H1 operating cash outflow is a one-time unwind of HDFS working capital versus the new run rate, because the 10-Q does not separate the two. Until a full year of the capital-light structure is on the record, my $140M base is an estimate built from segment guidance, not a measured number.

Data quality. The brief raised NO flags, and both of the numbers it is built on are wrong. (1) Enterprise value is understated. The 2026-06-30 balance sheet shows short-term debt $613.1M, current portion of long-term debt $498.5M and long-term debt $1,130.8M — $2,242.4M of debt — plus $516.5M of brokered certificates of deposit, against $1,895.8M of cash. Correct EV is ~$3.25B excluding deposits or ~$3.76B including them. The brief's $2.7B reconciles exactly to market cap plus long-term debt and its current portion less cash, i.e. the extractor picked up the LongTermDebt family and dropped the $613.1M of unsecured commercial paper and the $516.5M of deposits. This is the ALTG revolver/ABL bug of 2026-09-02 in a new tag family, and it runs in the same direction: missing debt lowers EV, lowers implied growth and manufactures cheapness. (2) The FCFF base of $633.1M — the mean of $415.2M, $867.3M and $547.5M — is the cash flow of a company with a $7B captive finance book that was SOLD in the second half of 2025. This is the Lands' End lesson of 2026-09-04: a structural transaction can make the entire FCFF history obsolete without firing a single flag, because it changes the shape of the business rather than the share count or the balance sheet. I have overridden the base to $140M built from the company's own FY26 segment guidance. Because the recorded fair value is still computed against the engine's understated net debt, it is roughly $4-5 per share too generous; read the verdict, not the number.
