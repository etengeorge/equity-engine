The case for the price. Dorian owns twenty-five modern VLGCs into the strongest freight market the sector has ever seen, and the market is paying for that. On 2026-09-04 the company disclosed that for the quarter ending 2026-09-30 it has fixed 99% of its calendar days at a rate in excess of $88,000 per day, before any demurrage. That is above the record $75,926 per available day it earned in the June quarter, which itself produced record TCE revenue, revenue of $187.9M, net income of $138.3M and $1 billion of cumulative shareholder returns. The drivers named on the call are real and dated: Middle East LPG supply disruption and Panama Canal congestion, both of which lengthen ton-miles. Twenty-five ships at $88,000 a day is roughly $760M of annualised TCE revenue against vessel operating costs of order $100M, so at spot rates the company is generating something like $600M of EBITDA against a $2.5B enterprise value — barely four times. The bull case needs nothing more than for the current market to persist, and the stock is up 97.2% over 252 days because that is what people are underwriting.
