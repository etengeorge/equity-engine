The case for the price. Tidewater is the largest pure-play OSV operator in a market where supply is genuinely fixed: essentially no newbuild orderbook, a fleet that shrank through the 2015-2021 depression, and demand rising across all three legs (production support, subsea/EPCI, drilling support). Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $342.3M (+4.9% sequential), adjusted EBITDA of $133.8M and 46.9% gross margin, with realized day rates up $655/day — the biggest absolute sequential move since Q3 2024 — and the weighted average NEW term fixture up 7.5% sequentially to $24,341/day. Management guides day rates $3,000-$4,000/day higher in each of 2027 and 2028 on a ~$22,938 base, i.e. roughly +14%/yr on the single most operationally levered line in the P&L, against a cost base that is largely fixed. The WSUT acquisition closed 31 Aug 2026 and takes the Brazilian fleet from 6 vessels to 28 in a market with long contract tenors and BNDES-subsidised debt rolled over at attractive terms. On that arithmetic the buyer of the stock at $100.61 is paying for two to three years of visible, contracted rate escalation on a fleet that cannot be replicated at anything near book cost.

What changed. Three things after the last 10-K and after the 30 Jun 2026 balance sheet the model is built on. (1) On 31 Aug 2026 TDW CLOSED the Wilson Sons Ultratug / Atlantic Offshore acquisition: per the 8-K, $500M aggregate purchase price on a debt-free cash-free basis, settled as approximately $283.1M of cash paid at closing plus assumption of approximately $229.3M of existing BNDES and Banco do Brasil debt ($170.1M BNDES construction loans, $36.7M BNDES C&D loans, $22.5M BB loan). Both legs post-date the balance sheet in the model. (2) Q2 2026 results (3 Aug): revenue $342.3M, net income $21.7M, adjusted EBITDA $133.8M, free cash flow $64.4M, and 2026 guidance revised to $1.42-1.47B revenue at 49-50% gross margin pro forma for WSUT. (3) BTIG upgraded to Buy on 1 Sep on expected day-rate escalation, and the stock is +9% in 5 days and +41% in 21 days into that.

Base case. Day rates are contracted upward for roughly two years and then the visibility stops. Management's own $3,000-$4,000/day guidance for 2027 and 2028 is ~14%/yr on rate, and because vessel operating cost is near-fixed, that drops through at a very high incremental margin — so years 1-2 of a five-year FCFF path plausibly compound in the mid-to-high teens. Years 3-5 are the problem. OSV is the most violently cyclical business in energy services; Tidewater itself went through Chapter 11 in 2017, and the current cycle has already taken realized day rates from roughly $9,000 to $22,938. The supply argument that justifies today's rates is exactly the argument that gets falsified once rates stay high long enough to make newbuilds economic, and 22 more vessels arriving via WSUT is itself capacity being added to the market. Blending two strong years into three flat-to-declining ones gives roughly +6% compound FCFF growth off the $264.6M normalized base — which is essentially where H1 2026 actually ran ($64.4M of free cash flow in Q2 annualizes to ~$258M), so unlike most names in this screen the base is not the argument.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: The +117.6% gap that pulled TDW into an opportunistic slot is manufactured, and once the manufacturing is removed the name is not cheap at all — it may be expensive. Three separate defects push the same way. (i) The naive baseline is a +25.0% revenue CAGR (clamped down from +27.8%), and that CAGR is not organic growth: it is the Swire Pacific Offshore and Solstad fleet acquisitions inside the window. Applying acquired revenue growth to per-share free cash flow double-counts, exactly the TEX error recorded on 2026-09-02. (ii) The enterprise value of $5.0B is built on the 30 Jun 2026 balance sheet and therefore misses the entire WSUT settlement: $283.1M of cash out and $229.3M of debt assumed, ~$512M of understatement, so true pro forma EV is ~$5.5B. Market cap already prices the combined company; EV does not. Understated EV understates implied growth and manufactures a positive gap, the same signature as the convertible-notes and ABL bugs already in LESSONS. (iii) Most seriously, the WACC of 8.26% rests on a beta of 0.70 whose R-squared is 0.082, recorded as beta_source=regression because BETA_MIN_R2 is only 0.04. A noisy regression attenuates its slope toward zero, so the engine has concluded that an offshore support vessel operator is materially LOWER beta than the Russell 2000 — which is economically absurd. At a beta of ~1.4, still conservative for OSV, cost of equity goes from 8.67% to ~11.9% and WACC from 8.26% to ~11.2%; on the brief's own sensitivity of roughly +3.7pts of implied growth per +1pt of WACC, the growth the market requires moves from +5.6% to roughly +16%, before the EV correction adds more. Against a +16% hurdle my +6% base case makes TDW rich, not cheap.
  • What would prove it: Two observables. For the beta: recompute the regression with a longer window or accept the sector-median Energy beta and re-derive WACC — if implied growth lands in the mid-teens, the gap was never real. For the cycle: the 2027 term-contract fixture book. TDW discloses weighted average NEW term fixture day rate each quarter ($24,341 in Q2, +7.5% sequential); if that series flattens or rolls over for two consecutive quarters while utilization holds, the $3-4k/day guidance is not going to be met and the two strong years at the front of my growth path disappear.
  • Already visible today: Partly. The beta defect is fully visible and verified now — I checked beta_source and beta_r2 in data/screen.json directly, and TDW's 0.082 is among the worst in the index. The EV understatement is verified against the 31 Aug 8-K. What is NOT yet visible is any deterioration in the fixture book: Q2's leading-edge rate accelerated rather than decelerated, and management called the quarter 'nicely above expectations'. So the model-artifact half of the counter wins outright; the cycle half does not yet.
  • Left unresolved: I cannot settle what the correct discount rate for this business actually is, and the whole disagreement turns on it. The schema has no field to override cost of equity, so I cannot record the corrected valuation even though I believe it is the right one — the same limitation hit on GLRE on 2026-09-02. I also could not size WSUT's standalone EBITDA contribution: the 8-K gives purchase price and assumed debt but not acquired earnings, so I cannot tell whether the ~$512M of added EV is accretive or dilutive to the FCFF yield.
