The case for the price. Cactus is a high-return wellhead and pressure-control manufacturer with essentially no net debt, and the market has paid up for it — the stock is +62.4% over twelve months and +13.7% over sixty-three days. A buyer is underwriting the Cactus International integration (the former Baker Hughes surface pressure control business, a 65%-owned group acquired with a 35% redeemable minority interest) plus a differentiated North American wellhead franchise with a genuine share position. The engine cannot express any of that, because it has computed neither a free cash flow base nor an enterprise value for this name.

What changed. The 2026-06-30 10-Q (filed 2026-07-31) shows the Cactus International acquisition accounted for in full: a 35% noncontrolling interest and a net put/call valued at $146.7M sitting in Mezzanine Equity, remeasured to redemption value each period at a multiple of EBITDA, plus $19.6M of other noncontrolling interests. Liquidity: $365.8M of cash, of which $92.5M is held for restructuring related to that acquisition, $223.7M available under the Amended ABL Credit Facility with no outstanding borrowings, a $100.0M delayed-draw Term Loan Facility that remains UNDRAWN and whose maturity was extended in June 2026 to 2026-12-31 (8-K items 1.01 and 2.03, 2026-06-02), and a separate $75.0M secured facility for Cactus UAE. A September 2026 investor presentation was filed as EX-99.1 on 2026-09-08.

Base case. No growth rate is supplied because there is no base to grow. capex_series is EMPTY — not understated, absent — so CFO less capex cannot be computed at all, and the brief reports 'Normalized FCFF base n/a (mean of CFO-capex over 0y)' with no_cashflow_history and no_enterprise_value flags.
