The case for the price. Flex LNG owns thirteen modern LNG carriers, almost all on long-term time charter, and the market pays $28.64 for a $3.00 annual dividend - a 10.5% yield - against about $1.79bn of debt with no maturities before 2029. The reverse DCF says the market requires roughly -6% five-year FCFF growth at the engine's discount rate, which for a fixed fleet with no newbuildings on order is very close to the right answer: revenue cannot grow by adding ships, only by re-chartering at better rates, and the fleet ages. Q2 2026 was the best quarter since Q4 2021 - $106.8M of vessel revenue, fleet TCE of $86,119 a day against $65,729 in Q1, adjusted net income $42.5M - and yet the company HELD full-year guidance at $345-370M of revenue and $73,000-78,000 a day, which against a first half of roughly $192M implies a second half of $153-178M. A company guiding the second half DOWN off a record quarter is telling you the quarter is not the run rate, and the market has listened.

Base case. The +299.5% gap is the naive baseline and nothing else: '5y revenue CAGR +16.1%' measures the 2018-2021 newbuilding delivery programme, when Flex took delivery of its fleet, and applies it to FCFF as if the fleet were still being built. There are no newbuildings on order, so that growth rate is structurally unrepeatable and the baseline is void - the same shape as PATK's COVID-trough-to-peak CAGR, measured across a corporate transition rather than a cycle.
