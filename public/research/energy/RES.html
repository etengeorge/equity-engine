The case for the price. RPC is a debt-free oilfield services company with $179.5M of cash, no borrowings on its $100M revolver, and a business that has been deliberately shifted away from commodity pressure pumping toward higher-margin technical services. Q2 2026 revenue of $460.9M was up 1% sequentially and up 9.5% year-on-year with adjusted EBITDA of $66.0M against $53.5M in Q1 — a 250bp sequential margin expansion to 14.3% achieved in a flat activity market, driven by job mix in Pressure Pumping and double-digit growth in Snubbing, Cementing and Downhole Tools. The buyer at $6.57 is paying roughly 5.4x EV/EBITDA on an annualized run rate for a balance sheet that can survive a downturn intact and, per management, 'invest opportunistically' while levered competitors cannot. That is a call option on Permian consolidation priced as a melting ice cube.

What changed. Q2 2026 (30 Jul): revenue $460.9M, adjusted EBITDA $66.0M, margin +250bp sequentially. Critically, year-to-date through Q2 net cash from operating activities was $74.6M and FREE CASH FLOW was $3.8M — essentially nothing — with working capital a large use of cash on higher receivables. 2026 capex guidance was RAISED to $170-190M to support targeted growth. Management stated it has no plans to reactivate frac fleets at current pricing and flagged that New Mexico natural gas takeaway constraints could cap customer activity. The Pintail Completions wireline business (acquired 1 Apr 2025 for ~$245M) saw revenue DECLINE sequentially, and a $20.0M principal payment was made on the Pintail note early in Q2. In June the revolver maturity was extended to 30 June 2031.

Base case. North American pressure pumping is structurally oversupplied and has been deflationary for three years; RPC's own management will not reactivate idle fleets at current pricing, which is the clearest possible statement that marginal returns do not clear the cost of capital. What RPC has instead is mix — Snubbing, Cementing, Downhole Tools and Rental Tools growing while Wireline shrinks — and that improves margin without adding much to the free cash flow base, because the growth is being funded by a capex budget just raised to $170-190M against roughly $240M of annualized adjusted EBITDA. Netting those leaves $50-70M of pre-tax free cash flow in 2026, and year-to-date free cash flow of $3.8M says the conversion is worse than that in practice. I therefore underwrite zero real FCFF growth over five years off the $132.3M normalized base: mix improvement and eventual activity recovery on one side, secular frac deflation and a permanently higher capital intensity on the other. That is materially below the naive +22.1% baseline and I explain why in the devil's-advocate section — the baseline is not a growth rate at all.
