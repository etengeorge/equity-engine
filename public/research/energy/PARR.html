The case for the price. Par Pacific owns 219,000 bpd of refining across Hawaii, the Pacific Northwest and the Rockies - logistically advantaged, import-parity-priced markets - and is in the middle of an extraordinary refining cycle. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $571.3M against $137.8M a year earlier, refining segment adjusted EBITDA $552.0M against $108.4M, and adjusted net income $499.2M, or $10.10 per diluted share, on a $76.97 stock. Second-quarter operating cash flow before working capital and turnaround outflows was $614.3M. At a $4.4bn enterprise value the market is paying under 2x ANNUALISED PEAK EBITDA, which is what refiners always trade at near a peak, and the engine's +2.7% implied growth off a mid-cycle base says the market is neither extrapolating the peak nor pricing a collapse. That is a defensible place to be.

What changed. Q2 2026 results (2026-08-05): the numbers above, plus throughput of 181 Mbpd against 187 Mbpd. Working capital consumed $(312.2)M in the quarter and deferred turnaround expenditures $(19.5)M, which management expects 'a substantial portion' of to reverse as commodity prices normalise and Hawaii inventory returns to typical levels post-turnaround. Separately, the 2026-08-25 8-K discloses that Laramie Energy - in which Par owns a 46% non-controlling interest - agreed to sell substantially all its oil and gas assets for $485M cash; Par expects to receive roughly $146M (about $27.5M of it payable on the fifth anniversary) plus up to ~$30M of earn-outs, and will exit the investment. Expected to close by end-2026. That is about 3% of market capitalisation and not decisive. Balance sheet at 2026-06-30: total debt $739.2M, cash $185.0M.

Base case. I am not supplying one, because the input that decides the answer is the cash-flow base and it is not knowable within a factor of two. The engine's $301.5M is the mean of [$296.5M, -$51.8M, $496.9M] - a series that contains a negative year and spans 2.2x of its own mean, as the flags say. Against it sits a company that just earned $571.3M of adjusted EBITDA in ONE QUARTER. So this is the AMR/LPG problem in its sharpest form: the base is not a peak or a trough, it is a three-year average of a cycle whose current point bears no relation to it. Running the reverse DCF across every base and discount rate I would defend, the growth the market requires spans +14.4% (base $250M, WACC 11.5%) to -14.3% (base $650M, WACC 9.2%), and the sign flips somewhere between a $302M and a $400M base. A twenty-nine-point span driven by two inputs I cannot pin is not a valuation, and the discount rate is no help: beta comes from the Energy sector median because the name's own regression has an R-squared of 0.004, and 0.89 is almost certainly too low for a levered refiner.

Devil's advocate.

  • Strongest counter: That refusing here is cowardice, and the tape is telling me something: at $4.4bn of enterprise value against $571.3M of quarterly adjusted EBITDA, Par trades at 1.9x annualised current earnings. Even assuming a violent reversion to the FY2025 level of $296.5M of free cash flow, that is 14.8x - unremarkable rather than expensive - and the stock is +52% in 63 days on exactly this recognition. A refiner bought at 2x peak EBITDA with $554M of net debt and large NOL carryforwards has historically been a good trade.
  • What would prove it: Q3 2026 results and the Q4 crack-spread environment - specifically whether the working capital outflow reverses and whether Singapore/USWC product cracks hold above mid-cycle.
  • Already visible today: Partly, and it cuts both ways. The Q2 strength is real and documented. But the 2024 year in the engine's own window is NEGATIVE $51.8M of free cash flow, which is what this business does at the bottom, and no free source tells me where in that range 2027-2030 sits. The counter is strong enough that I am explicitly NOT recording this as rich.
  • Left unresolved: The entire question - where mid-cycle is. I could not find a defensible mid-cycle free-cash-flow number for this asset base from free sources, and the company does not guide to one. I also could not correct the discount rate: the beta is a sector median standing in for a failed regression, and moving it from 0.89 to a refiner-like 1.2 shifts the required growth by roughly four points on its own.
