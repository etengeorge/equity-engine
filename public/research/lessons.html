Standing lessons

Priors about how this kind of thesis goes wrong. The analyst reads these before every session and is held to them. Not scored results — no thesis has matured yet.

LESSONS — self-critique that compounds (seeded 2026-08-22; no thesis has matured yet)

No thesis has reached its pinned evaluation window, so there are no scored patterns yet. The retrospective rewrites this file once theses mature (12-36 month horizons; first grades expected mid-2027). Until then, these are the STANDING priors the red team should hold the first analyst to:

  • Small-cap mean-reversion theses are usually early. A margin trough is not a catalyst; a dated

event that forces the market to re-price the margin is. Horizon under 12 months on a cyclical_mean_reversion call is almost always wrong.

  • expectations_sentiment theses have the weakest base rate: "the market is too negative" needs a

specific, dated reason the negativity resolves, or it is not a thesis.

  • A reverse-DCF gap that flips sign inside the +/-15% FCFF band is noise. Conviction is capped

at 2 mechanically; do not argue it back up.

  • The market is usually right about a small-cap with fewer than 3 analysts. Coverage gaps cut both

ways: less competition for the idea, but also less forced re-rating when you are right.

  • One-time items (tariff refunds, legal settlements, insurance recoveries, asset sales) inflate

normalized FCFF. Every gap over 40% is a data question first.

  • Sources: a single uncorroborated story is a lead, not a fact. Two independent sources or an

8-K is the bar for re-rating.

  • A peak can fill the whole window. The peak-cycle flag compares the newest FCF in the window

to the oldest, so it stays silent when all three years sit at an elevated level — the case that matters most. Before accepting a stable FCFF base, check it against the company's pre-window history, not just against itself. (Added 2026-08-31 from the BBW pass: three flat years at ~$38M, no flag, against a fiscal 2018 pre-tax LOSS and $1.6M of pre-tax income in fiscal 2019. Flat is not the same as normal.)

  • The balance sheet is as stale as the fiscal year end, and nothing flags it. Three of ten names

on 2026-08-31 had an enterprise value or a share count invalidated by a transaction that closed AFTER the last 10-K: ACR's manager internalization (share count +88% three days after the shares_asof date), SSRM's $1.49B Çöpler sale (EV overstated ~$2.2B, and the non-controlling interest still in the extract belongs to an asset that was sold), ALKS's Avadel acquisition ($1.525B of term loans drawn six weeks after the year end, so the model reports EV below market cap). fundamentals_age_days is printed but not acted on. Read the 8-K list before trusting any EV: items 2.01, 1.01 and 3.02 in the last two quarters are the tell.

  • A gap on a financial is a bug until the equity line is decomposed — but decompose it against

the FILING, not by assuming. justified_pb computed tangible common equity as equity − goodwill − intangibles and deducted neither non-controlling interests nor preferred. Preferred always belongs out. NCI only sometimes does, and which it is depends on the XBRL concept the extractor happened to pick: StockholdersEquity is parent-only and already excludes NCI, while StockholdersEquityIncludingPortionAttributableToNoncontrollingInterest and IFRS Equity include it. JXN's carries $533M preferred and $389M NCI (TBV/share $146.95 against a correct $133.34) and needs both deducted. MFIN's does not: its balance sheet reads "Total stockholders' equity 408,617" with "Non-controlling interest 99,429" listed SEPARATELY below, and its $10.46 was right all along — deducting would have understated tangible book 41%. This is the correction to a claim previously recorded here, that the omission "silently overstates cheapness across all 338 Financials". It does so only for the subset whose extract used an including-NCI concept, and on 2026-09-01 an analyst acting on the broad version of this lesson published a wrong verdict on MFIN — asserting a 71% overstatement of tangible book that did not exist. A prior lesson is a hypothesis to test against the filing, not a conclusion to apply. fundamentals() now records equity_concept and justified_pb deducts NCI only when the concept includes it, flagging the name when the concept is unknown rather than guessing in either direction.

  • GAAP net income is not a return for anything whose earnings are a mark. justified_pb averages

three GAAP ROTCE ratios into a "sustainable" return; for JXN one of the three is 0.3%, because 2025 GAAP was a $(17)M loss to common against $22.67 of adjusted operating EPS on non-economic hedge and MRB marks. The unstable_rotce flag names the symptom and the model averages the artifact in anyway. When one defensible input choice moves the answer from −48% to +77%, the output is not a valuation.

  • Convertible notes are invisible to the debt extractor, and the error always reads as cheapness.

FIELDS["total_debt"] covers the LongTermDebt* / Borrowings family and debt_current covers DebtCurrent and LongTermDebtCurrent; neither touches the ConvertibleNotesPayable* / ConvertibleDebt* concepts a convertible issuer actually tags. TDOC on 2026-09-01 reported total_debt of $42.4M against $996.7M of 2027 convertible notes sitting on the balance sheet — enterprise value understated 3.4x ($413M vs ~$1.41B), which moved the growth the market implies from −43.0% to −15.8%, and to −2.0% with stock comp expensed. The whole +220% gap that won it an opportunistic slot was missing debt. Missing debt lowers EV, lowers implied growth and manufactures a positive gap every time, so this bug does not produce noise, it produces false buys. A scan for non-financial FCFF names whose interest expense cannot be serviced by the debt the model can see returned 142 of them, with gaps like BIPC +1962%, COLL +1702%, RPD +413%, UPBD +202%. Treat any large positive gap on a known convertible issuer as this bug until proven otherwise, and check interest_expense / total_debt as the cheap detector.

  • The EX-99 exhibit path has never returned anything, and the brief says so in language that reads

like a fact about the company. filing_documents keys off item["type"] from EDGAR's directory index.json, but that endpoint carries no exhibit type and no description — its type field is the icon filename ("text.gif", "compressed.gif"). So d["type"].startswith("EX-99") is never true for any filing, and every brief prints "No EX-99 exhibits filed under a material 8-K item" regardless. All ten names on 2026-09-01 printed it while filing 8-Ks under items 2.02 and 9.01. ANF's Q2 press release was sitting in that same directory as q22026pressrelease.htm, 416KB, and it contained the fact that decided the name: $100M of one-time IEEPA tariff refunds inside a headline EPS beat. adhoc.py calls the same function, so the workaround is to fetch the filing directory index and then the exhibit URL directly. The exhibit type lives in the SGML header (<accession>-index-headers.html), not in the directory listing.

  • One-time tariff refunds are a 2026 cohort event, not a company story. ANF (~$100M pre-tax in Q2,

$1.75/share, $120M and $2.10/share guided for the year) and CRI ($132M including interest inside H1 operating cash flow, with the tax payable the following quarter) both appeared in the same ten. Both flattered headline results while the underlying line went the other way — ANF's ex-refund Q2 operating income was ~$153M against $168M adjusted a year earlier. When one name in a screen shows an IEEPA refund, check every other importer in the cohort before reading any earnings beat as operating improvement.

  • Before carrying a prior verdict forward, go and get the observable the last devil's advocate

nominated. The 2026-08-31 morning pass on BBW named the settling evidence exactly — "revenue falling while pre-tax margin compresses" — and then recorded that it was "not yet visible". It was visible and one search away: Q2 gross margin −340bp to 54.2% on occupancy deleverage and promotions, pre-tax income $11.6M vs $15.3M. The verdict went cheap → no_edge on the afternoon pass. A red team that names its own test and never runs it is doing half the work.

  • Rental and leasing fleet purchases are invisible to the capex extractor, and the error always reads as

cheapness. CTOS's capex_series is [$0.87M, $3.07M] — under $4M across two years for a company that bought $191.6M of rental equipment in the six months to 2026-06-30 and guides $170-200M of NET fleet investment for 2026. The extractor takes only non-rental property capex, so every dollar of fleet spending stays in CFO. The model therefore reported a $241.5M normalized FCFF base against the company's own guidance of levered free cash flow "in excess of $50 million", and the whole +184% gap and 95th-percentile cohort rank came from that. ALTG has the same shape ([$9.2M, $15.4M, $12.4M] for a dealer with a rental fleet). Understated capex overstates free cash flow and manufactures a positive gap every time, so like the convertible bug this produces false buys, not noise. Suspect it on any equipment rental, leasing or fleet name; the cheap detector is capex as a percentage of revenue against the fleet purchases disclosed in investing activities.

  • Revolver and ABL balances get dropped from total_debt, and the same interest-coverage detector catches

it. ALTG's extract records $497.2M — the $500M of second-lien notes net of issuance costs — while the 2026-06-30 10-Q reports $730.6M of debt and finance leases, because the $209.6M line of credit is missing entirely, and $85.2M of used/rental floor plan sits outside both. interest_expense / total_debt reads 17.8% against stated coupons of 9.00% and 5.3%, which is impossible. Screening the universe for an implied rate above 12% together with a gap above +50% returns 25 names, including UPBD, COLL, THRY, HOV, ARKO, BXC, STNG and UVV — several already flagged as convertible suspects, which suggests one detector is finding two different missing-debt bugs. Missing debt lowers EV, lowers implied growth and manufactures cheapness, always in that direction.

  • A name under an announced all-cash merger is not a valuation, and nothing in the engine knows it. SLAB

agreed on 2026-02-04 to be acquired by Texas Instruments at $231.00 cash, closing H1 2027; at $219.12 it is a 5.4% merger spread. The reverse DCF dutifully reported +48% implied growth and an -80.9% gap, and will keep reporting it every session until the name delists. The tape says it plainly without any news at all: 5d/21d/63d returns of +0.3%/+0.5%/+0.2% are a price pinned to a fixed number, not a market opinion about cash flows. Treat near-zero dispersion across all three return windows as a deal-stock tell and check for a pending acquisition before spending a slot.

  • A failed beta regression still gets used, and on a financial it is the whole gap. GLRE's cost of equity

of 6.5% comes from a beta of 0.31 whose R-squared is 0.068, recorded with beta_source = regression — the gate did not fire. Combined with a "sustainable" ROTCE of 10.6% averaged over a range the model itself flags as unstable (6.7%-14.6%), against a company whose book value per share rose 0.9% in six months, that produced a justified P/TBV of 1.91 against an actual 0.74 and a +170.8% gap. The tangible book was right — I verified it against the 10-Q — so this is the JXN pattern again: on a financial, check BOTH inputs to the justified multiple, not just the book value. OSCR carries the same defect (R-squared 0.051). Note that the analyst JSON has no field to override cost of equity, so a name whose gap is produced by a bad beta cannot be corrected in the record — only the verdict can say so.

  • **The failed-beta problem is not a handful of names, it is a ranking bias across the whole screen, and

BETA_MIN_R2 = 0.04 is why. The GLRE and OSCR entries above treated a bad regression as a per-name defect where "the gate did not fire". The gate fired correctly; the threshold is the bug. An R² of 0.04 means the regression explains 4% of the variance, so essentially every regression passes and is stamped beta_source = regression. A noisy regression attenuates its slope toward zero (classic errors-in- variables), so the worse the fit, the lower the beta, the lower the cost of equity, the lower the WACC, the lower the implied growth, and the larger the positive gap. Measured across the 1,644 regression betas in the 2026-09-03 screen, median gap by R² bucket: 0.00-0.10 → +6.0% (median beta 0.81, n=373); 0.10-0.20 → −7.1% (beta 1.08); 0.20-0.30 → −28.1% (beta 1.10); 0.30+ → −33.4% (beta 1.01). That is a 39-point swing in apparent cheapness driven by fit quality alone, monotonic across four buckets. 246 of the 373 worst-fit names carry a beta below 1.0, and 57 have beta < 0.9 together with a gap above +50%. Do not read this as "low-beta names look cheap": the top sector in that 57 is Energy** (15 names — CRC, REPX, CRGY, TK, NVGS among them), which is the highest-beta cohort in the index, so this is measurement error and not genuine defensiveness. On 2026-09-03 it took ALL THREE Energy opportunistic slots — TDW (β 0.70, R² 0.082), RES (β 0.73, R² 0.085), PTEN (β 0.69, R² 0.061) — with gaps of +118%, +221% and +189%. Correcting TDW to a defensible OSV beta of ~1.4 moves the growth the market implies from +5.6% to roughly +16%, i.e. from cheap to rich. Like the convertible, ABL and rental-capex bugs, this error has a direction: it manufactures false buys, never false sells. Treat any large positive gap on a name with R² below ~0.15 as this bug until proven otherwise, and check beta_r2 before the thesis, not after.

  • **Averaging a ratio across a credit trough understates a recovered bank, the mirror of the acquisition

case.** The justified_pb lesson above warns against averaging book LEVELS across an acquisition. VLY on 2026-09-03 is the same error running the other way in the numerator: sustainable ROTCE of 9.08% is a three-year average spanning the 2024-25 CRE provisioning trough, while the actual quarterly series runs 9.07% → 10.02% → 11.56% → 11.91% (12.05% adjusted), improving monotonically over four quarters with tangible book per share compounding ~8% annualized. A reserve release flatters earnings while book value stagnates; rising ROTCE and rising tangible book together is an operating recovery. Overriding to 11.5% moves the gap from −36.4% to roughly −15%. The general rule: before accepting an averaged ratio on a financial, look at the quarterly path — a monotonic trend means the average is describing a company that no longer exists, in whichever direction it is trending.

  • **The multiples fallback is not a weak signal on a transforming or non-operating company, it is noise

with a decimal point, and its own internal disagreement is the tell.** CLAUDE.md already says to treat a multiples number on a pre-revenue name with suspicion. Two names on 2026-09-03 show the sharper test: when the three rows disagree by a factor of three, the blend is meaningless. RIOT blends to $10.12 vs a $18.64 price (−45.7%) while its own p_tbv row says $27.34 (+47%) and its ev_gross_profit row returns $-1.00 at every percentile because gross profit is negative — and the whole table prices bitcoin-mining revenue for a company that has just signed a 20-year, ~$9.1B power lease that has produced almost no revenue yet. EPRT blends to $21.40 from components of $26.63, $32.00 and $5.57, where the $5.57 is EV/sales applied to a REIT's rental revenue against a cohort median built from operating companies in the same Real Estate bucket. Both briefs also said "not ranked — too few comparable names". Rule: if the rows straddle the price in opposite directions, or any row is negative, or the cohort is unranked, report no_model and say the multiples number should be disregarded — do not average a nonsense input away.

  • On a book-method name the engine prices today's share price against LAST fiscal year-end's tangible book, while the multiples table on the same brief uses the current one. AII on 2026-09-04 reported book value per share of $18.86 at 2026-06-30 (up 10.1% in one quarter), and the extract's own equity_now of $369.5M reproduces it exactly — but justified_pb used $17.20, which is the 2025-12-31 equity of $337.0M over current shares. The tell is on the face of the brief: the p_tbv row of the multiples table read 1.387 while the book model read 1.52, and they are the same ratio on the same day. The error is not small and it has a direction — it understates the gap on a financial whose book is compounding and overstates it on one whose book is shrinking, by exactly the rate of book growth (here about ten percentage points of gap). Check actual_p_tbv against the multiples p_tbv on every book name; if they disagree, the book model is a period behind.
  • A "sustainable" ROTCE can be a single observation, and nothing says so. AII's 28.9% is not an average of anything: the extract holds exactly one year of net income ($97.4M) and one of equity ($337.0M), because the company listed in 2025. The unstable_rotce flag only fires on a RANGE, so a one-observation ROTCE is silently the most confident-looking input on the brief. It won an opportunistic slot with a +134% gap built on n=1, measured in a year with no catastrophe losses, on a single-state Florida homeowners insurer, priced at the peak of hurricane season. Before trusting any ROTCE, count the observations in net_income_common_series — a series of length 1 deserves the same suspicion as a range of 12% to 51%.
  • For a life or annuity writer, tangible common equity is the wrong denominator AND GAAP net income the wrong numerator, and the two errors compound. This is the JXN lesson taken to its conclusion. FG on 2026-09-04: the engine computed a 31.7% ROTCE against tangible book of $15.83 per share and produced a +166% gap, ranking it the cheapest 1% of 339 Financials. The company's own Q2 2026 release reports adjusted ROE excluding AOCI of 8.0% and ex-AOCI common equity attributable to common shareholders of $45.93 per share. Both engine inputs are wrong, in opposite directions. The mechanism: AOCI on an insurer's bond portfolio is the dominant swing item and GAAP equity carries it, while the tangible-book construction then subtracts $2.12B of FNF purchase-accounting goodwill and $551M of intangibles, leaving a small residual as the denominator. That residual computes NEGATIVE in 2023 (equity $3,103M less goodwill $1,749M less intangibles $4,207M = -$2,853M), so the 31.7% is the mean of just two surviving years, 12.0% and 51.4%. The intangibles series itself runs $4,207M -> $562M -> $551M, a concept switch rather than a write-off, and it is what makes the 2024 denominator small enough to print 51.4%. Rule: on any insurer whose earnings are marks, get the company's own ex-AOCI book and adjusted ROE before reading the engine's gap as anything at all.
  • fy_end can be mislabelled while the underlying series are current — do not cap a verdict on the staleness flag alone. HWC on 2026-09-04 carried last_10k_5361d_old with fy_end: 2011-12-31, which reads as fourteen-year-old fundamentals and looked like the most serious defect in the day's ten. It was not: net income to common of [$486.1M, $460.8M, $392.6M] and equity of [$4,460.1M, $4,127.6M, $3,803.7M] are all current, goodwill matches the post-Whitney franchise, and tangible book per share of $43.23 reconciles exactly to the 2026-06-30 balance sheet. The 13.9% ROTCE recomputes by hand and is right. The flag is cosmetic on this filer. The general point cuts both ways: an age flag is a prompt to reconcile the series against the filing, not a conclusion — and on the same name the genuinely invalidating event (the One Florida Bank acquisition, $377.6M cash, closed 2026-08-01, one month after the balance sheet) carried no flag at all.
  • A structural transaction can make the entire FCFF history obsolete without firing a single flag, because it changes the cost base rather than the share count or the balance sheet. The engine watches for acquisitions, peaks and troughs. It does not watch for a company selling the asset its cash flows depend on. Lands' End on 2026-04-01 contributed all of its brand IP to a joint venture, sold 50% to WHP Global for $300M, and licensed the brand back under a guaranteed minimum royalty of $50.0M per year through fiscal 2036 — a fixed charge larger than the mean of the three-year FCFF series the model was discounting ($43.8M ex-interest). Royalty expense was $15.4M in Q2 alone. None of the three flags raised (interest_expense_implies_no_debt, lumpy_fcff, possible_trough_cycle_base) points at it. Worse, the same transaction repaid the $234.0M term loan, so the model was ALSO adding back $27.5M of after-tax interest on debt that no longer exists, while the ABL drawn $60.0M at 2026-07-31 was missing from enterprise value (the ALTG revolver bug again). Three errors, all in the cheap direction, producing a +116% gap; on management's own guidance the price is roughly right. Read the 8-K list for item 1.01 in the last four quarters on any name whose gap survives the usual checks — a licence, a royalty or a long-term supply agreement can gut a cash-flow base as thoroughly as an acquisition, and leaves less trace.
  • The stale share count is not a per-name flag, it is a ranking bias across the whole screen, and it is concentrated in dual- and multi-class filers. share_count_..._stale_market_cap_unreliable fires on 104 of 1,956 names, median staleness 1,769 days and maximum 5,800 (HUBG). 22 carry a positive gap, 19 of those above +50%, and 15 sit in the 90th-plus percentile of their sector cohort — being ranked among the cheapest names in the index on a market capitalisation that does not exist. The mechanism: dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding is tagged PER CLASS on a member axis by a dual-class filer, so an un-dimensioned read falls back to whatever stale un-dimensioned fact survives. The population is exactly what that predicts — CENT/CENTA, BELFA/BELFB, HVT, GTN, HUBG, VLGEA, JBSS, MBUU, FNKO, TBLA, ZGN, TTAN. TTAN on 2026-09-07 reports 82,746,425 Class A plus 12,651,154 Class B = 95.4M shares, so market cap is $8.39B against the brief's $3.11B, and its "own" EV/sales reads 2.8x against a true ~7.0x — top quartile of its cohort reported as the median, and a "+11.6% blended discount" that inverts. Two names are catastrophic rather than merely wrong: MBUU's market cap computes to $334.56 and FNKO's to $568.00 — dollars, not millions — producing gaps of +160,066,846% and +140,231,237% and cohort percentiles of exactly 100.0, i.e. sitting at the top of the Consumer Discretionary cheapness ranking that the HOG and WEN briefs cite in the same session. Like the convertible, ABL, rental-capex and low-R² beta bugs, this error has a direction: an understated share count understates market cap, understates EV, lowers implied growth and manufactures cheapness, never the reverse. It also contaminates every OTHER name, because the corrupted rows sit inside the cohort distributions used for the percentile rank and the multiples quartiles. Check share_count_..._stale before the thesis; treat any multi-class ticker as guilty until the cover page is read.
  • A CLOSED acquisition stays in the universe and spends a slot; the SLAB lesson only covered pending ones. APGE was researched on 2026-09-07. AbbVie completed its acquisition of Apogee Therapeutics on 2026-09-03 at $135.11 per share in cash and the stock ceased trading on Nasdaq before that morning's open. The engine priced a 49-day-stale $133.96 quote, computed a +249% 252-day "return" that is the deal premium, and handed the name a rotation slot four days after the shares stopped existing. Four tells were on the face of the brief: stale_price_49.0d, an 8-K carrying items 2.01 (completion of acquisition), 3.01 (delisting notice), 3.03 and 5.01 (change in control), no modellable fundamentals at all, and a return series that is a premium rather than an opinion. The SLAB entry above teaches the pending deal tell (near-zero dispersion across 5d/21d/63d); this is the completed one, and it is louder. Gate on 8-K items 3.01 and 5.01, and on a price staler than about five sessions, before selection — not after.
  • On a financial, the stale-tangible-book bug is not a ten-point error when a write-down sits in the intervening quarter — it is the entire verdict. The AII entry above records justified_pb pricing today's share price against LAST fiscal year-end's tangible book, worth about ten points of gap on a compounding bank. BUR on 2026-09-07 is the limit case. The brief reports tangible book of $10.54/share and an actual P/TBV of 0.41, ranking it the cheapest 1% of 340 Financials at a +177.9% gap. Burford's own non-GAAP reconciliation in the 2Q26 10-Q gives $10.57 at 2025-12-31 and $3.12 at 2026-06-30 — the engine used the December number. True P/TBV is ~1.39x: a premium, not a 59% discount. What happened in between is that on 2026-03-27 the Second Circuit reversed the $18B YPF judgment, and the consolidated fair value of the YPF-related assets fell from $2.6B to $160.4M, taking equity attributable to Burford from $2,448.0M to $819.1M and retained earnings from $1,800.9M to $157.2M. The general rule: the size of this error is the size of whatever happened since the last fiscal year end, so it is largest on exactly the names where something happened. On any book-method name, reconcile tangible_book_per_share to the company's own latest disclosure before reading the gap at all — and note that the actual_p_tbv vs multiples-table p_tbv cross-check named in the AII entry only catches it when the multiples table is populated, which it was not here.
  • Selling a captive finance book breaks the FCFF history and the debt extraction at the same time, and no flag fires for either. HOG on 2026-09-07 raised no flags and both of its inputs were wrong. Enterprise value: the 2026-06-30 balance sheet carries short-term debt $613.1M, current portion of long-term debt $498.5M and long-term debt $1,130.8M, plus $516.5M of brokered certificates of deposit, against $1,895.8M of cash. The brief's $2.7B EV reconciles exactly to market cap plus the LongTermDebt family less cash — it dropped $613.1M of unsecured commercial paper and $516.5M of deposits, so true EV is ~$3.25B ex-deposits or ~$3.76B including them. That is the ALTG revolver bug in two new tag families (ShortTermBorrowings, and deposits, which are not debt-tagged at all); watch for it on any issuer with a bank or finance subsidiary. FCFF: the base of $633.1M is the mean of $415.2M / $867.3M / $547.5M, which is the cash flow of a company with a $7B retail loan book that was sold in the second half of 2025. Net finance receivables fell 64% year over year to $2.6B, HDFS operating income is guided to $55-65M for all of 2026 against $133.8M in the first half of 2025 alone, consolidated debt fell from $6,854.8M to $2,242.4M in twelve months, and first-half 2026 consolidated operating cash flow was NEGATIVE $59.7M against +$509.5M a year earlier. FY26 guided segment operating income sums to roughly -$5M to +$35M. That is a +299% gap and a 99th-percentile cohort rank on a company whose operating income this year rounds to zero. This is the Lands' End lesson — a structural transaction voids the cash-flow history without touching the share count — but the tell is different and cheaper: compare the sum of the company's own forward segment guidance against the FCFF base. A factor of twenty is not a modelling difference.
  • An insurer's ROTCE can be a reserve-release artefact, and the unstable_rotce flag does not fire on it because the series is stable. AMSF on 2026-09-07: sustainable ROTCE of 24.2%, cost of equity 7.0%, justified P/TBV 4.47 against an actual 1.91, a +133.7% gap and the 98th percentile of 340 Financials, with no flags raised — and the tangible book was correct ($13.57 against the company's reported $13.49), so the AII/BUR check passes cleanly. The defect is in the numerator and it is invisible to every gate the engine has. Q2 2026: net combined ratio 95.4% (from 91.7%), current accident year loss ratio 72.0%, prior accident year loss ratio -9.4 points from $7.3M of favourable development on accident years 2023 and prior. Strip the development and the accident-year combined ratio is ~104.8% — the business written today runs at an underwriting LOSS, and the reported return is the release cycle plus the yield on the float. The development is also shrinking ($7.3M from $8.6M), frequency is rising back toward 2023 levels, book value per share fell 3.4% year over year, and the company itself describes "moderating reserve redundancies". At a defensible 12% the justified multiple is 2.0 against an actual 1.91 and the +134% gap disappears. The detector is cheap and should be run on every insurer before the ROTCE is believed: prior accident year development as a share of the reported combined ratio. A GAAP ROE that needs nine points of releases is not a sustainable return, and a smooth three-year series of such ROEs is the most confident-looking input on the brief.
  • The D&A concept can be understated by an order of magnitude, and this one manufactures false REFUSALS rather than false buys. SND's dep_amort_series reads [$2.39M, $2.60M, $2.54M] for a company that owns sand mines, processing plants and rail terminals; true depreciation is roughly an order of magnitude larger.
